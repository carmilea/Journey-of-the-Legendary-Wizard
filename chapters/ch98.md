She knocked on Adrian’s door, and stood with her hands by her side until his dry voice called “Come,” just as she had done every time for the last two years. Then she entered.

The laboratory smelled exactly as she remembered. The sharp tang of chemicals, the regular tick from the burner, the polished brass that reflected the grey light from the high window in a brighter pool along the long stone bench. The pots of tiny clippings sat beneath the window as always, filling the air with their earthy scent, competing with the bite of the chemicals. Everything was the same, and that made her throat close up a little.

Adrian stood at the bench, his glasses glowing with an eerie green light. He held a test tube over the burner, a bubbling green potion in the tube rolling at a boil, while tiny black flames, like thin snakes, slid out of empty air to lick the glass.

[Glasses: green glow. Visual only. Black flame: heatless. Matches his lab on first day here.] [Filing green liquid colour under Winnie’s comment about harmful potions.] *He’s brewing something for someone else again. You’ll tell me later.*

Adrian let out a long slow breath, and put the test tube into the rack. As soon as it touched the wood, the black flames became thinner, then disappeared. The bubbling green liquid settled down, thickening until it was half set, a thick translucent gel.

The green light in the lenses of Adrian’s glasses faded, like someone turning a lamp down low. Behind the lenses, Adrian’s face looked old and tired. The lines were carved deep, grey in the light from the window.

Maureen bowed. “Mentor.”

Adrian pushed his glasses up and looked at her with a softness that she wasn’t used to seeing in his eyes. “Good. You have saved me a trip. I have put a few things together for you.”

He raised his hand, and the air in front of him rippled, like heat hazing over a road. A long thin box, dark gold in colour, slid out of nowhere, hanging in the air in front of her at the height of her hands.

“Take it.”

Maureen took it. It was long. It was cold. It weighed more than she thought it would. Its surface was faintly etched beneath her thumbs. She looked down at it. It lay there in her hands, as if it belonged to someone else.

[Dark-gold box. Contents: no data. Not opened.]

*Did you want to open it in front of him?* Tally asked.

*No,* she thought back silently.

*Good. Then we can be curious about it together later. Privately.*

She bowed again, but this time only briefly. Mostly because her hands needed something to do other than hold on to the box.

“Come back safe, Maureen,” Adrian said quietly.

Maureen held her breath for a beat, then chose her words as carefully as she would have chosen a reagent, measuring each drop with care, making sure that none spilt and everything stayed in its place. She felt the omission sit heavily in her chest, like a stone she had swallowed.

“I’ll be safe, Mentor. That much I’m sure of.” She said safe. She did not say back.

Tally let one beat pass before speaking. *You didn’t say back.*

*I know what I said,* Maureen thought back.

*So did he,* Tally pointed out.

Adrian smiled anyway, small and tired behind his glasses. He asked nothing, as if the answer were exactly what he’d expected to hear.

*I’m putting that in a column of its own again,* Tally said.

*Fine.*

“Do you have all you need for your journey?” Adrian asked. “Remember, the Panso Mountains are Olivia ground, and the Olivias keep long memories.”

“I’ll keep my head down, Mentor.”

*The list of things you didn’t tell him. The Seal. Patton. The tree. The stones.*

*I don’t need it. I know it by heart.*

*And that’s the problem with lists.*

She could feel the Seal lying damaged and cool against her ribs, beneath her robe. She stood before the one person who had ever refused to sell her, and she said none of it.

Adrian turned back to his rack and the gel, which was cooling now. “Go on, then. Don’t keep the horses waiting.” It was a dismissal, the gentlest one he’d ever given her.

She glanced at the potion once more from the doorway, the half-set mixture glowing a faint green from inside its glass vessel. Grey light caught the polished brass and made it shine in the dimness. Adrian’s white-and-gold robe, his grey hair combed neatly back, his grey eyebrows, his spectacles.

*Tally?* Maureen asked silently. *How many times have I been here? In this lab?*

*I have it.*

*Don’t tell me.*

*I won’t. But I have it.*

Maureen nodded and turned away, pulling the door closed behind her. The latch clicked shut and suddenly she couldn’t hear the ticking of the burner any longer. The corridor was quiet. So quiet.

* * *

The sound of her boots rang through the long black-stone corridor and the dark-gold box pressed cold against her arm beneath her sleeve. There was a smell to the air, dampness beading on the walls, old incense burned in some forgotten ritual. And there were footsteps, echoing slightly, the cellar-like cold coming up off the flagstones and into her bones.

A shadow servant glided toward her, making no sound at all upon the stone floor, and behind it came Mace. Summoned. Heading toward the door she’d just pulled closed.

[Mace: Unable to scan.]

Tally was silent for a moment, but then it began to read him by sight. His posture had changed, it said. *He doesn’t walk like Mace anymore. He walks like Frey. He walks like a family.*

The senior, the gentle apprentice who had once shown her the wonders of potion-work, was gone from the way he carried himself. His back was straight, his chin unhurried, his cuffs perfectly aligned, and he moved with the unconcerned ease of those who had never needed to hurry for anyone, ever, in their lives. His eyes found hers for the space of half a breath, and he gave her a polite nod and a warm smile that did not move at all.

[Mace: sister, Anna. Deceased.]

*He doesn’t know,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*He doesn’t know yet,* Tally responded. *Keep walking. Silver Light.*

Cold crept up the back of her neck as the last word that Anna had spoken in the realm came to her mind. Three long breaths, one short breath . . .

Her pace did not change by a single step.

They passed. She did not turn. By sound, Tally reported that the door to Adrian’s lab had opened and shut behind Mace.

* * *

When she reached her rooms, Maureen made her final check. The floor hollow was empty. The dark-gold box was wrapped in cloth in her pack. The Seal was on her body, as was the Silver Soul Token, the cards, and the bronze book. Freya’s slender Cross Sword rode her hip.

She paused for a moment to look around the rooms. There was nothing left of hers in them. Not even the washroom mirror that she’d meditated before for two years now seemed like it belonged to her.

[On body: Seal, damaged. Token. Cards. Bronze book.] [Carriage: grenades 34.]

*Enough,* Maureen said silently.

*Enough to count, anyway,* Tally responded.

Below the stable levels, a shadow servant waited for her next to her spell-hardened carriage and its horn-scale black horses. It pointed her toward the black iron freight lift. The platform shuddered as it began to move upward under the protest of its groaning chains and grinding gears. The black iron scent of machine oil mingled with the iron tang of horse dung, thick and cloying in the narrow darkness. The horses stamped and blew behind her as the lift climbed.

At the top, the Dark Forest guard held up a hand to stop Maureen. “Elevate,” she said, and Adrian’s brand flared into existence on her wrist, the black lines traced by a faint red light. The guard nodded and waved her through to the outer yard.

Tally did what it always did, and counted the turns. Sixteen turns later, thin grey daylight streamed down from above. *It’s colder than last time,* she thought.

*Data supports you.*

* * *

The yard was full of creaking leather harness and the sound of hooves clattering on the black stone. Names were shouted across the yard, their echoes bouncing around inside the dark stone walls, while the smell of oiled leather and horse dung hung in the cold air. Nobody met anybody else’s eyes for longer than a few seconds.

Maureen Olsen wasn’t being posted out alone, which meant that she had to find her party and ride out with them. She located them easily enough, by the wagon that was heading towards the Panso Mountains. Three apprentices, two men and a woman, and an escort of knights.

[Middle Knights: 6, by sight.]

*Not worth it,* Maureen said silently.

*Not worth looking at, or not worth worrying about?* Tally asked.

*Both.*

*That is my word,* Tally said.

She walked past the knights without turning her head to look at any of them. Once upon a time a Lower Knight such as Jax would have been a challenge to be met, but now only a peak Upper Knight such as Julo would have earned even a glance from Maureen. Instead she came to a halt in front of the three apprentices.

“Maureen,” she said. “Maureen Olsen.”

There was a short pause while her name reached them before she did. A realm survivor, she supposed they thought. Three pairs of eyes flicked down to the black-lit blade hanging at Maureen’s hip before returning quickly to her face.

The quiet man spoke first. “Hasen.”

Hasen. A shade denser than the woman, both below Maureen. Third-Class, most likely, although that was provisional until Maureen saw him working.

Hasen wore a plain travel robe, and his eyes were careful as they moved from Maureen to the woman standing next to him, pausing there a moment too long.

The woman tilted her chin up. “Lise.”

Her eyes moved slowly up and down Maureen once, making sure that Maureen knew that she was being looked over. There was no reason that Maureen could see for the other apprentice to be so haughty.

>That is not an appraisal of you, Maureen. That is status. She is judging your clothing.

*Let her.*

The look bounced off Maureen like a drop of water on oiled leather. She’d been looked over by Evans, and she’d been looked over by the wizards who guarded the gates, counting their sheep as they went through. In comparison, Lise’s raised chin was a puff of breeze.

“So you’re Maureen!” The golden-haired youth stepped forward, his hair shining in the grey light like it had a light of its own. His smile was broad, his laugh loud enough that it echoed in the outer yard, inappropriate for the place. “Good! I’m glad it’s you riding out to the Panso Mountains with us.”

He grinned again, as though he had just remembered something important. “Oh, my name’s Daniel.”

>Daniel, Daniel. That makes two. The first Daniel brushed powder across your left sleeve. Logged as dust.

*That Daniel is dead, Tally.*

>Which is why I mention it now.

Maureen’s hand moved to brush at her left sleeve before she even realised what she was doing. It was a reflex, built up from her time in Olit’s shop. Fortunately, she was able to turn the motion into an absent straightening of her cuff.

>Daniel: tells none. Not proof.

*Log it as friendly,* Maureen thought at Tally.

>Logged as friendly. Unconfirmed.

Maureen gave Daniel a nod and a few words in return, but did not encourage further conversation. While Daniel continued to talk merrily about the road ahead, Hasen signalled to the six knights who had been assigned to their party, and Lise mounted up without so much as a glance to anyone else. The carriage wheels creaked as Maureen climbed onto her seat, taking the reins in hand. The horn-scale horses snorted twin streams of steam as they adjusted to the new weight, and the sound of Daniel’s laughter echoed over the rumble of the carriage wheels rolling away behind Maureen.

*South and west,* Tally said, laying the route over Maureen’s vision in blue light. *Panso Mountains. Ninety-two percent.* The numbers were never exact.

*What about the other eight percent?* Maureen asked silently.

>I’ll worry about the eight. You drive.
