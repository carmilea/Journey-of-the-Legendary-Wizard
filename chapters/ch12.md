Maureen Olsen reattached the Cross Sword to her waist, sliding the blade home slowly. A single drop of Carey’s blood was still suspended on the point, before gravity finally asserted itself. Maureen's expression remained pleasant. The muscles in her jaw ached from the tension needed to keep it that way, and she kept her fingers locked around the sword's hilt, refusing to relax her grip.

*Carey wanted you for his servant,* Tally pointed out without being asked. *If we were out in the wilds you would have killed him already. Six black-clad knights visible at the fires. Two apprentices, known sources within the camp circle. Fighting is banned. Killing even more so. Carey is the most magically apt seed the Blue Ring has.*

*I know,* she responded silently.

*I know you know, but I am telling you anyway as your hand is still on the hilt of your sword. Scarlette says that the Blue Ring and Hader Castle are white wizard orders, dedicated to the highest ideals of virtue, justice and order.*

*They'll hang me very virtuously then.*

Maureen took her hand off the sword, one finger at a time. Her eyes were watering slightly from the smoke and the wool of her hood was scratching the back of her neck.

“You looked so cold just now,” Richard said, “I half expected you to actually kill Carey.”

“Of course not,” Maureen laughed, making sure she kept the bloodied tip of the sword away from him.

*Your laugh was a quarter beat late,* Tally noted.

*Keep your notes to yourself if you don't mind,* Maureen responded silently.

“Don't brood,” Richard said, patting her shoulder before craning his neck to look towards the clearing, a grin splitting his face. “There are plenty of pretty girls tonight.”

Torchlight and laughter spilled out from the clearing ahead. Maureen tugged her hood forward and followed Richard.

* * *

The central clearing was crowded with silk and velvet. The hems of all the finely dressed seeds were liberally dusted with grey. Pitch smoke from the torches mounted on stakes around the clearing drifted across the tables, spoiling the scent of the wine.

“My friends, I am Anthony of the Principality of Gasha, your host this evening,” said a young man, stepping into the light from the torches. He was handsome, with bright golden hair that sparkled in the torchlight. He bowed to the crowd in a perfect, textbook noble manner.

A murmur of appreciation ran through the crowd.

Anthony smiled. “Thank you for coming, thank Aaron for carrying my invitations, and have a good evening. Please, get acquainted.”

He stepped back, standing aside as the talk began to grow louder.

*Flawless,* Tally told her. It was almost smug. *That was the perfect nobleman’s bow. I measured it against every bow I had recorded at the Skran manor.*

*I thought the Duke's bow was stiffer,* Maureen answered silently, keeping the conversation inside her head.

*The Duke's? Oh yes, you're right. His back never did bend properly.*

The talk grew louder, and Maureen soon lost Richard in the press of bodies. She stood alone with a glass of wine, holding the stemmed glass more for something to do with her hands than any desire to drink the contents. The wine smelled sour and she had yet to taste a drop. Around her noblemen and women discussed matters of importance to the nobility. Matters Maureen had no knowledge of and even less interest in. So instead Tally counted heads, forty three, and tagged conversations as Maureen walked past. Noble affairs, noble affairs, horse, noble affairs. *Is anyone talking runes, breathing techniques or swordsmanship?* Maureen asked silently.

Tally analysed the conversations and marked a pair of seeds standing near the far table. Maureen wandered over.

On the other side of the fire Carey was laughing too loudly. His eyes slid onto Maureen for a moment before jerking away and refusing to return. Richard reappeared through the crowd.

“Maureen I’ve brought some friends,” Richard announced as a flaxen haired girl came into view, followed by another girl who looked gentle and refined like a well bred lady.

“I’m Lucy. Grade three,” said the first girl.

“I’m Alice. Grade four.” The second girl added.

Maureen nodded. “Maureen Olsen. Grade five.”

A boy appeared next to Richard and the two girls. He was smiling broadly, his bright golden hair catching the light from the torches. His noble features were perfect and he held himself with easy confidence, radiating good manners. “Anthony. From the Principality of Gasha. Grade five.”

Up close Anthony had a pleasing smell, clean and fresh, like soap. In the torchlight a glint of gold thread caught Maureen’s eye where it decorated the cuffs of Anthony’s white shirt.

“I wish I was as strong as all of you,” Lucy said quietly.

“Oh we’re not that strong really. We’re all quite ordinary, quite ordinary,” Richard assured her earnestly.

Richard’s voice had risen in pitch by half a tone, Maureen noted. She and Anthony exchanged knowing smiles. *Do we make a note that Anthony is observant?* Tally asked.

*Yes.*

“The best aptitude here is Carey’s,” Alice said. “He’s grade seven, high aptitude.”

“Carey told me himself he’s already meditated five Spirit Runes,” Anthony said. “And the fastest anyone has ever reached First-Class Apprentice is three or four months. Yet Carey already has five runes and he hasn’t even been trying. It’s only been twenty odd days since he started.” Anthony sipped at his wine thoughtfully.

*That claim should be noted as an outlier,* Tally told Maureen. *We have Scarlette on record saying the fastest anyone has ever reached First-Class Apprentice is three to four months. However, we do not have any data on how quickly Carey actually made progress. Just his claim.*

Pitch smoke wafted from the torches. Maureen felt her fingers reflexively tighten on the stem of the cold glass. She counted her own silently. Two runes, the third one nearly there. She didn’t say anything.

Lucy gazed across the fire to where Carey was holding court with loud laughter and talk. He was wearing a velvet coat tonight. “I just envy him so much,” she sighed.

“Don’t forget Anna of Hader Castle,” Alice reminded them quietly.

“Who?” Maureen asked.

Anthony leaned forward slightly and lowered his voice. “Anna is... cold. An ice beauty. But very, very skilled. I’m sure she was knight trained. And... they say she has an enchanted item.”

Richard, Lucy and Alice all gasped. Even as Maureen’s eyes sharpened and focused on Anthony, he continued. “Yes, an enchanted item. Made by a wizard. A very powerful one too from what I hear. Strange powers. Very strong. So, the strongest seed here is Anna, not Carey.”

*New file opened: Anna of Hader Castle. Cold, knight trained (by report), enchanted item (by rumour). Tags: hearsay, hearsay.*

*Gossip.* Maureen told Tally silently.

*Yes.* Tally replied, just a touch smug. *Archivist of gossip.*

“I must be going,” Anthony said, bowing politely. Richard made his own goodnights, drifting off after Lucy.

Maureen set her still full glass down and shook her head with a smile. Richard was sweet on the girl.

* * *

The gathering broke up gradually, with most seeds walking back in twos or threes. One or two couples were seen slipping laughing into the woods. Maureen turned to look at them, the hood of her cloak shadowing her face, the Cross Sword hanging at her side. In this world, chastity was something that sat lightly upon most people. Most of these seeds came from the nobility, raised to understand the ways of the world. None seemed inclined to follow after the amorous couples, nor did they seem to care about their actions.

Voices murmured on both sides, and there was the occasional sound of heavy breathing from the darkness beneath the trees. Maureen felt the heat creeping up the back of her neck under the hood, and she started to walk faster.

*Local fauna?* Tally asked. *Would you like me to catalogue?*

*No.* Maureen thought back silently.

*Very well. I have stopped.*

Maureen shook her head wryly. Fourteen-year-old girls had better things to do than listen to animals mating in the woods. The night air was cold on her face and the damp leaf mould squished softly beneath her boots.

Abruptly, all the murmuring and rustling stopped, as if a blanket had been thrown over the entire stand of trees.

Tally flagged the sudden silence before Maureen even realised it. *No insects. No murmuring. No wind in the branches above. Silence.*

And then the smell hit her. Coppery. Fresh blood. It had been on record since Angrey’s beating and Tally helpfully supplied the description.

*How fresh is it?* Maureen asked silently.

*Very fresh. Strong signal. Wet signal.*

Slowly, Maureen pulled out the Cross Sword and moved forward, the hooded cloak making a soft rustle in the stillness. Her feet sought the path and avoided the twigs that could snap under her weight. The trunks of the trees were striped with moonlight. Maureen's breathing slowed to the steady Silver Light rhythm without conscious thought.

Two bodies lay on the leaves, pale in the moonlight. A young man and a young woman. Crouched between them was a boy dressed in a black robe. He was going through their clothes, his hands moving quickly and expertly over their bodies.

He looked up at her immediately. He had a young face and slightly pale skin, but his eyes were as cold as the butcher's block. Maureen frowned and stood still, waiting to see what would happen next. There was no alert from Tally about radiation.

*A seed or a man,* Tally told her. *Not a wizard.*

That made up her mind. She wasn't going anywhere.

The youth looked her up and down, like a hunter estimating the size of his prey. “Ha, there’s another one,” he said, licking his lips. His hand flicked and two wickedly sharp daggers appeared in his grip.

Fresh blood ran down the points of both blades. A single drop fell from one, hitting the damp leaves with a small tap.

> Two blades. Both wet.

Tally's terse line floated before Maureen's eyes and she nodded silently.
