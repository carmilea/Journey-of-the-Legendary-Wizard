"Maureen," Anthony shouted from his saddle, "the mission is in the Fasol Mountains. It's roughly 586 km southeast of the Dark Forest. There shouldn't be any great danger close to the forest but occasionally strange creatures still turn up so keep your eyes open."

Maureen nodded, feeling the sun warm on the backs of her hands. After nearly two years of black stone beneath her feet and black cloud over her head, it felt good to feel the sun on her skin again. Dry thornbush and scrub covered either side of the dirt track they travelled along, with the scent of horse sweat and dry grasses in the air. But just because they were out from under the cover of the forest didn't mean they were safer.

> Left. Low. Fast.

Tally flashed as Maureen caught movement at the extreme left edge of her vision.

A black shape burst from the scrub vegetation aiming for the horses before Maureen's brain had even processed what was happening.

"Careful, everyone!" Anthony yelled.

As the creature came into full view Tally overlaid it with an outline from its library. Lizard. The creature's venom sac and a cluster of nerves lit up on the display, familiar anatomy plates providing the reference. Jay laughed.

"It's just a venom lizard."

There was a muttered chant from Jay and he flung his hand forward, sending a ball of sour green liquid spinning through the air towards the creature. Acid Missile. Rank Zero spell.

The lizard was thrown backward by the impact, a trail of acrid smoke following it. Pieces of its melted flesh ran off in rivulets of green fluid which hissed as they hit the dusty ground. The smell of acid hung in the air making the horses shy.

Tally added the chant to its heard-spell list before matching it against the Acid Missile entry in the spell shop of the trading district, priced at fifteen stones.

Wolf ran over praising Young Master Jay's skill while Jay sat preening himself.

You know it's not dead yet? Tally's voice sounded in her head. Its warm tone made Maureen feel reassured.

What? She glanced over at the lizard's twitching body, remembering the blue-blood frog's lecture. Its nervous system was different than their own, the relevant nerves still firing long after the creature had died.

Tongue.

Now.

The word flashed across her display and she didn't need to look down to check. Her fingers were already on the string.

The lizard twisted as it writhed on the ground before shooting its long tongue out like a crossbow bolt. Anthony and the others yelled out as Maureen loosed her arrow. The shaft drove through the lizard's skull, pinning its head to the dirt. The lizard's tongue dropped from the sky, mid-flight. Reflexes had caused Maureen to fire the arrow. Her heart caught up with her actions a breath later. In the sudden silence all around her, everyone else stood in a cold sweat.

She lowered her longbow and addressed the group. "Venom lizards have different nervous systems from us. Their nerves are very active even after they die. Don't get too close unless you're sure they're dead."

They all looked at her differently now. Jay's face flushed bright red, and he couldn't seem to sit still in his saddle. He looked awkward and ashamed of himself.

Well done, thank the blue-blood frog for me. She thought silently to Tally.

It'll be thrilled.

* * *

The Fasol Mountains were still days away, so they decided to camp that night. Maureen sat by the thornwood fire, engaged in quiet conversation with Aaron. The dancing firelight played across Aaron's face, making his cheeks look hollow like a skull. His thumb was working the hem of his sleeve as he spoke. His voice was barely above a whisper.

Jay walked over to them. "Maureen? It is you, right? Come on, get your potion out quick. I need it." He held out his arm. "I've been bitten by a venomous snake, and I need an Antidote Potion."

Maureen looked up at him. Tally, any sign of swelling or limping? Or a bandage on him today?

No. No swelling or limp, no bandage.

No data on if he's been bitten.

Antidote Potion was one of the elementary potions. She could brew that much now. She pulled out a test tube and began to speak. Jay snatched the tube out of her hands without another word, then walked away.

He didn't even look at you once, Maureen. Not even once.

I noticed, she replied silently back.

"Maureen?" Aaron murmured.

She waved it off. "It's nothing."

The thornwood fire continued to pop and spit. Her hands rested quietly in her lap, the cold night air settling against her back. The smoke drifted over the bedrolls.

Later, Jay, Wolf, and Anthony strolled back to the camp. They were laughing about something. "Forget something?" Maureen asked Jay, pointing to the empty test tube still in his hand. "One standard dose. Unpaid."

"Oh. Thanks." Jay turned to walk away.

"You're welcome," she said. "An Antidote Potion costs five magic stones."

Jay stopped and turned. His face twisted into an affronted expression. "You want my stones? After I said thanks?"

"Hey!" Wolf shouted as he walked forward. "You don't know your place! Asking Young Master Jay for his stones! Are you blind? Don't you have eyes in your head?"

Mouth open. Now.

Tally timed Wolf's jaw movement and painted a line from Maureen's hand to his teeth.

She plucked a stone from the ground and flicked it at Wolf. The rock shot through the air and smashed into Wolf's lips, knocking several teeth out of his mouth.

Wolf staggered back, clutching his mouth. Blood leaked from between his fingers and dribbled down his yellow beard. He tried to speak, but only gurgled noises came out. His small eyes were filled with fear.

Maureen stood up and walked over to Jay. She held her hand out. "Five magic stones. Give them to me."

Jay's face turned grey. "Do... do you know who I am?" He stammered, his body shaking.

Wolf's weight is on his heels. He won't come again. Jay's not shaking because he's brave. It's rage. Anthony is reaching for his purse.

I know, she replied silently. I know.

I know you know.

She smiled warmly. "Jay, I said five magic stones."

Jay shivered.

Anthony stepped in. "Now, now, Maureen. Calm yourself. I'll pay for Young Master Jay." The team leader pulled out a handful of stones and pressed them into Maureen's pocket. Jay snorted and stalked off, followed by the wailing Wolf.

The stones were cool through the cloth of her pocket. Wolf's wet wailing faded as they moved beyond the tethered horses.

"Maureen?" Anthony rubbed the gold thread that decorated his cuffs. "Why fight Young Master Jay over five magic stones? One potion for his friendship is a good deal, is it not?"

She smiled. "Anthony, unless I'm willing, no one gets a potion from me for free. No one."

"His friendship? What's that worth? Five magic stones?" She shrugged and patted him on the shoulder. "Goodnight, Anthony." She turned and walked to her bedroll.

Anthony smiled wryly.

[Magic stones: +5]

Anthony values you five magic stones less than Jay's friendship, Tally commented.

He values Jay at five magic stones, she replied.

Also true, both columns.

Tired of talking to Anthony, she lay down on the cold ground. Above her, the stars sparkled between the bare branches of the trees.

* * *

For the next several days, the team rode in sullen silence. The closer they got to the Fasol Mountains, the more snakes they encountered. Bigger snakes as well, many highly venomous.

Tally kept a daily kill count, which it recited each night. The numbers went up, and so did the lengths of the bodies.

"Enough," Anthony said, cutting down a grey python as thick as a man's thigh. Wiping his brow with his sleeve, he stood over the creature's corpse. "We're here. The giant black serpent is in the cave ahead."

"Ugh," Jay said, holding a perfumed handkerchief to his nose. "If I'd known it was like this, I never would have come."

Maureen Olsen, that python tops our snake list, Tally said.

Tally, stop ranking snakes like apprentices, she thought back.

Someone has to, it replied.

She flicked her wrist, and a line of blood ran off the Cross Sword's edge, landing on the hot rock.

"All right, let's go," Anthony said, striding towards the huge, dark black mouth of the cave, breathing out a foul smell.

They followed him into the cave.

* * *

As soon as they passed the mouth of the cave, the darkness closed around them. The torches lit the wet stone, and the smell became more rank and strange.

Maureen pulled a tube of yellow Pest Repellent Potion from her belt pouch. "This reacts strongly to snakes and insects. If you follow me, we should be all right."

"Fine," Anthony said.

"Yeah," said Sim.

Jay snorted coldly.

Maureen pulled out the wooden stopper and walked ahead of them, following the scent and colour of the liquid.

Amber deepening, Tally said, reading the hue against the field trick stored in its library. Left fork.

They were attacked by snakes all the way to the cavern. Large and small, thick and thin, the reptiles struck at them again and again. The casters burned them away as fast as they could cast, and Aaron danced through the slithering mass, his sword flashing in the torchlight. His knight strength and skill let him cut through the serpents that got past the magical attacks.

Nice clean cuts, Tally said. That Aaron is a good fighter. His breathing is a little short for him, though. Guess he hasn't been sleeping much lately.

Blood ran warm and sticky over Maureen's knuckles and down to the grip of the Cross Sword. The sound of hissing echoed off the walls.

The tunnel opened up into a wide underground cavern. The light from their torches slid over coil upon coil of black scales. The air was thick with the musk of the serpent and the stench of its long dead prey. The coils rose and fell slowly with each breath the giant snake took. Tally calculated the size of the coils against the known length of the torches. [Est. 24 m ± 4] Its alert stayed silent. No energy radiation source detected. No source logged. Tally had no data on why a creature this large would remain so still.

The giant black serpent lay coiled in the middle of the cavern. Its eyes were half closed as it slept deeply. This must be the serpent that the mission briefing mentioned.

"Everyone, be careful," Anthony whispered. He motioned with his eyes to Jay's group.

The casters stepped back, putting some distance between themselves and the beast. They emptied their minds and began to chant under their breaths. Maureen felt the particles of energy begin to gather around her.

[18... 19… 20] Tally whispered in her head, [slow… 21… 22…] [ready when you are.]
