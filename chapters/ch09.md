Maureen paused before stepping through the flap into the testing tent, taking a deep breath before entering. It was empty except for a rhombus shaped crystal that was spinning slowly in midair. There was no string or rod holding the crystal up, there was no stand it was sitting on. There was no sound either. The thick hangings of the tent absorbed all the noise of the camp outside, muffling it until it was almost inaudible. The canvas walls were glowing red around her from the setting sun reflecting off the stone canyon walls.

"Now, young wizard seed, don't be afraid. Put your hand on the crystal."

The voice seemed to come from every side at once, making it impossible to place where it was coming from.

[Warning. Energy radiation source. Location unresolved.]

Maureen ignored Tally's cold alert as she spoke silently to it.

*Hold the alerts please Tally.*

Tally fell silent, but [Source: unresolved] remained pinned to the edge of her vision.

Maureen bowed her head slightly to the empty air, then stepped forward and placed her palm on the crystal. Ice met her palm, a bone deep cold that seemed to sit inside her rather than on the skin. Pain exploded in her skull, blow after blow like someone was using a hammer to drive a nail into her brain. The crystal began spinning faster, faster until it became a vortex. Maureen's eyes lost focus and went blank.

"Maureen!"

She could see Richard, Carey, Ellena, Aaron, Duke Skran and Aunt Mary, one after the other as they called out to her. They all seemed to be standing behind some kind of glass pane. She reached out for them, but couldn't quite touch them. Their faces were blurred, just out of focus so she couldn't make out their expressions.

Grey stone flowed up over each of their feet. It hardened as it rose up their bodies, until they all froze in place. Then their images began to blur and fade, until they were gone.

She was alone. In darkness that stretched forever around her. She looked up and saw fire burning along the horizon. It stretched all the way from left to right. There was no end to it. It burned fiercely, but gave off no heat. Maureen opened her mouth to shout, but found her throat was closed and no sound came out. She shivered as cold crawled up her body to her knees, then locked her limbs in place.

"Maureen Olsen?" A mild voice spoke her name.

The cold fell away from her, as warmth flooded into her from a hand placed on her shoulder. The warmth seeped into her bones, chivying away the chill. Maureen opened her eyes and found herself back inside the testing tent.

Scarlette stood behind her, his hand resting on her shoulder. No alerts fired for him, he was a known source. *Are you here?* Tally asked rapidly.

*Yes,* she thought back silently.

Maureen bowed her head. "Master Scarlette." Her hands wouldn't warm up so she tucked them into her sleeves.

Scarlette nodded in approval. "You did well, Maureen. Grade five aptitude. Darkness and fire are your highest affinities, with a little plant, lightning and illusion."

Maureen turned her attention to the crystal, which now contained a churning mass of black and fire. There were other colours too, green, purple and pink that drifted at the edges of the roiling mass like motes of dust or sparks from Andrew's forge. A pale blue flicker crossed her vision as Tally logged the colours.

"You may leave," Scarlette said.

Maureen bowed again, then walked out of the tent.

* * *

[Warning: host affected by unknown force field.] flashed across her vision as the tent flap fell shut behind her.

*Why didn't you warn me sooner?* she asked silently.

[There was insufficient data to make an alert. Nothing to match against.] Tally explained.

*Build a warning,* she ordered.

*With so little data, I am unable to build an early-warning mechanism,* Tally answered.

*You're useless,* she thought.

*I am exactly as useful as you have shown me.* Tally retorted.

*Touché.* She conceded the point. *We need to feed you everything we can about wizards.* She admitted.

*Agreed.* Tally responded.

Maureen stood for a moment outside the testing tent, in the red twilight of Sunset Canyon. White robes, grey armour and the smoke from a hundred campfires filled her vision. She knew far too little about wizards.

"You done?" Richard ran up to her, eager and simple hearted as ever.

Maureen nodded. "How about you?"

Duke Skran's second son scratched his head and grinned. "Grade five aptitude. Middle."

His grin was so guileless that Maureen found it hard not to smile back. Richard was in the same grade as she was. That seemed like an amazing coincidence, and for some reason made her want to giggle.

"Ten grades," Richard explained. "One to three is low aptitude, four to six is middle, seven to nine is high. Ten is theoretical really, it might not even exist. No one knows. It doesn't count anyway." He looked at her with eager eyes. "The chances of becoming a Wizard are sixty to seventy percent if you're high aptitude, twenty to thirty percent if you're middle, and ten percent or less if you're low."

*Middle band. Twenty to thirty per cent.* Tally supplied.

*But those are the odds for seeds without a Tally.* Maureen answered silently.

*I am pleased you think so.* Tally responded.

*I'm pleased too.*

Maureen kept her cold hands tucked into her sleeves. The rushing of the small river and the stamp of horses on picket lines filled the pause in conversation.

"Carey is grade seven, high aptitude. He's the only high aptitude one in the entire test," Richard said.

Maureen frowned. Seven and five didn't seem that far apart, but the bands were miles apart in terms of odds. She blew out a breath. "Very well. Let's go over there."

* * *

Aaron, Ellena, and Carey were already waiting outside of Scarlette's carriage when they arrived. Ellena was grade four, while Aaron was only grade three.

Carey kept clenching his fists then relaxing them, rocking back and forth on his heels. Aaron and Ellena were watching him with curious, measuring looks.

*What did you see inside the tent?* She asked Tally silently.

*A crystal, Maureen. Your eyes were open on it the entire time. I have no record of anything else.* She described the faces she saw, and Tally dutifully filed them away under the tag she called 'stone'.

*Maureen Olsen,* Tally said gently, *that is six names.*

*Yes, I know. That's Grandpa.* Her breath caught once. *Leave it please.*

*Very well.*

The last reds of the light drained from the canyon walls. Maureen shoved her cold hands further into her sleeves.

"I don't mind telling you those Dark Forest black-robes are a thorn in our side," came a voice through the wooden wall of the carriage. "Black wizards who reek of blood from head to toe."

"I couldn't agree more," said Scarlette's voice. "It's just lucky we've been hitting them together so much lately, and causing them heavy losses. Otherwise things would be very troublesome."

Tally stored Dark Forest and black-robes, reek of blood in its database as new terms to be researched later when more information was available. Much more information, it added.

A hunched, extremely thin wizard apprentice clambered out of the carriage. He glanced the seeds over with a strange laugh before he... exploded?

His body burst apart with a pop and when the smoke cleared he was gone. A spike alert flashed across her vision. Tally dutifully recorded his silhouette, the way he walked hunched over, and the sound of his laugh so it could match it against future sightings.

The smoke had a sharp, bitter smell that lingered in her nose even as it drifted away, thinning into the haze of campfire smoke.

"All right, come on in." Came Scarlette's voice from inside the carriage.

The seeds pulled themselves together, and climbed into the carriage.

* * *

The velvet seats were worn smooth and shiny by years of use, the five seeds squished together under the hanging lamp. Their knees bumped against each other uncomfortably as Scarlette spoke, the smell of the old man and his ink mixing with the air.

Scarlette waited until everyone was seated before he continued. "Now that I know your aptitudes and affinities, I can begin teaching you how to meditate Spirit Runes." He leaned back on his seat and smiled, the white beard splitting into two. "The wizard world welcomes you, future wizards."

Even Aaron, the lowest graded seed, sat forward eagerly. His eyes brightened with interest.

"The structure of every Spirit Rune is the same. First you form the outer structure, then you reinforce it, then you add the internal details. Each of these parts must be done carefully, or it will fail." Scarlette explained how every rune was built, what to take care with and how to avoid common mistakes. This was why he began with Ancient Andas and Ancient Fusiform Script, so they would have a base to build their Spirit Runes upon.

Then he laid out the ladder for them. It took twelve runes to build a Rune Array, and once they could do that, they became First-Class Wizard Apprentices. The Second and Third Class Apprentice levels worked the same way, requiring more difficult runes and arrays to achieve. Thirty-six runes in all, and only then did they even have a chance at becoming a Wizard.

[Spirit Runes: 0/12.] Tally displayed as Scarlette spoke, filing away the information.

"I will be busy with the other apprentices, so unfortunately there will not be any daily lessons with me." Scarlette said. "Come see me tomorrow, then again in three, seven and fourteen days time."

The seeds bowed before they left. As Maureen stepped into her own carriage, she drew the curtains shut and lay down on the couch. She closed her eyes, waiting patiently until her mind was clear.

*Tally, can you file the rune structure properly if I recite the lesson back to you?*

*Yes, Wizard Seed Olsen. Please do so.*

Maureen went through the lesson that Scarlette taught, saying it all silently in her head. Tally filed each part away as she did so.

Before Maureen started to meditate, Tally counted her into Silver Light. Three long breaths, followed by one short. Then she told Tally to keep counting for her.

Her fingertips warmed up against her sternum, the first time since the tent. She sat up and crossed her legs, feeling the blood rush to her head. "Let's begin," she whispered to herself.

Then Maureen began to meditate the first Spirit Rune, just as Scarlette had shown her.
