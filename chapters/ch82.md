No one moved as Aaron finished his story, but somewhere a twig cracked beneath a heel. The sound seemed large enough to shatter bones. Maureen stood where Watson’s lightning net had nearly nailed her to the ground, Jack’s face still gauntly staring out from beneath the hood of her grey cloak. The charred grass crackled beneath her boots like paper.

Aaron’s voice did not change from its flat, emotionless tone. He might have been telling them what time it was, or what the weather would be like next week. “I came back.”

His eyes ran along the line of family heirs, who had once thrown him against walls for their amusement. “The Blue Ring. The Dark Forest’s precious apprentices. None of it matters anymore. It’s finished.”

The corner of his mouth lifted in what might have been a smile, but it wasn’t the same half-smile that Maureen remembered. It was cold, and it didn’t go away.

“So die.”

The words were so soft that the ones standing farthest away leaned forward, straining to catch them.

The apprentice standing nearest to Aaron opened his mouth and screamed once—then exploded. His body broke apart into a fine red mist that drifted down over the blackened grass, pattering against the leaves like a warm rain.

Some of the mist spattered against Maureen’s cheek. She could smell the coppery scent, and Tally immediately provided a file reference. Blood. The scent had remained in her files since Angrey’s beating in the slum, but she made no move to wipe the spatter from her skin.

Then the rest of them broke.

A girl from the Blue Ring fell to her knees, clasping her hands together. “Please,” she whimpered, “no, please, I never touched you.” Someone else screamed, a high, wordless shriek that went on and on until it was cut short. A boy from the Dark Forest drew a short sword and dashed forward, howling, “I’ll take you with me!”

Behind Aaron, the phantom reared up out of the smoky coils, a serpent of black smoke and fine flame. Its head towered over the trees, its earth-yellow slit pupils as cold and hard as stones at the bottom of a river. Its jaws opened wide, and it hissed without making a sound—but Maureen felt the hiss resonate in her teeth.

>Phantom. No band I can read. Do not move.

*I wasn’t planning to,* she told it silently.

The black serpent lunged at the crowd, its body rippling with fine flames that ran along its scales like sweat. Anyone its body brushed burst into flames, burning to ash in mid-stride. The boy with the sword, the kneeling girl, and the three who ran for the trees— all were gone within a few heartbeats. None of them got away.

The ashes fell slowly, grey flakes like snow over a rubbish heap. They landed on her cloak, settling in the folds of the cloth. Beneath the smell of burnt meat was something else, something sharper and more acrid. Maureen recognised it from the Fasol serpent’s blood, and it made the hair on the back of her neck tighten.

[Odour: sulphur, fire-smoke. Match: Fasol serpent. Confidence: high.]

Tally supplied the line before she even asked for it.

When the screaming finally stopped, the clearing looked like it had been hit by a dozen boulders thrown from a great height. White smoke rose from the craters, while black ash lay thick on everything. The ground was still warm beneath the soles of Maureen’s boots, like the floor of a volcano after it had spoken.

*Your pulse rate remained perfectly constant during that time. Not a single extra beat. I thought you should know.* Tally told her.

*I know,* she replied silently.

*Well, I’m writing it down anyway. Just like I did with Lauren.*

Two files appeared side-by-side across Maureen’s vision. One was Gregory’s test site, marked on the map of the Fasol mission as a few days’ ride from the serpent’s cave. The other file was the road beneath the mountain peak where she’d headed to Redbud, where Aaron had stood atop the head of a phantom serpent, before vanishing into the air.

And between them sat the note on the leaked Black Mando material.

*He found the site we never went back for,* Tally said.

*He got there first.* Maureen answered silently.

*Well, you were rather busy becoming a Viscountess at the time,* Tally pointed out.

*Don’t.*

The pieces slotted together in her mind. Aaron had found Gregory’s laboratory, and fused with the Black Mando Lava Serpent bloodline. He was a warlock now. Less than a year ago, he hadn’t even been able to cast a single spell. Now he had at least the power of an Official Wizard...

Her next thought wasn’t despair. The Black Mando was only a subspecies of the Pankora. Somewhere in Proan Canyon was the essence blood of the Pankora Scarlet Serpent that Gregory had left behind. With that blood and the Minai Totem, she would be a true Ancient Wizard. What would she be then?

Heat rose in her chest, and her fingers curled inside her gloves.

*Step two, blood. You’re breathing fast.*

*Let me have this.* Maureen told it.

After a moment’s pause Tally replied, *Allowed.*

* * *

The last of the smoke, the final flicker of flame behind Aaron, twisted and folded until they were nothing. Aaron descended from the air, his feet touching down lightly on the ash. He looked completely relaxed, as if he had just disembarked from a carriage. His silver robe had not a mark or a stain upon it.

His face was thinner than it had been on the Fasol road, but the grey tinge was gone. The smile that spread across his face was the old friend's smile, but it reached further into his face now, illuminating more than just his mouth and eyes. That just made things worse. The thumb did not move towards the hem of his sleeve.

"Maureen."

He said her real name gently, as if the clearing were empty and they were the only two people in existence.

"It's been a long time. I never thought we'd meet again like this."

She let Jack's rasp go, responding in her own voice, low and soft. "Nor did I."

Aaron stood there for a while, looking at her. Then he started to laugh quietly. "Thank you, my friend. When I was going mad over Ellena, you and Richard held me down and sat with me. On the ship you kept me alive. More than once. In the Dark Forest everyone abandoned me because of my aptitude, but you didn't."

As he spoke, Tally ran through Maureen's vision the moments he described. The rations she had put in his hand. The hold on the night of blood. The corridor. And then it added one more memory that Aaron didn't mention. The fifth day. Carey's day.

It wasn't an accusation. It was just there, part of the record.

*I know,* she told it silently. *Yes, I know.*

Tally responded. *I know you know. That's all.*

Maureen touched the bridge of her nose, not saying anything. She knew how much of what Aaron was thanking her for had simply been arithmetic. So many mouths to feed, so many blades to stand by her side. She also knew how much she hadn't told him. The Seal. Her rank. Everything.

"The realm is dangerous now," Aaron said. "I came in after something, and I'm not sure what stands between me and it. I can't take you with me. Go southwest. We've swept through that area already. It will be a while before anyone else shows up there."

She blinked at that, then nodded. "All right. I'll go."

*You're not going there,* Tally told her.

*I know,* she responded silently. *He's seeing an ordinary apprentice. I still have work to do. I need to know every rival's total and rough strength before I decide on my own number.*

*No, he's not,* Tally said. *He's looking at a friend. That's worse.*

A minute later, the smoke of the pyres had thinned into a haze over the craters. The ash still ticked as it cooled. The black-robed followers were in a loose ring around the clearing's perimeter. Aaron reached inside his silver robe and pulled out a token, holding it out to her.

For a moment there was a flicker of faint silver flame across the face of the token, so thin that Maureen almost didn't notice it. Then the fire faded away and the token was just a small, unadorned piece of worked metal.

Tally scanned it by sight and by her particle sense.

[Token. Function: no data. Value to them: high, judging by their faces.]

One of the black robes took a step forward before he could stop himself. "Your Eminence, that's the Silver Soul Token, you can't just—" Aaron turned and looked at him once, and the man backed up, pulling his hood down and rejoining the ring without another word.

*His pulse jumped at his throat,* Tally noted. *It was plain to see.*

*To what?* Maureen asked silently.

*No data. But keep the token next to the Seal. Nowhere else on your body is safe to hide it.*

"Keep it," Aaron said, his face earnest. "It might be useful to you someday."

She regarded it for a moment, then nodded. "Fine."

The token was cold through her glove, heavier than she expected for its size. It felt denser than silver had any right to be. That bothered her more than the massacre had. She slipped it into her inner pocket, where it clicked against the Seal.

Aaron said a few more things, ordinary things that old friends might say at a crossroads. Then he turned and walked away, leading his followers in another direction. The black robes closed around the single silver one, until the trees swallowed them all.

Only then did Tally tell her the number she'd asked it to keep off her display long ago. [Witnesses to the name: 0, outside his escort.]

*Everyone who heard 'Maureen' is ash now,* Tally said.

*I know. But he didn't do that for that reason.*

*No. But that's what he did.*

She let out a long breath, alone among the smoking pits. Then she shook her head and laughed. "Really..." was all she said aloud. There weren't words in any language for how many turns her afternoon had just taken.

* * *

A figure appeared from the forest.

Sieg.

The White Bone Tower apprentice stepped into the clearing, wearing his blood red robe and smooth white bone face catching the grey light. "Jack, you—" Sieg began, before stopping dead.

He looked around, his bone face turning slowly to take in all the craters, the drifts of ash and the smoking ground.

*Reading the ground,* Tally said. *Adding it up to you.*

*Let him,* Maureen thought back silently, without taking her eyes off of Sieg.

Maureen turned her head slightly, raising an eyebrow. Then, in Jack's low hoarse rasp, she said, "Sieg. So you caught up after all."

"No, no, no." Sieg shook his head, looking around as he backed away from the clearing. "I didn't. I'm not...I'm just passing through!" He threw his hands up, turning around and bolting back into the forest. His red robe billowed out behind him like the wings of a bat. He did not look back even once.

Maureen watched Sieg until he vanished into the trees, then stared at the place where he'd disappeared for another long moment, trying to understand. Then her eyes fell upon the pitted black ground and she understood exactly what Sieg thought he had seen. A laugh escaped her, low and soft.

*Your pitch went up two notes on that laugh,* Tally observed. Maureen forced a return of the rasp.

*And... Jack's reputation aside... As far as Sieg knows, you just burned a dozen family heirs to ash... alone.*

*Jack didn't do anything,* Maureen thought back.

*Jack's reputation doesn't agree with you.*

Maureen turned, walking in the opposite direction. It was time to start scouting, and over the next hours she would encounter apprentice after apprentice. The Dark Forest came first, in their dark robes. Then, the Mist Labyrinth fog-walkers. Finally, the White Bone Tower, in their grey robes.

None were any match for a girl armed with a bandolier full of alchemical grenades.

The fights ran together in a haze of violence. “Kerrash.” The word escaped from between her lips as an iron egg left her hand. The flat cough of the explosive blast. The sudden ripping of fog, tearing away from people like it was wet paper. Fire, licking up the sides of a tree trunk. The quiet of the realm forest, unbroken by birdsong or the cries of beasts.

And through it all, Tally built its table, filling in the Ancient Andas numerals scrawled on the back of every hand it saw. Dead or living, it didn't matter. [Seen totals: median 3. Top seen: Varo, 31.]

She chose which kills to take. Each death brought a faint tugging at the glyph on her hand. She accepted no more than two points from any one kill, refusing the rest with a firmness of will. Each refused total dissipated into a wisp of pale smoke, drifting away toward the northeast.

The line on her hand settled down at [Points: 9].

*You're climbing, Jack,* Tally said. *Like a careful person.*

*That's what I am,* Maureen thought.

*In the points column, yes,* Tally agreed.

At one point, she rested in the shelter of a split boulder, moving shells from the padded backpack to the chest bandolier one by one. The iron eggs were cool to the touch, and heavier than they looked. As Tally ticked the hand column back up, Maureen listened to the still forest, letting her breathing slow before she moved again.

* * *

A tall figure stepped out from behind the black rock, standing directly across the path. Pale, finely made, eyes so dark a blue they were nearly black, and clad in ornate leather armour. Tooling of silver traced the edges of the straps and buckles.

"A dark elf," Maureen murmured and an eyebrow crept up. The buckles creaked softly as the elf shifted her weight. Her feet were placed like a knight's, forward heel barely touching the mossy ground. Tufts of torn moss were wrenched free by the back of the elf's boot as she drew her blade with a whisper.

"Apprentice." The elf's voice was clear and loud, speaking Ancient Andas with precision, as though pronouncing sentence. "You have come this far. That means you have no reason left to live."

The elf's body dropped low and lashed forward like a cheetah springing from a tree branch.

> High. Then low. Feint.

The words flared in front of Maureen's eyes, and Tally caught the dark-elf's left shoulder dropping by a hair's breadth before the blade began to move.

Maureen's Cross Sword was out before the elf even saw her draw. Steel sang as the two blades met, the sound bright and clean, unlike the usual clash of arms. Maureen ignored the high strike, her own blade dropping to catch the real attack low. Her lips curled into a cold smile as she knocked the elf's blade aside. "You're the one in a hurry to die."

The elf's eyes widened in surprise, and in that same breath Maureen spoke the Seal's triggering phrase under her breath.

Blood red vines came out of nowhere, wrapping around the elf's arms, waist and legs, holding her fast where she stood.

There was a wet creaking as they tightened, thorns pressing against the finely tooled leather. The scent of sap and iron rose between them. The elf strained against the bonds, her breath hissing between gritted teeth.

[Seal: 2.4 s.]

Tally's countdown glowed in the corner of Maureen's vision.

[Release.]

Maureen's sword was already moving as the vines released and dropped away. The Cross Sword slid into the elf's throat with another wet sound, then slid back out. The elf sagged forward, coughing up a froth of dark blood. One hand grabbed for Maureen, catching at her cloak before sliding off. "I...won't let you in," she gasped in Ancient Andas. "Her Highness ordered it...keep the humans away. For our people. You can't..."

Maureen understood every word. Tally repeated one in particular. Her Highness. Maureen turned the phrase over in her mind while Tally looked for a match, placing it beside its file from the gathering ground.

*Freya.*

*The girl Eve held by the hand.*

*Yes.*

Another thrust finished the job. As she cleaned her blade on the moss, Maureen considered the dying dark-elf's words. "Now that's interesting," she said quietly, then began moving along the path the elf had died guarding.

Behind her, the elf's blood spread across the green moss like a purple shadow. There was a faintly sweet smell that came off it, not unpleasant, but cool in contrast to the hot, metallic scent of human blood. The Seal sat heavy and warm in her pocket against the chill token.

* * *

The higher she climbed, the stranger the air grew. It became sweeter, like a ripe fruit or boiled sugar, and the sweetness clung thickly to her tongue. She had to work harder to draw breath, feeling it fill her lungs like honey. Her breathing slowed, although she hadn't instructed it to do so.

*It's abnormal,* Tally informed her after processing data from several sources. *I can't pinpoint what, exactly. The air is sweeter than your baseline in this realm. Heavier. Your breath slows without conscious direction.*

*Yes, I noticed.*

*Abnormal,* Tally repeated. *But not harmful...that I can detect.*

*Are you trying to reassure me?*

*I'm just telling you the facts. That's the best I have.*

In the cave, the sealing array was set into the stone floor. A ring of light shone around the pattern on the ground, but it had dimmed until only a few points remained lit, and they were flickering. In the middle of the ring lay a small rock the size of a baby's fist. It would have been unremarkable, if not for the veins of blood red that ran through it. It would also have been unremarkable if it had not been beating.

With each beat, the stone's red veins grew bright, then faded to a dull colour. Maureen could feel the beat thumping up through the soles of her boots, a faint heartbeat. As the stone pulsed, segments of the array began to go out, one by one, like lamps along a street as morning approached.

*Pulse: 72,* Tally told her. *Steady. Not yours.*

*It's beating like a heart.*

"What are you?" she whispered.

She did not hesitate for long before reaching into the dying ring of light and wrapping her fingers around the stone.

It was solid, and warm against her palm, and throbbing like some small creature beneath her fingers. The last bit of light from the array sighed out of existence, plunging the room into darkness. Only then did Maureen notice the second opening at the back of the cave.

Through this opening, she could see grey daylight and beyond that rows of dark elves seated upon the rock, cross-legged like monks in meditation. They chanted, men and women both, in low, steady voices. Ancient Andas.

One of them glanced up and saw her standing in the dark ring, the stone clasped in her fist. He shot to his feet.

"Human! A human has the stone!" he shouted in Ancient Andas.

Tally translated the words, although Maureen had understood him perfectly well.

*The stone. Whatever it is, its owners might be able to track it. No data either way.*

The chant broke up. A petite figure sprang up from the front row. Her long black hair fell to her waist, her skin was fair, her ears were pointed, and she wore leather armour. She was a dark elf.

Freya.

An older attendant jumped up at her side. "Highness! A human apprentice has taken the Bloodline Seal Stone!"

Freya's face went white with anger. She turned and swung her small fist into the boulder beside her.

The boulder exploded in a shower of gravel and dust.

Maureen heard the crack of stone echoing into the cave and felt the pattering of grit against her boots. She didn't move back, but stayed where she stood, the stone beating in her hand.

Freya's voice rose over the dust, tight and furious. "Ani! Call to him. If he gives up the Bloodline Seal Stone, we'll pay. Whatever price he names."

The attendant, Ani, cupped her hands and called across to Maureen in Ancient Andas. "Human! Return the stone and name your price!" Then again, "Human! Return the stone and name your price!"

*That's an offer,* Tally said. *I understood every word of that.*

*I did too,* Maureen thought silently.

Maureen raised her free hand and waved at them. A small, easy wave. Then she tucked the Bloodline Seal Stone into a fold of her robe. Jack's narrow face split in a smile. She turned and walked out of the cave.

Behind her, there was silence. Stunned silence. Maureen could feel Freya standing in the gravel, staring after her.

Against Maureen's ribs, the stone continued beating, its pulse joining that of the Seal and the cold token.

*You waved at them,* Tally said. *Do you think Jack would wave now?*

*I think so,* Maureen replied silently. *He's in a good mood right now.*

*I'm logging that as mischief, then. For both of you.*
