The Second-Class Wizard Apprentice rank had twelve more Spirit Runes that needed to be built into a second Rune Array. That was a lot harder than the First-Class Wizard Apprentice rank. In the hours between studying pharmacology, Maureen had managed to build nine.

Maureen sat alone at her desk, staring at the dull brass lock that secured the door. How am I doing? She asked silently.

[Maureen Olsen. Upper Knight. Strength 8.3 / Agility 8.4 / Constitution 8.3. Spirit: unknown.]

[Second array: 9/12.]

Maureen remembered what plan six had been like. That was all behind her now. She still recalled the taste of the crystal grapes in her mouth and the gluey feeling of the fanged black wolf eyes she'd bought in the Trading District.

Unknown? What does that mean? She asked.

Tally's voice sounded in her head, warm and friendly. I have no unit for spirit, so I need to create one first. [Task created: spirit quantification. ETA 69d 23h 46m 58s]

Seconds? Really, Tally? Those seconds are just showing off you know. She told the chip silently.

There was a hint of smugness in Tally's mental tone. The seconds are accurate.

Fine, two months then. She accepted.

How long will it take me to finish the last three runes and the array if I keep going at my current pace?

[Tally display: Est. at current pace: 5 months.]

Maureen's fingers absently drummed on the desk as she considered the five month time frame. The tapping sound drew her attention back to her hands, and she flattened her fingers against the rough wooden grain, holding them still.

Is there anything I can do to make it faster? She asked.

There was a short pause before Tally's display began to show information. [Mox Fusion Stone] Buy this. The chip said.

You're sure? Maureen questioned.

Yes. The library says so.

Maureen stared at the entry for the Mox Fusion Stone that Tally displayed.

Mox Fusion Stone increased the density of energy particles in a small area around the user, which also provided a slight boost to meditation. It was a limited and prized commodity that was very expensive. However, if it could make the process faster, Maureen decided that she would buy some.

She pulled her black robe into place, and sat cross-legged on the cold tile floor in front of the small mirror. Tiny motes of greyish light flickered to life on her brow in the mirror's reflection before they danced down into her skin.

* * *

The Trading District noises followed her inside Gandolf's shop. "Hey! I got some rare spices from the East!" "Fresh glass, only been used once!" "Three magic stones for this jar of teeth!"

"Ah, Maureen. You again. Curse it, my stones are about to get a lot fewer." Gandolf said with a gloomy face.

Maureen rapped her knuckles against the counter. "I come bearing stones this time. Do you have any Mox Fusion Stone?"

Gandolf's eyes widened as he jumped up from his stool. He dashed into the back of the shop and returned with three black iron boxes. He set them down on the counter, and opened the lids so that Maureen could see the pure white stone within. A couple grey specks were visible in each of the stones, but otherwise they looked pristine. The smell that came out of the boxes was putrid. Like bird droppings.

Smell logged, Tally said.

No thanks, she thought back silently.

Too late. Tally replied smugly.

"This is all I have," Gandolf gestured to the boxes. "Three standard portions. That's a month's stock for my shop. Now, the price..."

"I'll take all of it," Maureen interrupted as she pulled out the black silk pouch from her robe.

Gandolf paused mid sentence as he stared at the pouch. He quickly counted out the stones. Tally kept track, and updated its ledger with every stone he removed. [Magic stones: 80 → 41]

That was about thirteen magic stones per standard portion...

Maureen winced at the cost. She caught herself doing that, and laughed quietly. Gandolf muttered under his breath about how apothecaries must be really rich if they could brew elementary potions and buy Mox Fusion Stone whenever they wanted.

Envying her wealth. Harmless. Tally said after analyzing Gandolf's mutterings.

Yes, I agree. Maureen thought as she took the three boxes into her arms.

The cold iron boxes felt heavy against her arm on the way home. Her pouch of magic stones rested against her chest, feeling much lighter than before.

* * *

Maureen placed one of the Mox stones in a glass vessel, then poured a small amount of the clear blending liquid over it. The liquid boiled on contact with the stone. There was a shudder from the stone, like something was trying to escape from inside. The room was filled with a sour reek.

Maureen sat down in front of the mirror, and immediately began to meditate.

[Tally's particle log]

Local particle density: rising.

Rune formation rate: up.

Maureen could feel the motes coming faster. She could almost see them dancing around her in the darkness behind her eyelids.

In the mirror, the glass vessel bubbled away behind her shoulder as a grey light flickered on her brow.

After the meditation session, Tally reported that a little less than half the stone was gone. That was about 13 stones a piece. "This is burning magic stones..." Maureen said aloud, since there was no one else in her study to hear her speak.

At a good exchange rate for months. Tally said.

Maureen was glad she had decided to become an apothecary, but the weight of her pouch still pinched when she weighed it.

* * *

Anthony had invited the old group to dinner, and she accepted.

"It's been too long, Maureen!" He said with his customary laugh.

Richard, Lucy and Alice were all there, but Aaron was not.

Aaron is not here. Did you notice? Maureen thought.

Yes. Anthony did not mention him. Tally replied.

Maureen remembered Aaron's bruises from the last time she saw him. Yes, Tally. Keep that in mind.

She sat at the table, folding her hands on top of it while she kept her face pleasant and neutral. Anthony's sleeves once again had bright gold thread running through the cuffs.

"You look to be in good spirits, Anthony." She said.

"I have reason to be." He replied with a self-satisfied smile. "I've been earning quite a few stones on outside missions."

"Amazing." Richard said with envy. "You are amazing, Anthony."

"Thank you, Richard." Anthony said. "I must admit, I am rather proud of my accomplishments."

"I admire your efforts as well, Anthony." Said Alice with a gentle look at Anthony. Her gaze earned a tag.

Complicated. Tally said.

Yes, Tally. Complicated, so log it... complicated.

It was obvious that Anthony's ability to earn mission stones was drawing the former seeds back around him. "It's not just the missions though." He continued. "I've also met people from wizard families." The expressions on the others' faces shifted again.

Tally noted that Anthony mentioned 'wizard family' four times by the end of his conversation. Tally also noted Anthony's thumb rubbing against the gold thread at his cuff when he claimed that his missions weren't too dangerous.

Anthony told stories of the missions he had been on, and both Richard and Lucy gasped in wonder at many points. Maureen only asked one question. "Who posted the work?"

Routes, prices, who hires. Tally filed away.

"Maureen, you should start going on missions as well." Anthony said. "I think we will all be Second-Class soon, and we can go on missions together. In fact, I think that we should. What do you say?"

"We'll see. If I can, I won't say no." Maureen said.

It stung a little, beneath her polite and pleasant exterior. Anthony and the others were already ahead of her, while she sat there taking their measure.

The black stone corridor leading back to her room was cold, and the sound of her footsteps echoed alone.

Pharmacology has cost me time, but I'll reach Second-Class sooner now. She thought to Tally silently.

The Mox is waiting. As am I. Tally responded.

* * *

For almost two months now, Maureen had meditated on the Mox during every session. Her mind clear of all else, she focused on the task at hand.

[Second array: 11/12], Tally showed, along with the Spirit task still running, and the projected completion date.

Ten days left. Tonight, she told it silently. She thought back.

The Mox stank like bird droppings in the sun. Bubbles popped and danced on its surface. The tiles were chilly on her bare shins. Her breath came in and out, three long breaths to one short.

A ball of grey light gathered between her eyebrows, and flowed upwards in a line tracing out the last Spirit Rune.

In the mirror, the new rune glowed brightly. Then one by one, the other eleven lit up, tracing an arc around her head. They flew towards each other, converging until they locked into an array. The Rune Array sat glowing on her brow like a branding iron before it faded and dimmed.

Maureen let her breath out. "Second-Class." It came out naturally, like water finding its channel.

>Second array: 12/12. Rune Array formed. Time: 59d 18h.

>Congratulations, Second-Class Wizard Apprentice Olsen.

Maureen opened up her mouth.

No need to say anything, Second-Class.

She smiled. "Thanks Tally. Nearly three months early. All the Mox."

Yes, well, my five months didn't include any Mox. It's nice to be wrong sometimes, especially in this direction.

"True."

She held her hand out, palm down, and willed it.

Dark energy particles flooded in from all around, more than she could count. The air darkened as the particles converged on her palm. A film of inky blackness settled onto her hand, deepening as the particles grew thicker. The darkness condensed into a glove that completely covered her hand. It was cold, and had a slight weight.

Maureen curled her fingers inside the darkness. Her particle sense exploded with information. Tally opened up a new page in the particle log, one that would track what she could now hold in her hands.

"Second-Class apprentices can wield a wizard's energy." She looked at herself in the mirror. "From here, I've truly crossed the threshold."

Maureen's smile refused to go away. Then her eyes started to sting. She blinked back her tears, but there was no one here for her to cry in front of except Tally.

>I'll log none of this.

Maureen laughed.
