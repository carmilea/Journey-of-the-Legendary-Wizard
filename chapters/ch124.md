Two days later, in the dark stillness before dawn, in the safety behind her own bedroom ward, Maureen recast Mimicry at the washroom mirror. The spell’s effects crawled across her skin like ants, sinking into her flesh and reworking her muscles. The jaw muscles lost their strength so her mouth hung slightly open and weak, her facial muscles reshaped to the mousy features of Richard, and even the muscles of her throat constricted until she sounded like him, speaking through a low, flat voice box.

Tally laid her pale-blue muscle map over the reflection, showing what the spell was doing, and reporting [Mana: 16 → 15.6].

*Your left cheek is lagging again,* Tally noted.

*It always lags,* Maureen replied silently.

*Exactly. That’s why I keep mentioning it.*

Maureen nodded, then reached for the one-way brand she had pressed into Joanna’s wrist. For the first time, she spoke through it.

The gate of 34 Connpa Street, first bell.

Joanna could reply, but not initiate a conversation. The brand only allowed communication in one direction.

There was a small pressure against Maureen’s mind, acknowledgement from Joanna that she had received the message.

*Latency noted,* Tally said. *Less than a breath. Almost instantaneous.*

*Frightened, then,* Maureen responded.

*Or ready. I can’t tell those two apart yet on Joanna.*

* * *

The household of 34 Connpa Street gathered at their windows as the carriage pulled up to the gate. A faded green leaf was painted on its side door.

The cook wiped the last of the flour off her hands as she peered out the window.

The stable boy gawked at the strange horses, their ridged horn plates running down their flanks like armor, their black and grey scales shimmering in the predawn light. Their breath smelled of hot iron and smoke.

Joanna stood at the gate, dressed in a freshly pressed robe, and pulled the bronze bell chain twice, even though she already knew what time it was. “Wizard Richard,” she said. The living sprig of pine pinned to her collar had wilted.

Maureen came down the gravel path without hurrying. “Good. Let’s go.”

*And so now your household has seen a Jadeleaf carriage pull up to collect you,* Tally noted. *And now the Jadeleaf know which gate Richard steps out of when he visits them.*

*That was going to happen eventually. It can’t be helped,* Maureen responded.

*I know. I’m still logging it.*

Inside the carriage waited Christine and four other apprentices: two young men and two young women, all quiet and nervous. No one else was in the carriage. That was everyone the Jadeleaf Ring was sending to the Alliance’s ranking tournament.

[Jadeleaf present: 1 Official (you), Joanna, Christine, apprentices 4.]

Tally noted as she updated a list beside her old one.

The carriage had seats for twelve. Along the empty bench where no one was seated, someone had left folded cloaks as though their owners had only stepped out briefly. Old leather, lavender water, and the cool draught from a window caught open by a loose latch.

Joanna spoke quickly about the route they would take, the order of events for the morning, and how Steward Senge had sent his regards from his bed where he was slowly recovering thanks to the potion. Each time the conversation threatened to wander to the unoccupied seats, Joanna would steer it back to safer topics.

*Her pulse jumps there, in her throat,* Tally noted. *Every time someone says a number. She’s hiding something. A guess.*

*Let her,* Maureen thought silently.

*Kind?* Tally asked.

Maureen did not answer.

*I’ll log that as kind then,* Tally said, *and I assume you dispute that.*

*I do.*

*Any guesses why so many have left over the last few days?*

*Fear. Fear of what the Black Spirit Society’s friends might do? Or fear of a Steward who couldn’t get out of his own bed?*

*I have no data on which, or if both.*

Maureen watched Emilia’s pale streets roll past outside. She had promised a tournament, and nothing beyond that. A flicker of pity for Joanna went through her, watching the woman’s brittle composure as she talked about inconsequential details. Across from Richard, Christine sat as far away from him as the bench would allow, her chin held high with pride.

The carriage rolled through the gate and out onto a vast square. Directly ahead rose the headquarters of the Alliance: a tower that spiralled up into the sky, level after level winding around one another like an endless helix. Hollow and enormous, it dominated the grey heavens.

* * *

Maureen stepped down from the carriage, and narrowed her eyes in appraisal. Up on the balconies, robed figures were stepping off from the sides, gliding through the air to reach the next level instead of using the stairs.

[HIGH sources: 30+. Stopped counting.]

*Such a waste of mana on your daily commute,* Tally said.

Then her voice dropped to a whisper. *Of course, they’re all scanning auras right now as well. You should probably keep yours where it is. Richard’s aura reads Official Wizard. It reads as a normal Official Wizard.*

Maureen kept her field compressed in her chest, feeling it held down as though she was breathing in and trying not to let it out. She felt the combined heat of thirty strangers’ power on her face, like standing too close to an open forge.

“Well, Joanna. And little Christine.” A figure approached across the flagstones. He wore fine clothing, and his smile grew as he came closer. Maureen saw Joanna’s face change.

“Wizard Manhattan.”

Joanna’s voice sounded stiff with effort. Fang Blood Shadow was a rival organization, and a far more powerful one than the Ring. The two had been butting heads for years.

A sweetish smell drifted off him, like the scent of lilies that had been bruised or trampled. It came from the thin green vapour that curled around his wrists at the cuffs of his sleeves. His hair was green and combed flat against his head with oil, and there were rings on his fingers that clicked against each other when he rubbed his chin.

[Manhattan. Density: near Soros. Not elementalised. Danger: weak-to-medium.] *The green vapour indicates transformation school, I believe. This is my first sample of that,* Tally said.

His eyes moved over Joanna, taking their time, then moved over Christine and stopped.

“No Senge?” he asked. “Has that old fellow finally taken to his bed for good?”

Joanna took a breath, then seemed to gather herself. “The Steward is unavailable. Our honorary professor, Wizard Richard, will be leading us through the tournament.”

Manhattan turned his head, as though he had only just noticed Richard’s presence. He paused for a moment, tasting the aura. Then he laughed through his nose. “A fresh one. How many days have you been a wizard, boy? Ten? And you’re here to rescue them?”

*His gaze is tracing a line between Joanna’s throat and Christine’s,* Tally said. *That’s not appraisal... that’s appetite.*

*I know what it is,* Maureen thought.

Manhattan leaned forward, bringing his face close to Joanna’s. “If the Ring needs assistance, you only need to ask me. You and your sister as well, if she’s willing... anything’s possible. Isn’t it?”

Christine shrank back into her collar, glancing at Richard with frightened eyes.

“Show some respect, Wizard Manhattan,” Joanna said.

He just laughed.

Maureen’s body moved before she made any decision.

She found herself standing in front of Joanna, placing herself between Manhattan and Joanna. A heat was rising somewhere under her breastbone, burning its way up behind her eyes.

Crimson flooded the inky blackness of her eyes. The pupils narrowed to thin vertical slits. The whites of her eyes remained white. She cast no spells. This was the Pankora blood, rising. Her voice was Richard’s when she spoke. “You’re forgetting something, Manhattan. The Ring brought a wizard today.”

The green-haired man froze. His colour turned a sickly grey, his mouth opening and closing without producing any words.

*That isn’t Mirror Pupil,* Tally said. *That’s the blood. Mimicry can’t conceal it. Thirty wizards just saw Richard’s eyes. And Richard’s voice went up half a tone on the word “Manhattan”.*

*Let them remember Richard,* Maureen thought silently.

*They will,* Tally said.

“Now, what’s going on here?”

A warm, unhurried male voice sounded from the side. A young man strode up to join the group. He was handsome, with a sharp-chiselled face and bright, clever eyes. He was dressed as a core wizard, with the emblem of a white tower worked into the cloth over his breast.

“I’m Gus,” he said.

Joanna and the apprentices flinched at the sight of the white tower. That was the High Ring White Tower, the pinnacle of power that the entire Wizard Academy Alliance bent itself around. Even Manhattan paled slightly at the sight of the man.

[Gus. Official. Density above Soros, near Maffie. Not elementalised.]

Then Tally went on quietly. *His weight is sitting on the balls of his feet. His shoulders are set as if he’s wearing a high collar. His density has the same grain as Bat’s.*

*Confidence?*

*Low.*

*Low isn’t nothing,* Tally pointed out.

Manhattan finally let out a long breath, his false smile returning to his lips, stretching too wide. “Trouble? None at all, none! Just greeting old friends, Wizard Gus.”

Joanna and the apprentices nodded rapidly next to him, too afraid to do anything else.

Maureen watched them nodding, letting the crimson fade from her eyes until they were just deep black again. Where the heat had been burning in her chest, there was now an empty coolness.

“Well,” said Gus, his voice smooth as honey, “I hope you all have a good tournament.” He strolled away, turning to walk up the steps. As he did so, something flickered across his face, at the very edge of Maureen’s vision. A heartbeat of emotion, then it was gone. Deep disgust.

Tally caught it, and outlined the expression in her display.

*Target of expression: no data. That could be Manhattan... or that could be you.*

Manhattan found his voice once Gus was out of earshot. “Richard, is it? You wait. Out on the platform I’ll show you—”

He turned and hurried away across the flagstones. Joanna turned back to Richard. “Wizard Richard, don’t let him worry you, he’s only—”

“Let’s go, Joanna,” Maureen interrupted.

* * *

Joanna led them through the crowd with the assurance of someone who had sat through many a tournament before, and found the Jadeleaf Ring’s bench without needing to ask anyone for directions. It was on the lower tier, and Christine took a seat at one end of the bench, putting as much distance between herself and Richard as possible. The tournament assembly hall was thick with the smells of chalk dust and candle wax. The tiers were filling rapidly with delegations dressed in green, rust and grey, each delegation keeping to their own section, sitting on the cold stone benches. The murmur of conversation was bouncing around the spiralling walls, reverberating back down from the ceiling to produce a deep humming background noise.

Names flared into life on the great slate, and moments later had resolved themselves into pairs. Even a fast scribbling clerk would have taken far longer to make the pairings. Against Richard of the Jadeleaf Ring, the slate read Manhattan of Fang Blood Shadow.

*Pairings made by a spell,* Tally noted. *P(random pairing with Manhattan): ~1 in 19. Coincidences do happen,* Tally said. *So do arrangements.*

*Should I care?* Maureen asked silently.

*Not about the match, no. Perhaps about who arranged it.*

Maureen looked across the hall to where Manhattan sat. He caught sight of the slate just seconds after she did, and let out an open laugh, slapping his knee. He turned and grinned at Richard, something cruel and self-satisfied on his face. As though the terror on the steps had never happened.

*Look at the pulse jumping in his throat, even as he grins. The fear hasn’t gone away. He’s just buried it beneath this noise.*

“Wizard Richard—” Joanna said, turning to her first, her face full of concern.

A moment later, one of the apprentices spoke. “Wizard Richard, you...” The other three apprentices leaned forwards, faces filled with worry.

Maureen just smiled. “Don’t worry. It’s nothing.” Christine looked away with a sniff.

The wait stretched out forever. Bout followed bout, thudding away on the far platform while Maureen let her eyes wander, taking in the details of the hall. Pillar bases, upper balconies, archways, doorways. Learning the shape of the place.

Her gaze paused on a side passageway, identified by a carved sign overhead. “Pharmacy Department?”

Tally lit up before Maureen had finished reading it. A department of apothecaries, that meant there was probably a library here.

*You sound excited,* Maureen said silently.

*I’m being precise,* Tally responded. *Very precisely excited.*

Maureen felt something stirring within her too, something old and familiar. Jing Sheng’s white bench, racks of glassware, the quiet tick of a burner. The Dark Forest study, where her first green vial had left a ring in the stone.

Her fingers remembered the weight of a pipette.

“Wizard Richard?” Joanna leaned in, her breath a hot puff against Maureen’s ear. “There are lots of regular Officials here, but the Alliance also keeps departments of wizards with specialized skills. They’re not part of any delegation - they work for the entire Alliance. Pharmacy is one of those departments.” Her voice grew a little envious. “If one of an organization’s wizards joins the Alliance, all the privileges and resources available to that organization go up a long way.”

“So if I joined,” Maureen said softly, “the Ring would come out of this better than any ranking. And it would last.”

Joanna nodded. “Far better, and far more steady. It’s...” She trailed off, then her eyes widened, staring at Maureen. “Wizard Richard, are you...?”

Tally laid it all out. Better for the Ring than a ranking. Better for them than a fight. Both columns going in the same direction. For once.

“Just what you’re thinking,” Maureen said. She looked around at the four apprentices, all staring at her now. “I’m an apothecary.”

They sat in stunned silence, mouths open, eyes wide and unblinking. An apothecary. And an Official Wizard.

Christine’s face was the first to twist with disbelief. Then something sour settled into her expression, as if the news had cost her something. Maureen just let it slide past her.

*Harder to file than Joanna’s,* Tally noted. *Logging... resentment. Unconfirmed.*

*Leave it unconfirmed,* Maureen told her silently. She put a hand on Joanna’s shoulder and gave it a pat. “I’ll be at the Pharmacy Department.” With that, she turned and started walking towards the passage.

* * *

“Richard of the Jadeleaf Ring! Manhattan of Fang Blood Shadow!” called the presiding wizard from his dais.

Maureen Olsen kept walking.

“Richard? Wizard Richard! Wizard Richard!” came the cry from the presiding wizard, his voice cracking with surprise. Maureen didn’t turn around.

The sound of Maureen’s footsteps continued over the stone. The presiding wizard’s voice came back to her as it bounced off the spiral walls. She felt cool, calm, and quietly pleased, as if she’d been going over a ledger and found that everything balanced perfectly.

On the platform, a cloud of green vapour rose from the ground and began to knit itself together. Arms flung wide, Manhattan stood before the Jadeleaf apprentices.

“He’s running! The Jadeleaf wizard is running away!”

The sweet smell of bruised lilies floated up from the platform and out over the tiers. Heads began to turn in the hall, and the low murmur became more focused, more eager.

Christine frowned down at the platform. “Sister, he’s—”

Joanna took Christine’s hand and held it firmly. “It’s fine, Christine. It’s fine.”

*I am scanning the tiers for you, Maureen,* Tally said. *Faces amused. No one moving to stop him. No arena rule I have covers an Official Wizard killing a head of house. I have no data on what that would cost. I suggest caution. The presiding wizard is a HIGH source.*

Manhattan sauntered along the edge of the platform. “And I took you for somebody,” he called after Maureen. “An Official Wizard, and you fold like a wet sheet. Look at you. What even are you?”

Laughter rippled through the blocks of green and rust. One of the wizards leaned over to whisper something in his neighbour’s ear, then both of them smirked down at the Jadeleaf bench.

“Those two whores picked a coward to carry the Ring,” Manhattan shouted after Maureen. “They should have come to me. At least then they’d have known what they were paying for. Two whores!”

The Jadeleaf apprentices stared at their knees, their cheeks burning with humiliation. Christine bit down hard on her lip so that it went white. Joanna’s free hand clenched into a fist. Her knuckles shone in the light from above.

Maureen stopped in the middle of a step.

*Pulse: 64 → 91. Your pulse has moved,* Tally said. *Did you mean to kill him?*

*No.*

*Well then, you had better decide how much.*

Her shoulders set, and she could feel the heat return beneath her breastbone. It surged back slowly this time. It was under her control now. She turned around, and Richard’s plain face revealed nothing.

Manhattan spread his hands. “Oh? Did that sting, Richard? Then come up here. Come and earn your ranking.”

“I didn’t come here looking for trouble,” Maureen said, her voice low and even. One corner of her mouth lifted. “You did. So you’ve only yourself to blame.”

She lifted one hand slowly as Manhattan continued talking, and with a single thought, black-red flame bloomed all over Manhattan’s body.

> Purgatory Fire.

> > Cast ~2

> > Mana: 15.6 → est. 13.6

> > Upkeep: running

Tally had the readout pinned to him. An ember-red halo count hung over the burning man.

It was thick, like oil. Manhattan slapped at it, tried rolling on the ground, beat at himself with the sleeves of his robe. It kept on burning. The smell of scorched hair and roasting silk began to roll through the tiers.

The hall fell silent. The only sound was Manhattan’s screaming, over and over, as the fire took his legs.

>Halo: 100% → 40% → 8%

*Cut upkeep?*

*Now.*

The flames winked out as though an unseen hand had reached down and pinched them off. Manhattan collapsed to the stone, eyes open wide and staring, twitching on the ground. Smoke curled from what remained of his robes, burned to rags in places. His whole body was charred as though he had been roasted alive.

No one spoke. Somewhere above, in the tiers, came the sound of a bench scraping back. It echoed through the hall, a loud, lonely noise.

Tally checked for a pulse by sight. *Yes.*

*He’s alive. That leg? Never going to stand on it again.*

*Good.*

*Logged. Not showing.*

Maureen lowered her hand slowly, then let her gaze sweep once across the tiers. She made eye contact with many of the watching wizards, and every single time, the eyes slid away. She didn’t look at Joanna or Christine, because that would have meant something. Instead, she turned her back on the platform, then walked on toward the Pharmacy Department passage, maintaining the same slow, unhurried pace.

*That’s going to cost you, Maureen.*

*Black-red flame. In front of thirty Official Wizards. Anyone who’s ever read about Purgatory. Whoever sent the doll.*

*Then Richard needed a better name for it.*

*I’ll start working up something now.*
