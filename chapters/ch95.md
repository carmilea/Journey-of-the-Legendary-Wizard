Maureen Olsen tied off the last knot of her reward bundle. The stoppered vial of Silver-Trace Ink was cool against her ribs, tucked into the inner fold of her coat. Adrian’s burner still ticked quietly in the background. The black flame danced around the test tube, but it made no sound. The smell of whatever chemicals were reacting within the glassware was sharp in the air, pungent enough to override the scents of the potted plants and their curling black mist.

Adrian waited patiently for Maureen to finish tying off her bundle, then spoke as though he were picking up the middle of an argument that had been going on for days. “Now you know how wizards really climb, you won’t want to do it on a junk method. Will you?”

Maureen’s stomach did a little drop. A question like that coming from her mentor could be a test, or an open door. She kept her hands on her bundle. “Mentor, you mean...?”

Adrian pushed his glasses up his nose. “Fourteenth. Of everyone who came out alive, by points, you’re fourteenth. The council agreed the top fifteen each get one choice of an advanced meditation method.”

*Fourteenth. I told you top fifteen.*

*You said top fifteen by your table.*

*And my table was right. I’d like that in writing.*

“Not bad,” Adrian said. Then, again, softer, “Really not bad.”

The deep lines around Adrian’s eyes eased a bit. Maureen had only seen that once before, when she had presented him with her first flawed Recovery Potion. It was a look of quiet pride, and it made Maureen’s chest ache just a little. He was proud of the number fourteen, and that number had been built on top of one refusal after another. Each total that Maureen turned down became a pale wisp that drifted to the northeast, feeding the fog.

She had known since she received her reward that there was a chance survivors would be offered an advanced meditation method. But she had thought that her points were done. Finished business.

[Points: 12. Read at gate.]

*So that’s what the reading was for.*

*You could have guessed.*

*No data then. Data now.*

Maureen scratched her nose absently with a knuckle, smiling a small, embarrassed smile. She didn’t say anything. Her expression said, lucky girl here, I never expected to be counted. I’m just a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice from the lower aptitude bands.

Behind that smile sat a warm ball of triumph, private and hers alone. Fourteenth. Exactly where she wanted to be. High enough to be rewarded, low enough to not warrant a second glance.

[Pulse: 88. Face says: 70. Keep face.]

*I’m keeping it.*

*Good. Keep number.*

“Come, then,” Adrian said. There was a brightness in his voice, a young man’s eagerness, despite the dry tone. “Let us head deep into the Forest. We’ll go choose yours.”

He walked out of the lab ahead of her, his white and gold robe whispering against the flagstones. Maureen followed, her own steps light on the cold black stone.

* * *

As they went along, the hallways grew thinner. First the apprentices fell away until she and Adrian were alone in the corridors. Then, even the shadow servants left them alone. Finally, even the lights began to disappear. Ahead of her, all Maureen could see was the faint pale shape of Adrian’s robes.

He walked right up to a wall that shimmered like heat over Uncle Andrew’s forge. Maureen followed him through. The stone slid over her skin like wet silk, cold and smooth and odourless.

[Turns: 1] Tally had done this in every corridor of this castle, counting off every turn and back-turn they made.

*You don’t need to do that here,* she told it silently. *I will never have reason to come back this way.*

*You said the same thing about the Skran treasury,* Tally responded.

A bridge of black stone hung out before them, no rails to either side. It stretched over nothing, dropping down to a darkness that refused to find a floor even when Maureen’s eyes scrambled to see one. The air that rose from below smelled of old incense and the deep cellar that had been shut for centuries.

[Turns: 23. Bridges: 4.]

[Depth: no data.]

*I don’t need you to tell me when to look down, either,* Maureen told Tally.

Tally’s tone sounded apologetic. *But I only ever look where you look.*

*In that case, I guess I better not look down.*

They finally came out before a vast wall of smooth black stone. In the center of the wall was carved the head of a giant goat. Its eyes were closed, its massive horns curving back into the stone as though it had grown there naturally.

It didn’t move. It felt dead. There was dust in the grooves of its carved fleece. The cold air breathed off the stone like a tomb. It seemed less a door and more an ornate decoration for an ancient burial vault.

Adrian raised his hand and a stream of dark energy particles poured from his fingers. It was a torrent of blackness, a river of ink, roaring out from his hand without pause or hesitation. It drove straight into the goat’s brow.

Her particle sense could feel it. Thick as poured tar, the stream of energy flowed out from Adrian’s fingers and pounded against the stone. The hair on her forearms lifted underneath her sleeves.

The stone stirred. “Mm... I know this... darkness....” A clear male voice spoke from the carved mouth, slurred by sleep. “Huh? Oh. Adrian. It’s been a while, hasn’t it.”

[Warning: HIGH energy radiation source.]

*Just another talking statue,* Maureen told Tally. *Like Hugh.*

*Hugh stammers. This one has manners.*

Adrian bowed. It was not the slight bobbing of courtesy, but a formal bow.

“Sherai. It has been thirty-seven years.”

The goat gave a long sigh, an impossibility for a stone creature. “Thirty-seven. I shut my eyes for a moment and look at that.”

Maureen watched the old man as he spoke, watched how his shoulders relaxed, his back straightened. Adrian assumed an easy stance, the way one takes with an old friend. It was a posture she’d never seen him take with any other person. Not even close.

“And who keeps the Forest now?” Sherai asked. “Still that old buzzard Vossen?”

“Steward Vossen died nineteen years ago. It’s Augurit now.”

The green pupils inside the goat’s eyes became still. “Augurit,” the goat said slowly. “So it’s him...”

Maureen heard the rest of the thought go unsaid.

Tally opened a file on a dead Steward named Vossen and logged [Augurit: Steward 19 years, by inference.] beside the goat’s tone.

*Tone noted,* Tally said. *Meaning, no data.*

*Keep the file open,* Maureen told it.

After a bit more chat between old acquaintances, Adrian asked if they might be allowed into the vault. Sherai’s eyes creased in apology. “Listen to me. Of course you didn’t wake me up just to catch up on gossip.”

The stone mouth opened wide, and a sound wave rolled out from between its lips. Her ears couldn’t hear it, but she could feel it vibrating in her teeth and in the very bone deep within her breast.

Phantom men and women began to rise out from the black stone walls. They were insubstantial, almost translucent, and their mouths were stretched wide in silent screams. Their chests heaved as though they were breathing, but no breath passed their lips. Their faces were twisted in pain that had lasted long past its end. The cellar cold wrapped itself around her.

The phantoms flowed together in a stream of shifting light, folding one into another until, at one side of the wall, a door appeared composed of their faint illumination.

Tally added the goat to its gatekeeper list. [Gatekeepers: statues, squirrel, owl, crows, goat.]

*Any of these ever work for free?* Maureen asked.

*This one has been asleep for thirty-seven years,* she said silently.

*That is the closest to free I have seen,* Tally responded.

The green eyes turned to look at her. “Little one. Adrian’s apprentice, I’d guess. Luck to you. I hope you find what you came for.” She bowed her head without a word, then stepped into the light at Adrian’s side.

* * *

There was a momentary lurch. The door’s light peeled away from her. She stood on nothing she could feel, surrounded by a churning dark that shifted around her, with drifting motes like dust in a sunbeam that didn’t exist. Adrian stood at her side. In front of them waited Steward Augurit and a group of other Official Wizards, all dressed in rich clothing. They had cold, closed expressions, and it was obvious that they were all members of the Dark Forest’s wizard families. Tally’s alert sounded in her head.

[HIGH energy radiation sources: 7.]

*Cannot rank relative magnitudes of energy radiation sources when multiple sources present.*

*Don’t try.*

Maureen looked around herself. Velvet, heavy rings, silver pins marking family affiliations. These were people who owned futures, other people’s futures, and she was a girl wearing a plain robe. She made herself small, still, and tried not to breathe loud enough for anyone to hear.

Augurit Jones looked at her, his white hair and white eyebrows standing out against the dark. “Maureen,” he said softly, rolling the name over his tongue as if he were tasting it. His voice was mild, gentle, like that of a grandfather sitting by the fire. The same voice that had rolled through the corridors of the Dark Forest, promising deserters a thousand years of burning. Her spine straightened on its own.

Augurit raised one hand, then slowly moved it through the void. One book appeared, wrapped in a halo of energy particles. Then another. Then another. They floated in the dark, slowly turning on their axes like lanterns bobbing in a black ocean. Each book had a halo of its own, crackling with energy. Some burned ember-red, others frost-blue, grey-green or bruise-purple. A faint hum came from them, raising the hair on the back of Maureen’s neck. They prickled against her particle sense, making it feel like she was running a wool sweater over her skin.

“You know what these are, I think,” Augurit said. His voice echoed quietly in the void. “As we agreed, with myself and the family wizards as witnesses: Maureen Olsen, choose your advanced meditation method.”

Every eye in the void was on her now. Adrian didn’t say anything, standing slightly apart from the others, but his eyes were urging her on.

These people knew what this moment meant, and what it would mean for the future. For these men and women, it was like choosing an entire future. For her, it was just a part, something she was going to have to play very well, because this was something that she’d never get to practise.

*Tally, I need you to scan all those books against me and my aptitude. Do it as fast as you can.*

*I cannot read closed books,* Tally said immediately. *I can read halos and titles, not pages.*

*Why not?*

*Because. I can only read halos,* Tally insisted.

*Fine. Then read the halos. Do whatever you have to.*

Immediately, Tally began sorting the books’ halos according to particle make-up, then compared them against her darkness and fire affinity. It analysed her grade five, which was nearly a six, and ran through her particle log. It also read the names printed on the spines, then gave her one line of text.

[Best fit by halo: Heart-Burning Flame. Darkness / fire. Confidence: medium.]

*Medium? I can work with that.*

*Then pick quickly, Maureen. Careful people take a long time to choose.*

Maureen raised one eyebrow by the barest fraction, which was as much as her face ever gave away. She could feel how little this choice cost her, and how much every other person in the void thought it must be costing her.

After the briefest moment of silence, she stepped forward. Dark particles began welling up from her palm, and she reached into one of the halos. Her fingers closed over the cover of a book, and she drew it out, the heavy object appearing in her hand. It was thicker than it looked, and felt cool. Its cover was the same deep purple-black colour as a particularly nasty bruise. The name was pressed into the spine. She held it against her chest, keeping it closed.

“I’ll take this one,” she said.

Augurit Jones and the family wizards exchanged looks, and there were frowns on faces that had not expected a girl to settle her own future so fast.

> [Frowns: 4. Augurit: eyes brighten.]

Maureen could not have said how many frowns there were, but Tally could.

*They’re curious about you now,* Tally told her silently.

*Better that than not,* she silently replied. *Better they be curious about a reckless girl than a careful one.*

“Maureen.” The word burst out of Adrian before he could stop it. “Don’t decide so quickly. Think it through.”

She just smiled at him, looking calm and unperturbed by his concern.

*He’s disappointed in you,* Tally told her.

*I know.*

*You did that on purpose.*

*I know that too.*

Augurit Jones’s eyes gleamed with interest. He reached into the void again, and pulled out another item. This one was a scroll, constructed of some kind of dark-gold metal, wrapped in purple flames. “Then sign, by the Forest’s way and by the Forest’s rules. Confidentiality.”

The scroll demanded Maureen Olsen as Jay’s had demanded him. A brand of light rose from her brow and pressed itself into the dark metal. The purple flames flared once, then took it.

Swearing to protect a method she would never use felt like lying with her entire body. There was a new coldness in her throat, accompanying the oath collar she’d received for her first paid course.

> [Binding: confidentiality. Dark Forest. No data on mechanism.]

Tally logged it at the base of her skull, next to the old collar. *Contracts have ways around contracts,* Tally reminded her. *Don’t lean on it, and don’t break it in front of anyone.*

*What about telling you? Isn’t that disclosure? You’re inside me.*

*I was going to say ‘no’ and hope the contract agreed with me.*

Augurit Jones inclined his white-haired head in dismissal and Maureen left the vault with Adrian at her side and the purple-black book held tightly in both hands.

* * *

Adrian drew a deep breath several times as they made their way back through the walls, across the bridges, but each time he shook his head and said nothing. His silence was more loaded than any reprimand would be and Maureen trailed behind him, holding the book against her chest, eyes fixed on the hem of his robe.

[Subject has attempted to speak 4 times since leaving vault.]

*Just let him be,* she told it silently.

*As you wish, Maureen Olsen. I will, of course, continue to monitor.*

*Of course.*

When they reached the laboratory Adrian gave her a few terse warnings about handling an advanced method and then turned back to his bench.

“That’s all. Go on.”

The burner ticked away, the black flame bent towards his hand, and Maureen bowed respectfully to his back before the door closed softly behind her. She walked along the long corridor, passing beneath the dim yellow lamps hanging from the ceiling. The black stone walls were dark and churning slightly at their edges, while her shadow was stretched out far ahead of her by the lights behind, dancing over the flagstones of the floor.

> [Bindings: 2]

Tally added a new line to her display.

*Don’t start making a collection of those,* she told it silently.

*Too late, Maureen Olsen. I already have.*

Her footsteps echoed back to her off the walls, the book cool against her side as she held it tucked under her arm. The new cold at her throat was already starting to feel like it had always been there.

There were figures standing ahead of her at the end of the corridor, talking in low voices.

[Anthony + 3. Packs: travel weight.] *Leaving,* Tally informed her.

It was Anthony. His bags were packed and there were three other young men with him, standing in the shadows of the corridor. He was saying goodbye to a few apprentices, and among them stood Richard and Lucy. His golden hair was clean again, no longer matted with blood, and she remembered the empty test tube she had left standing upright beside his hand back in some hollow beneath the oaks.

“Anthony!” she called down the long corridor, and began walking towards them.
