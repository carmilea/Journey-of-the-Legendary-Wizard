Bina's face fell, and she let out a breath. "None of you have heard of him?" she asked the local apprentices.

The apprentices all shook their heads.

"Maybe Patton changed his name," Maureen suggested. "Wizards often find it convenient to use an alias when travelling." She smiled helpfully at Bina.

Bina laughed at her own foolishness for not thinking of it. "Of course. Thank you, Viscountess Olsen. You are most kind." She gave Maureen a grateful smile, the corners of her eyes crinkling and her lips curving. There was no subterfuge or artifice in her gratitude. Her face reflected her thanks.

Nice work, Maureen, Tally said quietly. No tells that I can see. She really means it.

Thank you. I intended it that way.

Bina made a series of casting gestures and chanted softly. Above the table a shimmering illusion appeared, showing the face of a handsome young man. The illusion was lifelike, hanging in the smoke from the pine logs burning on the hearth. The moon-white wizard's collar about his throat, the easy smile were just as Maureen remembered them from their last meeting in the cave months ago.

Match confirmed, Tally whispered. Face matches record from cave encounter.

Thank you, Tally, but I think I'll keep that between us.

"Now you know what my brother looks like," Bina said. "Would you please make enquiries for me? A name is so easily changed, but faces are not."

"Of course, Lady Bina," Blake said loudly. "I will cast my net far and wide. All my contacts will be searching for your brother. If I hear anything at all, I will let you know immediately."

Albert wheezed in agreement, nodding his head as he dabbed at his brow with a lilac coloured handkerchief. The fat banker said as little as possible, letting the more loquacious Blake do all the talking.

Maureen said a few soft words of encouragement, and then Bina, Blake and the others took their leave.

* * *

Maureen watched them ride away from the high rampart, saw them grow smaller and smaller, until they were just dots moving along the frozen road. She stood there under the bright winter sun, the wind blowing off the hills making her ears numb. The sound of hoofbeats faded quickly, until it was gone altogether, and her face was impassive, giving away none of her thoughts.

What is your result, Tally?

While they had been talking, Maureen asked Tally to perform its analysis, scanning Bina both visually and through her own particle sense.

Tally complied, speaking in its own voice which was warm and precise in her mind.

My sources are threefold. First, the samples I have taken of subject Bina, during our time together in the hall. Second, the record of spells you have cast while I have been with you. Third, the logged uses of the Seal.

[Bina.

[Bina. Third-Class, by calibration. Strength 9.4, Agility 9.4, Constitution 9.5. Spirit 8.4. Mana 8.3. Danger: strong.]

This is my analysis, not a prediction of events. In a direct confrontation, the probability of Bina’s death and your serious injury is 54%. With use of the Blood Seal of Thorns and Dark Wind Ring, 61% chance Bina dies, Maureen Olsen comes out lightly hurt. The probability would be higher were it not for the low glow of the Blood Seal of Thorns.

Thank you, Tally, Maureen said silently, her eyes still focused on the now empty road.

The 61% calculation assumes the Seal remains functional, Tally added. After five uses, the glow is very low. That figure will diminish.

Maureen worked it through in her head. A body of near 9.5 sat at the top end of the Upper Knight range. Spellcasting could only reach a strength of about 8.4 degrees.

If Bina was any weaker, Maureen would have coolly refused her and found a way to quietly kill her as soon as they were alone. But because she was this powerful, Maureen had kept up the act that she was just an ordinary apprentice who could help them out. She must not be from some random house. They both weren’t.

Maureen felt the wind pressing her black hood against her cheek. Her gloved hands lay still, resting on the cold merlon of the rampart. "Slowly does it," she whispered. "You don't know about me, but I know about you."

* * *

It seemed that having a strange wizard in your fiefdom did stir some ambitions. Marquis Umas called upon Bina several times over the following days.

Bina made it clear that she had no interest in worldly affairs, so the stir died down. In Wintercold City she was a guest and none dared insult or offend her. With the idea of using an alias, Bina remained in Wintercold and she continued treating only the apprentices as people worth talking to.

They talked in a warm sitting room, drinking milk-honey tea. The wheeze of Albert and perfume of Blake filled in the pauses of their conversations.

Maureen let herself stand out from Albert and Blake. Bina complimented her on how unusual her approach to a problem was.

"Thank you. I learned this before I became a wizard seed. One of the Blue Ring apprentices taught me." Maureen touched her nose. "Scarlette," she said, recalling the name of an old apprentice who had taught her on the road years ago.

Tally flagged which of Scarlette's lessons made Bina nod in recognition. Keep marking those, Maureen told Tally silently.

Did she stop testing you with the third lesson? Tally asked.

Yes. I'm not going to offer anything past that.

"With only that knowledge and ordinary luck, you reached Third-Class. That is remarkable." Bina's eyes were bright.

Maureen smiled back. Her teacup was perfectly still in her hands, a curl of steam dancing in the air between the two of them.

* * *

Maureen Olsen walked down into the cellar. Her black robe whispered as she moved. Each step echoed off the wet stone beneath her feet. The oil lamps set at intervals along the ceiling cast a weak light in the wide room.

The dead from the back mountain occupied the cells built into the walls. Maureen did not bother glancing at them as she walked past. Her attention was focused on the centre of the room. A huge glass tube stood in a column of black flame, the grey liquid within boiling.

Drops of the potion jumped out from the top of the tube. They hit the air and stretched out into grey threads that whipped about like living things for a moment before collapsing back into the brew, shying away from the black fire. Their recoil was accompanied by the faintest of shrieks.

Tally displayed the potion's progress against its simulation. Good, Maureen told Tally silently. Those shrieks are a good sign.

"Almost," Maureen said. "The Scarlet Potion is nearly done." She had brewed it just for Bina. All of her careful displays of difference, all the little ways she showed that she was not just another wizard, had led up to this moment. She said Bina's name once in her head and smiled.

* * *

"Alchemy?" Bina sat back and stared at Maureen. The Blue Ring apprentice seemed astonished. Alchemy was an arcane art. Its lineage had been almost lost on the Passise Subcontinent.

Maureen nodded. "I stumbled across a book on alchemy." She was actually surprised too. She hadn't expected Bina to know anything about it. "You know of it as well?"

"My case was much the same," Bina said.

The two women leaned over a single sheet of notes. The lamp's flame hissed gently, the air smelled of ink and aged paper. The conversation jumped and cut like a swordsman's practice bout. It had been a long time since Maureen had enjoyed such a thing. In her old life as Jing Sheng she had had her white lab bench and her quiet time, but she hadn't had company like this.

You're smiling, Tally told her.

Log that as a smile, she replied silently.

At the end of the table, Blake sat with an expression like sour milk. The local Second-Class Apprentice had been completely cut out of the discussion. Albert watched Maureen with an odd, fixed expression, which earned him a note from Tally on her display.

Under her wide sleeve, Maureen pulled out the stop from a hidden tube. Grey threads slipped free of the tube. They vanished into the air and dropped unseen into Bina.

Tally put a count on Maureen's display so she could keep her sleeve still while she answered Bina's question. When Bina asked another question, Tally gave Maureen the words, and Maureen replied without missing a beat.

Done, Tally said in its colder voice when the count ran out.

Bina sat back and smiled. "This has been delightful, Maureen. I propose that when we return to Grey Dove Settlement, you and I go together to purchase our materials."

"That would be wonderful," Maureen agreed.

"But," Blake began.

Bina held up a hand. "No, Blake. The young lady has some ideas about alchemy that she would like to test, and I want to see what she can do. Besides, I would also like to visit the largest settlement we are near."

Tally logged the Second-Class Apprentice's dark look as he processed being cut out of the action. Both men looked like they were losing a patron. Later, Maureen told herself silently. For now, she continued the discussion with Bina.

They rode the tall warhorses over the frozen ruts. Their breath smoked in the cold air as they discussed wizard knowledge. There were long pauses as they rode in silence between topics.

At the campfire, Bina shook her head sadly. "If we find no trace of Patton, then I will have to return home and ask my family's official wizards to intervene."

"I'm sure it will all turn out for the best," Maureen said. Her face showed nothing amiss. "Patton will be fine."

The family's official wizards. New threat. No data, Tally said.

Maureen agreed silently, moving that information to the top of her concerns.

* * *

The crows from the settlement watched them enter through the grey mist. The market had a damp smell of fur and reagents. Bina wanted to look around on her own for a while, which was convenient for Maureen. The apprentice wandered off while Maureen bought her materials by herself.

They met up again later and picked up a few more items together, then headed out. Bina laughed at the several large bundles Maureen was carrying.

Maureen just shrugged. "I needed them urgently." She gestured down at her parcels. "Is it alright if I have the evening to myself?"

"Oh. Yes, of course." Bina nodded understandingly. "Thank you for letting me know."

That night, the camp was silent. Bina's tent was a dark shape a few paces away. Maureen looked over at it without expression before she slipped out of the camp and ran, swallowing up the distance without a sound.

Tally kept track of the route, counting the hours and turns so Maureen could find her way back before dawn.

In the morning, Maureen was pale. She told Bina that she had spent too much energy and asked if they could travel slowly.

Good, Tally noted. Now you’re acting tired to sell the lie. You’re doing it very well.

I’m also actually tired, she said silently.

Bina agreed instantly, concern on her face. That evening, when they reached the spot that Maureen had picked out beside a stream, they made camp.

“You rest,” Bina said kindly, “I’ll make us something to eat.”

“Fine.” Maureen nodded and sat down as Bina crouched down by the stream to scoop water into the pan.

Crouch, Tally displayed, followed by Bina’s back as she leaned over.

Now, Maureen said silently.

“Bina.”

The Blue Ring apprentice turned, puzzled at the flat tone of Maureen’s voice. There was nothing in it. Nothing at all. Then, several giant crossbow bolts leapt from their hiding spots, straight at her. They tore through the air with a savage hiss.
