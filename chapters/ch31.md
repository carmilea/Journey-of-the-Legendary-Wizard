Maureen turned away from George’s book stall, and walked slowly towards the sound of shouting. With each step the voices grew louder and more strident.

There was a ring of apprentices packed between the stalls. Wet wool from their cloaks mixed with the stink of reagents, and a whiff of something scorched hanging in the air. *There were thirty-one faces pressed around the edge of the crowd,* Tally said.

*Not now, Tally,* Maureen said silently. *No need to do a full census.*

*I don’t have to,* Tally answered, just a touch smug. *Nineteen of them are on the blonde girl’s side anyway.*

In the middle of the crowd, the blonde girl was jabbing an accusatory finger at Lilith, her shrill voice rising over the rest of the apprentices.

“You ruined my White-Bone Flower! You’ll pay for it, now!”

Lilith’s face was pale, and it looked like she was about to burst into tears. “Julie, no, I didn’t. I only touched it, and it withered by itself.”

A tall, burly boy with brown hair stood beside the blonde girl. He sneered down at Lilith as she made her excuses.

“Jax, make her pay,” the blonde girl snapped.

*Opening new file on this Jax,* Tally said. *Moving it well away from Mr. Jack’s file.*

*Yes, that’s a good idea,* Maureen said silently. *Keep Mr. Jack’s file closed.*

Whispers were running around the ring. Doubtful eyes regarded the slender, pale-skinned Lilith. In the centre of the ring, on the black stone floor, was sitting a small crystal box, with a withered flower inside.

“You’re making Wizard Adrian look bad,” the burly boy, Jax, said. “Give us seven magic stones to fix this, or else.”

“I don’t have any magic stones,” Lilith whispered, looking close to tears.

Tally alerted Maureen when it noticed the boy shifting his weight onto his front foot. The boy was taking up the standard grabber’s stance.

*He means to touch her,* Tally said.

Maureen didn’t need to be told twice. She had already moved into position.

The burly boy grinned evilly. “Well then, we’ll just have to search you for them. To prove your innocence. That you didn’t do anything wrong.” With that he reached out a large hand towards Lilith’s shoulder. The girl froze, staring at his hand in horror.

An inch before Jax could touch Lilith, Maureen’s hand shot out and clamped down on his thick wrist like a vice. “In front of everyone, you want to search Lilith...” Her sudden movement made him stumble back a step. “Is that appropriate?”

Maureen’s fingers had closed over his wrist with enough force that she could feel his pulse racing. Her voice had dropped instead of rising, giving more emphasis to her words.

* * *

Lilith ducked behind Maureen like a startled fawn. Maureen shook her head at Lilith before turning back to face Julie and Jax, chin slightly raised.

“Aw, is that your little guardian angel?” Julie sneered. “Fine! You pay the seven stones then!”

“They have to pay,” Jax said, rubbing his wrist.

“I didn’t do anything!” Lilith protested.

Maureen put a hand on Lilith’s shoulder and looked up at Jax and Julie. “I know, Lilith. I know.”

Then Maureen smiled and laughed softly. “It’s not that we won’t pay. It’s just that we don’t need to. You see, that thing isn’t a White-Bone Flower at all.”

Ignoring the stares she was getting from Jax, Julie and the press of faces that had turned towards her, Maureen crouched down and lifted the flower out of its crystal box.

Tally flagged the cold on her palm. The White-Bone Flower’s entry in its library mentioned the persistent cold, but this cold was already thinning out on one side.

Tally enlarged its own capture of the petal Maureen had just been looking at and fine wrinkles appeared that her eye had passed over.

A small private pleasure came from noticing the wrinkles, the same sort of pleasure she once took when she’d get a slide perfectly clean underneath the glass of her microscope.

Maureen’s particle sense detected a layer of negative-energy particles clinging to the petals, and Tally matched them against its library notes on disguises.

“A real White-Bone Flower is known because it grows in graveyards,” she told the crowd of potion apprentices, “its petals are smooth and round, and it has a faint cold that never fades.”

She held the flower up in the lamplight so they could all see. It did indeed have rounded petals, but there were also very fine wrinkles in the petals. The cold she felt only came from the treatment with negative-energy particles, a simple disguise to make the flower appear cold.

“The real name for this flower,” she said aloud, “is a Snake Phoenix Orchid. The ‘withering’ is what the orchid does by itself after the particles corrode it for a while.”

*It would have taken less time to say all that,* Tally commented smugly inside her head.

*No one can hear you say it though,* Maureen responded silently.

“That’s nonsense!” Julie shrieked. “This is a seven stone White-Bone Flower!”

“Are you sure you want to keep insisting on that Julie?” Maureen asked calmly, not looking away from the flower. Tally displayed the price of Snake Phoenix Orchids for her, pulled from the board and the prices at various stalls. One stone could buy a hundred standard portions of Snake Phoenix Orchid. Tally also displayed the reaction the orchid gave when immersed in Deoxygenated Water.

Maureen turned around and paid the apprentice running the reagent stall next door for a vial of Deoxygenated Water. She held the small glass vial up for everyone to see. “Every apprentice here uses this to process their materials.”

Maureen reached into her belt kit, pulling out one of three clean test tubes from the roll there. She poured some of the liquid into the tube, then carefully dropped the Snake Phoenix Orchid into the test tube. Holding the tube up to the lamps, she waited as the liquid briefly bubbled around the flower, before settling down. The clear water in the tube suddenly turned a cloudy purple, while the crowd leaned in to get a better look.

“Violet Orchid Solution,” Maureen said aloud. One of the most common mixing agents, there was no way anyone present wouldn’t know what it was.

*Textbook.*

Tally’s voice sounded in her head, smug and self-satisfied as it flashed up the library record for Violet Orchid Solution. She rolled her eyes at Tally’s display, which floated in front of her eyes.

*The textbook is you.*

*Exactly.*

The crowd’s looks turned on Julie and Jax now. “One stone buys you a hundred portions of this orchid,” Maureen said loudly enough for them to hear over the whispers from the rest of the crowd. “And you expected seven stones?” Julie opened her mouth and Maureen flicked her wrist, smashing the test tube onto the black stone floor. Julie’s voice died abruptly.

Purple liquid splashed across the floor, mingled with splinters of glass. For a moment Maureen savoured the silence she had caused. Then she turned back to Lilith, who was cowering behind her. “It’s over Lilith, we can go now.”

Lilith nodded and fell into step behind her.

* * *

Maureen walked towards the door, half turning to keep Lilith at her left shoulder.

Behind them came the hiss of steel being drawn from its scabbard. Maureen felt the air shift behind her as Jax roared his fury, his face dark red and eyes bloodshot, his brown hair falling around his head in a mop. “Die, bitch!” he screamed, raising a broadsword high over his shoulders and bringing it down hard towards Maureen’s back.

The crowd gasped and Maureen heard Lilith scream “Maureen! Look out!”

At the very edge of her vision Maureen saw a dark arc rise high in the air.

> Behind-left. Broadsword. High.

Tally flashed.

Maureen felt a cold spike run up her spine. Her body was moving before she felt the cold stab of fear in her gut. Tally flashed again as Jax’s swing committed itself to its target.

> Now.

Maureen’s right hand snapped across her hip, drawing the Cross Sword with a blur of speed. Silver Light flooded down her arm and spilled out of the blade in a silver luminescence, like cold moonlight streaming out across the floor.

There was a crash as the glowing blade met the broadsword. Maureen’s arms jarred with the impact. The force of the blow drove Jax back several steps, changing his expression from rage to surprise.

> Blue edge.

Tally reminded her.

*I know, I know,* she thought silently, keeping the coated edge of the blade away from Jax.

Maureen closed the distance between them immediately. Tally highlighted his centre of mass with a pale blue dot.

Her right heel planted itself on the floor a finger’s width further in than it normally would, as Mr. Jack had corrected her so many times. Then her left foot lashed out, kicking Jax square in the stomach with a crunch.

The boy flew back through the air like a sack of potatoes torn open by a hungry dog. He hit the ground hard, the stone floor knocking the wind out of him. He rolled around with his hands clutched to his belly, howling.

[Strength 3.5]

[Agility 3.1]

[Constitution 3.6]

[Lower Knight]

[Confirmed]

Maureen looked down at him coldly.

“Just a Lower Knight. And you dare ambush me.”

[Hall has seen a knight’s secret art]

[Hall has seen a girl above Lower Knight]

[No mention of wizard rank]

*Good.* Maureen kept her wizard rank completely hidden. *Good,* Tally noted.

Her eyes slid over to Julie, who blanched and let out an ear-splitting scream before scrambling backwards several steps. [Tremor detected in shrill breathing]

Tally noted.

*Humiliated people keep ledgers too,* Tally warned.

*So do I,* Maureen replied silently.

Tally added, *Yours is better kept.*

Maureen sheathed the Cross Sword into its scabbard.

“Lilith, let’s go.”

The ring of apprentices parted before them as they walked away. Glass crunched underneath Maureen’s boots. The slightly cooler air from the corridor blew over her neck as she approached the doorway.

Her left hand rested on Lilith’s thin arm. It was trembling slightly under the cloth of the sleeve, but Maureen’s hand was as still as a statue.

[Apprehensive breaths: 8]

[Steady breaths: 1]

*Yours are already steady,* Tally noted. Maureen’s eyes were fixed forward, her face calm. *Count Lilith’s then,* she said silently.
