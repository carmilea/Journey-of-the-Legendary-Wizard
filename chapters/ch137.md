It was looking at her with raw violence in its crimson stare.

The puppet’s body was still a blur beneath the surface, its details lost in the churning liquid. It roared a challenge, the sound torn and muffled by the thick medium. The roar was like a bellows packed with gravel.

The energy pool crackled as its colour shifted again. It began to slide towards a deep violet, like a bruised plum. Coal dust drifted in from the cellar bay, sparking as it touched the hazy particles. The only steady sound in the room was Maureen’s own breathing.

She didn’t miss a beat in her chant, as though the puppet’s roar had never happened. Twisted runes were being pulled out of empty air, writhing in place before they glowed faintly. One after another, they found purchase on the puppet’s frame, like leeches finding skin.

Tally’s numbered plan hung over the energy pool on Maureen’s display. Each step was sourced: the White Tower construct spreads, Gregory’s enchantment shelves, the unsealed pages Nightingale had traded for. Tally reported her confidence level in the projection as medium-low, but said that if it worked, this was the strongest result she could get.

*Not a number.* Maureen thought back silently.

*It’s an honest one,* Tally replied. *Last time I had a number for something this new, it was ninety-one. The nine was the blood.*

Maureen’s mouth continued to form the words, even as her shoulders burned with strain. A cracked lip flavoured her chant with iron, and her hunger sat like a hollow space beneath her ribs. She pushed it aside, just as Jing Sheng, the woman she used to be, would ignore her own lunch while sitting at her lab bench.

[Lattice: stable. Sand window: now. 4 s.]

A line of text flashed across her vision, and she pointed her finger.

A jet of ink-black dark energy tore free from her fingertip, accompanied by a high-pitched hiss. It scooped up the Fallen Star Sand scattered across the stone floor, the bright silver grit flying up obediently.

*Fallen Star Sand,* Tally said inside Maureen’s head.

*Plate match to a White Tower construct spread. The lattice metal we were missing, from the nine materials we took off the field.*

*Hired killers, carrying construct metal?* Maureen thought back.

*Logged. No data on why.*

The grains of Fallen Star Sand hung motionless in the current of the black energy, glittering like stars above a moor in winter. They were cold points of light in the darkness, slowly rotating as they floated.

Maureen willed the grains to fall.

The sand fell.

It settled over the dark-red body of the puppet, until every inch of it was freckled with silver. The pattern was wrong. It looked like frost, forming on raw meat.

The puppet thrashed in the pool, slamming itself against the rim again and again. The liquid splashed up and over the side, hissing against the stones as it evaporated away. The array stones flashed in their ring as the puppet threw itself against them.

[SIGNAL SPIKE: 3 s]

A short line of text flashed across Maureen’s vision, bracketed by a sharp spike in a readout. Then Tally’s voice spoke again inside her head. *It isn’t hurting. It’s the core’s array fighting the new lattice. It’s arguing.*

*Sounds like it’s hurting,* Maureen thought back.

*So did you, at the rite.*

Maureen didn’t flinch. Instead she let her voice drop into the next low line of the binding chant, carrying the words through the puppet’s violent thrashing. The Fallen Star Sand continued to settle onto the body, and Tally kept track of how many grains had sunk into the lattice and held fast.

* * *

A few days later, Maureen Olsen climbed out of the cellar. The cloth of her garments had been creased to near stiffness, and the smell of coal smoke and sour pool sweat hung around her. Her eyes were networked with red capillaries. She still wore Wizard Richard’s plain face over her own features.

The smell of baking bread from the kitchen hit her like a slap to the face. Her legs found the stairs, each step coming a half-beat after her brain told her to move. The pale grey daylight that streamed in through the windows and illuminated the flagstones in the hallway made her squint as though she’d spent a month locked underground.

Ike was coming down the hall, one arm loaded with linen. He stopped dead in his tracks, and the linen sagged with his sudden halt. For once, the elderly man’s gaze lifted above her collarbone. “Master!”

Tally could read her body from the inside. Without prompting, she projected an analysis to Maureen so that it sat in front of her eyes. [Pulse 112. Hydration: poor. Sleep debt: 41 h.] Ike’s lips began to shape a question about her health before Tally spoke again.

Before Ike could ask what was wrong, Tally listed off the things that Maureen needed. Meat, for protein and energy. Hot water, to soak the day’s grime away. Calming herbs, to ease the tension in her muscles.

“Meat,” Maureen said, using Richard’s flat voice. “A great deal of meat. Hot water, with calming herbs in it. Now, Ike.”

*You’re welcome,* Tally said.

*I would have thought of that myself,* Maureen replied silently.

*Yes. In about an hour. On the floor.*

Ike ran, his thin voice cracking through the villa as he shouted orders. The servants’ feet slapped on the floor as they rushed along passages. A pot lid clanged in the kitchen and someone cursed at the fire.

Two hours later she sat in the bath, her chin just above the water line. The bitter, green smell of the herbs filled her nostrils, the knots in her shoulders unwinding one by one as the heat penetrated. A groan escaped her that she would never have let another soul hear.

She raised a hand to her face, pressing her fingers between her brows while she reviewed the choice she had made. Of all the plans that Tally had presented, she had chosen the staged refinement option because that would produce the most powerful puppet. Stage one was completed and there were still more stages to come. *Very strong,* Tally had promised. *How strong is very strong?* Maureen asked herself silently.

*I don’t know.* Tally answered honestly. *I’ve never done this before. That’s why we’re doing it in stages, remember? Each stage will give you data for the next.*

*So you say. I guess I’m just worried. How can we be sure that “very strong” is good enough?*

*We can’t. But if it isn’t, then we’ll know when we get there and we can start again.*

*You sound excited about this.*

*I am. This is the first time we’ve ever built something from scratch together. Isn’t it exciting?*

Maureen sighed, leaning her head back so it rested on the copper lip of the bath. She stared up at the warm halo of the lamp above, listening to the sounds of the villa around her as the water cooled around her knees.

The white bench flashed into her mind, the tweezers and glass beakers, and the small prototype lying beneath it.

She wrote up the notes on stage one sitting at the bedside table, while Tally filed them away alongside her readings. Then she slept like a stone dropped in a well, and the next morning, she went down again.

* * *

For almost a month, Maureen followed the same pattern. Down through the coal bay, working until she dropped. Up for food and sleep, then down again the next morning, at full strength. She never got tired of it, never felt any desire to leave it behind. Each time she came up, though, her eyes were brighter.

Tally kept track of how many times they did it, the counter in the corner of her display slowly ticking upwards with each cycle. When Maureen tried to get her to display the total number of stones and materials spent, Tally refused. She said it would make Maureen sad, and Maureen needed her hands to be steady.

*You show it anyway.*

*No.*

*Show it!*

*No.*

The hidden chamber became familiar, even homely to Maureen. A crust of dried salts rimmed the pool, which she’d scrape off with a thumbnail before starting work. The smell of coal had grown so strong that she no longer noticed it, and a pair of her handprints in the dust remained pressed against the wall where she always rested for a moment or two to catch her breath.

Then, on the last day, the final line of runes settled onto the frame. The pool fell silent, its surface becoming flat and still like a sheet of black glass.

The silence was deep enough that Maureen could hear the array stones ticking as they cooled down. The circle of light began to dim, one point at a time, until her own breathing was louder than anything else in the room.

Then, the puppet rose from the pool. Sheets of liquid ran off its dark red body, speckled with silver like blood streaked with moonlight. It stood there, then woke up properly.

Its eyes opened, blood red.

For a single breath, Maureen saw the pattern running beneath the veilsilver. Like veins under skin, coiling thorned vines ran through the metal, pale eyes dotted along their length.

The old smell filled the chamber, sweet and rotten at once, like sap and iron.

[Core pattern: match. Blood Seal of Thorns, first activation record.]

*It’s the armour. It’s still the armour, it just stands up now.*

Maureen’s throat closed over and she said nothing. Her hand went to her chest, to the inner pocket where the Seal had ridden against her ribs for so many years. It stayed there.

*Nine and a half seconds on the ship. I counted every one of them, you know.*

*I know.*

*I’ll count this one too.*

Her body remembered before her mind did. Thorns pressing but not piercing, in the dark corridor of a ship. The cave, rotten and sweet. The pit.

Then the elf clearing, where she’d pushed it past its limit, feeling it crack.

The puppet roared once. The stone room held and amplified the sound, then let it die. And then the puppet stood motionless. Perfectly motionless. Motionless as no breathing creature could manage.

Maureen snapped her fingers. *Analysis?*

[Str 19.7 / Agi 20.1 / Con 20.1. Core: Blood Seal of Thorns. Lattice: veilsilver + Fallen Star Sand. Casting: none. Fixed formal spells: 3. Est. combat: Liquefied. Liquefied sample on file: none. Confidence: medium.]

Tally’s voice was warm compared to the readouts. *That’s a Liquefied, by the advancement accounts I hold from the Alliance archive. I have never measured a Liquefied Wizard myself.*

*It’ll do for now.*

The three fixed spells were release arrays that Maureen had engraved into the lattice herself. Two of them were Official models she’d bought in the Dark Forest, the other was Necrotic Black Wind. They were all single-shot spells that would recharge from the core when they were used, and there wasn’t a trace of Purgatory in any of them.

A laugh caught in her dry throat.

Maureen Olsen, Official Wizard and walking down the Ancient road, had been fighting at approximately the level of an ordinary Liquefied Wizard, and the best materials and all the stages done at her best had produced something that matched her. Even knowing that, it still took her by surprise sometimes.

*What will you name it?* Tally asked.

*How about Edward?*

*Second Edward on file flagged. Dark Forest register. Offered free teaching to any apprentice who turned over their own body for him to dissect.*

*He wanted to take bodies apart, this one I put together.*

*That is either very dark or very funny.*

*Both columns.*

*Noted. Edward, under protest, lovingly.*

Maureen pulled a plain black robe out of the pouch and dressed Edward in it. Then she laid an illusion over the top. It was the appearance of a tall, thin man with unkempt hair and deep-set eyes. He looked as nondescript as a clerk.

Tally set the anchor on one of the rune nodes in his lattice. [Illusion anchor: core-fed. Drain on you: none.] Then Tally had Maureen’s eye run over the borrowed face. *Left cheek is lagging.*

*It’s not even his cheek.*

*It’s still lagging.*

Maureen waved her hand, smoothing out the illusion on Edward’s left cheek. Then she studied her handiwork. The man in the coal-dust light was a stranger. He had no history. That was exactly what she wanted.

“Manas.”

The coal-bay wall closed behind them.

Tally double-checked that the Rental Centre ward-knot still ran up against Gregory’s second ring. Satisfied, Maureen led Edward through the cellar and up the stairs. His feet made no sound at all on the wooden stairs.

* * *

“Everyone, come in!” she shouted. The household filed into the dining room and stood by the long oak table. Maureen positioned Edward beside her chair. “This is Edward, my friend. From today he lives here with us. You will treat him as you treat me. Nobody is to so much as brush his sleeve the wrong way. Understood?”

Edward stood too still at the table. The beeswax lamps cast their light upon him in the same way they illuminated the statuary in the garden—without the subtle shimmers and shifts of the flame that accompanied breathing men. The servants took an extra half step away from him as they came in, without being told. Ike stared for a beat, then bowed deeply. “Yes, Master. I swear that none under this roof shall cause offence to Mr. Edward.”

Tally watched as eight pulses jumped in response to Maureen’s decree to treat Edward “as you treat me”. Medium confidence prediction: The young groom would avoid Edward for a week.

Maureen ate like a starved dog. Grease from the mutton dripped from her fingers, crusty black bread was torn apart in her fist, plates were cleared and replaced twice. Edward never sat down.

Over the next few days, Tally logged the prediction as correct. The groom always took the longer path through the stable yard when he needed to get from one side of the house to the other. Maureen silently called Tally smug. Tally corrected her. *Precise.*

After another few days’ rest, Maureen Olsen rode out to the Alliance as Wizard Richard, leaving Edward standing guard over the house.

* * *

It had been over a month since she walked these corridors last. Wizards nodded as she passed. “Wizard Richard.”

The very young pharmacist was known to all within the Alliance. A pharmacist whose skill none could match.

(Seventeen.)

Two voices, lowered behind a pillar, reached her ears. Complaining that Ambrose had allowed the boy to disappear for a month without a word. Favouritism, they said, though neither would risk saying it to his face.

The smell of beeswax, the cold breath of the marble, the purple robes that whispered across the floor. Somewhere behind a closed door, glass chimed against glass. Richard’s plain face maintained its unremarkable lines as she walked through it all.

She knocked on Ambrose’s door, then entered. The office was crowded with visitors.

“Richard. Good, you’re here.” The old wizard smiled. No questions about where she had been.

[Pause: 0.7 s]

He had noticed but chosen not to ask. Maureen Olsen silently filed it away.

The room was over warm with so many bodies in it. The scents of camphor and old paper mingled with the rich odour of good wool and expensive perfume. Jars lined the wooden shelves, their surfaces catching the light from the single narrow frosted window like the dull eyes of dead fish.

Ambrose introduced the visitors, old families from the western Passise Subcontinent, families with weight across the entire West, even within the Alliance itself. Among their number was the eldest son of the Lenny family, Connie Bahr of the Bahr family, and an uncle to the current head of the Kanners family.

Tally read their densities against her samples. The Lenny heir came in at approximately Soros’s weight. The Kanners uncle was close to Maffie’s density, and he was the one who mattered in this room. The others ran thinner than him.

They exchanged formal wizard courtesies; bows down to the exact finger, warm voices, beautiful manners. Maureen Olsen read every one of them as a knife in a velvet sheath.

Ambrose introduced the last of them. “And this is Denise, of the Oserroba family. One of our most ancient and respected families on the Passise Subcontinent.” She was in her early twenties, her hair a golden brown, eyes calm and blue. There was a poise about her that spoke of fine armour well worn.

In the space of a single breath, Tally laid two traces side by side. Nightingale’s breathing and set of shoulder from the guild nights, and Denise’s, with the density readings added.

[Match: Nightingale.]

[Confidence: high.]

[Not proof.]

*That’s her,* Tally said, *unless there are two women in Emilia who hold their breath just before they lie in exactly the same way.*

*There aren’t,* Maureen said silently.

*There still should be written confirmation,* Tally said.

“Why does a fire-school stabiliser curdle when it is cooled too quickly?” Connie Bahr asked.

Maureen Olsen’s answer took three sentences, but they went straight to the heart of the matter, discussing the order of reagents. For a moment the room was quiet.

Denise’s eyes stayed on her as she spoke. Her smile was courteous, her fingers never moved upon her cup.

The conversation moved to spell lore and gossip about the orders. Finally, Maureen escorted her guests to their rooms, one by one, until only Denise remained, walking by her side.

* * *

Their footsteps echoed down the marble corridor.

“Your answer to Connie Bahr,” Denise said lightly, “went straight to the bone, Wizard Richard. I was impressed.”

“I spend too long wandering the library,” Maureen said. “It leaks out.”

Beeswax smoke from the lamps curled around them. Long shadows stretched away from each lamp, running down the corridor and across the pale marble floor. The cold from the stone floor came up through the soles of Maureen’s boots. Somewhere far behind them, a door shut with a soft click.

*Denise drew a small breath before she spoke,* Tally pointed out. The same little catch that had lurked behind Nightingale’s mask as she sat at the guild table.

They reached Denise’s dark oak door, and as the young woman turned her key in the lock, Maureen said, “We do seem to get along, you and I. Masks on or masks off.”

Denise’s brow creased prettily. “I’m afraid I don’t follow, Wizard Richard.”

“Then I’ll say it plainer.” One corner of Maureen’s mouth tilted upwards. “Should I call you Denise, or Nightingale?”

Tally timed the flinch: 0.3 seconds.

A pale blue mark settled over Denise’s right hand, which was drifting towards the cuff of her sleeve.

Denise’s smile did not falter. Not even a tiny bit. But under that perfect smile, her shoulders locked. The corridor seemed to grow smaller around the two women. Maureen’s own pulse didn’t move. Not even a fraction.

*She’s deciding whether to kill you,* Tally commented in Maureen’s head. Her voice was quiet and cold.

*She won’t.*

*Medium-high,* Tally said. *Name the trade before she finishes deciding.*

“Easy, Nightingale.” Maureen’s voice was soft. “Not long ago, you and I agreed a trade.”

The tension went out of Denise’s body. Her shoulders came down and the breath hissed out of her. “So it was you,” she said quietly. “Owl.”

Her hand fell away from her sleeve and she laughed once. It sounded a little unsteady to Maureen’s ears. But the corridor suddenly felt wide again.
