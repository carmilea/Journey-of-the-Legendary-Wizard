The ghostly serpent’s coils rose up over Barok Olivia. The phantom’s head loomed up overhead, blotting out the flat grey light as though someone were slamming a lid down on a pot. Its mouth hung open wide, revealing a long tunnel of darkness between the rows of sharp fangs. A black mist, composed of individual particles too small to be seen by the human eye, blew between those fangs like smoke. Cold air, the temperature of a root cellar, puffed out against the rising heat from the slate roofs.

Barok twisted in midair, looking up in time to see the great head of the serpent hovering over him. All of the blood fled from his face, leaving it pale and drawn. “What is— what in the— Maureen, you—!”

He never finished that sentence.

Tally slapped a small timer onto the rim of Barok’s halo. [Halo: 100%.] She called the beat ahead of time. *He will try to blow outward with his power, Maureen. He’s done it three times already this fight, trying to break your hold on him.*

*Let him,* she told Tally silently.

Barok Olivia did just what Tally predicted. He threw all of his wizard energy outward, and for a moment the air around him was a visible thing. Rings of transparent force exploded away from him, bending the air, twisting the smoke into ripples, ripping slates off the roof beneath him. The Phantom-State wizard was trying to blast himself free of the serpent’s grip so he could run.

The rings crashed against the black coils, breaking over them like surf against a basalt beach. Maureen felt them as they slammed against her, but only as a sudden increase of pressure on her eardrums. They weren’t strong enough to penetrate her new Totem, which shaved the power off the rings before they reached her body, like a whetstone grinding an edge off a sword.

[Ring 3 absorbed.] The coils hadn’t moved a finger’s width. He was spending his power trying to escape, not succeeding.

Maureen raised one hand toward him, palm outstretched, unhurried. It was a casual gesture, like someone reaching across a table to grasp a cup of tea.

Behind her breastbone, the Darklight Orb turned over. Heat began gathering there, building in intensity until it felt as though someone were blowing on a coal to make it catch fire. Maureen did not speak any spell aloud. She thought it, and her body obeyed.

Purgatory Fire ignited inside Barok Olivia.

Black-red flame sprang to life beneath the wizard’s skin, burning outward from his center. For a moment, the Phantom-State wizard hung suspended in midair like a lantern, the flame shining out through his ribs, casting the bones dark red.

[Mana: ~9 → ~7. Purgatory Fire: burning.]

*The cost per cast is coming in at less than a tenth over my estimate. I will correct my notes accordingly.*

Barok screamed and clawed at his chest, his orange-red aura flaring to envelop him. It wrapped tightly around his body, trying to smother the black-red flames that were burning him alive from the inside out. The black-red flames ate the aura.

The smell reached Maureen then, the sharp scent of burnt hair, followed by the sweet stink of scorched honey and sulphur. The smell of the Fasol serpent’s blood.

Barok’s scream thinned, became a whistle, as if the air was passing through a cracked flue.

Tally’s timer counted down on the halo: [61%... 34%... 9%]

*Enough,* Maureen told Tally silently.

The serpent’s jaws closed in one snap, the sound wet and soft. The burning man disappeared down its throat. One swallow, and he was gone.

The fire continued to glow inside the phantom’s throat, like coals seen through a pane of smoked glass. Then the flame began to spread out, creeping along the coils of the serpent. The black mist of its body seemed thicker for it, almost like flesh.

[Phantom: density rising. Feeding? No data.]

*It ate him.* Maureen thought back.

*Not him. The fire. I would rather not speculate on the rest... yet.*

A few grey flakes drifted out between the scales, alighting on Maureen’s sleeves. They were weightless, like dust from a moth’s wings. Thin threads of smoke began to leak out from the coils of the phantom, drifting away on the hot air.

Barok was gone.

There was no body, no family pin, not even a scrap of robe tumbling gently toward the lawn. There was nothing left of Barok Olivia.

Maureen let out her breath in a long, slow stream. She felt her shoulders drop. It had been too long since she had breathed easily. Since . . . ever since she’d walked into the mercenary hall in Frostleaf to hire her first decoy, really. Certainly no longer than that.

Tally heard the breath leave Maureen’s lungs. *That’s the first full exhale since the garrison burned,* she said quietly.

*Log it as breathing,* Maureen told her silently.

*I logged it as relief,* Tally responded.

Maureen let it stand.

Below, the heat had cracked several of the slates, and they popped like knuckles. A hedge flared to life suddenly, flames bursting all along its length, and the manor’s single thin chimney leaned and collapsed into the fire.

Maureen found herself slightly surprised at how easy it had been. She’d kept five alchemical grenades on her bandolier, just in case, and had an entire plan ready for if things went wrong. But neither had been necessary.

* * *

Tally laid a comparison across Maureen’s sight, pale blue against amber:

[Ordinary new Official Wizard: Spirit ~12, small body gains. Source: Dark Forest promotion notes.]

[Barok: Spirit 14.8. Recorded.]

[Maureen: Spirit 16.]

Maureen read it twice. Barok had been a Phantom-State wizard. A very ordinary one. Yet even a day after her promotion, he was still below her on every line.

*That’s all?* Maureen asked silently.

*That’s all an ordinary one is,* Tally replied. *You climbed a different mountain.*

Something like vertigo took hold of Maureen then. She thought about what that meant, of years of it: Holan grubs stinking up the side bench, the ship’s ration lines, Silver Light pulling at the tortoise’s rail before dawn. She’d always believed that the summit was where Barok stood.

*It was worth it,* Maureen told Tally.

*Logged as ‘worth it.’ Unanimous.*

Maureen smiled briefly, to herself. Now she had a measure for ordinary wizards, and Barok, of all people, was the ruler by which she would measure them.

Maureen looked down again. A maid lay face-down just beside the scullery door, her basket spilled. One of the gardeners lay among the rose beds, felled by Barok’s bolt before the fight had even started.

[Four house servants, radiation, inferred. Two gardeners, Barok’s bolt.]

Tally provided without being asked, giving the list in a single line, before folding it away from the display.

*Thank you for not showing me,* she told her companion silently.

*You asked me to not show you on the ship,* Tally replied.

*Yes. I did.* Maureen could remember giving the order, though it seemed long ago.

They had been ordinary people. Wizards fighting this close unleashed energies like heat from an open kiln door, a torrent of radiation. Mortal bodies could not stand in such a flood.

Maureen let go of the serpent, allowing the phantom to pour back into her body like smoke drawn down a flue. She began to sink towards the yard, and landed as lightly as a discarded glove.

Tally marked a large mortal light in the stable. It was alive, and afraid. Maureen could feel its pulse thundering through her particle sense.

Maureen hauled open the scorched stable door and grabbed the horse by the halter. She led it out of the smoke, then tied it to a fence rail upwind of the fire.

The horse coughed once, expelling a breath that smelled like hot iron. The ridged horn plate on its forehead was sooted black. It shoved its nose into her ribs, looking for more raw beef. If it was bothered by the conflagration it showed no signs. After a moment, Maureen moved again. Something else was alive.

Tally registered a density inside one of the trees near the garden wall. Third-Class, upper column, by calibration. *Plant school,* Tally said. *Your eyes passed over it twice. The person inside is holding their breath. Not doing a very good job of it either.*

* * *

The tree looked perfectly ordinary. Grey bark, a scar in the trunk where a branch had fallen long ago, leaves beginning to brown from the heat. But there was nothing there, as far as she could tell.

Tally traced an outline into the trunk. It was faint gold, the shape of a person crouched down with their knees drawn up. Next to it, she pinned a pulse, running much too fast.

Maureen turned to the tree and spoke in a flat voice. “You know you can’t hide from me. Come out.”

Nothing happened. A leaf let go of its perch and spiraled to the ground. Behind her there was a sudden crack, like a musket shot, as a beam broke in the library wing.

She raised her hand. A thin skin of black-red flame sprang to life on her palm, and she reached toward the bark.

Tally made the final prediction: *The person inside will bolt left for the gap in the wall. Their weight is already shifted to that side.*

Green flashed in the air. The bark peeled back like wet paper, and a blur exploded from the inside of the trunk, sprinting toward the gap in the wall. Just like Tally said.

Maureen let her field expand, the plain weight of an Official Wizard’s authority. The runner slowed at once, as if she’d stepped into water that came up to her chest.

The runner was a girl, about twenty-one years old. She staggered in the weight of Maureen’s field, mud caked thick on the hem of her travel robe. She was wearing a sprig of something green and budded at her collar, pinned with what might have been a hairpin. It quivered with every step she took.

[Third-Class. Upper column. Plant school.] Tally filed away. [Sprig: living. Particle sense.]

The girl turned around. “H-honoured wizard,” she stammered.

Maureen held up her hand and kept it moving, inch by inch, towards the girl’s throat. Slow and certain.

The girl’s legs gave out beneath her. She sat down hard, shaking in the grey flakes.

A cloud of ash puffed up around her, forming a grey ring as it settled. The scent of crushed grass came wafting up from where she had fallen.

Maureen read the girl’s body like a reagent. Palms upturned, sweat beading at the temple, pupils blown wide. No hidden weapons in her hands.

Maureen stopped her hand a finger’s width from the girl’s neck and smiled. “I’m in a good mood today,” she said. “Anything you’d like to say before you die?”

“I didn’t see anything!” the girl blurted. “I don’t know anything, I swear, nothing at all—”

[Pulse racing. Feint not observed.] *Not proof. She was likely in that tree for the whole fight. Probably heard you give Barok your name.*

With fumbling fingers, the girl pulled everything out of her robe. A small clay pot, containing a wizard plant that she claimed to have raised herself. A pouch of magic stones. A row of vials, filled with reagents or magical mixtures.

“Take it. All of it.” The girl shook with fear. “Please.”

The plant had silvery veins in its leaves, and the leaves turned to face the warmth of Maureen’s hand, like a cat stretching out towards a stove.

Maureen’s face did not move.

“Joanna,” the girl said. “I’m Joanna, of the Jadeleaf Ring. My standing there is high, I can vouch for you, get you into the Emilia Wizard Academy Alliance—”

[Jadeleaf Ring, no relation to the Blue Ring.]

Tally noted in her warm voice in Maureen’s head.

“I’ll do anything,” Joanna pleaded. Tears ran down her cheeks. “And me. Everything I am, sworn to you. A service oath, the way our houses take them, I’ll—”

[Walker offer: 150 years’ service. 80% of all potions.]

A line from Tally’s old files popped up on Maureen’s display beside the girl’s offer.

Maureen’s throat tightened. She remembered Lilith at her door, hooded in black, crying for her. The little notebook was still sitting on her shelf in the Dark Forest. A muscle in Maureen’s jaw twitched once, and that was the only thing her face gave away.

[The girl means service,] Tally said gently after a moment, breaking the silence before the girl could speak again. [It’s the closest thing she has left to sell.]

*I’m not buying people today.*

*You bought four once,* Tally reminded her.

*I bought guards.*

Maureen ignored the girl’s offer of a service oath as if she hadn’t heard it. One word had caught her attention. “Emilia?”

* * *

A new layer appeared on Tally’s map, and the Passise Subcontinent was sliced neatly in half down its middle. In the East, the Dark Forest, Hader Castle, and the Blue Ring were marked, black orders tied in a knot with white ones, bound by grudges as old as they were dark.

In the West, a single pale band stretched across the map.

There was one fact that mattered above all others. In the West, the white orders had joined together. They formed academies, a single alliance. The black wizards there had formed an alliance too, but it was so tenuous that it couldn’t possibly move as one for anything less than the apocalypse.

Maureen thought about it for a minute. Emilia was one of the great wizard cities of the West. This girl was a very long way from home. Here she was, kneeling in the ash only a few days’ ride from the edge of the Dark Forest.

[Joanna: why here? No data.]

Tally filed the question away, but left it unanswered.

Joanna nodded eagerly, making the little sprig of plant bob up and down. “Yes, Emilia is my home. I’ll return there directly. You won’t hear another word from me, I swear!”

Hope was breaking across Joanna’s face, and a wet track had appeared on one ashen cheek.

Maureen narrowed her eyes. She considered her position for a moment. Barok had died by her hand. She could not go back to the Dark Forest herself, not now, not ever. The eastern orders were about to go to war with the underground races. Maureen did not want to remain anywhere in the East when the fighting started.

The West was much more tranquil, Tally pointed out. Emilia was said to be rich in resources. It was also an academy city, which meant alliances... which meant libraries. Libraries meant pages, and pages meant—

*Me!* Tally exclaimed.

*You’re drooling,* Maureen told her silently.

*Only metaphorically.*

Maureen added it up. If Joanna vouched for her, she would have a real name in a real city. And with that, access to basic subjects and paid subjects. Access to enough pages to record all of Tally’s knowledge. Spread by spread, until the store was full. *Knowledge,* Tally sighed happily. *Libraries...*

*Kill? Spare?*

Tally set it out neatly in two columns before Maureen.

[Kill: One less witness.]

[Spare: A name. A door into Emilia. The pages beyond the door.]

Tally argued once. *But the girl heard you tell Barok your name. She knows you’re Maureen Olsen.*

Maureen overruled her, and Tally backed off. *Fine. Then we make the leash short.*

Maureen considered the way she was making this decision. It was as though she were deciding whether or not to buy some reagent. *Is this worth the price? How much am I willing to pay for this component?* Life and death were no different, it seemed.

The horse stamped at the fence rail, and one last slate slid off the roof and shattered on the stones.

“Joanna.” Maureen smiled at the Third-Class Apprentice suddenly. “All right. I won’t kill you.”

“But just in case, we’ll take a few precautions. Agreed?”

The word had barely finished hanging in the thinning air when Tally’s display was already working up a list of precautions they should take. Maureen did not look at it yet.

Slowly, Joanna lifted her head. She stared up at Maureen, her face blank with bewilderment.
