He crossed over to one of the scarred oak tables and sat down on one side. Maureen pulled out the chair opposite so that her back was to the wall. Her boots made soft crunching sounds in the damp, sour sawdust. Light filtered into the room through the cracks between the wooden shutters, illuminating motes of dust that danced in the stale air. The bronze mask lay face-up on the table between them, its empty eye sockets staring at nothing.

*My name.*

In a room where, for weeks, she had worn the face of Richard.

Her shoulders set before she could stop them.

*Not memory, Maureen,* Tally said. *He wasn’t in the trial realm with us. Your aura changed when we performed the rite.*

*I know,* Maureen replied silently. *Let him explain it.*

“How?” she asked in Richard’s low flat voice.

She set the silver token on the table in front of her, and it caught the grey light. The man nodded his head towards it.

“His Eminence only gave away one of those tokens,” he said. “On the Passise Subcontinent. One.” Maureen’s fingers closed over the token and slid it back into her pocket.

“And he talked about you,” the man continued, his voice still without inflection. “Often. The way a man talks about the only person who never laughed at him.”

*The way a man talks about his only friend,* Maureen thought.

“And in the garden last night, when you lifted the token, I felt my blood move. Something in yours answered it.” It was such a soft thing to say, and underneath was the simple warning: kin can find kin.

*Maureen,* Tally said, *I logged that pulse in the garden. You felt it too. I sensed your blood stir, and I sensed the answering pulse from this wizard. This is a new standing risk for you, Maureen: Black Mando kin will be able to sense you when you are using your Mimicry face, so long as your blood is sufficiently stirred.*

[Tells: none observed. Not proof.]

*That is my favourite sentence, Tally,* Maureen responded silently.

*It is the most honest one, I think.*

He bowed his head from where he sat, his right hand flat against his heart. “Clair. I serve His Eminence.”

Maureen thought of the garden last night. Before she had shown him the token, Tally had already matched the grain of his aura with Aaron’s, from his escort’s samples. Same meditation method, close enough. That was why she had summoned silver instead of a fireball, and that was why she had come here alone.

*You said wait,* Tally reminded her. *I said kin.*

Maureen nodded. “Why was a Liquefied Wizard not at his side when it happened?”

Clair flinched as if she had struck him. A bitter smile twisted the ends of his beard.

“I was in Helens City,” he said. “At His Eminence’s command, searching for Silver Sparks. His blood was drawn from a Black Mando Lava Serpent of the Purgatory World. The Sparks were to attune his body to it.” He paused to take a slow breath. “He was already an Official Wizard. Two, three of ours went in with him. We thought...”

His thumb ground into the table’s edge.

“If I’d known, I would never have left. Never.”

Maureen saw Aaron again, stretched out along the black serpent’s head. His silver robe torn, his face the colour of ash. The cold of the shuttered tavern sank into her knuckles, chairs stacked legs-up on the tables, the floor thick with sawdust and the stink of sour wine hanging in the air, grey light leaking through the cracks in the wooden shutters.

“The leaders of the White Bone Tower, Hader Castle and the Blue Ring all attacked him at once,” Clair said. “That he got out at all is a miracle. He hasn’t woken since.”

Some of them remained by Aaron’s side day and night, while the rest searched the land for the rare materials they needed.

Maureen remembered the leaders splitting into three groups at the gate. One group had gone after Aaron.

*Four breaths a minute,* Tally said. *My guess. Maybe.*

*I remember,* Maureen responded silently.

*You said maybe then too.*

*I remember.*

“I took Claude’s coin because he promised me a Blue Dream Crystal for His Eminence,” Clair said. “It has not come yet. We moved him to an estate on the edge of Hastan City, so that we might be close when it arrived.”

He said Claude’s name as a man spits out a bad nut. Maureen kept her expression still, filing away the tone next to the words.

“Take me to him,” she said.

Clair paused for a long breath before he nodded. “Of course, Lady Maureen.”

* * *

The Silver Soul Hand wizard’s face went back to normal as they reached the door. The scruffiness vanished, his hair smoothed down until it lay flat and neat against his head. A long dark coat appeared to hang from his shoulders. His aura pressed outward, like the still air before a storm.

A carter pulled his horse off to the side to let them pass, but didn’t seem to know why. Maureen could feel it too, a pressure on her skin like the heat coming off an open oven door. She kept her own aura folded down.

Hastan City’s lanes smelled of coal smoke and frozen dung. Laundry hung between the eaves, stiff as boards in the cold. Their boots cracked the thin layer of ice in the cart ruts.

*Turns: 31,* Tally noted helpfully. They’d turned left or right thirty-one times since leaving the tavern. *He’s taken us in circles twice since then,* she added. *On purpose.*

*He’s careful,* Maureen responded silently.

*He’s good,* Tally replied. *Careful is what the bad ones call themselves.*

The estate was at the edge of town, walled by ancient grey stone.

“The house of a noble,” Clair said. “We acquired it when we arrived, so that we might be near when the Blue Dream Crystal was delivered, as Claude had promised.”

The lawn outside the wall was brown and frost-bitten. The fountain in its centre had frozen mid-spray, the water now a crown of white stone. Small pale-yellow flowers bobbed their heads in the wind, stubbornly blooming despite the season.

Tally read several dense sources in the house through Maureen’s all-round sense. Two of them were almost as dense as Clair’s. And deep beneath the floor, like a blacksmith’s furnace banked under ash, lay another.

Silver robes emerged from the doorway and hands moved towards sleeves.

“Clair, you’ve brought an outsider here? Are you mad—”

Maureen opened her hand. The token sat upon her palm. Silver flame crawled over it.

“I think you ought to remember me.”

She watched the anger leave their faces, recognition flare like lamps catching light. One after another, they pressed a fist to their chests and bowed their heads.

Tally noted that one woman’s hand stayed close to her sleeve for just a breath longer than the others. Maureen ignored it.

Clair led her past them and down a stair of cold stone.

* * *

The cold met her as she descended. It crept up from the depths, a living thing. An aroma accompanied it, a thread of sulphur and fire-smoke. It was the smell that Tally had first picked out from the Fasol serpent months ago, and had never stopped matching since.

Aaron lay sealed within a crystal that reached to Maureen’s shoulder. His eyes were closed. His face was grey. A wisp of black mist curled around him, like smoke from his lungs with every breath.

He looked thinner than he had at the gate. His right thumb rested against the hem of his sleeve, where it always went when he felt unhappy or worried. Maureen’s chest tightened at the sight and, for once, she let herself feel it.

*Worry. For a friend.* Tally said quietly. *That’s what I logged last time too.*

A network of pale lines lay over the crystal, projected by Tally’s lens.

[Crystal: preservation, wizard-made. Tamper ward: present, simple.]

A black serpent rose from the heart of the crystal, crossing its surface with sinuous grace. Its earth-yellow slit eyes opened for a heartbeat before it sank back into the glow.

Maureen’s blood answered the creature’s call. It rose in her like a held musical note, vibrating in her chest and limbs. Heat began to build behind her eyes, slow and sweet and hungry. The taste of scorched honey filled her mouth.

[Bloodline: active. Totem: drawing. Pulse 64 → 104.]

Tally named it before Maureen could even think about it. *That’s just the Totem’s appetite, not yours. Count with me. Ten. Nine.*

Maureen’s fist clenched tight, her nails biting four crescent moons into her palm. Beneath Tally’s counting, another thought surfaced, cold and clear and horrible. *How much he would give her.*

Maureen nodded, the smallest motion. Tally damped down the surge through the Orb.

[−0.2 Mana.]

*It wants him.* Maureen spoke directly to Tally, without any sound or movement.

Tally answered in kind. *I know. We don’t feed it friends.*

The heat ebbed from her, sinking back into her bones. Maureen asked silently, *Did my eyes stay black? Clair didn’t see anything?*

*Yes and yes.* Tally’s voice was firm. *Richard’s eyes were still black. Clair’s pulse didn’t change.*

Maureen unclenched her hand, letting it drop back to her side where no one could see it tremble.

“We’ve brought apothecaries,” Clair said from beside her, his voice low and quiet. He sounded worn to the thread. “Many. Not one could touch this.”

*None?* Maureen stood very still. Some part of her fell silent and became very sharp, like it did when she stood before her bench, about to make the first cut.

*Tally.* Maureen spoke without speaking. *Read him. All the way through.*

Behind her eyes, a flicker of pale blue passed, invisible to all in the room. Tally read Aaron, first by sight, then through the lens, and finally through the particle sense. She checked her readings against Gregory’s culture hall notes: twenty-three specimens, nine labels legible.

She matched that up with the bronze book’s bloodline column, then with Gregory’s sketch of the black coils, then the Fasol, phantom and Blue Ring corpse samples. One by one, she laid them over top of each other, like cards dealt face up on a table.

[Bloodline: Black Mando, unstable, consuming host. Cure: possible. A potion to settle the line. Basis: Gregory culture notes, 9 legible labels; bloodline column. Confidence: medium.]

*Medium?* Maureen asked silently.

*Medium on paper,* Tally replied. *Higher once I see what you have in your hands.*

“I can cure him.” Maureen said it aloud, making sure there was no emotion or doubt in her voice. It came out flat and certain. She needed it to be true.

One of the Silver Soul wizards, an older man with a long grey beard, gave a short laugh. “Apothecaries from three cities said those same words. Sir.”

Tally gave a medium confidence prediction that the doubters would fold immediately upon Clair backing her.

Clair took a long breath before he spoke. “We have nothing else. And His Eminence calls her his closest friend. He trusts her. So we trust her.”

Maureen looked around the room. Every set of shoulders seemed to drop at the same time. The prediction was correct, and Tally logged it. *I am so smart,* she added.

The list was built in a stated order, starting with the easy to acquire materials first, and ending with two rare lines flagged in amber. Maureen read the list out loud in Richard’s voice as one of the women scratched it into a wax tablet. When she reached the two amber lines, the woman’s stylus paused for a moment and someone drew a sharp intake of breath. Then the scratching continued.

Maureen looked at the crystal containing Aaron one more time, but didn’t touch it.

*Hold on.* She told him in her head. She knew that he couldn’t hear her, but it made her feel better anyway.

As they climbed the stairs, Tally warned her that being close to Aaron would remain a risk until his line had settled. On the day they brewed the potion, Tally would have the damping ready to go.

* * *

The wizard escorted her out to the gate. “Name your price, Lady Maureen. Any price. I’ll pay it.”

*Don’t name anything now,* Tally advised. *An open favour from a careful man will gain interest. You’ll be able to call on him later if you need to.*

*You sound like Hank.*

*Hank kept good receipts.*

“Later,” she said. “I’ll know it when I need it.”

Clair bowed even lower than he had in the tavern. “Very well, Lady.” He held out one hand with the palm upraised. “Let me place a signal brand upon you, so I can send word when the materials are gathered.”

*No!* Tally warned. *Don’t do it. Daniel tried the same thing and used it as a leash to walk you into the pines. He told you he was doing it for your safety too.*

Maureen remembered the cold sting that came from under Adrian’s mark, and the sound that Daniel’s neck had made. She didn’t pull her arm back, but she didn’t offer it either.

“Show me first,” she said.

Clair nodded and shaped the sigil above his palm. He held his hand motionless and the grey lines hung in the air between them, turning slowly.

Through the Mirror Pupil lens Tally traced every strand.

[Structure: signal, one way, to you.

Locating knot: none visible.

Read limit: what is in view.]

*And as far as I can read it,* Tally added.

*That is far enough.* Maureen told her.

She pulled back her sleeve and extended her arm. The brand sank into her forearm with a chill touch like ash. It was dry and cold but not wet or hot. When it flared, Clair said, it would burn its message onto the nearest piece of paper.

[Brands: Adrian. Daniel, inert. Manor palm. Clair, signal.]

Clair looked at the wall for a moment before he spoke again, his voice dropping low. “Be careful in the Oserroba house. Claude is not as simple as he appears. He comes and goes too often and he has been meeting with people. He is planning something.”

Claude. His own hired wizard was warning her against him. The only thing holding that man to him was the Blue Dream Crystal.

“I’ll remember,” Maureen said. She turned and walked out through the gate without looking back. Then she walked back to Conta Manor.

* * *

The gate closed behind her as she arrived. One by one the lamps along the side wall sprang to life. Frost crackled under her boots as the air chilled in the falling dusk. Her breath hung white before Richard’s borrowed face.

A girl in grey skirts came running across the flagstone yard towards her, red patches of colour on each cheek, her breath tearing in her throat from the effort of running.

“W-Wizard Richard...”

[Jenny: pulse fast. Ran about 200 m, by her breath.]

“What is it, Jenny?” Maureen asked. The girl swallowed before she replied.

“Master Claude brought a wizard home today. He’s in the hall with the young lady, and something’s wrong. I don’t know what. Something.”

Maureen’s eyes narrowed and the last softness of the day left her shoulders. Her edge came back like a blade sliding smoothly back into its sheath.

Tally checked her before she entered. Face holding, eyes black, aura pressed flat, Clair’s brand cold and quiet on her forearm.

Maureen walked quickly along the long gallery. Portraits of Oserroba ancestors stared down at her from the walls, their hands folded primly in their laps, their mouths pressed tight against any words of criticism.

“Wizard Richard!”

Denise turned from the fire at Maureen’s entrance and her face lit up.

Maureen gave her a small nod, one ally to another.

Denise’s hand rose involuntarily to her sleeve, hesitating for a second before dropping back to her side. It was the same tell as in the corridor at Emilia. When she had talked about coming home. She seemed unable to stop herself from making the gesture when she thought about Conta Manor.

Claude sat at his ease, a glass in his hand. Beside him sat an old man. His hair was silver white, combed back neatly from his deeply lined face. A hooked nose jutted out from between sunken cheeks, and deep within the dark hollows of his eye sockets, a pair of bright green eyes regarded her with a sharp, waking intelligence.

Tally compared the old wizard’s density against Claude’s, her first Liquefied sample provided courtesy of Denise’s labelling. Then Tally compared it against Maffie and Barok. [Old wizard: Liquefied band, provisional, by comparison with Claude only. Grain: Dark Forest black-wizard type. Confidence: medium.]

The old man looked right at Richard, his gaze never wavering. One corner of his mouth twitched upwards in a smile that transformed the lower half of his face into an expression of knowing amusement, as if he already knew the joke and was simply waiting to see whether Maureen did or not.

*Likely read your black-wizard grain under there,* Tally predicted with medium confidence. *What he’s smiling about though? Can’t help you there.* Maureen’s pulse jumped once before settling back down. Tally logged it.
