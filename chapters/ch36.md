Maureen entered the room and looked at the rack of weapons beside the door. There was a great axe, a broadsword, a Cross Sword, and a spiked hammer.

Her footsteps echoed back to her, the sound strangely flat against the black stone. The flowing wall made no sound at all.

No one would dare spy on wizards testing spells, she had heard. It was an insult to do so, and it defeated the purpose of the tests by exposing the wizard's trump cards. Even the ruler of the Dark Forest wouldn't dare to try and spy.

Is someone watching us in here? Maureen asked silently.

The taboo on spying is hearsay only. No data available on whether this room is monitored or not.

Hmph. Maureen thought about what Tally said. Fine, then I'll just have to trust the taboo when it comes to spells. When it comes to the Seal... well, I'll have to think twice about that.

Three times, preferably.

Maureen closed her eyes and concentrated on her two spell models. They appeared perfectly in her memory, each one as crisp and defined as if they were printed onto paper.

Tally helped coach Maureen's tongue into the correct position for the Ancient Andas vowel required by the command word. The Ancient Andas vowel needed to be held low in the mouth, and towards the back. That was how Scarlette had instructed her to perform it.

When Maureen felt ready, she opened her eyes and spoke the word of power. "Famir!"

The flowing black light obeyed her command, pulling together into a tall knight covered in full plate armour. Only the eyes showed through the helmet.

A shimmer of light like mercury ran across the plates of the armour, flowing from one piece to the next. The knight didn't move, or even appear to breathe. There was something wrong about the figure, but Maureen couldn't quite place what it was.

Black knight armour doesn't match the pattern for Evans's knights. Radiation: zero, Tally noted.

Maureen drew her Cross Sword and lunged at the figure. The blade flashed with silver light as it pierced deep into the knight's chest. A hole opened up where the sword went in, and then black light flowed in from the sides and sealed it shut.

"Physical attack," said the cold female voice of the room, "power: 3 degrees."

The degree was the standard unit of measurement for wizard attack power. It was used everywhere in the wizard world.

Three degrees? Tally questioned. I have some competition here.

Fine, adopt that as a unit. Maureen thought.

[Unit: degree. Converting history...]

Just so you know, I can't convert anything I haven't measured.

Maureen swung the sword again, this time using the secret art. There was a dull thud, and the hole opened all the way through from front to back. "Physical attack, power: 3.8 degrees."

The jolt travelled up her arm to her shoulder. The hole started to stitch itself back up, the edges crawling together.

Maureen sheathed her sword and let out a breath. She held out her hand and began to chant. A fist-sized ball of dark red fire formed above her palm. The air distorted and bent around the heat of the small fireball. Tally highlighted her model with pale blue lines, tracing each node in succession before flashing > Hold. Now. The heat of the fireball singed her cheeks, and the smell of scorched stone filled the air. This was the first time her own power had burned her, and the corner of her mouth curled up.

"Hah!" She threw the fireball at the knight with a low shout.

The fireball exploded on contact, blowing away the knight except for its legs below the knees. "Energy attack, power: 5.1 degrees."

Tally added the colour of the dark red fireball to Derrick's Blast Fireball and Evans's deep red. They were all part of the same family. Any wizard could cast any spell, but individuals tended to prefer certain families of spells based on their personalities and magical potential. Have you recorded my state while I was casting? Maureen asked. Yes. Tally answered. Name it. The casting state. [Casting state stored.]

After the knight reformed, Maureen held out her hand and chanted the words for Hand of Negative Energy. A shadowy hand reached out from the darkness behind her and grabbed the knight by the neck. It clenched tight, and there was a loud crack as the knight's neck shattered.

Grey-green smoke poured out from where the hand crushed the knight's neck. The rotten egg stink of corrosion filled the room. The smoke didn't stop, and the upper half of the knight's body started to eat itself away.

Corrosion still active several seconds after strike, Tally noted, while displaying the mana cost of the spell. [Mana: 5 → 2] Two more casts at most before you're empty.

Three.

Two.

Fine, knight work then.

* * *

In Ancient Andas Maureen called up other races she was familiar with. She began with an ugly blue skinned monster with two heads, and finished with a pitch black one that drooled. In between each cast Maureen sat and meditated until her mana was back up. Her verdict was that her attacks did real damage against human knights, and also other races. There was some variation in damage between races, but none was immune to her attacks. The Hand spell was silent and stealthy, designed for an ambush.

The two heads of the blue monster swung at different times. Constructed from light, none of the monsters had any smell. They just had a slight wet shine when they dissolved.

Tally kept a column of degrees for each race. Butcher's ledger. Maureen thought silently.

No, a ledger is a ledger. Tally responded.

Maureen called back the black knight and decided to test the Blood Seal of Thorns. She paused as her hand hovered over the inner pocket of her robe, remembering Derrick lying dead on the ground, his white beard peeking out from the leaves and brush. The old cold feeling returned, and then she pulled out the Blood Seal of Thorns. It was perfectly round, with many faceted sides. Blood red vines with pale eyes carved into them decorated the Seal. It felt cold against her palm.

[Blood Seal of Thorns. Medium-grade enchanted item. Botany / Illusion / Mutation. Power: strong.]

Tally recited from the library's enchanted item entries. That matched the grade that Scarlette gave it.

"Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa." Maureen spoke quietly, and thick blood red vines crawled up her body and covered her in armour. Only her eyes were left visible by the end, and there was a faint phantom pair of eyes that appeared on her chest. Her nose was filled with the smell of sap and iron, while the thorns pressed all over her body but did not pierce her skin.

[Seal: 0.0 s / ceiling 9.5 s]

Tally added a timer to her display. The seconds started to climb towards the ceiling.

Maureen surged forward, her muscles swelling along her arm as she punched. Her fist burst through the black knight's head.

"Physical attack, power: 5.5 degrees," said a cold female voice from the wall.

As the knight reformed, Maureen unleashed the blood light at it with a single thought. Vines rose up in the blood light, and wrapped around the knight before crushing it. There was a final burst of black light before the knight dissolved back into the wall.

"Energy attack, power: 6.2 degrees," said the wall again.

Seven seconds, Tally said in Maureen's head. Its voice was clipped and urgent.

Maureen let the Seal go. The armour rustled dryly as it drew back across her body. Her knees hit the warm floor where the fireball had struck, and her face lost colour as her hands began to shake. She sat there for a moment, breathing in the scent of scorched stone that lingered in the air.

The Seal's glow is dimmer again, Tally noted, its voice going warm with concern. I don't have enough data on it yet to know how much reserve it has.

Is the Seal dying? Maureen asked silently.

I only know that the glow is dimmer than last time, Tally replied.

A few spells, and then I run dry and fight as an Upper Knight, Maureen concluded in her thoughts. The Seal doesn't last as long, but it hits harder. It's a trump card, but I can never show it. Not when I got it by killing Derrick.

Maureen stood up after she pulled her robe back into place over the pale eye mark that decorated her breastbone.

She walked up to the large glass service window and paid three magic stones for her hours in the Spell Testing Area. Tally displayed [Magic stones: 0] as she left.

* * *

Low on stones, Maureen brewed every day, making Recovery, Stamina, Berserk Blood and Pest Repellent. Tally’s simulated plans meant that she had good success rates for most of the potions. Maureen’s study smelt like crushed leaf mixed with the soot from the burner. The glass containers on the cooling racks would tick as they cooled down.

Maureen bought an Antidote formula from Gandolf for a few magic stones. The shopkeeper grumbled about how apothecaries were bleeding him dry. Tally’s potion board listed five potions, and also displayed Maureen’s success rate when she brewed by her feel. That number was significantly lower, but Tally recorded that as progress. That number is poor, Maureen said silently. That is your number, Tally responded smugly.

Tally provided one field trick from the library. When the Pest Repellent encountered snake and insect secretions, its vapour would sharpen and its yellow colour would darken towards amber.

For safety, Maureen sold in more than one place. Her Recovery Potions went to Gandolf under their exclusive deal, while everything else was sold at the gathering.

The apothecary apprentices haggled over the cloth covered benches, speaking in low voices so they could not be overheard. Glass vials clinked in every hand.

"You're rich now," George said, "you should buy my combined-spells book. It was left by ancient wizards."

Maureen considered the offer. "Three magic stones?"

George gasped with incredulity. "You're so stingy!"

She sighed. "A paid higher discipline costs at least three hundred magic stones. How can you ask that with a straight face?"

Good point, Tally noted. The cost of a paid higher discipline is always at least three hundred magic stones. You can't ask for less without looking foolish.

Tally reminded her that it only had the spreads she skimmed through.

"It's, it's a com..." George stammered.

She patted him on the shoulder. "I'll come back when I have some potions." She left him speechless.

As Maureen walked away, Tally noted how her stone counter was slowly climbing. A few more stones a week and you will have a respectable collection. You've made a friend there.

Log that as unconfirmed.

* * *

She ate with her back to the tables of apprentices. The clatter of plates and low conversations were a din around her. The days passed without incident.

A figure sat down across from her one day. Anthony had golden hair and his smile was bright. "Maureen, long time no see. Interested in heading out on a mission together?"

Tally noted that this was the fourth time Anthony had tried to recruit Maureen on a mission. What changed?

Maureen could cast now.

Anthony absently rubbed the gold thread on his cuff with his thumb as he waited for her answer.

Maureen paused with one fingertip resting on the table. She tapped it once.

Then she smiled, mostly due to her appetite. "Yes. Of course."

Tally started a new column to keep track of the mission. What should it count?

Everything.
