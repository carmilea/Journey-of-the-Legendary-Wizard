Maureen sealed the last flask of the batch with wax before settling it into its straw. The glassware on the ash benches ticked like a clock as it cooled. Stacks of pine crates filled with the sealed flasks towered all the way up to the low canvas roof of the Fourteenth Potion Group’s workshop. The air was thick with the smell of burnt reagent, barely covered by the scent of Viska’s smoke-and-mint tea.

[Batch completed: 40 flasks. Flawed: 0.]

Tally noted on her pale-blue display in a neat column. And then, in her own voice, she added, *I’m only displaying that because no one else in this room will ever compliment me on a zero.*

*That would be my zero,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*I guess we can share it.*

Then Tally fell quiet. Her voice dropped to a cold whisper when she spoke again. *Dense source approaching. Walking directly towards us, moving through the camp lanes. Internal structure matches one that paused outside your quarters last night, 40 m out.*

Maureen kept her hands on the straw, feeling the texture of the fibres through her gloves.

Wright, White Bird’s star and most celebrated young talent, stood in the doorway. His brown hair and deep-set features were dark in the flat grey light of the realm, but his pale blue eyes caught and reflected the illumination from above. The bronze broadsword hanging at his hip gleamed as well. The pharmacists working at the nearer bench fell silent, and Viska’s mug froze halfway to his lips.

“Richard,” Wright said, his voice ringing out like struck bronze so that every word carried across the room. “I’ve been carted back from the field more than once, and without your flasks I’d be a stain on some hillside back in there.”

Wright paused for a moment, his eyes scanning the room before settling on Richard. “Would you join me for dinner tonight? As thanks, and in private. In the permanent block.”

Tally highlighted a small area on the inside of Wright’s wrist, right where the inert paired brand sat under his cuff. The display matched the residual grain to Gus’s record. *His pulse is steady, Maureen,* Tally noted silently. *Slightly quickened, but steady. Polite men don’t usually have quick pulses when they talk to other polite men.*

“Brewing the reagents is only my duty as group leader,” Maureen answered, using Richard’s flat voice. “But I wouldn’t turn down an invitation to dinner.”

Wright smiled, as if he’d fully expected that response. “Excellent,” he said aloud. “I’ll wait outside while you clean up.”

Viska rubbed the back of his head once the door closed. “The White Bird’s golden boy, asking you to dine. Half the camp will be green by morning, group leader.” He still handed her the second mug of tea.

*You’re going to go eat dinner with the man who carries Gus’s grain in his brand?* Tally asked.

*I know. That’s why I’m going,* Maureen responded silently.

*I’ll have to log this one under “brave,” then. Also “stupid.” Two columns for that one, all right?*

Maureen followed Wright out through the camp, toward the permanent block. A squad of four Alliance Officials on patrol passed them, nodding deferentially to Wright without even bothering to glance at Richard.

The air in the ancient realm had a faint sweetness, like sugar left too long on a stove. The fronds of knee-high vegetation growing alongside their lane flipped over their blue undersides as the two wizards walked past. There was no wind to blow them, no breeze to rustle the trees ahead. Only footsteps.

*Nine turns between here and the blackstone block,* Tally noted. *Filing the way back now.*

*Why?* Maureen asked.

*It’s what I do. Habit I have no intention of breaking tonight.*

Wright walked half a pace ahead of Maureen. Not once did he look back to see if Richard was following, like a man leading a horse he’d already paid for. Maureen kept her gaze on the set of his shoulders, and said nothing.

* * *

The door was black stone, as thick as Maureen’s hand. It closed behind them with a soft, heavy sound, final as a tombstone being lowered into its place.

Inside, beeswax burned with a clean and pleasant scent. The roast game steamed delicately, fragranced by juniper. The white tablecloth glowed under the realm’s grey light, like a drift of snow against a slate roof.

Tally read the runes etched into the lintels with her Mirror Pupil lens, and a faint outline appeared before Maureen’s eyes, the colour of forget-me-nots.

[Sound ward: closed. Exit: one door. Wright between you and it.]

Nothing they said in this room could escape it.

*And,* Tally added, *these are the places where people get killed, according to your history. Like Lauren.*

*Lauren came to me,* Maureen thought.

*Exactly,* Tally said. *That’s what I’m worried about.*

Wright raised his goblet. “To the man who kept me breathing out there.”

Maureen raised hers a bare inch. “I’m group leader. Brewing is what group leaders do.”

She did not drink.

She ate very little, as well. A thin sliver of the game, its meat smoky and bitter with juniper seasoning. A corner of bread. Her own goblet remained full. She watched his hands more than his face, the long fingers that moved so gracefully around the cutlery. The cuff of his robe hid his wrists, but she would have looked there, too.

As the meal progressed, Wright reached under the table and withdrew a box. He set it on the tablecloth, between the dishes.

“A gift,” he said, his voice ringing with bronze. “To say thank you properly. Pharmacology. I thought it wasted on anyone but you.”

The box was black stone, featureless and plain except for a simple catch.

It was cool under Maureen’s fingers, and heavier than it should have been for its size. She had bought Official models with Adrian’s access, and they had this same weight, this same sense of importance. The catch gave with a dry click.

A scroll rose from the box, unwinding itself. It was worked in dark silver, decorated with patterns of gold. It rose to hover in the air above the table, supported by currents of silver that ran over its surface like sunlight dancing on a moving river. The air around it grew cooler, and the flames of the beeswax candles grew still, burning tall without a flicker.

Tally performed her books-that-eat check before Maureen touched the document.

[Active book: none detected. Biting: none observed. Floating: purely theatrical.]

*I disapprove of documents that make an entrance,* Tally said.

Maureen Olsen reached out and plucked the scroll from the air. She allowed a thread of her perception to run into it.

Ancient characters appeared in her vision, rolling out from the page as though the writing were a living thing that knew she had come to read it. They resolved themselves line by line, each spread becoming readable as Tally sorted it the instant Maureen’s eyes passed over it.

For several long moments, Maureen forgot the man sitting across the table from her. Her pupils dilated as she read, her fingertips hovering over the gold lines as she scanned for meaning. The old greed, the need to know, the joy of discovery that she had first experienced sitting at Jing Sheng’s white bench rose within her. This was a dataset. A complete one, and all she had to do was read it.

[Moon’s Dark Radiance: first half 100%. Second half: sealed.]

Tally recorded what Maureen saw, and no more.

A knot of light appeared in the center of Maureen’s vision, and she could feel Tally examining the seal through the Mirror Pupil lens.

[Caster-bound. Unlock: his spell + phrase. Forced: self-destructs.]

*Don’t touch that,* Tally warned.

*I’m not going to,* Maureen thought.

*Yes, you are. Your fingers are.*

Maureen looked up at Wright. “This is ancient,” she said quietly. “And complete... or it would be.” She looked back down at the scroll, where the unreadable second half still lay before her. “And it works on spirit.”

Wright smiled, as though he’d timed how long it would take her to reach this conclusion. He didn’t say anything.

Tally compared the text to Gregory’s recorded pharmacology. [Ancient formulas on file: 7. Complete: 2. This: complete, if unsealed.]

Maureen let out a long, slow breath.

*You’re doing the treasury breath again,* Tally noted. *The one you did in the Skran vault.*

“Impressive,” Wright said. “Any other apothecary would still be trying to translate the cipher. That’s the Moon’s Dark Radiance Potion. It drives a wizard’s spirit up and strips it pure. Nothing muddy left in there. Years of meditation... skipped.”

A drop of wax ran down the nearest candle, leaving a white trail on the brass. The roast game was beginning to cool on Maureen’s plate. Its fat was congealing, turning pale as it solidified. Neither of them had touched the wine.

Maureen set the scroll on the tablecloth, folding her hands on top of it. “That’s not a gift, Wright. That’s a down payment.” She looked up at him. “Say what you want. No one leaves something like that lying around on a dinner table for free.”

* * *

The Wizard’s smile grew warmer. “A partnership. You brew, I bring the formula and every material it needs. When it’s done we split the flasks: seven parts to me, three to you. I’m paying for all of it. You only have to stir.”

Maureen Olsen’s fingers began to drum on the edge of the red sandalwood table. Her tapping was soft and even, three long beats and one short, the rhythm of a heartbeat. The wood was smooth under her touch, faintly scented. The soundproofed dining hall let no noise escape, so it was the only thing to be heard.

Tally set the potion’s stated effect alongside Maureen’s benchmark. Phantom State at the upper edge, the next rung was a long reach up. [With repeated doses: Liquefied threshold, plausible.] Tally gave the new reach a quick measurement. [Confidence: low-medium. Basis: formula text only; no sample.]

*Plausible is enough to sit at a table for,* Maureen thought.

*Plausible is exactly how you got yourself into this dinner,* Tally responded.

After weeks spent in the Spring’s waters, Maureen’s spirit was full. It pressed inside of her, like water behind a dam. She could feel the next rung above her, it was close enough to touch. She wanted it.

Tally reasoned through why Wright would not go to Ambrose: the head of the Alliance Pharmacy Department couldn’t be leaned on. He controlled the entire supply chain for Wizardry ingredients, he sat on Irina’s council, and he would ask where an ancient formula came from.

*He reads near Maffie to me,* Tally added.

*But he’s never shown me everything before,* Tally said.

*Richard looks weaker than Wright,* Tally noted. *Richard must look like something Wright can force.*

*He thinks I’m the softer door,* Maureen thought.

*You’ve let everyone think that,* Tally pointed out. *And it’s working... rather too well.*

Maureen Olsen’s fingers stopped drumming against the table. “Fine. I’ll brew it for you. But I’ll want a few conditions added.”

Something in Wright’s face snapped as Maureen Olsen remained so calm. The warmth evaporated from the Wizard’s expression all at once. “You don’t get to set conditions. You don’t get to bargain at all.” Heat rolled off the White Bird Liquefied Wizard. It came slowly at first, like a lazy wave. The candle flames leaned away from his side of the table. The air grew thick and close in the sealed room, and it began to smell of hot bronze. Somewhere under Wright’s skin, a foundry door was opening.

Wright leaned forward. “Richard,” he said slowly, savoring the syllables. “Or rather, Maureen. Maureen Olsen.”

Maureen Olsen did not move.

Richard’s weak jaw and close-set eyes stayed frozen on her face, but a cold line ran down her spine and settled itself between her ribs. Her hands rested calmly on the table, perfectly still.

[Pulse: 64 → 97. Face: holding.] Tally began counting Maureen’s breath, measuring it against the Silver Light’s cadence.

*May I have that rhythm?*

Maureen silently granted Tally permission, and the two of them found a point of calm in the steady count of the breath.

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Maureen asked, keeping her voice perfectly level and flat.

Wright laughed again. “A borrowed face, and a man’s at that. Did you really think you could just pick up a new name and leave the old one behind? You’re Dark Forest. You’re a black wizard.”

The White Bird Wizard sat back in his chair, laying out his grounds like a hand of cards. His brand with Gus had flared as Gus died, leaving an impression of the energies that surrounded him as he passed away. Black-red fire. And something else... something that ate spells. He couldn’t quite place it until he stood near Richard.

The White Tower kept records of the corroded craters. Wright set those beside Manhattan’s burns from the tournament. With Jonk’s clearance, he’d read an Eastern inquiry about a deserter from Dark Forest, a girl with black hair who burned black-red. The Olivias wanted her back. Richard had come west on Joanna’s caravan.

Tally checked each of Wright’s grounds against her own records. She found them all consistent with what the White Tower and the Olivias should know about Maureen. *The Eastern envoys are likely Oran Olivia’s, medium confidence,* Tally noted, bumping his file up one line on her watch list. *The dossier might very well be sitting right now in Jonk’s office.*

Maureen said nothing.

Wright’s pale blue eyes shone like twin flames. “If anyone else read your file, they’d see a clever apothecary. I’m not just anyone else.”

The Bronze Flame’s pattern glowed in Tally’s vision, a lens overlay showing the way the mana coiled within Wright’s body. It was an older flame, etched into him by a much older wizard. Lineage... no data.

*I know that,* Maureen replied silently. *He’s not just anyone else. He carries someone’s old inheritance, just like I do.*

Wright’s face was flushed now, and his breath came quickly. A sick excitement gleamed in his eyes. “And you killed Gus,” he said. “I know it was you. I know it.”

*Frans killed him,* Tally pointed out. *You made sure that no one else saw it.*

*That’s near enough,* Maureen answered silently.

*Near enough to hang you,* Tally added. *His brand is warming with his heat. Grief, by tells. Not proof.*

A ball of heat formed behind Maureen’s eyes, and Tally immediately asked permission to damp it. With Maureen’s consent, she pressed down the Pankora fires, shunting the heat through the Darklight Orb.

[Mana 23.0 → 22.8. Eyes: black.]

Tally began comparing what Wright had said against what he hadn’t.

[Stated: name, Dark Forest, black wizard, Gus. Not stated: Night Owl, the Bloodcoat seat, Fors.]

*That’s the hole in his hand,* Tally told Maureen.

*Then I will not fill it,* Maureen replied silently.

For a single heartbeat, Maureen felt the breath clench in her throat. Two cracks in the wall. The name and the night. Either crack might be widened to bring the whole house down, so Maureen sat straight in her sandalwood chair, then let her breath go.

The wine in his goblet remained untouched. It was as dark as old blood, smelling of black cherries. Now there was another scent: hot metal. A bead of sweat rolled down his temple, but he did not wipe it away.

“So you’ll brew,” Wright said. “You have no other way to go. But you wanted to change the terms? So do I.” He tapped his fingers on his chest. “Eight parts to me, two to you.”

Tally read the flame building under his skin.

[Charge: Bronze Flame. Rising. Release: not yet. Ward contains sound, not damage.]

*Will he?* Maureen asked.

*Not before he gets his potion,* Tally answered. *Medium confidence. He wants you alive more than dead. For now.*

Maureen looked at him, then she laughed. It was a real laugh—cold, and unguardedly real. “Interesting. Really interesting. You and Gus are very much alike, Wright, aren’t you? Cornering someone with what you know. No wonder.” The laugh went on longer than she had intended.

*It stayed within Richard’s register,* Tally noted quietly. *That was for you, not for him.*

*That is true,* Maureen admitted.

*And so it will cost,* Tally replied.

Wright’s face became empty. He stared at her across the white tablecloth and the silver scroll. “Do you want to die, Maureen Olsen?”

She did not reach for fire. She picked up her full goblet and placed it back on the table without making a sound. She folded her hands again on the red sandalwood and waited. The candles leaned away from him, and the frost-pale runes held every word inside the room.
