Carter lay sprawled on the scree, a final wisp of smoke curling from his robes. Behind her, the charges she’d buried were still popping and spitting in their individual craters. Maureen’s pulse was finally slowing down after its mad gallop, though her palms were still warm from the fireball. In the lower right hand corner of her vision, Tally displayed [Mana: 0.5] while it said, *That last fireball was Carter’s. There are no others.*

*I know,* she told it silently. *Where are Durin and Sieg?*

Maureen turned her back on the body and swept the area behind her, just as she always did when checking a field before trusting that it was clear. There was a lot of smoke, drifting and settling, and cracked rock and fire, but no running figures. Durin and Sieg were simply gone.

Tally didn’t have a sighting on either of them, it told her. Nor could it see any bodies. *Lost does not equal dead,* it said smugly. *You should leave that file open.*

*Yes, I know,* she agreed silently.

A few strides away, the girl dressed in white Hader Castle robes lay face down on the grey scree. The hem of her robe was singed brown, and stank of scorched wool, and her breath rasped painfully as it dragged in and out. She didn’t move.

Curiosity pulled at Maureen, sharp and insistent. The scree slid and clicked under her boots as she walked across. She remembered that straight spine, those set feet, from watching her cross the clearing all those days ago.

Tally pulled up its old low-confidence gait match. *People disappear all the time,* she told it silently.

*And then they turn up again,* Tally said. *You said so.*

*I said that,* Maureen corrected. *And this time they might not.*

*Then we were both right,* Tally told her.

She crouched down, sliding the black-lit tip of Freya’s slender sword under the veil and lifting it away. Underneath was matted chestnut hair, marred by soot, and a pale face, one cheek scraped raw from sliding across the stones. The display settled before she’d even finished looking.

[Anna. Hader Castle. Match: ship file. Confidence: high.]

For a long moment she was back on the ship, standing in the wider cabin as Anna held court from her bunk. Her loyalists sat around her, and Cromwell’s collar was pinned to the bulkhead a finger’s width from his neck.

Anna. Chestnut hair. The bracelet that cast Usta. One of only two seeds with an enchanted item— the other being Rheas and his ring. Vanished the day they reached the Dark Forest.

*Aptitude?*

*No data,* Tally said. *There is hearsay that she was the most powerful seed, but I have no data to substantiate that claim and will not pad my files.*

It nagged at her. Anna had only been an apprentice for as long as she had, with none of the benefits. No man-eating tree, no realm, no stolen book. But she had still stood among the elite, trained by knights. How did she do that?

*There is no data on that,* Tally told her.

Maureen let the question cool.

“Anna.”

The name came out of her mouth in Jack’s rasping voice, flat and hard, like a stone dropped onto a wooden table.

Anna’s eyes flew open and she coughed up a lungful of soot, staring into a strange face, gaunt and unshaven.

“You . . . know me?” Her eyes searched the face for clues, but the Mimicry held strong. Her short hair was dyed a dull brown that did nothing for her complexion, and the mask was perfect. A stranger knew her name.

*The rasp worked,* Tally told her. *You maintained it perfectly when you spoke her name. If you must speak again, keep your lips almost closed.*

*I have no plans to,* Maureen told it silently.

 Slowly, she opened her hands, laying them palm-up on the scree. “Don’t . . . please. Please don’t kill me. I’ll—I—” Each word was pure, unadulterated terror.

*Pupils dilated, hands open, no reach for the bracelet.* [Tells: terror. No feint observed. Not proof.]

*Noted,* Maureen replied silently.

A bit of rune-fire popped behind them. Anna sucked in a sharp breath, her lungs sounding like a blacksmith’s bellows. Tally said nothing, and Maureen’s own breathing didn’t change. Freya’s sword rested in her hand, as unmoving as a pen held in a writer’s fingers.

In that silence she turned her wrist.

The slender blade slid through Anna’s throat mid-plea. There was a short wet sound, and a stream of blood ran out from the wound, hissing gently as it steamed against the cold grey scree.

Anna’s eyes opened wide. “You . . . you killed . . . me . . . Mace . . . my brother . . . he’ll . . . avenge . . . ” Her words were drowned beneath a bubble of blood and spittle, and her head rolled to one side. She was still.

Maureen held her breath one beat too long, before she exhaled slowly. A chill opened up at the base of her skull, like someone had laid a coin against the back of her neck. Her body responded to the name before her mind did.

*Mace.*

Tally had the data instantly: [Mace: unscannable file. New line: sister, Anna. Deceased.]

*Is it a different Mace?*

*Mace is not a common name. You know this. He is Osiro on file, now has Hader sister. Your pulse logged.*

*Stop logging my pulse.*

*Already stopped.*

The small figure continued ticking away in the corner of Maureen’s vision.

Maureen put the pieces together, though she didn’t want to. The curly-haired senior in Adrian’s lab who had taught her potions and been so gentle with her had a sister in Hader white. That sister had just died on the end of Maureen’s blade. She filed that away with all the other things to watch.

A faint tugging came through the glyph on the back of Maureen’s hand. She refused the pull by force of will, and Anna’s total began to unravel, thinning until it was a pale wisp of smoke that drifted northeastward toward the fog.

[Points: 12]

*Unchanged. As you wish it.*

Maureen looked at the apprentice’s body. Her gaze went to the bracelet that encircled Anna’s wrist. The green gem at its center was cracked perfectly down the middle from the power of the array’s explosion. There was no flow of particles within it.

*Should we take it?*

*No, it’s a trail back to Hader. Not worth carrying.*

Tally said nothing more, and Maureen left it behind.

The wind shifted, and the smoke from the array blew across her. It stank of burnt wool, scorched stone, and beneath it all the copper tang of Anna’s blood running dark on the rocks.

She wiped the blade on the scorched hem of the white robe. Once more she glanced at the ring of craters behind her, still bursting with rune-fire. A short, cold breath of a sneer escaped her lips, then she chose her path, heading for the line of forest that ran away from the mouths of the valley, and went.

* * *

She ran through the forest, swinging from branch to branch, her knight’s legs covering ground in long leaps, the thunderous explosions of the craters falling away behind her until they were nothing.

Bark broke up beneath her boots. The sweet air was thick and heavy in her lungs, and the silence that should have been filled with birdsong rang in her ears. Her body could have kept running all day, but her head felt like a dry well.

[Do not attempt any spells until you rest.] [That last fireball you cast was Carter’s.]

*I’m fine, Tally.*

[That is disputed.] *A dry well does not get any wetter from running.*

She stopped and looked around, then chose an old oak tree and scrambled up into its high crotch. She settled back against the tree trunk and laid Freya’s sword across her knees. She let herself fall into Silver Light, taking three long breaths followed by one short, pressing the tips of her fingers to her breastbone. Slowly, as the minutes stretched by, the warmth began to gather low in her belly. Above, the flat grey sky never changed.

[Mana: 0.5 → 1.2] [You may afford a single fireball now. At 1.1 mana, with almost nothing remaining.]

As her other senses began to clear, Maureen felt them. There were dense dark traces below and behind her, moving slowly but surely along her trail, keeping pace with her. Her particle sense spread wide and she could feel the traces circling her tree at a distance, like fish beneath the ice of a frozen pond. She didn’t need to turn and look, for she could see them with her particle sense.

[Sources: 6-8, by particle sense. School: match Freya sample.]

*They’re not attacking,* she thought.

[No, they are waiting for you to grow tired. Which you are.]

Maureen did not look down. Instead, she let her face slacken, taking on the vacant expression of someone asleep, and kept her breathing slow. Hours passed, and the sky never moved.

[Mana: 2.3]

*You can afford two fireballs right now.*

*Good. Three before I speak a word.*

*That is a good rule. Did you just think that up?*

*Yes.*

Maureen took out the heartbeat stone. It was hard and warm in her hand, pulsing against her palm. One last test, she thought. She reached out with her mind and carefully touched the stone with a thread of her own dark particles, pushing into its red veins. Nothing happened.

She pricked her thumb with the tip of the sword, squeezing out a single bead of blood, and let it drop onto the red veins. The bead of blood sat there for a moment, then rolled off the stone as if it had fallen on a waxed surface.

*What do you make of that?*

[I must consult Gregory’s library entry on bloodline seals, as well as your eleven tests with the stone on the road. Also, your blood.] Tally said. *It draws on its own line only. No one else.*

*So, it’s up for sale.*

The stone continued to beat against her fingers, [72] in her display, unconcerned by who held it, or what they tried to do to it.

Below her, the dark traces shifted slightly, drawing a little closer while the stone was out in the open, before relaxing again when she tucked it back into her robe.

After many hours, Tally reported [Mana: 4.1], and that she now had enough mana for three fireballs, with change.

*Good. Then it’s time.*

*They've been circling me for hours, waiting for me to move,* she thought. *I haven’t budged an inch and still they don’t close in. They came for something other than my life.*

Maureen dropped lightly from the oak. She ran for a short distance, then stopped in a clearing of waist-high ferns, surrounded by grey tree trunks.

* * *

She narrowed her eyes at the trees. “Come out,” she said quietly, using Jack’s rasp.

Tally noted, *You drifted up half a tone there on the second word, try to keep it lower next time.*

*There won’t be a next time if they come out,* she thought silently.

Nothing answered. There was no rustle of branches, no intake of breath. Only the bitter green smell of the ferns wafted in the still air. She might have been talking to the air itself for all the response she got.

“No? Fine.” The corner of her mouth lifted. She reached into her robe and drew out the stone, closing her fingers around it. Slowly, as if she intended to grind the stone to gravel between her fingers, she clenched her fist.

[Heart rate: 72. Steady. Not yours. Grip: Crushing. You can actually crack that stone with that grip. Don't do that.]

*I know what it's worth,* she thought back.

[To us, it’s worth nothing. That's the fun part.]

The stone throbbed against her knuckles, warm as a small bird held captive in her hand.

The trees broke. “Stop!” “Don’t— damn you, stop!”

Eight pale faces burst from the trunks, eyes stretched wide with alarm. All hands were raised toward her clenched fist. One elf stepped forward, drawing a deep breath. “Please, lord. Do not break it. Do not break the Bloodline Seal Stone.”

She recognized the voice. It was the same one that had called out the offer in the cave days ago, twice. Ani. She was older than the other dark elves by several years, and the leather that clothed her was toolled with silver, though scuffed from days on the trail. Her blue eyes, nearly black, never left Maureen’s hand.

Maureen tossed the stone in her palm, catching it carelessly. “How did you find me?”

Ani hesitated, and Maureen’s fist clenched tighter. The elf’s face broke. “Her Highness. While she followed you, she cast word to us through sorcerous means. We came.”

[That tracks with Freya following you for days before the end. No data on how the message travelled, or what form it took. No tells detected beyond fear.]

“Why tell this human anything?” A tall male elf stepped forward, his eyes burning with anger. “He killed Her Highness! Stand aside, Ani. I’ll kill him myself.”

Ani turned on the elf. “Ash, don’t— the lord only—” She grabbed at his arm.

The elf’s chest swelled, preparing a chant.

> Breath.

> Window.

> Now.

Maureen’s hand flicked underhand from the bandolier at her side, releasing a black iron egg. “Kerrash,” she muttered as her fingers opened, and the egg flew free.

The male elf looked up as the egg arced towards his face.

Fire exploded outwards from the egg, ripping through the ferns in a wave of heat and light. Fronds were torn apart and flung into the air, while the dark elves ducked and threw their arms over their faces. The male elf was flung backwards into the air, screaming, before he hit the ground, his face red and smoking.

“You dare— human—” He began to chant in Ancient Andas.

Before he could finish his second word, “Simons-Sado” left Maureen’s mouth twice, and two deep red fireballs erupted from her hands, swallowing the elf where he had fallen. He exploded, his body breaking into pieces.

[Grenades: hand 9 → 8 / pack 26.]

[Mana: 4.1 → 3.0 → 1.9]

*Two fireballs was enough,* Tally said as the last sphere of flame faded. *You still have one in your pocket.*

*In my face, actually,* Maureen responded silently.

The fern clearing stank of burnt hair, and beneath that, the sweet scent of fallen blossoms. Dark purple spatter dripped from the fronds, running down the grey-green stems. Nothing in the clearing tugged at her glyph.

Six pale, finely made faces peered at her from among the grey-barked trunks. Ash had lasted a few heartbeats before he’d been torn apart. None of the remaining dark elves moved.

Maureen turned to face them, her expression as cold and hard as wet stone. “You followed me here to die? Good. I’ll oblige.” She raised her hands, assuming the gestures for casting spells.

*One fireball and a face,* Tally noted.

*The face is the expensive part,* Maureen thought.

“Wait! Lord Jack, wait!” Ani held out her hands in supplication. “We came to trade. For the stone . . . only for the stone.”

“Trade?” Maureen let the dark elf’s hands remain suspended in air.

Ani took a deep breath. There were many things in the Underworld, strange and precious things. Many were things no human wizard would be able to get his hands on, not even a wizard as powerful as Lord Jack. All of those things could be his.

*His?*

*Jack was doing very well for a man who didn’t exist,* Tally said.

Ani exhaled, and her voice grew steadier. “You are strong, lord,” she continued. Her words had an odd rhythm, like something practiced on the journey here. “But can you promise that what we brought here will survive our deaths? The backlash from a fight might render it useless. And if you strike us down, our first act will not be fighting you. It will be destroying this . . .” She swept her hand backward, indicating all they had brought with them.

Maureen understood now what the fear had been building toward. They had followed her for hours without touching her, and each word had been considered on the way here. This wasn’t courage. It was arithmetic, and Maureen respected arithmetic.

She regarded Ani for a moment, then her brow lifted. “Now, that’s interesting.” She chuckled.

Tally caught the laugh before it climbed too near her own voice. *Lower,* it suggested.

Maureen dropped it into the rasp before it finished escaping. The stone pulsed in her palm. It was worth nothing to her, but to the seven pairs of eyes regarding her with fear, it was everything. The joke was hers and Tally’s alone, and it was the only warm thing in the fern clearing.

Maureen tossed the stone once before catching it again. “You want this back. Your Bloodline Seal Stone.” In the back of her mind, she had already made her decision. They would pay dearly for it.
