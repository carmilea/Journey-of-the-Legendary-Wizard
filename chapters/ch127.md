Maureen stood at the center of her ring of purple robes, and knew exactly what was at stake. The contribution points were a measure of the resources that the Alliance would provide her, and those resources were why she’d come to Emilia in the first place. This test would determine what group of potions she would be allowed to work on, which meant how many contribution points she would earn.

Contribution: 0

Tally displayed, and then added, *It looks lonely all by itself.*

*It won’t be lonely for long,* Maureen assured her companion.

The Preserve’s air caressed her throat, warm and green with the scent of the outdoors. Even the largest room in the Dark Forest could not compare with the ambience of this single hallway. Styluses scratched against wax tablets, filling the room with a dry whispering sound, while Ambrose’s white-gold robe shimmered in the light from the lamps. He stood before her with his hands folded, as though he had all the time in the world.

“Richard,” Ambrose said, his voice mild, as if he were asking what time it was. “The cold reaction of the Dragon-Saliva Flower. What do you watch in hours two, four, and five?”

Two spreads appeared in the upper right corner of Maureen’s vision, one tagged with Gregory’s pharmacology, the other with Adrian’s Analytical Demonstrations. Tally did not supply a single word of the answer. She simply provided the sources, leaving the phrasing up to Maureen.

Maureen spoke without hesitation, her voice flat and unhurried. “In hour two, you watch how much Quartz Grass goes in. Quartz Grass tends to lean toward light-energy particles. In hour four...” She continued through each step of the process, from hour two to hour five, without missing a beat or stumbling over any of the instructions. Her hands did not twitch or move away from the edge of the cold stone bench. Maureen’s focus narrowed as she spoke. The scratching of the styluses on wax faded away, until the only things in her world were the question and the page displayed behind her eyes.

“Good.” Ambrose nodded. He dropped the next question while Maureen was still finishing up the first. “And enchanted bones?”

She gave him the answer. “The bones of dead high-energy creatures, treated in Violet Orchid Solution. Then they are put through an oxy-melt reaction.” She laid out all of the steps for making enchanted bones, placing them in her mind as carefully as she would place glassware on a rack.

Tally pointed out, silent in her head, that three styluses had stopped scratching. And Ambrose’s eyes flickered just a bit as she mentioned the enchantment of bones.

[Pause 0.7 s]

*Ambrose’s next question is going to be tougher. Medium confidence.*

*Why?*

*Because he’s testing your ceiling, not your floor.*

*Let him.*

Ambrose continued with several more questions, each one sounding deceptively simple. Each had some sort of catch. Maureen answered every single one, her voice cool and exact, laying out the instructions clearly, keeping them in order. Her hands stayed on the edge of the cold stone bench, feeling the chill against her palms. The air around them was warm and green, scented with crushed herbs and the smoke of lamp oil. The pharmacists could not have answered most of these questions themselves.

The scratching of the styluses faded away slowly, one by one. “Seventeen?” someone at the back said, his voice just barely audible. “Eighteen at most,” another replied.

Maureen didn’t need Tally to tell her that all of the purple robes were leaning forward as if the floor had tilted towards her.

*You forgot to include my diagram again. The best one. The one I drew for you.*

*No one here can see diagrams.*

*It’s their loss.*

Finally, Ambrose smiled, the smile of a man who had laid down his card and was waiting to see what happened next. “Remarkable, Richard. Truly. Then for the last part of the test, why don’t you brew me a Chak Fusion-Stone Potion?”

A long hiss of indrawn breath went around the ring of pharmacists. “Chak.” “He’s asking for Chak.” “The Head is being...” The pharmacist trailed off, looking from Ambrose to Maureen, their eyes filled with doubt.

Ambrose handed her the formula, and as Maureen began to read it, Tally began recording it, spread by spread.

[Chak Fusion-Stone. Plans: 4. Contingencies: 2. Est. success: ~69% on plan.]

*Not good enough,* Maureen thought.

*It’s honest,* Tally said.

Maureen’s jaw set in determination. The pale blue lines of the formula faded from her sight, and she looked up at Ambrose. “I’ll brew it.”

“Of course,” Ambrose smiled. “I hope you succeed.” He waved for the servants to collect all of the materials and transport them to one of the Pharmacy Department’s many private brewing rooms. Pharmacists were very protective of their methods, and didn’t want other pharmacists to spy on their techniques.

* * *

The door shut behind her, and the grey warding stone walls muffled the murmur of the hall into total silence. For the first time since this test had started, Maureen was alone, with no one watching her except for Tally.

The black flame burned without heat. The glassware rang faintly as her sleeve brushed against the racks. The grey-white stone sat on the stone bench, looking like nothing more than a lump of old chalk that wanted to be left alone.

Tally laid out the four plans on top of the stone bench, forming them into branching pale blue lines. *You’ve grown faster and sharper since your promotion,* she reminded Maureen. *But sixty-nine is sixty-nine.*

Then Tally made a suggestion. *What if we used the Minai Totem’s amplification effect, but only on the energy-driven step? Just step three, when we bind the ingredients together. We can use the Darklight Orb to read the results before we rely on it.*

*You want to experiment during the Head’s own test?* Maureen thought back silently.

*One step. One test. It’s better to learn it here than in the middle of a fight. When you really need it to work.*

Maureen nodded to herself. She wasn’t an ordinary wizard. She walked the Ancient road, and the Totem was hers to use.

She reached within, feeling for the Minai Totem. A faint, round totem appeared in her vision, sitting above the glassware on the bench and glowing with a dreamy silver light. As she concentrated, a cool pressure began to form behind her eyes. It felt like someone was slowly pouring cold water into the hollows of her skull. The water settled into place, chilling her thoughts with its presence. Her breathing slowed to match the new sensation.

*It is active. You feel it working through the Darklight Orb now, yes?*

Maureen did not have a word for what it felt like, but Tally was right, she could feel the Totem doing something, even if she could not quite tell what.

*Fine. We will log it without a name then.*

The energy particles of the room danced around Maureen as she focused on drawing them together. She drew them close to her right hand until they began to set, taking the form of a thick black glove that covered her hand.

[Glove: −0.3 Mana. ~15.0 → ~14.7.]

Tally reported the cost before Maureen had even finished forming the glove.

Maureen clenched her gloved fist around the single grey-white stone. With a low hum, the stone burst into a swarm of tiny white ants. Thousands upon thousands of the creatures poured out from the broken stone, boiling over the glove that enclosed her hand. They bit at the glove without hesitation, their tiny mandibles sinking into the fabric of the glove. They made a sound like dry rain on a wooden shutter. The tiny ants were not strong enough to bite through the glove, but their numbers were enough to make thousands of small pinches press through to her knuckles. The stuff of the glove began to wear thin under the ants’ jaws.

[Glove: 40 s. Re-form at 30.]

Tally matched the rhythm of the ants’ bites against the particle density of the glove.

Paying no attention to the biting sensation, Maureen began the incantation. She spoke with her lips barely moving, using a low murmur to intone the spell-speech of Ancient Andas. As she spoke, the swarm seemed to draw itself inward toward the black flame, as if the words had some kind of attraction. She breathed in time with the Silver Light rhythm, three long breaths followed by one short. The biting did not stop, but Maureen focused on keeping it to one side of her awareness as the glove’s substance continued to thin and the incantation continued.

*Thirty-two… thirty-one… now.*

Maureen rebuilt the glove without missing a beat in the incantation.

*Seamless.*

Step three arrived—the energy-driven binding. Maureen let the Totem’s silver press down into her particles where they gripped the reaction.

[Step 3 binding: +, by particle sense. Est. success: 69% → 81%. Basis: one step, one test. Totem draw: −0.6.]

The particles in the glass knotted tighter where she could see them. The liquid thickened. A chalky white haze curled off the surface and hung under the warding stone. The last of the ants dissolved into the vessel. The liquid changed color, from a milky white to an even pink that was almost clear.

*Totem in brewing,* Tally said, opening a new file. *As to what else it can do . . . still no data.*

*We’ll find out,* Maureen thought.

*Carefully.*

She poured the potion into a test tube and the liquid glowed rosy in the dim light of the black flame. Her hand was steady as she worked, her expression calm and focused. Beneath that controlled surface was a hunger, quiet as banked coals, waiting to be fed.

* * *

The pharmacists were waiting for her, without any trace of impatience. They stood around talking quietly in small knots, their purple robes shimmering in the light. Every one of them knew that brewing was a delicate business, and that it often took hours upon hours of work. No one would disturb someone working in a brewing room by knocking on the door.

That door opened now, and Maureen walked out. She didn’t hurry, her breath coming evenly, and her face mildly interested, showing none of the strain or exhaustion that might have been expected after hours of brewing. She held up the test tube to the nearest lamp, and the potion inside glowed with a rosy light.

“It’s done,” she said.

The hall grew silent. Somewhere near the front of the group, one of the pharmacists set his cup down. He did so without a sound, as though even the slightest noise might shatter the glass in Maureen’s hand.

Tally noted that Ambrose hesitated for 1.2 seconds before he spoke, the longest pause he’d made all day. Then he recovered himself, and his praise was sincere. “Congratulations, Richard. At your age, to create this? You are a genius of pharmacology.”

The old pharmacist had evidently come to a decision. “Join the Eighth Potion Group. Work with Vincent on the Fink Stone Bird project. You’ll be one of its deputy heads.”

“Actually, I had meant to have Vincent take you under his wing,” Ambrose added, chuckling as though something had amused him privately. “It seems I needn’t trouble him.”

The congratulations began to press in—purple sleeves, quick handshakes, and the scent of herb-sweat. Maureen saw Vincent through the crowd—a man of middle age, white-haired, and dressed in a white coat with ink ground into one of his cuffs. His nod was that of a scholar—the kind of person who was careful never to give more of his attention than he had to.

*Official Wizard,* Tally filed him. *Density lower than Barok, not elementalised. Ink on sleeve, good.*

*Anyone who keeps receipts,* Maureen said silently, *has my vote.*

*Ink is a receipt,* Tally noted.

Karl sidled up beside Maureen, his smile so wide she thought it must hurt. “Congratulations, Richard, really. The Eighth Group draws forty contribution points every ten days. And a deputy head? Fifty at least.”

The smile didn’t reach Karl’s eyes. *Envy,* Tally filed. *Mild. Not hostile.*

Maureen blinked as if the figures were news to her. “Is that so? Not bad at all.”

[Contribution: 0. Next: +50 in 10 days.]

*Still lonely, but won’t be for much longer now.*

Maureen kept her mild expression as she regarded the room. Beneath her ribs, something sat up hungrily.

* * *

The days settled into a routine, with the workbench becoming Vincent’s domain as the Fink Stone Bird project progressed. The slate-grey feathers laid out in their trays became as commonplace to Maureen as the tick of the burner, and every tenth day her display showed another fifty added to her contribution line. 50. Then 100. Then 150. Each time Tally announced the new total in her brightest voice, as if it were Maureen’s birthday.

At the end of the long bench lay a small, dark object. A Nightnight Bird heart, pinned open underneath a lens. The valves of the heart lay like wet pieces of paper. The curly-bearded pharmacist had been at the thing for weeks, scraping and picking at it, muttering to his beard. One afternoon, Maureen leaned over and quietly suggested how he might process the heart, drawing on Gregory’s notes that Tally had in storage. The pharmacist stared at her for a long moment, then tried her suggestion. It worked.

Tally logged a small bonus for the Nightnight Bird problem. After six ten-day periods, Tally displayed the new total on Maureen’s display.

[Contribution: 300 + 10 = 310]

The other pharmacists—men and women both—in their purple robes, who had sniffed at a newcomer promoted to deputy head on her first day, gradually stopped talking about it. Their nods as they passed by the bench were a little lower each week.

*Nightnight Bird? That hardly seems a very dignified name,* Tally said one day.

*Almost everything is less dignified than you,* Maureen responded silently.

*Well. I suppose I can take that as a compliment.*

* * *

The carpet sucked up the sound of every footstep, and candles filled the room with bright light and the smell of hot wax. Several of the pharmacists sat around a table with Maureen, tucked amid the shelves in the reading room.

The curly-bearded pharmacist shook his head at her. “Seventeen, Wizard Richard? Seventeen? I still can’t credit it. Corwin, Eighth Group, in case you’ve lost track of my name among all the beards.”

“Wizard Richard,” Corwin said, “the Nightnight Bird heart has been beating me for ages. I thought there was no way through, and you just strolled in and solved it. No trouble at all.”

*He is speaking to you more respectfully now,* Tally observed. *I have logged this.*

*Log it as a colleague,* Maureen told her silently.

Maureen smiled modestly. “Oh, I just happened to know a little about Nightnight Birds. That’s all.”

The other pharmacists joined in the praise, their voices open and admiring. For a breath, Maureen’s shoulders relaxed under the warmth of their approval, before she caught herself and sat up straight again. She kept their praise at arm’s length.

Eventually the conversation wound down, and Maureen took her leave. She parted from them amid the shelves and continued alone through the corridors. She rubbed her nose thoughtfully. *Am I almost famous?*

*Among one group of pharmacists, yes,* Tally corrected her.

*Well, fame is a column you have to watch,* Maureen replied silently.

Maureen knew where it came from. The teaching of Adrian, for there were few on the entire Passise Subcontinent who could outdo him in pharmacology. And the knowledge of the Ancient Wizards, as shared by Gregory, which no ordinary wizard would ever be able to accommodate.

*And me,* Tally added.

*Yes, and you,* Maureen agreed silently, and Tally sounded very pleased with herself.

With points in hand, Maureen decided to visit the Exchange District and find out what the Alliance would trade for them. She was surprised to find herself expecting a great deal.

As Maureen navigated the turning corridors towards the Exchange District, Tally counted out the turns for her, and recited the districts they passed through. Tally kept [Contribution: 310] glowing at the edge of Maureen’s sight like some invisible purse she could feel on her hip.

At the Exchange District’s counter, a Wizard Apprentice sat dozing. His head dipped forward, jerked up, and then dropped again. He wore a grey robe that smelled of stale tea and dust.

When Maureen appeared, the Apprentice jumped to attention. His hair was messy, his grey robe dirty, and he looked older than an Apprentice should. With a visible effort, he twisted his face into a fawning smile.

“Welcome, welcome! Honoured Lord Wizard, welcome!”

*That smile is very practised,* Tally noted.

*So is mine,* Maureen responded silently.
