Eve’s herald had gone door-to-door earlier in the day. Tonight there would be a feast, one for each of the hired wizards, held in their rooms, celebrating the death of the wolf king. And an honour at dawn. Three soft knocks rang against the smooth stone door of Maureen’s guest room. She checked Richard in the glass of the window before she opened it. His weak jaw and mousy eyebrows were in place, his low-pitched and flat voice ready in her throat. The guest room smelled of wet black stone walls, mixed with the sharp scent of the oil lamp’s flame. Her own breath fogged slightly in the cool, damp air.

A dark-elf girl in a dress of black gauze stood before the door when Maureen opened it. She was sweet-faced, and her smile was practised to just the right tilt. “Wizard Richard. For the Dark Wind Wolf.” She stepped aside to let three silent maids enter carrying silver dishes covered by domes. “The Night Mother sends her thanks.”

Maureen opened her mouth to turn them away, but stopped. Tally’s particle sense brushed against the steam that rose from the silver dishes, reading their contents. Trace elements, vital bits that Maureen’s body craved like water after a long journey. Any Official would feel them, too. She stepped back to let the maids in.

*So loud. So obvious,* Tally noted.

*They want to be noticed,* Maureen thought back.

*Yes, bait. You have to watch out for the hook.*

*I am already looking for it.*

The girl seated Maureen and removed the first cover with a grand flourish. “Wild mountain swimfish caviar. The roe of our river fish, refined with Venlan honey through many steps.” She placed a small spoon beside the dish.

The egg roe glistened beneath the lamp’s light. Each individual bead was larger than a lentil, and they all gleamed in an amber-black hue. On the tongue, the roe was cold, followed by a sharp saltiness that quickly gave way to a sweetness that grew as the taste sat at the back of the throat.

*Swimfish,* Tally filed away, noting where the creature had been found on the bestiary scrolls of the Oserroba archive. *Barrov black bear,* she said as the next lid was removed to reveal the paw of the creature resting in a dark lacquer glaze.

 Then the dark-elf girl uncorked a slim flask. “Pure Blood. Our most treasured wine. They say it cleans a wizard’s energy.” She poured slowly, her eyes fixed upon the stream rather than the guest.

The wine opened up like a slow red bruise in the bottom of the tall glass. Its scent rose to fill the room immediately, rich and heavy, so thick and sweet that it seemed to sit on the flame of the oil lamp.

Tally was reading the girl through her body. Her pulse was steady. Her smile never wavered. Her eyes never left Richard’s cup. *She wasn’t interested in you,* Tally said. *She was interested in whether or not you drank.*

*Few were ever interested in Richard,* Maureen replied silently.

*I am,* Tally answered on a separate line.

Tally had twice run her particle sense over the plate and the glass before the toast. The elements were good.

The dark-elf girl lifted her own cup, her cheeks faintly coloured, her near-black blue eyes warm and practised. She leaned in across the table. “Wizard Richard. To you.”

[Warning. Wine: sealing agent. Partial match: Bina file. Confidence: medium-high.]

The cold words sliced across the girl’s toast. Then, unbracketed, in her own voice, Tally said, *That will empty your mana by morning.*

Maureen’s hand shook once, causing the wine to shiver against the crystal. A single ring ran around the rim of the glass, and then her wrist was hers again, as steady as the stone beneath her feet.

“Wizard Richard?” the girl asked. “Is something wrong?”

Her eyes sharpened by the barest fraction.

“A thought,” Maureen said in Richard’s low voice. “It’s gone.” She smiled Richard’s mild smile.

*Steady,* Tally said. *She’s watching your hand.*

*I’m going to drink it,* Maureen told her silently.

*No, you’re not. You brewed this for Bina. You know what it does.*

*Then mark it all the way down.*

*I will.*

Maureen raised the glass to her lips and tilted her head back, her wrist level with the floor, her eyes fixed on the girl’s. The cold red liquid slid down her throat and she felt it moving past her breastbone. She swallowed once, then twice, feeling the muscles of her throat working around something she knew was poison. Then she set the glass down upon the table, without a sound or a tremor.

Tally tagged the liquid as it moved down, showing it pooling beneath Maureen’s breastbone on the body map. The wine was a red stain against Maureen’s pale-blue form, and Tally laid a single damping thread against the Orb at its edge.

[Orb: damping. Mana −0.1.]

When the plates were bare, the girl lingered beside the table, offering to remain and keep the honoured guest company through the evening.

“The meal was enough,” Maureen said. “Goodnight.”

The girl’s smile never faltered. The maids gathered up the empty dishes and followed her out the door. It shut behind them and soft footsteps sounded in the corridor beyond. They grew fainter, then were gone, leaving the room with only the hiss of the oil lamp and the lingering scent of the wine.

* * *

Maureen sat on the edge of her bed, her hands resting open on top of her knees. Her face reflected back from the mirror hadn’t changed at all. The only difference was that her breathing had grown shallow and very even.

*Now,* Tally said. *Before it finds the lines.*

The body map showed the red stain beginning to emit thin threads towards the mana lines, only held back by the damping thread at its edges.

Maureen raised her right palm. Then she reached inside, feeling for the water-thread control of her mirror spell. The same tugging force that had pulled a disc of stream water into her mirror now answered her summons, and the wine obeyed. It rose from her stomach, drawn out through her palm.

*All of it,* Tally said as it came. *Three drops left in the stomach wall. Two. None.* [Draw: −0.3 Mana.]

A globe of red wine the size of her fist hung in the air over her palm, trembling slightly. The light from the lamp danced off its surface. The smell of the wine still hung sweet in the air. A single bead of the stuff quivered at the bottom of the sphere, like a tear that refused to fall.

Maureen snapped her fingers.

Black-red flames blossomed from empty air around the globe of wine, consuming it without a sound, without sending up even a trace of steam. When they vanished there wasn’t a single drop left to hit the floor.

[Purgatory Fire: −2. Mana: 19.7 → 17.7. Residue: none]

*Without me you’d be on the floor tomorrow,* Tally said.

*I know,* Maureen responded silently.

*Noted. I am writing that down,* Tally said.

Finally, alone in the room, Maureen spoke aloud for the first time since the girl had left. Her voice was low, and it was her own. “Dark elves... what are you after?”

Her breath blew out, long and slow.

It was a good present. A gift that any other wizard would have emptied eagerly. It was too good, and the poison too well hidden. If she didn’t have Tally...

If she didn’t have Tally, she would have.

Maureen walked over to the window and pulled the heavy curtains shut. The fabric was coarse under her hands, and dust rose at her touch, tickling her nose. The faint blue glow from the plants outside vanished behind the thick cloth.

Tally created a new file, sealing agents. The dark elves’ variant went into the file beside Maureen’s own brew for Bina, with a note about the new source. Tally set a standing check on every cup and plate that passed near the elves from now on.

Then Tally gave Maureen her forecast. The wine had been intended to take out every hired wizard at the Spring tomorrow. Confidence medium. Basis for assessment: the three sources had sworn together last night; the hirelings were expendable; Denise’s intelligence fit too well.

Maureen sat on the edge of the bed, thinking through the options. She could refuse the honour, but that would mark her out as the one guest who hadn’t partaken. Or she could go ahead and fall for real.

*There is another way,* Tally suggested. *Edward goes in Richard’s face. You watch.*

“Agreed,” Maureen said without hesitation. “Then Richard goes.” Her voice was quiet in the darkened room. “And I see who catches him.”

She lay down on the bed, fully clothed. The sleeve crossbow’s arms rested on her wrist. Tally kept vigil throughout the night, particle sense and the ward active. As the corridor lamps brightened she gave Maureen the log.

[Night: dark-elf footsteps, guest corridor, 4 passes. Pause outside this door: 9 s. Doors opened: 0. Ward drew 0.1. Mana: full.]

* * *

Maureen gave the corridor a few breaths to wake up before she placed a small fresh ward over the door and reached into the Void Dimensional Pouch. Edward unfolded out of it, taking up most of the cramped space of the guest room. It was the third time he had ridden through that dark.

Tally ran the count while Maureen let herself relax. Ten breaths, watching the core, and it remained steady. [Pouch transit: tolerated, 3 tests. Core: intact.] *Three is the number you wanted,* Tally said.

Edward stood too straight. All his weight was equally distributed on both feet. The faint cold smell of metal from his lattice rose above the plain black robe. The face of the gaunt friend hung on him like a mask on a peg.

Maureen’s fingers brushed the illusion’s anchor and moulded it. The features of the illusion flowed and reshaped. The hollow cheeks filled out, the chin became weak, the eyes mousy and close set. Richard’s face slid into place over Edward’s. She dressed him in Richard’s travel-worn grey coat. [Illusion reset: −0.1. Upkeep: core-fed.]

Tally cast her pale-blue muscle map over Edward’s face. There was a fraction of a second lag on the left cheek, just as there had been with Jack’s illusion. Maureen placed two fingers against it, gently pressing it back into alignment with the right. *Richard doesn’t speak much, and when he does it’s quiet, like a whisper,* Tally noted. *That should work for someone who can’t speak at all.*

“Walk with the wizards,” Maureen said quietly, giving him three simple orders, the way one might wind up a clock. “Stand where they stand. Fall when I signal.” Edward’s borrowed eyes did not move, but the glow from under the robe brightened once, as the core processed the instructions.

Two Richards stood in the small black stone room. Seeing her own borrowed face from the outside, blank and patient, made the skin between her shoulder blades crawl, as if someone was standing too close behind her. Maureen pressed her aura down flat against her chest, willing it to stay inside, before letting Tally damp the Pankora heat through the Orb. Finally she draped the “Manas” concealment over herself, the trick from the cache wall. For the first time, she spread it as thin as her breath over her skin.

The display flashed up behind her eyes.

[Orb damping: −0.2 Mana/hour.]

*Tally, can a Night Mother see through this?*

*No data on that, Maureen,* Tally answered honestly.

*We’ll find out together.*

*I’m afraid of that.*

Maureen opened the door and walked out into the settlement. She made her way to the gathering hall, a long room supported by black stone pillars. The walls glowed with clusters of blue-black fungus, emitting just enough light to see by. The air was cold, and smelled of wet rock.

Inside the gathering hall, Eve stepped forward before the assembled wizards. She stood with a simple poise that drew every gaze, and spoke with a gracious voice. “For your assistance, I will take you to our forbidden ground, so that you may share our sacred heritage. The Spring of Life.”

The gathering hall exploded with excitement.

Breaths grew heavy around her, eager voices clamoured over one another. “The Spring!” “The true Spring!”

Maureen stood in the shadow of a pillar, letting the glowing fungus around her cast light on everyone else but her. Edward stood among them, wearing her face. No one noticed him.

Tally counted the hired wizards as they moved past her, noting Jackman near the back of the group. He was an old wizard, his silver-white hair hanging long and straight down his back. His hooked nose and green eyes were filled with contempt. Claude, Denise’s uncle, was middle-aged with greying hair. He wore a long tailcoat, and stood watchfully at the back, exchanging a look with Jackman. Then both men turned to look at the excited faces of the others, and smiled thinly and coldly.

Denise stood near the front of the group, flanked by her friends Rita, Glorette and Rachel.

“Come,” Eve said, and turned, leading the way towards a passage behind the gathering hall.

The wizards hurried after her without a second thought. Maureen slipped in last, keeping a body’s length away from the walls and the darkness.

The passage narrowed and sloped downwards. The smell of wet stone and something sweet hung in the air. A sweetness that grew thicker with every step, until she could taste it in the back of her throat.

Tally kept a watchful eye on Edward up ahead. His footfalls were too even, his pace exact. Not a single wizard looked twice at him.

*Richard has never been so popular,* Tally noted. *And no one is noticing that either.*

* * *

The cavern opened up like the inside of some great dark bell. Stalactites hung in rows from the ceiling, each one glowing faintly at the tip. In the centre was a broad pool of perfectly clear water, breathing a pale vapour. The sweetness of the air had settled in her chest, as if a warm hand had wrapped itself around her heart.

While every eye was drawn to the water, Maureen climbed the rough wall, making her way up into the gloom. She settled down on one of the ledges created by the stalactites, high above the pool, crouching amongst the stone teeth that hung from the ceiling. Her illusion was thin but effective, invisible to ordinary eyes, though untested against the Night Mother. Tally studied the vapour, taking inferences from what she knew of Maureen. Her breathing was easier, her pulse three beats slower than before they entered. There was a sweetness on her tongue that she couldn’t describe. By sight alone, and by what it did to her, Tally identified it. The Spring of Life. She logged the sightlines provided by the ledge. The pool, the platform at the far end of the cavern, every individual wizard.

“The Spring,” they murmured. “It is the Spring.”

Eve turned, smiling. “Go forward and take your share. Only take care not to damage it.”

They thanked her, in a rush of voices, and broke for the water.

Three steps, four steps, then they dropped where they stood, as if their strings had been abruptly cut. Maureen flicked two fingers. Below, Edward fell with them, right on cue. His borrowed face struck the stone.

Then came the screaming.

The wizards curled on the pale stone, hands clawing at their own chests looking for mana that was no longer there. “It’s gone! All of it!”

The sound bounced off the stalactites, layer upon layer of pain and frustration, filling the cavern like a dark tide.

*Forty-three... forty-four... forty-five...* Tally counted them down as they dropped. *Rita... Glorette... Rachel... and Denise. Denise was still on her feet. Untouched.*

*They didn’t drug her at all,* Tally said.

Denise knelt amongst her fallen friends, hands reaching out, then stopping, searching for some way to help. Her mouth opened and closed. “Rita... Glorette... Rachel...” No one answered her save with screams of pain. And Denise didn’t know where to touch.

“Ah,” said Claude, strolling up from the back. His hands hung loose by his side. “What’s happened? They’re no use to us now. So they die.” He looked at the writhing bodies with a smile. “Did you enjoy the wine, the food, and the company last night?”

Grey faces lifted from the stone, streaked with sweat. They turned from Claude to Eve, who stood by the water, serene as ever. There was nothing left for the wizards to throw but their fury.

Claude never looked at them. He watched Denise as she backed away, eyes huge.

“I could have killed you long ago, niece. I did not. We need you unharmed, your soul intact, to draw out the Spring’s full power. This entire mission was created for you, my niece.”

Maureen’s stillness went absolute. In amber Tally moved a line on her display.

[Denise: sacrifice. no data → known.]

*That’s what the bait was for,* Tally said.

Maureen didn’t answer.

Tally checked the cold collar of the Nightingale contract around Maureen’s throat, searching for any flare of energy that might signify an attempt to enforce the terms of the contract, but there was none. *The terms did not cover guarding Denise outside her family’s house,* Tally noted. *This is not her family’s house. Logged.*

“Enough talk, Claude,” Jackman said, bored. “Clear out the trash.”

One of the wizards managed to drag himself up and lunge at him, cursing. Jackman’s boot caught him in the chest and sent him skidding across the stone floor.

“You were never fit to taste the Spring,” he said.

Together they pinned Denise with a binding spell, then began. Jackman’s lightning danced across the cavern floor, forking white fingers through the air. Claude’s dark blade sliced through one helpless victim after another. He worked quickly, without interest.

Blood flowed across the stone in sheets, so bright it was nearly pink against the pale floor. The cavern smelled of ozone and copper and torn cloth. But over all of it rolled the sweet vapour from the pool.

A fork of Jackman’s lightning crossed the body in Richard’s grey coat. It left the puppet lying exactly where he had fallen, one arm flung out, as ordered. Edward did not move.

Tally logged the data by sight:

[Edward: lattice scorched, left flank. Core: lit. Illusion: holding.]

*The face didn’t slip once,* Tally noted.

On the ledge above the pool, Maureen’s nails dug crescents into her palm. At the smell of so much blood, something deep inside her stirred and pulled. The Totem was waking. Heat flared behind her eyes, pushing toward the crimson.

Tally dampened the surge through the Orb and began counting Maureen’s breaths: ten, nine, eight.

*Not yet,* Tally said.

*You said not yet,* Maureen answered silently.

*Not yet.*

The floor fell quiet. Claude rose to his feet and crossed to Denise’s side. He bent over her and, pinching her chin between finger and thumb, lifted her head until she was forced to look at him. Then, abruptly, she jerked, her body lifting off the stone floor.

Claude let go. Denise’s body flew backward, across the cavern, as if pulled by an invisible hand, and landed gently beside Eve. At the far end of the cavern a high stone platform jutted from the cavern wall. In a moment, Denise was standing there, beside Eve.

The dark elves appeared, as if they had been standing there all along, waiting in their own time. Men and women of surpassing beauty filled the cavern, arranged in rows before the platform where Eve and Denise stood. Their armour shone, black and ornate, and their skin was covered with strange runes. They sat cross-legged upon the stone floor and began to chant.

The sound rolled through the cavern like a great hymn sung in the dark-elf tongue. A strange darkness rose from their bodies, motes dense as smoke, one by one, from each chanter’s lips and chest and eyes. The blackness drifted upward toward the stalactites, slow as steam rising from a kettle.

Tally traced the structure through the Mirror Pupil lens. Lines rose from every dark elf in the cavern, converging on Denise and the platform where she stood. The lines split and threaded beneath the stone where Claude and Jackman still stood.

*They stand inside of it,* Tally said in Maureen’s head.

And then, too late, Jackman felt it.

He stepped forward. “Eve,” his voice had gone sharp. “What is this? What are you doing?”

Eve turned to face him. She smiled. “Nothing,” she said. “Only what we dark elves should do.”

In that moment she was no longer a gracious hostess, a queen entertaining her allies. She stood as the mistress of a great house who had never, not once in all her days, doubted that it was hers. The entire cavern seemed to rearrange itself around her.

Eve turned back to Denise, who stood beside her, spell-bound, her face white with terror. The Night Mother studied the girl’s face. “So, you’re Denise,” Eve murmured. “I was watching the humans so closely, I never really looked at you before.” She reached out and stroked Denise’s cheek with a pale hand. “Denise... Denise...”

Tally filed away Eve’s murmur of the name, alongside Claude’s words a moment before.

*Your call,* Tally said.

Maureen crouched on the ledge, motionless, eyes hot. *Not yet.*
