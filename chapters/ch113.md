Maureen sat among the wagons, wearing Richard’s borrowed face. The Mimicry was only a tightness along her jawline now, but it would grow stronger as she grew tired. Her full-wizard aura was pressed down into her chest, like a breath held beneath the surface of the water.

“Please, sir,” said a small voice. “Can I travel with you?”

A ragged girl stood in front of the caravan master, twisting her hands in the frayed fabric of her grey dress. “Alice is looking for Sister Maureen. My Maureen. She is here. Alice knows she is here.”

No one refused her. Before the master could even nod in answer, men crowded around the girl, wrapping a blanket around her thin shoulders and pressing a heel of bread into her hands. They jostled each other for her attention, vying for the thanks of this poor waif.

Maureen glanced once at the girl, filing her appearance away for later inspection. A pretty face to be dressed in such rags. Skin pale as skimmed milk, dark eyes clear and black as water over black stone. Every man standing by the fire turned towards the girl, like flowers turning towards an opened window.

The master ran a thick finger down his roll. “I don’t have any Maureen Olsen’s on the books, girl. You got the name wrong? Some cousin or something...”

Alice didn’t argue with him. She simply shook her head once, firmly, and waited.

The men crowded forward, eager to have the girl look at them. They laughed, straightening their collars and brushing dust off their sleeves. The caravan master lined them up along the edge of the fire, while the women hooted at them from the steps of the wagons.

Maureen rose to her feet and stepped into the line, taking her place between one of the wagon drivers and a groom.

*You want it to look at you,* Tally said.

*I want to look at it,* Maureen answered silently.

*It’s the same distance in a different direction,* Tally pointed out.

On the other side of the fire, Joanna’s eyes flicked once to Richard’s face, then darted away. Joanna’s hand went to her breastbone, where the black-red seed lay dormant beneath her skin, and stayed there. Pressed tightly against her chest.

Alice walked slowly before the line of men, but she didn’t seem to study their faces. Instead, she tilted her head forward, bringing her ear close to each man’s chest, as though she were a child listening at a door. Then she would step to the next man without a word. “Not Maureen. Not Maureen.”

She paused before Richard, though, for the space of another breath. Maureen held back, but let one thread of her spirit sense flick out to the girl. The touch was featherlight, no heavier than a fingertip brushing a sleeve.

Cold shot through Maureen’s limbs, locking them in place. For an instant, she was back in the tent, alone in the darkness while the fire blazed along the horizon, giving no heat. A shiver ran down her spine, the hairs rising on the nape of her neck.

[Unknown force field. Scan: failed.]

Tally reported the sensation of prickling on Maureen’s skin and the sudden jump in her pulse, even before Maureen herself had finished feeling it. Her all around sense, probing delicately, slid away from the girl. *Like trying to grab hold of someone with wet hands on smooth glass,* Tally explained.

Then, warmer: *I have something like this on file. In the line-tongue sections of the bronze book’s imprint and Gregory’s, which we never finished decoding. Decoding would take forty-seven hours.*

*Do it,* Maureen told Tally silently, opening her own line.

*Already started,* Tally answered. *Don’t touch it again until then.*

“Not Maureen.” The ragged girl drew back, sounding small and disappointed. She moved on, and Maureen tugged Richard’s coat a little closer around herself, maintaining the face. Maureen’s aura remained flat and colorless. There was no way she intended to be discovered this night.

*Alice,* Tally said. *Alice again. The other is Lucy’s friend, living in the Dark Forest.*

*A different Alice,* Maureen answered.

*Common name,* Tally noted, using almost the exact words she had used to describe Mary’s General Store. She sounded quite pleased with herself.

When the girl reached the end of the line without finding her Sister Maureen, she sat down by the fire, hugging her knees up to her chin. “Then Alice will wait,” she said. “Maureen is here.”

*She hasn’t shivered once,* Tally pointed out. *It’s cold enough for frost on the ground and she’s wearing rags.*

*Log it,* Maureen ordered.

*Already did.*

The frost crept slowly along the tongues of the wagons. The wet pine wood in the cookfire spat and hissed, throwing sparks into the air and filling the chill evening with the smell of burning resin and horse dung. The ragged girl sat in the midst of it all, unmoving, like some statued creature carved from stone. The men brought her more bread, which she didn’t eat.

* * *

The wagons bounced over frozen ruts all day. Axles squealed in protest at the punishment. Maureen watched from the best carriage, sitting with the curtain half open, as the horses of the noblemen made their way, one by one, back to the wagon where the girl rode.

By the time they stopped for the evening, Louis was lounging by the fire, his eyes narrowed on the girl.

“Florian,” he said. “I’ve never seen anything like her before. Never.”

“You mean you’re going to have her?” Florian asked with a laugh that was too loud.

“Me? Not just me. Us.”

The others took up the challenge immediately, boasting about what they would do and in what order, slapping leather wine skins against their legs with each claim.

*Eleven boasts by dark,* Tally noted to Maureen.

*Stop counting them,* Maureen ordered.

Tally ignored the instruction and continued counting, if not quite so loudly.

The courting began in earnest. The young noblemen offered the ragged girl sweetmeats from their silk pouches, dropped a fur cloak around her shoulders, told long stories as they sat around the fire with her. Alice accepted everything with a blink of her dark eyes and a laugh. The noblemen leaned closer to her with every hour.

The laugh was thin and high, almost like the sound of a bell rope swinging back and forth across the bell, and the cup of hot, spiced wine they pressed into her hands stayed full until it went cold. The drivers watched and said nothing aloud, but as one passed another he muttered, “Out west, no one crosses the noble houses. Not even wizard apprentices.” “Only a real wizard would,” his companion answered. “And when’s the last time one of those was out here?”

*You’re here now,* Tally pointed out.

*Press me flat and I’m here,* Maureen answered silently.

*Then technically he’s right.* Tally sounded pleased with herself for thinking of the loophole.

Christine was sitting on the other side of the fire, balanced on a log. Her good silk scarf was wrapped around her shoulders, and she was holding a split stick from the firewood in one hand. She was squeezing it so tightly that the bark bit into her fingers. The knuckles were bone white and Maureen could see the blue veins pulsing beneath the skin.

Not one man had looked at her all day, not one.

“Alice,” she whispered, as if it were a curse.

*The blood has left her knuckles,* Tally noted. *Jaw is set, just like Lucy’s was on the ship when the ration bags were handed around. This is jealousy. Confidence . . . nine point five.*

Christine glanced over at Richard, who was poking at the fire with a stick and staring intently at Alice without moving a muscle. She snorted in disgust. “Richard, naturally. Even you.” She tossed her stick onto the fire and stalked away, heading back toward her wagon.

*She thinks you’re courting it,* Tally said.

*Let her think what she likes,* Maureen answered.

Tally considered this. *Very well. I will record you in my logs as a suitor. Disputed.*

Maureen paused for half a breath, trying to figure out if Joanna’s little sister was quite right in the head, but then she returned her attention to the fire. The end of her stick was black with soot, and the air was heavy with the scent of scorched tree sap.

Courting? No, definitely not courting. Maureen was watching the thing that made her skin crawl, the thing that had the boys running toward it with their arms open. Some people, Maureen thought, went looking for death.

* * *

The nobility had made a court of the fire. Velvet cloaks were thrown open, wine skins passed from hand to hand. A joint of mutton turned on the coals, the fat hissing in the heat. In the center of the ring sat Alice, her knees drawn up to her chin. Her eyes were clear, too clear, and the light of the fire danced within them.

They’d decided it was time. They argued over who would go first, like men rolling dice for a horse.

“I’ll go first,” Louis said. “I saw her first.”

Florian plunked a fat purse down on the bench beside him. “Ten thousand gold. First turn.” The coins within clinked as the purse settled into place. The others howled, and Louis threw his arms into the air before crossing them with a scowl.

[Florian: 10,000 gold. First turn.]

*Mortal money, not stones,* Tally noted. *That is important. And . . . Maureen Olsen’s breathing has not changed. Not by a single beat.*

 Maureen’s stick rested in the coals. The tip began to glow, and the scent of sap grew sharper. Her hand did not move.

Louis knelt beside Alice, all softness and gentleness. “You’re looking for your Maureen, aren’t you, sweetheart?”

“Sister Maureen,” Alice said. Her eyes blinked, black and bright. “Alice wants to find Sister Maureen. Then we go home.”

Florian leaned forward, his face intruding on Alice’s vision, waving a hand to encompass the circle of young noblemen. “Here’s a secret, Alice. We’re all your Maureen. Each and every one of us.”

Alice’s brow furrowed. “But Father says . . . Father says Alice only has one Maureen.”

[Father: no data.]

*Keep it there,* Maureen told her silently. *And nothing more.*

Florian was ready for that. “Remember the story we told you? One person, a thousand faces, but still one person. ‘Maureen’ is just a name, love. Any one of us can use it. Have a little fun with each of us, one at a time, and you’ll see what we mean.”

Maureen sat where she was, the borrowed face draped over her own features. She felt the Mimicry tugging at the corners of her jaw as a drunk young man described her party trick to a monster as a line to seduce a girl.

*He’s describing you,* Tally said.

*He’s describing himself,* Maureen answered.

*That’s worse,* Tally said.

Alice nodded slowly, as though digesting the information. “If Alice hurts you in the game,” she said after a moment, “you’ll forgive Alice. Won’t you?”

“Of course!” Louis said, cutting across Florian before he could open his mouth.

“Whatever Alice does,” Alice said, “anything . . . you’ll all forgive Alice?” Her eyes darted from face to face, growing anxious.

“Yes!”

“Of course, love!”

“Anything!”

Every young noble around the campfire shouted his assent, holding up their wine skins and laughing.

*That question has a structure,* Tally said. *A condition asked, and a condition granted. Like an oath.*

*It’s a contract, Maureen. They just signed it.*

*I know,* Maureen answered.

*You could stop this,* Tally said.

*I could,* Maureen agreed.

There was a pause. Then, almost inaudibly, Tally said, *I log that you could have.*

Maureen Olsen’s pulse did not quicken. The stick stayed buried in the coals. She let the moment pass through her hands like a rope she’d decided not to grab hold of.

Florian couldn’t wait any longer. He yanked the dazed girl up by the wrist, then dragged her toward the lantern that hung at the mouth of the path into the woods that they’d picked out and readied earlier that day.

The nobles bayed at his back. “Make it last, Florian!” “Go easy on her, we want our turns!” One of them threw an empty wine skin at him; it exploded against a tree trunk, spraying red liquid in all directions.

The lantern swung from side to side as Florian pulled the girl along behind him. Once or twice her grey ragged hem flicked between two trees, caught by the light. Her hand hung limply in his grip, and then the darkness had swallowed both of them.

The woods were silent behind them. Only the crackling of the fire could be heard now, and somewhere one of the horses blew and stamped its foot against the frozen ground.

The drivers sighed into their cups. “That poor girl,” one of them said.

Christine smiled into her scarf, satisfied. Joanna shook her head once and let out a long breath before turning away from the fire. She did not look at Richard.

Maureen Olsen breathed out slowly and smiled beneath Richard’s face. “Interesting,” she said in his low voice. “Really interesting.”

* * *

Florian never came back.

The young nobles began to sour, and then they got loud. They cursed him for keeping her all to himself. He wasn’t sharing. The greedy pig wasn’t sharing.

The wine skins lay flat upon the bench, the fire sinking to red coals. The night grew cold, sneaking up to wrap around everyone’s shoulders. The woods stayed dark. *Maureen,* Tally said without being asked.

Far beyond the treeline, roughly eighty metres away if her senses weren’t lying, a mortal light had flickered and gone out.

Maureen reached over with a stick and poked at the fire. She said nothing.

*That was him,* Tally said.

*Probably,* Maureen replied silently.

*Not probably,* Tally said. Footsteps sounded from within the woods. Slow steps, crunching upon the trampled and frozen grass.

“Florian!” One of the young nobles called out without turning around. “Finally! Well? Was she worth ten thousand gold for the first go, or . . .”

The man trailed off as he turned around. The last syllable died in his throat.

The colour drained from his face, leaving it the same waxy hue as the tallow candles. One after another the faces around the fire went through the same transformation. Louis was the last, but not by much. It was as though the cold that crept up at everyone’s back had finally found all of them at once.

Tally’s display flooded red where the girl stood. A field was rising up in rings around her, but of no particle school that Tally had on file.

[Hostile. Class: no data. Distance: 22 m. Retreat: recommended.]

Maureen did not get up from her stool. She reached out and poked the fire again. A few sparks danced into the air before dying.

Alice looked exactly as she had before. The same fine features, the same pale skin, the same ragged grey clothing. But her eyes were gone. Two empty holes remained where her eyes had been, and a steady stream of blood poured forth from the sockets and down her cheeks. It did not stop.

Blood rose around her like smoke from the chimney on a still day. Ring upon ring it spread into the darkness above the pines, and the frost on the trampled grass turned red in its light, as did the faces around the fire.

The smell of it wafted to the fire. Copper. The old smell. The smell Tally kept in Maureen’s memory from Angrey’s beating. But there was another smell beneath it. Something sweet and rotten, like fruit left to spoil in the sun.

The cold from the Sunset Canyon rushed back into Maureen’s limbs, and took up residence there.

The grey rags were turning red now. Not just at the bottom but rising up, like a wick soaked in the stuff. Thread by thread it drew up the clothing, as if she stood in it.

“You... lied to Alice,” Alice said. Her voice was soft, puzzled. “You’re not... not Maureen. Maureen... Maureen...”

In her hand, held by the hair, was Florian’s head.

It hadn’t been severed. There were no clean edges or careful cuts. The neck was a torn, ragged mess, and the boy’s expression was frozen in terror.

No one breathed. One of the wine skins slipped from someone’s fingers and slapped the ground. The joker’s little smile was gone from Christine’s face, and Joanna had both hands pressed firmly against her breastbone.

*Retreat is still recommended.*

*She’s not even looking at me.*

*I know. Keep your aura flat.*

Alice opened her fingers. Florian’s head fell to the ground with a soft thump. It rolled three times, kicking up clumps of trampled grass before it came to rest against the boot of the young noble who had promised first.

[Decode: 18:41:07.]

*Eighteen hours.*

*Yes,* Tally said. *Eighteen hours.*

And Maureen did not remove her hand from the stick.

Alice stood in the midst of her spreading rings of blood, the sockets of her eyes still pouring out their scarlet streams. She turned slowly, that empty face searching the fire and the young men who had all promised to forgive her. “Maureen... where is Maureen...” she whispered.
