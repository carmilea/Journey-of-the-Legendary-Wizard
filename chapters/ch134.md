The barrage had ended. Maureen stood in the frost, feeling her knees humming, like a bell continuing to resonate for some time after the clapper has stopped moving. Her breath condensed against the inside of the lacquer mask, leaving a thin film of mist. Her Mana sat near empty at 0.6. Tally led her through the dead in order of density, then by sight. The ghostly blue tag appeared over each body as she identified it. [Bodies: Gus, Black Bear, Black Bear’s companion, hirelings ×2 (fragments), Frans (headless). Purgatory: no remains ×2. Devices: none pulsing.]

*No one called home,* Tally said. *I listened for forty seconds at each body and heard no messenger pulse.*

*Again.*

Tally repeated the process. *None,* she said again. *But I don’t mind being asked twice. I know some who would have been annoyed with such a repeat.*

Gus lay on his back about ten paces from the largest crater. Even in death he was handsome. The frost was already settling into his black hair, and the Bat mask lay in the frost beside him. Maureen stood there looking down at him. She looked at him exactly as long as necessary to be certain. Nothing moved in her chest. In the corner of Maureen’s eyes, Tally updated her counter.

*Zero,* Tally said. *Just like you promised me.*

*I always keep my promises when they’re easy to keep.*

Since the bodies would be found, Maureen pulled out a small vial of scouring reagent from her belt kit. It was something she had brewed up using a formula from Gregory’s pharmacology. She’d carried it with her for just this kind of morning. As Tally identified the traces to be cleared away, one amber mark at a time, Maureen uncorked the vial and carefully applied a few drops where needed.

The Purgatory signature upon the frost.

The Mirror Pupil crystal shards that sparkled like broken glass in the high grass.

The faint scar the Orb’s cycling had burned into the air near the ground.

The reagent hissed against the scorched earth. It gave off a sharp odor of wet lime and burnt vinegar. The frost melted into a grey slush wherever she applied the reagent. Behind her, it refroze as she moved from mark to mark.

*We’re going to have to leave the craters,* Tally observed. *You can’t really hide them. They are too big.*

Maureen nodded. The pock marks on the landscape would still read as acid and fire, both the province of black wizards. *Let them read what they want,* she told the voice in her head silently.

So she left the story lying out for the investigators from the White Tower to find. Young Gus had come here for a reason, and he had hired his own black wizards to help him accomplish that goal. Then something happened, and all the killers fell out with each other. Two of the black wizards Gus hired had burned without leaving any ash behind. They would simply be missing when the investigators came. Maureen went through the bodies, keeping her gloves on. She plucked the stone cards out of inner pockets, and then pulled loose stones out of belt pouches and spell materials wrapped up in squares of oilcloth. Each item was fed into the void pocket at her waist. She’d never liked to leave anything useful on a corpse. As Maureen dropped the stones into the pocket, Tally kept the ledger.

[Pouch: +cards 3, +loose ~400, +materials 9. Magic stones, all columns: ~5,100 → ~8,500.]

*That’s better than a good night’s sleep,* Tally said.

*I wish I could have had both,* Maureen thought back to the impish voice.

Then Tally spoke again. *You know, you should take those as well.*

*Take what?*

*The guild tokens. There’s one on young Gus’s belt and another on Black Bear. If the White Tower finds a Bat token on Gus’s body, they’ll come right to the Bloodcoat guild. And if they find the guild, they’ll be pointing right at Night Owl.*

Maureen considered the voice’s words for a moment before she pried the blood-red tokens free from their belts. She dropped them into her pouch as well.

[Pouch: +2 tokens.]

Now there would be nothing on these bodies to tie Gus to the guild, and nothing to tie the guild back to her. The tokens had been cold against her fingers, even through her gloves. Colder than the frost. She wiped her hands off on the grass out of habit before she rose from the corpses. Her hand did not shake.

Before the first market carts began to roll out onto the Emilia road, Maureen turned and ran back in the direction she had come from. She was a black shape racing across the white fields, staying to ditches and hedge lines when she could.

* * *

She arrived over the garden wall and behind her bedroom ward before the household began to stir. She let Richard’s face fall away, then hung the black robe up. After that she slept for a few hours, while Tally kept the ward fed and listened for trouble.

*Maureen? Time to get up.*

Tally’s soft chime rang in her head, waking her gently.

[Night: 1 pass, cat, garden wall. Ward drew 0.1. Mana: 16, full.]

The upkeep on her totem had not drawn enough mana to impede her recovery rate, and her empty well from last night was filled again.

Maureen sat up and recast her Mimicry before the glass in her bedroom. Her jaw became weak, her facial features mousy, her throat constricted to produce Richard’s flat, low voice. [Mana: 16 → 15.6.]

*How do I look?* she asked silently as she met the eyes in the mirror with her own.

*Black,* Tally said in her head.

*Those are the eyes of a man who has slept.*

*I look like Richard,* Maureen told her.

*This week those were the same thing.*

Maureen reached into the wardrobe for her robes, pulling out the green trimmed robe of a pharmacist from the Alliance. The wool was stiff with cold where it had hung in the wardrobe, and it smelled faintly of yesterday’s reagents.

Ike was waiting for her in the corridor, her cloak draped over his arm. His eyes never rose above her collar. “Master,” he said as Maureen approached.

Maureen nodded at him and then made her way down through the house, across the garden with its frost covered lawns and shrubbery. The gate opened at her touch, her palm brand recognized.

* * *

The corridors of the spiral tower smelled of camphor and hot glass. Wizards glided along the balconies high above her head. She nodded to each face she knew, one after another. It was an ordinary morning, and she was an ordinary man, just a little tired.

Viska caught her as she entered the workroom, his red hair sticking up at awkward angles where he had run his hands through it.

“This batch, group leader,” he said. “Whoever wrote this formula hates us personally.”

“Then brew it angry, Viska,” she said, using Richard’s flat voice. “Angry hands are quick.”

Two of the pharmacists laughed at that and Viska clutched at his chest as if she had wounded him mortally. She set out her test tubes and lit her burner. The first reagent responded to her touch, turning from cloudy to clear. For a few minutes the room was filled with nothing but the ticking of the burners’ flames and the clink of glass against glass.

Then there was the sound of boots pounding in the corridor outside. Many boots, moving fast. There were shouts somewhere, then the clatter of a stirring rod slipping from someone’s fingers and ringing on the stone floor.

Stools scraped backwards along the long benches. The pharmacists looked at one another over the tops of their glassware, their faces stiff with the knowledge that running in a wizards’ tower never boded well.

The news arrived in bits and pieces from the doorway, then all at once.

Gus of the High Ring White Tower was dead. The disciple of Wenger himself. The face of the young generation in the High Ring White Tower.

One woman near the end bench brought her knuckles up to her mouth. Slowly, Viska’s red head turned until his eyes fixed upon Richard.

*Eleven faces frightened. One bored,* Tally said. *That would be you. Try to look mildly shaken.*

Maureen let her brows rise slightly. She drew a slow breath in through her nose. *Better,* said Tally. *Now give an order. You’re a group leader. Group leaders give orders.*

“Enough,” Maureen said, loud enough for the end bench to hear. “Whatever’s happened out there, it isn’t happening in your flasks. Back to work.”

One by one, the burners ticked back up. Hands returned to the glassware, though not quite as deftly as before. The steam began to billow again beneath the frosted window. Viska drifted down the benches toward her, not looking up from the tubes he was working on.

“Group leader,” he said. “The department head wants you. Now.”

*Viska’s pulse is quickening but his hands are steady,* Tally noted. *He looks worried about you, Richard, not suspicious of you.*

Maureen set her test tube down and gave Tally silent instructions to keep an eye on the faces as they walked to the department head’s office.

* * *

Maureen smoothed the robe at her shoulders, a junior male’s gesture before going in to face his superior. She knocked twice on the door and entered the camphor-scented room. The air held the dry, musty smell of old paper. Dark wooden shelves were packed with stoppered glass jars. A single, narrow window looked out through a fur of frost, letting only cold light shine on the worn desk at the room’s center.

Ambrose looked up from his desk as Maureen entered. His silver hair was neatly combed. His white-gold robe was rumpled slightly around the cuffs, as if he had been rubbing his eyes. “Ah, Richard. Sit. I have several things to tell you, and none of them pleasant.”

“You’ve heard about Gus,” he continued without preamble. “Wenger is beside himself. He has begun pulling Alliance forces into Emilia with his own seal, turning over every stone.”

He leaned forward in his chair, his voice dropping to that of a father warning his son. “So you must stay clear of the White Tower. Do not argue with them. Do not even stand near them. If they catch your eye this month, the troubles will not end with you.”

[Wenger: searching Emilia. Hearsay, one source (Ambrose).]

*He’s protecting his department, not accusing you,* Tally said. *Medium confidence. His eyes haven’t stayed on your face.*

Maureen nodded slowly. Richard’s mousy face gave away nothing. The potions had cleansed the field. Frans had killed Gus, not she.

Ambrose flicked two fingers. A cluster of energy particles collected above the desktop like dust motes in a sunbeam. They gathered together, forming a sheet of paper that fluttered down into Maureen’s hand. The list of tasks for the Fourteenth Potion Group.

She read through it, and her eyebrows rose. “Department head?” she said. “These are all standard potions, simple brews. But look at the quantities. We already have a three-month stockpile for the entire Alliance.”

*Three months’ worth of stock is already on the shelves,* Tally noted. *He’s stockpiling for something that isn’t happening here. Medium-confidence prediction: a journey.*

*Or a war there,* Maureen replied silently.

Ambrose rubbed the bridge of his nose. “We need every flask, Richard. Things have happened out East. The entire Passise Subcontinent has turned over like a pot.”

He spoke plainly, not sparing Maureen’s feelings or trying to hide the facts. “Every order has allied against the dark elves and the other Underworld races. Then, no one knows how, they started killing each other instead.”

“The Dark Forest’s potion prodigy, a boy called Mace,” he continued. “Hader Castle had a wizard’s clone sitting inside him. A man called Oris.”

[Mace: unscannable. Bearing changed. Sister: Anna, deceased.]

*And it wasn’t just a clone,* Tally said. *Something else was wearing him. That’s why nothing ever matched up.*

Maureen’s hands lay flat on her knees. Anna’s last word was in her throat again, scorched and begging in the scree. My brother. She kept her eyes on the paper.

“Oris waited until the Dark Forest was fully engaged in seizing dark-elf spoils,” Ambrose said. “Then he struck with Hader Castle, the Blue Ring and the other white orders. The Blue Ring and Hader Castle broke into the Dark Forest’s own seat. Fewer than one apprentice in ten came out alive. And the first man Oris went for was the boy’s mentor. Adrian. Nearly killed on the spot.”

“Augurit’s poured out a fortune trying to keep him breathing,” Ambrose said. “From what I hear, it isn’t going well.”

[Pulse 64 → 101. Breath held 6 s.]

Maureen’s face stayed Richard’s, and she let her eyes drop to the frost on the window so that her eyelashes masked whatever might be there.

*Your brand,* Tally said, very quietly. *It isn’t inert. Daniel’s went cold the moment he died; Adrian’s is still warm, by sense. That’s all I have.*

*It’s enough,* Maureen told her silently.

*No, it’s not, but it’s all there is.*

[Richard (the real one): no data. Lucy: no data. Winnie: no data. George: no data. Hugh, Iman: no data.]

*Stop,* Maureen told her.

*I’ve stopped, but I’m still holding them.*

The silence stretched long enough that Ambrose shifted in his chair. Frost crept up the window glass, forming ferns of white against grey. Maureen stared at a single branch until her breathing was Richard’s again.

“The East,” she said at last, in an even voice. “What’s it like now?”

“A stew,” Ambrose said after a moment, making a sour little laugh. The white orders wanted to see the end of the Dark Forest, but the White Bone Tower had made that difficult by activating agents hidden within the white orders, and those orders had suffered for it. The Dark Forest had even opened negotiations with the dark elves, while apprentices from White Bone Tower still fought those same elves for Underworld riches.

“But there is one bright spark in it.” Ambrose smiled again, this time with a trace of approval. “One Oran Olivia, of the Olivia family. Not very important before this, but he caught a Hader spy at the last moment. If not for him, there would be no more Dark Forest.”

*Not on file. Olivias are Jay, Frey, and Barok... all dead. A nobody who suddenly shines,* Tally said.

*Like Aaron.*

*Like Aaron. Logging shape.*

Ambrose’s face changed again, and he sat straighter in his chair. “But that’s all coming to an end. Because they’ve found a secret realm, Richard. From the ancient era, left by the great wizards themselves.”

“It runs from East to West.” Ambrose held out his hands, sleeves sliding back off his wrists as he did so. “No one order can swallow it whole. Herbs not seen for a thousand years, materials for spells, treasures. Walk into that and you walk into a hoard.”

Tally compared Ambrose’s account against Gregory’s imprint for “ancient secret realms” and reported: Consistent. No further details. Gregory says only that truly powerful wizards could leave such a realm, and that each would contain a wizard’s lifetime of work.

Maureen’s eyes narrowed by the barest fraction before she lowered them again.

“We’re looking for everything we can find out about it,” Ambrose continued. “Envoys have already come from the east, looking to negotiate shares. If this goes well, we’ll be sent in soon. So get every flask, strong or plain. Understand me?”

*Then it’s ours to find first.*

*It’s ours... and everyone else’s too.*

*Then first.*

Maureen folded the task sheet once, rose to her feet, and tucked it into a pocket of her robe. “I didn’t think it was all going to come out like this,” Maureen said quietly.

“Ah,” said Ambrose, glancing out at the frost. “Yes, it must be a surprise for a young man.”
