The head bench for the Fourteenth Potion Group smelled like fresh cut wood and lye. Maureen’s sleeve came away from the ash wood with the scent of sawdust clinging to it. A rack of flasks sat cooling beside her, and as the steam curled off of them, beads of water formed on the frosted window above her head.

Maureen was back to work in the Pharmacy Department, brewing for contribution points. The points could be exchanged for rare materials and higher knowledge. With every batch she made, her hands became more sure as they moved among the tools at her workbench. It was good work, the type of work Jing Sheng had always loved.

Her time in the Eighth Group was over. She had served as Vincent’s deputy on the Fink Stone Bird project, and now she had her own group to lead. One so new that the brass number fourteen on the door still shone brightly.

>Contribution rate: deputy +50 / 10 days → group leader +80 / 10 days.

Tally added the data to the corner of Maureen’s vision, sounding a tiny bit smug as if she’d earned it herself.

Maureen’s thoughts turned to the Necro Flower she still couldn’t buy. *You can’t earn points to buy something that isn’t listed,* Tally told her. *We’ve checked all one thousand two hundred and fourteen listings.*

*I know,* Maureen told the voice silently.

*Yes, you do know. I just want you to stop hoping when you get to the counter,* Tally retorted.

Across the room Viska bent over a burner, his red hair falling into the glow of the blue flame. He coaxed a flask into a slow simmer before he headed over to Maureen with two cups of tea. The smell of smoke and mint wafted up from the cups.

Viska put the tea down by her rack, then he looked at her for just a moment too long. “Group leader. At your age. And they’ve handed you an assignment all on your own, Wizard Richard. I’ve been here eleven years and I’ve never seen that.”

[Heartbeat steady. Eyes on you, then away. Admiration, not flattery. Confidence: medium.]

*Log him as a colleague.*

Tally replied, *Already done. I’ve logged him as a colleague who brings me tea. I like him already.*

The praise hung in the air, and Maureen did not respond to it. A small smile touched Richard’s lips, a simple and plain gesture. The heat from the tea warmed her palms, and under Richard’s ribs her own pride sat quietly, well fed.

“Simmer’s running hot, Viska,” she said in Richard’s low and flat voice. “Please log the batch times yourself.” Then she turned and walked out towards the stacks.

Two purple-robed pharmacists fell silent in the corridor as she approached, their eyes following her. “That’s him. Richard. The one Ambrose took off the Eighth.” The name was getting around. Across the department and deeper into the headquarters, people were talking about her.

She kept up Richard’s walk, short steps with his weight balanced just slightly forward. The stone walls of the corridors gave off a slight chill, and the lamps burned beeswax instead of tallow. Her robes brushed past the pharmacists without a sound.

*Three whispers in one corridor,* Tally said in Maureen’s head. *Personal best. Should I start a column for it?*

*No,* Maureen said silently. *Don’t start a column for that.*

*It’s already open. Very tidy too.*

* * *

The free library was half empty, and warm with the slanting afternoon sun. Maureen’s shadow stretched long across the polished oak tabletop, and the only sound was the dry turning of pages and the distant cough of a clerk. The bookshelves here had no locked grilles and no fees, but they ran wide and deep, so that browsing could be a long affair. Maureen worked her way through a thick volume bound in grey leather, reading a spread at a time: Conjecture on Energy Particle Distribution and Spellcasting. Tally filed the spread as Maureen looked at it, and then reminded her that this was the last of three sources for the combination spell they had been simulating at night. The other two, Grimlando’s Conjecture and Sam’s Taxonomy, had come from the White Tower’s library spreads. The High Ring White Tower had paid for this spell twice without realising it, Tally noted, just a touch smugly.

There was a pale-blue flicker behind Maureen’s eyes every time a page went into the record. A sunbeam slanted down through one of the tall windows, throwing a shaft of light across the table. Dust turned lazily in the still air above the open book, and there was a faint smell of mice about the dry pages.

Before a footstep reached Maureen’s ears, Tally marked a source of dense particles moving up behind her on the all-round particle sense. The grain of the source was one that Maureen already had on file.

> Behind. Official. Known.

A shadow fell across the page before the voice did. Maureen’s brows drew together by a fraction. She’d felt him approaching three heartbeats ago.

“Sharp senses,” the man said, waggling a book in his hand. “But then, you pharmacists live by your noses. Wizard Richard. I hope you remember me. Gus, of the High Ring White Tower.”

He bowed, a quick, precise bow from the waist. He was a young core wizard, and handsome. His voice had a warm sound to it, and he wore a white tower emblazoned on the breast of his robes.

Tally sketched an outline over the man. His weight was balanced on the balls of his feet, his shoulders set as though he were wearing a high collar.

> Gus = Bat. Confidence: high. Not proof.

*Don’t put it over his face,* Maureen told Tally silently. *I’ll laugh.* Tally dimmed the overlay until it was just a thin line at the man’s collar.

Bat. This man had worn one of the guild’s masks, and had tried to beat her to every target. And had lost, every time. Richard’s face gave nothing away, but Maureen felt her own jaw clench beneath it before she could stop it.

She responded to the man’s greeting with polite small talk. She talked about the weather, about how cold it was in the mornings, about the library’s opening hours, letting each of her answers trail off one word short.

He smiled, leaning against the shelf. “So. What do you make of the White Tower?”

Maureen looked up at him. “Big. Strong. Good.”

He nodded, a self-satisfied smile spreading across his face. “Exactly. Now put the Jadeleaf Ring next to it. Same Alliance, opposite ends. You can’t pretend otherwise, Richard.”

Maureen closed the Conjecture and slid it back onto the shelf with a single finger. There was a certain timbre in Gus’s voice when he spoke, a certain weight that reminded her of the Duke at the long table all those years ago. He had been talking prices, working out how much he was willing to pay for her, through Richard… Maureen’s back straightened involuntarily.

She turned to face Gus directly, looking him in the eye. “What is it you actually want, Gus?”

Tally highlighted Gus’s tells. His throat pulsed more quickly as he worked Master Wenger’s name into his sentence, and his smile didn’t reach his eyes. Tally predicted with high confidence that he would offer Maureen some sort of title next, followed by a timeline. Men like this thought that the future was something that they could give as a gift.

“Leave the Ring,” Gus said, his voice rising and falling slightly as though he were offering her an irresistible deal. “Come to the Tower. That place is too small for you. You’re a genius, Richard. You’re not like the trash those little houses scrape together, you know you’re not.”

By “trash” he meant the Jadeleaf Ring. Joanna’s frayed green banners and the Jadeleaf Ring’s own ten tired apprentices. Maureen’s people. The word stuck in her chest, like a cold stone. She forced her hands to stay still at her sides.

*Gus is watching your shoulders now, not your face,* Tally warned.

Maureen let her shoulders fall loose, as if they were having a casual conversation about the weather.

“Join us and in twenty years, thirty at most, you’d be Deputy Head of this whole department. Under a Head the Tower appoints, naturally. He’d let you really shine, you’d have—”

*I told you so,* Tally said. *Title, then timeline.*

*You did,* Maureen replied silently. *Now you’re going to be insufferable.*

*I’ve always been insufferable, Maureen. Now I’m correct as well.*

Maureen cut across him without raising her voice. “Sorry. Not interested. I’m busy, Gus. If the Tower wants recruits, find someone else.” She turned on her heel and walked back towards the library door. Her footsteps fell softly into the matting.

As she turned, Maureen caught Gus’s face out of the edge of her vision. First it flushed red, then it went white, then it settled into a dull iron colour. She heard the book creak in his fist.

Behind her back she heard Gus hiss a sharp breath between his teeth. “Damn you, Richard.”

Tally read wounded pride in the colours of Gus’s face, and forecast with medium confidence that he would strike the nearest object within reach that was Maureen’s own.

*My group.*

*Your group.*

* * *

She took her time walking back along the long corridor to the Fourteenth Potion Group’s workroom. The grey light from the windows lay like a slab across the flagstones, and the cold crept into her soles through the leather. Her face was smooth as water in the calm before a storm.

She picked up the test tube containing the cloudy potion, and began to swirl it slowly by its neck. The potion was a cloudy mixture; as it spun against the light of the lamp it looked like milk in water, the edges of the cloud beginning to thin.

The glass was warm in her fingers, and the air around it smelled of crushed plant stems and copper.

“Group leader,” said Viska, trotting over with an expression of puzzlement. “Group leader, our batch never arrived. The whole allocation. I’ve checked all the forms, and they’re signed for every stage, but it won’t be released. Why not?”

Maureen walked over to the empty pinewood materials crate which stood beside the door, its requisition slip attached to its lid. She put the test tube down on top of the crate. New wood showed through the gaps between its slats. Inside, there was no straw and no smell of packed roots. An empty box standing in an empty room, where cold light lay on cold stone.

Tally brought up the requisition slip she had recorded when Maureen had signed it off, and displayed it next to the version she had recorded this morning. Each stage of the process had its stamp except one. Tally highlighted that stage with a red circle.

“Did you requisition this?” Maureen asked Viska.

“Yes, group leader.”

“And the requisition went through all the other stages?” She indicated the string of stamps on the form.

“Yes, group leader.”

The Fourteenth Potion Group’s materials had been stalled at the single stage in the chain which the High Ring White Tower could directly control.

Tally’s verdict came, warm and familiar in Maureen’s head while Viska was still standing by the empty crate, waiting for her response.

*Faster than I predicted,* Tally noted. *Miss logged. And Gus is angrier than I modelled him.*

*Yes, faster than I expected too,* Maureen answered silently.

Maureen turned to address Viska. “Probably an accident. Go and see Head Ambrose. He keeps a reserve batch in case we need it, and it covers exactly this run. Tell him I asked.”

While Viska stood there, still puzzled by why he was being sent to speak to the head of the Pharmacy Department, the silent conversation continued inside Maureen’s head.

*You could report the Tower to Ambrose,* Tally pointed out.

*Then it would be his quarrel, not mine,* Maureen thought back.

*And you would rather keep it your quarrel,* Tally said.

*It is my quarrel,* Maureen responded, firmly.

Viska shrugged, rubbing the back of his red head in puzzlement, then left the workroom. The door swung shut behind him, leaving only the sound of the burners ticking away, measuring off the seconds as Maureen stood in front of the empty materials crate. Richard’s mouth settled into a thin hard line. His eyes narrowed with amusement and a narrow intense focus that would have done a hunter proud. Maureen put the tube back into its rack, then spoke aloud to the empty room. Her voice was low. “Gus. Or should I say Bat. You’re playing with fire.”

*You said that aloud,* Tally noted in a dry voice. Then Tally checked the particle field of the room. Empty. No one had heard but the burners.

* * *

Behind her ward the Mimicry was gone, and when she looked in the frosted glass she saw her own face staring back at her. A pale face framed by short black hair. It had large black eyes and thin lips. The lamp was burning low now, and there was a faint hiss as its flame ate away at the oil. The sheets beneath her were rough and smelled of cold linen.

The blood-red token rested in her hand, and Maureen felt its slow pulse begin to beat faster against her thumb.

[40 s]

[25 s]

[12 s]

[Guild gathering: tonight. Confidence: medium.]

*A job set up this fast was probably arranged this afternoon,* Tally said in Maureen’s head. *Confidence: medium. Gus didn’t waste his afternoon either.*

*Neither did we,* Maureen answered silently.

*And your commissions are sitting on the guild’s job-board,* Tally reminded her. *Right now they are sitting behind the tavern panel, waiting for someone to pull it out. They are listed under Night Owl’s sigil for every seat to read. I flagged them when they were posted.*

*Noted,* Maureen thought back. She kept her thumb resting against the token, feeling its pulse quicken. She kept her breathing slow. Three long breaths, one short breath, in and out. Beneath the calm, something in her was hungry, and leaned forward expectantly.

She reached into the pouch and drew out the plain black robe and full lacquer mask, laying them on the bed beside her. *Owl, keep your eyes black tonight,* Tally said in her head.

*Don’t call me that,* Maureen told her silently.

*I’ll stop when it stops suiting you,* Tally said, sounding very pleased with herself.
