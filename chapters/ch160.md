Maureen Olsen walked out of the Alliance camp at first grey light, the Cross Sword strapped to her hip. The sword’s slender shape felt alien, even through the layers of robe. She wore Richard’s face again, as if returning to an old coat after a journey. Behind her, the pillar trees closed in as if she’d never passed. With every breath she could taste the faintest sweetness, like steam rising from a pan of boiling sugar.

Out of old habit, she snapped her fingers and mouthed a silent request for Tally to read the air. *Taste the air, please.*

*Abnormal against outside, by inference. Your breathing is slower, your pulse sits three beats lower. The sweetness is back.*

*Numbers?*

*You still haven’t given me a nose, remember? I’m working with what I have here. I’ve set it beside the trial-realm file and my nine percent guess. Inferred property: atmosphere. Error band is wide, but I think you’re getting the idea.*

*The extra wizard energy in this air is affecting everything that breathes it. That’s why a goat in here is no longer quite a goat.*

Yesterday’s goat had horns that curved around like a ram’s skull. “Then everything here is a sample,” Maureen said aloud to the empty trees.

*That’s right! Everything here is a sample!*

The realm would remain open for a long time yet, and Maureen would be inside it for most of that time. It made sense to map out what lived here before she needed to know— which plants healed, which beasts bit, and which things did both.

Jing Sheng had always conducted field surveys with military precision. So did Maureen, crouching among the knee-high fronds with their blue undersides. The dew was cold, soaking through her robe as she worked with steady hands, humming quietly to herself. This was the old comfort of the lab bench, rediscovered in a land that had no walls.

Maureen pushed her way through a stand of fronds and halted abruptly. Rising before her was a plant three or four people tall, its roots tangled into knots like ship’s cable. Thick vines had grown around a central brown mass, covering it completely except where it split down the middle, revealing rows of sharp saw teeth.

A long tongue flicked out between the teeth, grey-pink and wet, and drew back with a snap. Drool hung from the lip of the plant, then dropped, burning small smoking holes into the black soil. The smell reached Maureen, sharp and tangy: vinegar over rotten pear.

[Spray: 3 m. Corrosive, by sight. Second-Class Apprentice, by comparison.] Tally painted a pale ring around the plant on the ground.

Maureen stood a pace outside the ring, regarding the plant.

“Back home this would be some man-eating flower that scared farmers and no one else,” Maureen murmured. “In here…”

It had grown into something magical, a plant strong enough to kill an apprentice.

Maureen smiled. *Tally, you might want to take note of this one. It’s a step up from the Sodara tree.*

*Ha ha. Very funny,* Tally said. *That Sodara tree at least had the decency to laugh at you before it tried to eat you. This thing just drools. Not nearly as much personality.*

Maureen held out her hand, palm upward, and concentrated. Dark energy particles gathered in her hand, then began to spread outward, forming a translucent film of oily blackness. The film enveloped the plant from root to crown, pinning it where it stood. [Mana: 23.0 → 22.7]

The film was cold and taut, like oiled silk stretched over a drum. Within its confines, the plant’s vines whipped back and forth with wet slapping sounds, while the teeth snapped repeatedly at nothing. The tongue pressed itself flat against the inside of the skin, like some slug caught beneath a sheet of glass.

[Recording: 100%]

*Can you give me one more angle on that root mass, please?* Tally asked.

Maureen sighed silently, walked around the outside of the black film until she reached the far side, then crouched down until the tangle of roots filled her vision. Once Tally had what she needed, Maureen stood up again and nodded once. *Thank you, Maureen,* Tally said primly.

With another thought, Maureen drew the translucent film closed around the flower. There was a sudden burst, as the plant exploded in a spray of flying vines and splitting brown mass. Black-brown sap spattered across the soil, hissing as it touched the ground. Maureen gathered the scattered pieces of plant using the dark energy particles, then collected cuttings and some of the sap into the empty tubes of her belt-kit. She had to cut under oil—sap from plants within the Ancient Secret Realm had the unpleasant habit of turning brown the instant it touched the air.

[Species new today: 6]

*You’re keeping score again,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*Well, yes,* Tally replied. *I mean, that is sort of my name.*

Several days passed in this manner, with Maureen rising each morning to find the blue undersides of the fronds rimed with frost. Each day, more stoppered tubes clinked as they were returned to her pouch. The survey column grew longer each evening, tallying new species discovered and examined. Alone in a laboratory that covered half a country, Maureen Olsen found herself… content.

* * *

Maureen re-entered the camp as dusk fell, her pouch heavy with stoppered tubes. A patrol of four passed her on the way out, ward-light flickering at their cuffs. It was cold enough now for the frost to stiffen the canvas of the tents, and they crackled faintly as the evening wind pressed against them.

Joanna was waiting outside Maureen’s quarters. The moment she caught sight of Maureen, she stood up straight. “Wizard Richard.”

Tally began reading Joanna’s pulse against the baseline of 82 beats per minute that she had on file for the girl. *Her pulse is higher than normal, Maureen.*

*Nervous, not afraid. She has her hands folded tightly together in front of her white robe.*

Joanna spoke rapidly. “The Jadeleaf Ring has come into the realm as well, and we’ve set ourselves up in the compound that the Alliance has assigned to us.

I’m sure that you’re very busy, but… do you think you might have time to come and visit us?”

“You’re our honorary professor,” Joanna said, her eyes intent and hopeful. “Half the seeds who’ve joined since we started heard about you and decided to apply because of your name. They’ve been waiting to meet you.”

There was another rumour circulating about her now, Joanna added. That Richard was not only the youngest Group Leader in the Pharmacy Department’s history, but also the greatest genius in the entire Wizard Academy Alliance. Tally noted the inflation of the rumour—she had no data to support or deny the claim regarding anyone’s age at promotion.

Maureen rubbed her nose.

*Mark that,* Tally said. *That is the tenth time on file this month.*

*No,* Maureen disagreed, *I don’t think so. It doesn’t count if it’s not on purpose.*

“I haven’t visited the Ring in a while,” Maureen said aloud. “I’ll come.” Joanna’s shoulders slumped with obvious relief. She smiled—one of her good smiles, the kind that reached her eyes. The smile made her look less like a serious student and more like a carefree girl of twenty-two. Her thumb twitched, touching the stitched leaf on her robe’s upper left breast.

Maureen Olsen nodded and they walked through the camp together, Joanna a half step behind. The dusk air was cool, frost making the tent canvas stiff. They passed several cookfires, the smell of burning mutton fat and wet wood mingling in the air. The frost hadn’t penetrated deep into the ground yet, just forming a thin crust over the mud. Their boots broke through it with every step.

“We’ve got a good crop of new seeds,” Joanna said. “Your presence here has restored our standing in the Alliance. No one talks about striking us from the Alliance anymore. None of it would have happened without you.”

Maureen smiled but didn’t commit to anything.

“How is Senge?” she asked.

Joanna’s expression hardened, and her hand moved to touch the embroidered leaf on her robe again. “We’ve brought in as many wizards as would come. He grows worse every month. I don’t know if he’ll see the year out.”

Maureen kept her expression politely apologetic and sorrowful. As Joanna talked about coughing fits and blood-stained cloths, Maureen’s thumbnail traced a slow line along the black leather grip of Freya’s sword.

*Tally, you said he had weeks, right?*

*That was my forecast at the time, yes.* She set the two pieces of data side by side in her memory. He has survived far longer than I predicted. *Well, I suppose it was my potions that helped him live this long, wasn’t it?* Maureen asked silently. *Both columns,* Tally responded. *The old man’s stubbornness and refusal to die also played a role. And the potions were yours.*

“The Jadeleaf Ring is one of the smallest members in the Alliance,” said Joanna. “However, by Alliance rules, each member must be allocated its own compound. Our quota only allows us to bring in a dozen or so people, but we have our own space.”

They arrived at the compound’s gate, a simple pole with the faded green leaf banner hanging limply in the cold air. Raised voices could be heard coming from behind the wooden planks.

Before Joanna even touched the gate, Tally marked the sources on the other side. *Density,* she noted. There were about a dozen thin ones, and one much denser. She placed that one near the Soros sample. *That one doesn’t belong to the Ring,* she told Maureen.

* * *

Joanna pushed the gate open and halted abruptly.

Two knots of people stood opposite each other, separated by the frosty mud of the yard. The Jadeleaf apprentices stood stiffly, faces reddening with anger, fists clenching into knots. The other group lounged against the stone wall of the squat shelter, stretching out across the lean-to attached to one end of the structure. Every slouch and tilt of their bodies was infused with contempt. One of the men was even cleaning his fingernails with a small knife.

“Mistress!” one of the Ring apprentices yelled as he ran up to Joanna. “They’re from another order. They say that one of ours broke something valuable of theirs. They want stones for it, or... or worse!”

Tally ran through the statistics. [Hostiles: 7. Official: 1, density near Soros. Apprentices: 6]

*Nothing broken in anyone’s hands or on the ground,* Tally noted. *Same pattern as Frank’s cracked piece in the Dark Forest arcade.*

Joanna’s voice dropped into a flat tone, hard and emotionless. “I’ll appeal directly to the Alliance council. They’ll investigate every word of this story. Now get out of our compound.”

*Hearsay,* Tally noted. According to Ambrose, brawls between member orders were tolerated by the Alliance. Killings and serious maiming were not, and use of spells inside a member’s compound would be noticed.

*No one is going to die then,* Maureen thought.

The burly man in the silk shirt laughed. “Get out? On your word? The Jadeleaf Ring was something once, girl. What is it now?”

His gut strained against the thin fabric of his shirt, pulling it taut. His hair glistened in the smoke-filled air, reflecting what little light managed to squeeze between the tents and shelters. His laugh ran around his apprentices like he’d made the same joke in several other yards, and expected it to have the same effect here.

He stepped forward and shoved Joanna in the shoulder, knocking her back a step with the force of his blow. One of the apprentices behind him started a low chant, and particles began to form at his fingertips.

Maureen covered the distance between her and the burly man in a single step. Her foot drove into his chest, hurling him backwards through the lean-to. He hit the grey planks with the sound of a splitting barrel and fell still, half buried under the boards.

There was a rattle of splinters on the ground and dust hung in the cold air. Maureen put her foot back onto the ground, as though she’d just stepped off a stair. Her pulse hadn’t even jumped a beat with the effort.

The six apprentices yelled as one and rushed her as a pack. The one who had been chanting hurried the spell he was constructing.

Tally called out, *Live, Left pair. Cloud.*

*Caster. Fireball. Pinch it small.*

*Yes.*

A Corrupting Black Cloud rolled over the two apprentices on the left while two Blast Fireballs burst from Maureen’s fingertips. Pinched down to the size of a fist they exploded against the chest of the spell caster and the man beside him. All six apprentices collapsed onto the brown mud of the yard, their eyes rolled back into their heads.

Two fireballs, one cloud. [Mana: 23.0 → 20.2]

Tally checked every body by sight and sense.

[Pulses: 7. All breathing.]

*Good.*

The yard was silent, the scent of singed wool and the rot of the Corrupting Black Cloud thinning on the wind. The Jadeleaf apprentices all stood frozen, mouths hanging open in shock.

Maureen turned her attention to them. “Flies.” She shook her head. “They were annoying.” Then she looked around the yard before turning to face the new seeds again, her expression mild. “I’m Richard, Group Leader in the Alliance’s Pharmacy Department. The one you’ve all been waiting to meet.”

The apprentices looked at each other, some reddening with embarrassment, others paling in shock, and one girl took a small step back without realizing she’d even done so.

Joanna was first to recover from her surprise. She straightened her robe and addressed her people. “Wizard Richard, our honorary professor,” she said in a level voice, as though nothing out of the ordinary had occurred that day.

*Fastest pulse recovery in the yard,* Tally noted. Joanna’s heart rate dropped back to normal quicker than anyone else present. *Because the crew pushed first,* Tally added, *Joanna’s threat of an Alliance appeal is still good.*

* * *

Maureen’s attention returned to the man lying unconscious on the ground, where he had fallen through the broken wooden planks. She frowned.

Tally caught it before Maureen did. Underneath the man’s own dense grain was a second, folded in upon itself, very dense and very dark. Like a piece of coal within a wet log. [Masked, partial]

Tally recognized it immediately as being from the same family as the shadow-masked rule that the leopard had given her.

*Doesn’t match anyone on file,* Tally told Maureen. *Same school family. Dark. Like yours and Jackman’s. But not the same animal.*

*Familiar,* Maureen thought back silently.

*Familiar, not known,* Tally responded.

“Joanna. An empty room. Now.” Maureen interrupted the introductions.

Joanna looked at her, reading the room well. Asking no questions, she immediately escorted Maureen back to the shelter. It had one room in the back. She ordered all the apprentices out to the front courtyard, as far away from Maureen and the unconscious man as the compound’s layout allowed.

Maureen dragged the man into the back room by the collar of his shirt. He was dead weight, his heels digging two furrows through the mud. The small room smelled of cold stone walls beaded with moisture, and the stale scent of old tallow from the single lamp that flickered in the corner. She dumped him on the narrow cot before quickly drawing Gregory’s runes on all four walls. One after another, she traced the lines, sealing the room against everything outside.

[Ward: closed. Drew 0.4.] Tally laid her own overlay over the silver lines as Maureen drew them, confirming the completed spell. [Mana: 20.2 → 19.8] Maureen let her check it twice. The lines sank into the stone, like frost slowly spreading across a window. The voices from the courtyard suddenly sounded deadened and flat. The flame in the lamp flickered once before standing straight and tall, burning evenly in the still air.

Maureen turned to examine the man more closely. He sat bolt upright on the cot.

The man moved awkwardly, as though he were trying on a coat that had been cut for a different man. He twisted his shoulders and back, trying to work out the kinks in his spine. His joints popped and cracked one after another, like beans in a dry frying pan.

[Source: rising. Above Barok, Maffie, Claude, Jackman.]

Tally’s density bar began to rise, and it kept going past every Official sample she had stored in memory.

Tally’s voice had dropped into her cold and alert register.

“Shoddy body,” said the man, but it was not his voice that spoke. It was old, shrill, and strange. “Still, much better now.”

A faint green light rose in the man’s eyes. The smell of blood poured off him, so thick that Maureen could taste the iron on the back of her tongue. She’d bitten it often enough to know the flavor.

[Nearest sample: Wenger. Second-Tier, hearsay.]

Tally flashed a cold warning beneath the data. [Warning: source exceeds every labelled Official sample.]

The man twisted his head around, looking straight at Maureen. His eyes were half-lidded and amused. “Well, well. There’s a little one here too.”

Dark energy began to pour off the man, like the tide rushing in under a door. It washed against Maureen’s shins, cold and heavy. Her skin prickled at the touch of it, and her shoulders drew back.

*With me,* she told Tally silently.

*Always,* Tally replied.

*He can smell you,* Tally said, her tone quiet. *I don’t know how.*

Maureen could feel the heat rising behind her eyes. *Keep them black,* Tally warned.

Maureen took three long breaths, followed by one short one. The rhythm of Silver Light calmed the fire in her head, and her eyes stayed dark and flat.

Maureen didn’t step back from the possessed man. Instead, she narrowed her eyes as she regarded him. This was a dark wizard. A very powerful one.
