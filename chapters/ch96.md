Maureen reached the group, a few steps behind the echo of her voice calling his name. They turned to face her as she approached—four packs, bedrolls strapped down tightly. Anthony’s hair gleamed dully under the yellow light of the oil lamps set in iron brackets on the walls. Richard and Lucy stood a step back, pressed against the stone wall of the corridor. Lucy held herself stiffly, like someone trying to keep from crying. Richard stood with his hands at his sides, his face serious.

The cold draught rose around Maureen’s ankles from the lift shaft below, carrying a faint smell of chain grease and wet stone. The cuffs of Anthony’s white shirt were grey again from travel, the thread of gold the only bright spot in the whole group.

Maureen glanced down at the travel packs, then looked back up at him. “Anthony. You’re . . .”

He took a deep breath, letting it out slowly, and his face settled into resigned lines, the expression a man wears once the worst has already been decided upon. “Leaving the Dark Forest. Today. For good.”

Tally noted that the followers’ travel packs were strapped for a long road. At least a month’s supply of food by the bulk.

*I can see that,* Maureen told it silently.

*Yes,* it responded, *I know. I’m just being precise.*

“I never belonged here,” Anthony said. “A black-wizard order was never my place. I thought that a name spoken at the right table was worth more than any sword. Then a realm.” He shook his head. “Jack the Ripper had no friends at any table, and none dared touch him when he walked in there. Strength— that’s all they count here.”

*Jack the Ripper.*

*Accurate,* Tally noted. *Jack had me.*

Maureen’s face was perfectly still. The corner of her mouth quirked up ever so slightly.

He had said all this to himself many times before, she read. The words emerged polished and smooth, like a coin passed through too many hands.

“I’d be dead myself if I hadn’t had luck,” he added lightly. “Came by a few tubes of potion.” His eyes never left hers as he spoke the word luck.

*One tube,* Tally noted. *Yours. He is rounding up to save his pride. Perhaps someone else fed him as well? No data.* She looked over his body again and noted that his thumb had sought out the gold thread at his cuff.

*Let him have his story,* Maureen told Tally.

*He is looking at you as he tells it,* Tally responded.

The hollow beneath the oaks came back to her, the sound of his breath, six breaths a minute, the empty test tube standing upright beside his hand. Her own hands rested loosely at her side. She said nothing, and he asked nothing.

He laughed suddenly, a young nobleman brought up on rules for every occasion in a world that played by completely different ones. “Perhaps I was never cut out to be a wizard.”

There was an edge of self-mockery in the laugh. It was thin fabric, and the grief beneath showed through like a dark stain.

Maureen could think of nothing to say. Inside her head there were no words. There was no entry in Tally’s files for this situation, no file for a friend who deliberately chose to be smaller and safer.

“Good road to you,” she said finally.

Anthony gave her one last perfect textbook nobleman’s bow, then turned and walked down the black-stone passage toward the freight lift with his three followers trailing behind. The sound of their boots on the flagstones receded down the corridor, growing fainter and fainter, until the stone swallowed them up. Far below, the chains of the lift groaned once in complaint at the sudden additional weight, and then there was nothing but the cold draught rising from the shaft.

She let out a long breath, feeling it shake inside her. Richard came up behind her, his hand coming up automatically to slap her on the shoulder the way he’d done a hundred times on the convoy road, then stopped in midair.

[Hand: stopped 0.4 s short.]

*Yes, I saw,* Maureen said silently.

*I know,* Tally said. *I am keeping the frame for you so you don’t have to.*

Richard drew his hand back, rubbing the back of his neck self-consciously. He looked red in the face, and Lucy stood beside him, fingers twisted together. Maureen Olsen turned to face the two of them, looking into their earnest faces. She smiled.

“Come on,” she said. “Let’s get a drink.”

The smile was for them, but somewhere deep in her chest sat something small and tight. She let it stay there.

* * *

They took a table in the corner. Maureen sat facing away from the apprentices’ tables, as she always did, and the blackness of the ceiling seemed to descend low over her, hanging thick with the tallow smoke.

When the wine came, she set the stones down on the board before Richard could reach for his purse. He let her do it, which would not have happened two years ago.

The red liquid swayed in the tall glasses, throwing a dark, wavering light upon the tablecloth. It smelled of iron and sour cherries, close enough to blood that she noticed it.

“Maureen,” Richard said, lifting his glass. His face was very earnest. “Thank you. Really. If it weren’t for you, Lucy and I would have died in there.”

Tally flagged his voice, climbing half a tone. That was his old tell, back from when he’d first gone sweet on Lucy and his voice used to climb half a tone whenever she entered the room. Now he only got that way when he was nervous. *I know,* Maureen told Tally silently. *I remember when it used to climb for Lucy.*

*Fourteen vials.*

*Fourteen vials,* Tally answered. *Five Berserk Blood, six Stamina, three Incendiary. Fourteen vials. Two people still alive. Good trade.*

*Kind trade,* Maureen thought.

*Those are rarely good ones,* Tally replied.

*This one was,* it added, after a beat.

Maureen waved a hand. “It was nothing, Richard. You don’t have to—”

Lucy interrupted her, speaking too quickly. “No, it wasn’t nothing! They were precious, so precious! Thank you, Maureen. Really. Thank you!” She leaned forward as though her thanks were a toll she needed to pay before she could be allowed to drink.

Under the table, her hands were knotted in her lap, the knuckles white. Her wine remained untouched. She never looked Maureen in the eyes, but watched her hands instead, just like she had on the ship.

[Thanks: Richard 3, Lucy 5.]

*Lucy is afraid you’ll get cross with her if she drinks it,* Tally said.

*I’d never get cross with Lucy,* Maureen said silently.

*She doesn’t have that data.*

Maureen lifted an eyebrow. In the half-breath of silence before she spoke, Lucy flinched, as though Maureen had raised a hand to strike her.

Then Maureen smiled, accepting the thanks whole. “You’re very welcome. Both of you. Now drink, before it warms.” She tapped her glass gently against each of theirs.

The conversation loosened up a bit after that. They talked about Vivian and her endless frog drawings. They discussed the Gasha chef and his veal kidney. Richard laughed at one of Maureen’s jokes a beat late, and a fraction too loud.

“And since we’ve started telling people you’re our friend who’s Third-Class,” Richard said, his voice full of earnest pride, and something like awe, “no one in the corridors bothers us at all.”

Tally measured him against the table. He was sitting a hand’s width further back than he had on the road.

*I’m measuring him too,* Maureen said silently. *I won’t close it tonight.*

She took a long drink of her wine. It went sour at the back of her tongue. She left the distance standing on the table between the glasses.

* * *

The black-stone corridor back to the dormitory district was long and empty at night. The lights woke by themselves as Maureen passed, one after another, her footsteps echoing off the stone beneath her, cold coming up through the soles of her boots. She walked slowly, drawing her hands into her sleeves. By the third turn the warmth of the wine was gone and her fingers were cold.

Aaron came to her mind, the way she had seen him last, in a silver robe, the Child of the Silver Soul. Richard still believed the rumour that Aaron had gone out on a job and just not come back. She had let him go on believing it. Anthony was somewhere under the forest, on a groaning lift. And Richard, her first friend, had become a true apprentice. He bowed to strength now, respected her, kept a hand’s width of distance between them.

*Shall I list who is gone?* Tally asked, its warm voice inside her head.

*No,* Maureen thought back at it.

*Then I will just walk with you,* Tally said.

It began counting her footsteps instead, a soft number ticking over at the edge of her sight: 73 . . . 74 . . . 75 . . . The sound of the numbers was easier than listening to the corridor echo around her. The dull brass key was cold in her palm when she reached her door. She stood with it for a moment longer than she needed to. Then she breathed out, stepped inside, shut the door behind her, and turned to the work, the way a door shuts.

* * *

Everything was as she left it. The soft hiss of the lamp, the faint green ring in the bench stone where she’d left her first Recovery Potion, and the chill coming off the black stone walls.

The black bag lay on the study desk, along with the general reward and the thick purple-black book she had picked from the vault. From the inner fold of her coat, where it had ridden since Adrian's laboratory, Maureen drew the small purple vial of Silver-Trace Ink and placed it beside them.

[Desk: bag 1, book 1, Silver-Trace Ink 1.]

Tally said, *I like having all your possessions in one place.*

*Don’t get used to it,* Maureen replied silently.

From the bag, Maureen drew a slim bound volume and a scroll—the standard meditation method of the Dark Forest and a spell template marked Official Wizard grade. She placed the meditation tome under the lamplight and began flipping through it, spread by spread.

The paper was cheap, the glue still smelled fresh. The margins were greyed with the thumbprints of other apprentices who'd used the same copy. Mass-produced by the hundred, Maureen thought. All apprentices learning the same method, all kept inside the same cage.

Tally dutifully filed each spread away as Maureen flipped past it. By the third page, Tally was calling it junk. *This is the method that caps apprentice development in First-Tier, you know.*

*Keep going,* Maureen told it silently. *You study the cage you intend to stay out of.*

She unrolled the spell template and read it the same way. When both were done, Tally asked, *Shall I sort these into memory for future reference?*

*Yes.*

Maureen felt the pressure begin to swell behind her eyes. Numbers and glyphs rose and fell in her head like weeds in a current. There was a flicker of pale blue across her vision, and then it was gone.

[Meditation Methods: category opened.]

[Meditation Methods: Entry 1: Dark Forest junk method.]

Tally moved its old file on meditation methods into the new category.

*That file was getting lonely,* Tally said.

Maureen reached over and drew the heavy purple-black book closer. She ran her fingertips across the gold embossed letters on its cover—Heart-Burning Flame—and opened it.

Purple-black smoke streamed up from the pages.

The air around the book blurred as if she were looking through heat haze rising off a forge. There were whispers at the edge of her hearing. Almost words. Almost her name.

Tally’s voice turned cold and impersonal. [Warning: active book. Books-that-eat protocol.]

Maureen didn’t pull her hand back. She wasn’t some weak First-Class seed anymore. A few quiet spell-speech words, and the smoke, blur, and whispers all vanished, as if someone had pinched out a candle’s wick.

*Not entry five. Yet. Watch your fingers.*

*My fingers are fine,* she told it silently.

She turned to the first spread and started working her way through Heart-Burning Flame. Tally recorded every page. The lamp burned lower as the gold-edged pages turned in Maureen’s hands.

Tally confirmed that Heart-Burning Flame was a match by content, not just by halo. *Best fit on file,* Tally repeated. *You could be an Official Wizard. The orders would fight over you.*

*I’m not practising it,* she told it silently.

*I know. I’m just telling you what you’re walking away from.*

Her reason held firm. She’d set herself upon the Ancient Wizard path, and she intended to walk it. She would study the book, expanding her view of the road ahead. Its one or two Official spell models were worth having on file, even if she never cast them herself.

*Ancient path,* Maureen said silently.

*Ancient path,* Tally agreed.

Tally noted that the terms of the vault contract covered disclosing the contents of the book, not filing them. *Not disclosure. Filing.* Nothing flared at Maureen’s throat, neither glow nor constriction. No response at all.

Tally reported this.

Maureen closed the book. The patterns on its cover grew cool beneath her palm. There was a faint smell of burnt sugar where the smoke had been.

It was time to raise the Totem.

Maureen took the purple vial into the washroom. As always, she sat cross-legged before the small mirror, waiting until her breathing settled into the Silver Light rhythm.

Tally used its analysis to paint a thin, pale blue line across the brow of Maureen’s reflection. *Here,* it said. *Thin.*

The Third-Class Wizard Apprentice pulled out the stopper from the vial. The ink within shimmered, like wet sand in a river bed under starlight. She dipped her finger into the liquid, then smeared it along the line on her forehead.

A wave of coolness spread from her brow, down her spine. A long sigh of relief escaped her lips, and her shoulders dropped for the first time since she’d risen that morning.

Darkness and fire began to thicken in the little room. Black sparks and ember-red motes swirled in the air, drifting towards her. One by one they sank into her brow.

Time began to slide.

The hissing lamp danced, its light wavering across the walls, then steadied itself. Again the light flickered, dancing more wildly now. The dark and bright motes approached Maureen more slowly. Then they came less frequently. Then not at all.

Maureen opened her eyes, clear and sharp.

In the mirror, for the barest moment, it almost appeared as though two thin beams of light crossed the room, from the lamp to her eyes. The ink was gone from her brow, soaked deep inside.

[Minai: 69% → 82%.]

[Maureen Olsen. Third-Class Wizard Apprentice. Strength 10.4, Agility 10.2, Constitution 10.6. Spirit 11.2. Mana 11.2.]

[Minai: 3 of 4 used. Missing: Demon-Face Flower.]

*Just us,* Maureen told it silently.

*It has always been just us,* Tally said.

*Quieter now, though,* she thought back.

*I will talk more,* Tally promised.

The empty purple vial sat upon the washroom’s shelf. In the mirror, a black-eyed girl with her hair cropped close sat alone in the wavering lamplight.
