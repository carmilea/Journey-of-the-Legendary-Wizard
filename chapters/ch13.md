The black-robed youth launched himself at her like a panther, the two daggers held before him crossed at the hilts, aimed directly for her neck.

> Crossed. Neck height.

Tally flagged the youth's left shoulder as the primary point of attack. Maureen had already started moving before the black figure arrived. The youth's daggers stabbed at an empty place between the trees. The image of Maureen's neck appeared over the space, a translucent blue outline showing where she had been standing when Tally had analysed the attack.

Her Cross Sword flashed downward, a silver waterfall of a blow that caught on the crossed daggers. The force of her strike threw the black-robed figure backward until he hit the solid trunk of a tree with a dull thump.

Maureen stood, unmoving, her hands still gripping the hilt of the Cross Sword. Her breath came in three long pulls followed by one short exhale, the rhythm of the Silver Light Breathing Technique. The black-robed youth clambered back to his feet, leaving a trail of blood on the pine needles. It bubbled from a corner of his mouth as he climbed back up off the ground. Maureen didn't move, her eyes fixed on the figure.

"Who are you?" she asked without expression.

The black-robed youth ignored her question, wiping the blood away from his lip with the back of his hand.

Then he charged again.

Even without knight secret arts, you could cut down two or three Upper Knights, Tally said. The knights back at the camp are within earshot. If you make too much noise, they'll come running and you'll have witnesses to your real strength.

Plain swordwork, then, Maureen answered silently.

Good.

On her next stroke with the Cross Sword, Maureen deliberately allowed the blade to miss its target. Instead of cleaving through the black-robed youth's shoulder, the tip of her Cross Sword bit deeply into the tree beside him. Her backswing caught the youth across the chest, ripping open a long wound in his black robes.

White splinters drifted in the moonlight like fat snowflakes. The cold night air carried the smell of fresh sap mingled with the copper tang of blood.

His breathing is ragged now, Tally told Maureen. Weight is back on his heels. Then a single line appeared before Maureen's eyes.

> He'll run.

I know, Maureen answered silently.

The black-robed youth stared at her for a long moment with a look of pure terror, then whirled and dashed away into the darkness between the trees. Maureen yanked the Cross Sword free from the splintered trunk. "Running?" she called after him. "You think you can?"

She threw, following the pale-blue arc Tally laid out for her, throwing toward his back. The sword was a silver streak through the moonlight.

Thunk. The sound of steel biting into wood was followed by a scream that was cut off abruptly. In the distance, somewhere in the direction of the caravan camp, a horse whinnied.

Maureen walked forward and found the black-robed figure pinned to a tree trunk by her sword. He was whispering, "Don't kill me. Don't kill me."

Tally said nothing.

You disapprove? Maureen asked it silently.

No. Just trying to stay out of your way.

Good to know. That's a first, isn't it?

I counted.

"Sorry," Maureen said to the youth, "but you won't live."

She yanked the blade out of his chest and let him slide to the ground. The light died in his eyes.

His blood was warm on Maureen's knuckles. She cleaned the blade with steady hands, wiping it on his robe.

She searched the body quickly, finding a pouch of gold coins in his belt, several gems tucked into various pockets, and a small silk pouch. The silk was fine enough to catch on Maureen's callused fingers as she drew the pouch out. Inside were five small black stones that glowed with a faint light of their own.

Black, steady glow, no data available, Tally noted. Gems graded against Skran treasury trays: two high quality, three moderate. Coins flagged, unfamiliar mint. May prove a trail if discovered on your person. You should discard.

Maureen nodded. She kept the five stones and the best of the gems, but left the gold and the rest of the gems on the body.

* * *

As she stepped from the cover of the trees, screams broke out and pairs of seeds who'd fallen in the trampled grass ran half dressed for the relative safety of their carriages. Knights formed up and drew steel while the apprentices, Scarlette included, fell on the black robes. Light flared among the trees.

Warning. Multiple energy radiation sources. Tally's voice sounded in her head, cold as ever. Then it spoke again, this time in its own warm voice. Eleven knights. Twelve knights.

Keep counting, she told it silently without pausing her walk back to her carriage. She drew the curtain closed behind her, unwilling to display her full strength. Scarlette and his apprentices had the situation well in hand.

Maureen scrubbed at her fingernails with the bar of soap provided, cleaning the dead youth's blood from underneath as the screams outside turned to shouts and the shouts turned to orders. The water in the basin turned pink as she worked, the smell of lamp oil mingling with the wet wool scent of her sleeves. Her skin stung from the scrubbing and she ignored it, focusing on removing all traces of the youth from her person.

Tally waited until her hands were still in the water before commenting. Your roots look darker in the water lit by the lamp than they did in the tinker's mirror. I have no data as to why this should be so.

Later. Maureen told it silently and Tally let the matter drop.

* * *

The morning dawned grey and overcast. A cold grey river lapped gently at the bank. Last night's fires hung close to the ground, like a blanket of smoke smothering the camp.

"By sheer blunder we stumbled into some new Dark Forest seeds running an experiment out in the woods," Aaron said standing motionless on the riverbank.

Dark Forest was one of the orders of the black wizards. They were a group that worshipped slaughter and blood.

Maureen thought about the daggers the youth had carried and frowned, her fingers feeling the shape of the silk pouch under her clothes.

"Scarlette is furious," Richard announced, splashing water in his face from the river. "He is blaming all of us for letting our guard down when we got together."

"It was an accident on both sides," Maureen replied.

Tally displayed the image of the robe the youth wore next to images of the robes the attackers had worn as recorded by its vision. Same cloth, same cut, same raid.

Scarlette's pride caused this whole mess.

If Scarlette wasn't such a proud old fool we wouldn't be in this mess, Maureen thought back at it.

He is but a Third Class Wizard Apprentice of the Blue Ring. He is not used to thinking of harm coming to him at the hands of mere mortals.

I know, Maureen agreed silently.

"Master wants you all in his carriage," Jack rumbled up behind them.

The three wizard seeds walked along the line of carriages, Tally keeping count of how many there were against yesterday's line. It marked each empty space with a pale blue outline where a carriage should have been but wasn't.

The interior of Scarlette's carriage was cramped, and the Third-Class Wizard Apprentice's face was dark with anger. Carey sprawled on the worn velvet seats.

"Seven wizard seeds dead. Nine wounded," Scarlette muttered. "We were careless." He stopped for a moment, then continued in a softer tone. "I am glad you are all safe, especially you Carey."

Maureen could read the relief on the man's face, but Tally told her what the man was really feeling. The money! This was a paying job for him, he got paid per seed delivered and he had brought Carey with a grade 7 aptitude to the Blue Ring. His fee would be substantial.

"What now?" Maureen asked.

"I and several apprentices scouted the area. It looks like Dark Forest only has one squad of seeds here," Scarlette said. "We will not return swiftly back to the Blue Ring. First we will slay that pack of black robed brats."

Scarlette pounded the little table in front of him once for emphasis. "We've met them before, and we've thrashed them soundly. This time it will be worse."

No one said anything after that, Richard and Aaron sat stiffly, and Scarlette's anger hinted at some bad memories he did not wish to relive regarding Dark Forest. After several more warnings to the group, he wrapped himself in a dark and misty wind and vanished.

Spike: known source

Tally reported and did not raise any alarms.

* * *

The caravan kept up its high tension for days, watches doubled and every stop started with knights riding out along the tree line. Maureen kept up her training during the cramped hours between, and her body was getting harder and stronger.

Five Spirit Runes.

Maureen sat cross legged on the couch in her carriage, eyes closed, and that was how many she could hold in her mind. Five Spirit Runes, at the same time. It was a first for her.

Five runes. That is an accomplishment, Maureen Olsen. Tally said smugly inside her head. That number is due in no small part to my version of your method. Tally updated its ledger, and the five Spirit Runes were displayed in pale blue.

Fine, you can have the credit. Once. Maureen responded silently.

I will remember this date, Maureen Olsen. Tally said.

The caravan made a halt at a market town to resupply. While the merchants and nobles wandered through the muddy stalls, Maureen sat in her carriage and unlocked the Duke's iron bound strongbox. She counted out several coins, as Tally automatically deducted them from its Skran column.

The market town smelled like raw hides and wet mud. A haze of woodsmoke drifted through the air, mingled with the sounds of haggling and bickering. A rack of staves stood in front of a bowyer, who watched with waxed string wrapped around his fingers as Maureen tested each stave for flexibility and strength. From him, she bought a short hunting bow made of horn and ash, along with a quiver full of arrows. Tally kept track of the bow's draw weight, as Maureen pulled and tested it, while asking how many arrows a beginner wastes before learning how to shoot properly. Maureen told it silently that it would find out.

In the shadowy shop of an apothecary, a sour old shopkeeper retrieved a small clay flask stoppered with cork from the back shelf. "Corrosive," he said. "That will be..." He gave her a steep price.

Maureen nodded and paid it. Tally logged the sharp bite of the fumes leaking from the stopper.

By dusk, the caravan had made camp and the knights were returning from hunting at the tree line. Game was slung over their saddle, and Maureen watched them draw and loose with expert precision. Tally recorded each shot, logging it as its first set of archery data.

That night by the fire, Tally took the bow, the quiver, and the clay flask of corrosive out of the Skran column, while Maureen inspected the strongbox under her carriage seat. There was well under two hundred gold.

Better chance of staying alive. Maureen told Tally silently.

Tally just responded smugly, I'll keep the receipts.

At dusk, the knights hunted game again at the tree line. Maureen stood a few feet away and let fly her own arrows at a nearby rotten stump. The bowstring rubbed her fingertips raw.

Tally recorded the knights' shooting stance and every single one of Maureen's shots. When an arrow flew wide of the mark, Tally helpfully noted, Every miss teaches us more than a hit.

My fingers don't agree. Maureen responded silently.

Maureen snared a brown hare in the tall grass. The hare thumped in her grip, so she touched a single drop from her wax-sealed mixed vial to a small nick on the hare's back leg.

The blue spread out from the drop, staining the hare's light brown fur dark around the tiny wound. The hare kicked twice before falling still.

Forty heartbeats, approximately. Tally announced. Now I have data on the vial.

The blue one. Maureen named it silently. Tally filed away the designation next to the forty heartbeats.

* * *

Maureen Olsen wiped the sweat off the back of her neck, the low campfires and trampled grass welcoming her to sleep after her sword practice.

A red-faced Richard wrestled with a bloodshot-eyed Aaron.

"Let go! Let me go!" Aaron screamed.

"Maureen! Help me!" Richard gasped.

Maureen pinned down one of Aaron's arms. Together, she and Richard were able to hold him.

Richard is staring at how easily you are holding Aaron down. Tally said quietly in her head.

I know. Maureen responded silently. She let her arm tremble slightly as if from strain.

That was a good tremble. Tally noted softly.

"He wants to go kill Carey!" Richard panted.

Ellena had been spotted getting into Carey's carriage last night. That was the rumour that was going around.

Something flickered across Richard's face when he said that, something Maureen could not read, and then it was gone.

Aaron thrashed violently in the trampled grass. The heels of his feet dug furrows in the dirt as he tried to get away. His eyes never left Carey's carriage. It was at the far end of the line of carriages and even in the darkness they could see the dim glow of lamplight escaping through the shutters. Richard leaned all his weight on Aaron's shoulders, his face red with the effort. Maureen kept his arm pinned down.

For years Aaron had been in love with Ellena, and she had seemed tempted. But after the aptitude test she turned to Carey.

Rank before feeling.

Maureen kept that opinion to herself.

Carey is grade 7. Aaron is only grade 3. Scarlette will not spare him if he harms Carey. Tally noted quietly.

I know. Maureen responded silently.

Maureen leaned close to Aaron's ear. "He'd kill you for it," she said quietly. "You know that." Richard gasped, still struggling with Aaron.

Slowly Aaron's arm went slack under Maureen's hand.

After another moment he calmed down. He thanked them both hoarsely, then sat down in the grass staring up into the night sky. He pulled at his hair as the low flames of the campfires burned down. That night the count's son cried like a child. Maureen stayed with him longer than necessary, her hand resting gently on his arm. The chill of the night air crept into her sweat-dampened neck.

And once more Tally spoke, so quietly that Maureen almost didn't hear it. You stayed with him, Maureen Olsen. That was well done.

Thank you, she thought back silently.
