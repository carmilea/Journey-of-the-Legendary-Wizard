The wheels ground through the gravel road, sending up a fine spray of stones. No one in the entire convoy spoke. The whips of the drivers barely cracked over their heads as they rolled down the road.

The Blue Ring and Hader Castle seeds kept to their carriages. No one wanted to risk giving Emily the slightest excuse for offence.

A few days earlier, every guard knight from Upper Knights on down had been called together. They'd ridden off between the valley's rocky walls, never to be seen again.

Maureen remembered the line of oiled black leather riding between the walls, the screams, the pulses of light over the rim of the valley, then nothing. Silence.

Tally had recorded the pulses of light as multiple spikes from a single source.

How many? Maureen asked silently.

Fifty-one knights entered the valley, Tally answered. None walked out.

She lifted the curtain, feeling the coarse cloth beneath her fingers. So this is a black wizard, she thought. Kill every last one of them.

Jack's corrections to your footing are still part of your Basic Swordsmanship, Tally noted unasked. He is in the record.

Maureen didn't respond. The impish voice fell quiet, leaving only her thoughts for company.

Her right heel turned out a finger's width from its normal position on the floor of the carriage, just like Jack had growled at her to set it.

"Maureen," Richard said softly beside her.

She turned and gave him another warm smile, the same gentle lie.

The new guards rode outside their carriage. They wore full plate black armour, with only slits for eyes visible. Their warhorses were black and never stamped their hooves against the ground, and the men or creatures on top of them never moved. They stood perfectly still like fence posts, never shifting their weight even a little. Cold radiated from them, but Tally's data display didn't show any radiation coming off them. Every gait matched exactly. There wasn't a single shift of weight as they rode.

Are they men? Maureen asked silently.

No data, Tally answered.

Maureen let the curtain fall back into place. She'd seen Jack and his men ride. These guards were far stronger than those men.

* * *

Emily gathered the seeds and looked at them with open contempt. "You are wizard seeds of the Dark Forest now. Understand that." The Third-Class Wizard Apprentice clapped her hands once, and immediately each seed had a black robe placed before them by one of the black armoured knights. "Wear the black robe. It is your mark. All of you are wizard seeds of the Dark Forest."

None of the seeds moved at first. Emily's expression darkened, until finally one of the seeds pulled the robe over their head, and the rest hurriedly followed suit.

Maureen, can you note the weave? Tally asked. I need to update the materials database.

It's not a fruit, Tally, she replied silently. It's a uniform.

Everything is data.

Maureen sighed, and pulled the robe over her head. The wool was thick and heavy, and smelled faintly of the dye it had been treated with, and the sweat of another person. It settled on her shoulders with a finality that felt like a confession.

Emily nodded once at the rows of black, identical forms in front of her, and left without expression. The new black-robes and the old ones didn't mix, and quarrels soon broke out, some resulting in blood.

By dusk, Maureen had washed herself behind her carriage. The baggage wagon's tarp had been rigged up as a screen, and a basin of water heated up by the camp fires had been brought to her.

The steam from the water thinned as it cooled in the chill air, wreaths of smoke from the cook fires drifted across the screen, and the water in the basin lay still.

Maureen looked down into the still water. A girl in a black robe was there, looking back at her. The girl had black hair, and black eyes. The face of Jing Sheng at fourteen years old, thin and delicate. Her hands rested on the rim of the basin, and for a long moment she was nobody's maid.

Tally compared the face with the scan taken on day one. [Pigment: Hair, Black. Iris: Blue → Black.]

No data on why, Tally said.

I know this face.

No record of this face, Tally said. Only the one you have. Jing Sheng wore this face, Maureen told it quietly, and Tally filed the name next to the face in its database.

Maureen touched the water with one finger, breaking the image into a series of ripples.

* * *

"No-one has come to teach us anything," Richard said again. "I've got so many questions now, that I don’t know where to start." Maureen gave him short answers as she puzzled through the implications. Knowledge was power, and the Dark Forest would charge the seeds for any knowledge they could. Who knew what the black-robes might teach them, and how? She'd been very lucky to meet Scarlette first.

Richard has asked that question three times this week, Tally told her.

He's allowed to, she told it silently.

"Damn it!"

Maureen looked around at Anthony's approach. The other seed had a grim expression.

"Hello Richard, Maureen. One of the old black-robes got into Kelly's carriage last night. This morning she killed herself, in tears."

Anthony punched the tree trunk, hard. The dark wood shuddered under the force of the blow, and flakes of bark pattered down. Richard's face went white as he looked from Maureen to Anthony and back again.

"How was it handled?" she asked.

"Lady Emily said Kelly killed herself, so she's dead. That's all there is to it." The Third-Class Wizard Apprentice of the Dark Forest would only intervene if someone was killed, and there was solid proof as to who the killer was.

Her throat closed up, as if someone was squeezing it. Without willing it, her thumb found the catch of the sleeve crossbow hidden in her robe cuff, and checked it.

Emily will step in if a killing is proved, Tally intoned in its cold register. Anything less than that is permissible.

Including me, Maureen finished silently. Tally didn't argue the point.

"Lucy!" Richard cried out.

"If this happens to her..." her childhood friend began, then stopped. "Goodbye, Anthony. Goodbye, Maureen," he blurted out and sprinted off into the gloom between the trunks towards the camp. Leaf mould splattered up from beneath his boots.

Maureen Olsen looked at Anthony.

"I've had quiet words with many of our group that we travelled with on the road," Anthony said. "So we can stay together, not be bullied by the black-robes, and prevent this sort of thing from happening again." He glanced down at his feet. "I know I instigated things, but you're too clever to let me pull the wool over your eyes. Your knight training was first class, and you have strength as well as calm. Anna has joined us as well. You'll be safe with us. Come join us."

He looked at your sword before he started talking about your skill with a blade, Tally said.

Maureen bristled at the use of the word 'safe'. I don't need anyone's protection, she told Tally silently. Tally didn't argue. By its count, she could hold off three or four Upper Knights at once.

"I'll think about it," she told Anthony.

Anthony stiffened, then smiled warmly. There was just the barest fraction of a second between the two expressions, Tally flagged. "You know where to find me, Maureen. I'm always in my carriage. You are always welcome." With that he adjusted the Cross Sword hanging from his waist, nodded, and left.

Smoke curled between the mossy, dripping black trunks. Somewhere behind her, someone was having a shouting match. The blond head moved away through the trees, and Maureen watched until it disappeared among the robes gathered around the campfires.

* * *

Maureen Olsen took a harness bell from the baggage, and strung a cord with it across the inside of the door. She armed the sleeve crossbow before settling down to sleep.

Tally's voice sounded in her head, warm and impish. I can't keep watch while you sleep. But I will hear what you hear. Make sure you hear it too.

I will, Maureen Olsen told it silently.

The carriage jolted as one of the wheels dropped into a rut. The harness bell rang out a single, mournful note.

Top among the seeds wasn't enough. Maureen Olsen frowned at the ceiling of her moving cabin as she considered the problem. Tally, do you have a plan? she asked silently.

[Maureen Olsen. Middle Knight. Strength 7.2. Agility 7.4. Constitution 7.5.]

Plan six: 1 redwood nut, 2 fanged black wolf eyes, 1 crystal grapes.

The plans had changed several times as her statistics improved. Redwood nuts and crystal grapes were common ingredients, but fanged black wolves did not live in this area. Where do they live? she asked silently.

No data, Tally replied. It recognised the eyes from its records, not from the map.

Idiot chip, Maureen Olsen snapped inside her head.

Yes, Maureen Olsen, Tally said without protest.

She lay on the soft couch, staring at the small lamp swinging from its hook on the ceiling. The scent of burning lamp oil and woollen blankets filled her nostrils. Her eyes refused to close, too restless to sleep.

Something hard was pressing against her chest. She shifted, trying to get comfortable. The object was in her robe's inner pocket, hidden beneath her clothes. She reached inside and felt the many faces, the tangled blood-red vines carved into its surface, winding around one another, twisting and turning, each bearing a pale eye.

She knew what it was called, remembered the night she'd crouched in the darkness, waiting for the right moment to move. The night she'd overheard Scarlette and the gaunt apprentice.

Words were carved into the back of the seal as well. Words written in Ancient Andas. Alone in the rocking carriage, she sounded them out under her breath. Tally corrected the movement of her tongue on the second word, just as Scarlette had taught her. "Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa."

The seal shuddered in Maureen's palm and a blood red light flooded out from between her fingers. New energy radiation source detected. Source on you. Warning. Cold alert.

Maureen tried to fling the thing away but it stuck fast to her hand. Vines burst forth, covering her like an advancing hedge, pale eyes sliding over her skin. The vines grew thicker, wrapping around her body until she was covered by armour made of thorny red branches. Only her eyes showed through the tangle of vegetation.

The points of the thorns brushed against her skin, pressing close without breaking the flesh. The air smelled of sap and iron. Maureen stood frozen in the cramped space of the carriage.

The vines, the eyes and the thorns all drew back into the seal.

Maureen looked down at her chest and saw that one eye remained, a single faint outline against her breastbone. She quickly pulled the robe closed to cover it.

She was ghostly pale and breathing in shallow gasps. "Ha... an enchanted item!" Her laughter was interrupted by a coughing fit.

[Duration: 6 seconds. Drain: severe.]

Tally logged the event, noting the length of time the spell was active, as well as the amount of energy that had been drained from Maureen's body and the lingering magical mark of the eye on her skin.

I told you to fix your pronunciation of the second word, not to put on armour, Tally said smugly.

"It is the Blood Seal of Thorns," Maureen whispered. "A mid-grade item, as I'd heard." She could only use part of its power and even then only for a few seconds, but her strength was now far above what it had been before she picked up the item. She smiled, glad that she'd risked taking it. Even Anna wouldn't be much of a match for her now, she thought, gripping the seal tightly.
