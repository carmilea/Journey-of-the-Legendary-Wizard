Inside, the brazier ticked in the corner of the sickroom. The room smelled of beeswax and bitter steam from the herbs that boiled in a small cauldron, but underneath, Maureen could smell the flat, copper tang of blood. It was the smell of every sickbed she had ever tended. It was the smell of every battle she had fought.

Lawrence turned from the window by slow degrees. His face, yellow as old candle wax, stood out against the mountains now going dark. His lips moved before the words came out. “Richard. Wizard Richard.”

Maureen gave him the full bow that wizards accorded one another. “Wizard Lawrence.”

Tally sharpened her first reading as Maureen bent down. Lawrence’s field was thinner than the sample she had taken from Barok Olivia. Its edges were fraying, like wet paper.

[Lawrence: Phantom State. Field: thin. Fading.]

*Tell no one,* Maureen thought to her. *Not yet.*

*I won’t if you don’t,* Tally responded.

Lawrence nodded his thanks for the courtesy. “It is kind of you to come, Wizard Richard. Our Denise has brought few friends back here, and I am an old man, happy to see her with a friend and an Official Wizard, besides. I thank you.” He reached across the coverlet and took his daughter’s hand in his own.

Denise let him hold her hand. The poise that she wore like fine armour in Ambrose’s office had grown thin in this room. Her thumb moved restlessly over her father’s knuckles, as though she were counting them to make certain they were all still there.

Then the cough came again, that tearing sound ripping up from the depths of his chest. He snatched up a white towel and clapped it to his mouth as his ribs heaved beneath the white linen of his shirt. When he pulled the cloth away, a bright red bloom stained the fabric.

Tally flagged Denise’s tell: a long exhale, followed by the movement of her free hand to her own sleeve. The same reaction she’d had in the corridor when she spoke of coming home.

*Afraid,* Tally said. *But not of the blood.*

*Yes, I saw it too,* Maureen answered silently.

“Father,” Denise said, her voice low and quick.

Maureen took a step forward, moving to the side of the bed. “Let me look at him.”

Denise turned to look at her with blank eyes, and Maureen smiled slightly. “Have you forgotten, Denise? I’m an apothecary.” She laid her hand gently upon Lawrence’s forearm.

Tally read through Maureen’s palm, using both her sight and particle sense.

[Field: thin, leaking at the core. Blood: fresh, bright. Match in Pharmacology: none. Cause: no data.]

*Nothing?*

*No matches at all. No names. I wish I could give you something, but . . . this bothers me more than if I had a name.*

Lawrence waved a hand dismissively, still coughing into the towel. “I am sure you are a skilled apothecary, Wizard Richard, but my malady is beyond—”

“Beyond what?” Maureen asked. “Tell me what you have tried so far. Perhaps we can find a new way to approach your illness.”

The cough subsided and he lowered the cloth. “You are very kind, I am sure. But I fear that this is beyond even the skill of an Official Wizard.” His eyes flicked to his daughter’s face, and Maureen saw the same fear that she had seen in Barok Olivia.

The arm under Maureen’s hand felt hot and papery, the blood pulsing quickly and shallowly beneath the skin like a bird’s heartbeat. She’d treated a dozen different ailments with a dozen different potions, and every shelf in her head came up empty. Her throat tightened before she could speak.

Maureen gently took her hand away. “Denise,” she said quietly. “I’m sorry.”

The light went out of Denise’s eyes instantly. “Father—” she whispered.

Lawrence patted her hand. He had known how things stood with him for a very long time.

“There, there, Denise,” he said. “Easy now. Easy.”

They sat with him as the window slowly shifted from grey to slate. Even though he was only Phantom State, Lawrence was an old man by anyone’s standards, and he’d lived long enough and seen enough to fill a shelf or two with stories. Ancient orders, long-forgotten wars, ruined realms . . . he had a dry humour that made even Maureen lean forward despite herself, hanging on his every word.

The brazier ticked once, settling into place with a soft crack. The mountains outside the tall window were now black against the last strip of light in the sky. For a single breath the room felt like a slum fire again, with Old John spinning tales late into the night. It was a feeling Maureen hastily put away before it could take root.

“Wizard Lawrence,” Maureen finally asked, half in jest. “If someone could give you your health back, what would you trade for it?”

He blinked at her, then laughed—a dry, papery rattle that hurt to listen to. “Trade? If I could be well again?” The laugh stopped somewhere before it reached the end. “Anything at all.”

*I have it logged,* Tally said. *With the daughter as witness.*

*It was a joke,* Maureen thought.

*He didn’t laugh at the end there,* Tally pointed out. *Here, look—*

[Pause before “anything”: 0.8 s.]

The word dropped into Maureen like a copper coin into a ledger box. Denise was looking down at the floor. Maureen bowed her head again and allowed Denise to lead her out into the cold corridor. The heat from the sickroom still clung to her robe.

* * *

Denise walked her back towards the guest wing as a host should, the gravel under their feet crunching in the night silence. Frost fuzzed every clipped edge of the box hedges. Their breath smoked in the single pool of light cast by the lantern. When they reached the corner where the paths split, Denise halted. She drew breath, no doubt to bid goodnight. Her arms were wrapped around herself against the cold.

Before a single word passed her lips, Tally spoke.

[Sources: 5]

[folding in, out of nothing]

[2 s.]

*I feel it,* Maureen told her silently.

Maureen frowned, then one corner of her mouth lifted. “Your family isn’t very peaceful, is it, Denise?”

Denise blinked up at her, obviously not understanding what she meant.

The air in front of them suddenly rippled with wizard energy, then made a sound like tearing silk. Five figures stepped out of empty space—all wearing black concealing robes, hoods pulled low over their faces, masks hiding their features. One mask had a single carved horn jutting up from its forehead, while another was unadorned and made of some kind of black bronze metal.

[3 Phantom State, against your own scan]

[2 Liquefied, against Claude]

[Label: Denise’s word]

*They’ll split,* Tally said. *Two stay on you, three go after her.*

[Confidence: medium-high]

Denise’s face went white. “Damn it,” she breathed. “Claude.” She didn’t need any other proof. Only her uncle Claude could afford to put assassins this far into Conta Manor’s grounds—and at night. Only he had both the means and reason to do so. Tally agreed.

Five fields pressed down on the little corner of garden, all at once. The frost creaked on the hedges beneath the pressure. Beneath Maureen’s ribs, her own field stirred—like an animal waking from sleep—but she kept it on a short leash.

Maureen took one step forward, moving to stand in front of Denise.

“Wizard Richard,” Denise began behind her, no doubt intending to protest.

Maureen tilted her head, cutting her off with Richard’s low, flat voice. “Guarding you is only part of our deal. I’ll hold them. Go and fetch your family’s wizards. They can’t all belong to your uncle.”

Denise bit her lip, then nodded. “Hold on, Wizard Richard. I’ll be back as quickly as I can. Just . . . hold on. Only a little while.” She turned and hurried down the side path toward the main house, her steps making soft sounds on the gravel.

The sound of her passing faded away. Then the little corner of garden was silent save for the faint hiss of the lantern. In its circle of yellow light, Maureen stood alone before four masks and a single horn that had turned to face her.

One of the Phantom-State wizards gave a cold laugh.

“Running? How disappointing. You’re not so fearsome after all, eh?”

He sprang sideways in an attempt to intercept Denise before she reached the path.

*Left. Chest height. Now.*

Maureen silently told Tally, and the Pale Hand obliged. A pale, sharp claw sprouted from thin air without warning or sound, and shot through the man’s chest from behind. It closed over his heart, plucked it loose, and crushed it to pulp before anyone else even realised what was happening. The Pale Hand cost 1.2 mana.

There was a wet crunch, and a half-screamed “What—” that was cut off abruptly. The dead assassin’s body folded into itself and collapsed onto the gravel. A dark steam rose from the gaping hole in his back. Maureen lowered her hand, turning to regard the remaining four men. “Don’t forget. I’m still here.”

[Pulse: 66. Unchanged.] *I’ll write it down for both of us. Like I did with Lauren.*

It was the unicorn-masked wizard who first recovered from the shock of seeing one of their number obliterated by a Phantom-State casting. “Ah. So that’s why you dared remain alone. You are good, I’ll grant you that. But you cannot read the situation.” He shook his head sadly, sighing. “What a pity.”

He turned to the two remaining Phantom-State assassins. “Take the target. Don’t lose her. Bronze Mask and I will deal with this . . . inconvenience.”

Tally marked her prediction of a split as correct, and began laying out the play. Fireball of Dark Erosion to tie up both Liquefied, while Maureen disposed of the two Phantoms before they reached Denise. Then she paused.

*No.*

*That’s your trump card, Maureen,* Tally objected. *Using it would reveal it, and the craters it left behind would still be there, visible to all.*

Maureen silently answered, *Then they’ll know Richard bites.*

A gigantic sphere of purple-black fire swelled into being over the heads of the two Liquefied wizards, and then dropped straight down onto both of them at once. 3.4 mana, Dark Erosion Fireball.

The smell of vinegar and scorched stone exploded in the air. The hedges danced with purple-black light. The gravel path hissed and bubbled, throwing off puffs of steam. The two masked men fought with the thing, layering wards upon wards to keep it from burning through their protections. The wards sizzled and popped as the Fireball ate through them.

Maureen was already standing beside the two remaining Phantoms by the time they reached the path. Her eyes flared pale red, a vertical slit appearing in each pupil. Mirror Pupil. A fine film of crystal formed around each man, as though a sheet of ice had suddenly formed over a still pond. Each man was held where he stood. Two castings, 5.0 mana.

A bloom of black-red flame spread within the crystal. The Purgatory Fire consumed both Phantoms from the inside out even as they struggled to beat against the film that imprisoned them. In seconds there was nothing left of the two men. Not a single bone. Not a scrap of the robes they wore. No pinch of ash. Two more. The screams of the dying wizards came muffled through the crystal, then stopped. There was only silence. Where they had stood was nothing but bare and dry gravel. The frost had melted back in two perfect circles. Nothing remained.

The unicorn-masked wizard ripped himself free from the last of the fireball. His robe smoked slightly, and he looked at the empty gravel with surprise. “Good,” he hissed. “Very good.” He shouted to his partner, “Bronze Mask! He’s no weakling! We do him together! Kill him!”

Maureen’s eyes flashed to Bronze Mask as he raised his hands, noticing how his field sat within him. Tally had it a beat later.

[Particle grain: match, Aaron’s escort samples. Silver Soul. Confidence: medium-high.]

*You sure?*

*Medium-high confidence. You’ll have to supply the rest, Richard.*

*Fine.*

She reached into her inner pocket and drew forth the Silver Soul Token. She held it up before the black-bronze mask, speaking in a flat voice. “Kill him.”

The silver token was so cold that it made her fingers ache. They were clenched tight around it, and for a heartbeat she saw a faint silver flame flicker across its face before it faded. It left behind nothing more than a piece of worked silver, dull and unremarkable in her hand.

Tally noted the sudden jump in Bronze Mask’s pulse the instant he saw the token. The spike appeared at his throat, just below the edge of his mask.

“A token?” the unicorn-masked wizard laughed. “You think a scrap of silver will turn Bronze Mask on me? You really are—”

His words stopped suddenly. There was a hand sticking through his chest, blood dripping from its fingers onto the frost.

He looked down at the hand in surprise, then twisted his head to look back at his partner. “Bronze Mask . . . you . . . you . . .”

The black-bronze mask was impassive, only cold eyes visible within. He gave a short twist with his hands, and there was an audible snap as the unicorn-masked man’s neck broke.

Something loosened itself behind Maureen’s ribs. She hadn’t even realized it was clenched there. Aaron’s reach extended this far, all the way out here in the western garden. Aaron’s gift did exactly what she had known it would, exactly what she had seen on the face of the man who gave it to her that day.

* * *

Bronze Mask let his partner’s body drop to the gravel. For a long moment, he looked through the bronze mask at her.

“Where did you get this token?”

The blood fell in slow, dark drops from his fingers onto the frost. His voice was level, even polite. Beneath the politeness lurked something else, something sharp and hungry that had nothing to do with her.

Maureen didn’t answer him. Instead, she turned away from him, looking toward the house, eyes narrowing. As she did so, she slid the token back into her inner coat pocket.

[Sources: 7, closing. 140 m.]

*The Oserroba people are coming,* Tally said. *Wizards, most likely. You’ve got . . . maybe half a minute.*

“The Oserroba people are coming,” Maureen said, keeping her eyes fixed on the approaching lights. “You should go.”

Bronze Mask didn’t speak for several seconds. Then, “Very well.” He named a place. A tavern in Hastan City—the town nearest to the manor—one that was closed during daylight hours. “Tomorrow. Knock until it opens.”

Tally immediately pinned the departure line and filed the tavern as well as the time.

Maureen nodded. *Do I trust him?*

Tally considered. *I trust the pulse spike more than I trust him. I trust the token more than either of those.*

Bronze Mask stepped into the shadow at the foot of the wall, and was gone. Tally felt his density begin to dissipate immediately, thinning out quickly until he was outside her range.

*He’s gone. The red is still in your eyes.* [Mana: ~15.7 → ~6.1]

Maureen looked down at her hands, then closed her eyes, taking three long breaths, one short, in the rhythm of the Silver Light. When she opened them again, they were black once more.

The garden smelled like vinegar and scorched stone.

The purple-black craters smouldered slightly, and the air above them danced in a haze. Two bodies lay on the gravel. One had a hole blasted through his chest, while the other had his neck broken. There was nothing left of the other two men, not even a speck of dust.

A clattering of lanterns rounded the corner at a run.

The family’s wizards had thrown on their robes over their nightclothes, and Denise ran among them, her face white with shock.

“Wizard Richard! Are you hurt?”

Denise’s hair was falling from its pins, her breath fogged the air in quick little clouds. Her hand crept to her sleeve again. She looked to Maureen first, then at the craters, then back to Maureen.

“Five came,” Maureen said. “Four are dead. The one in the bronze mask ran when he saw his leader fall. He didn’t feel much like arguing.”

Tally compared this story to what Denise had seen before she’d run, five robes with masks clearly visible, and found the story consistent. None of the seven densities accompanying the lanterns matched Claude’s signature either. The Oserroba wizards were spreading out around the craters, talking in whispers. One crouched down at the edge of one of the corrosion craters and reached for it, then snatched his hand away. Every face that turned toward Richard had a new kind of caution in its eyes.

“Thank you,” Denise said, her voice just a bit unsteady.

“It’s in our contract,” Maureen said. She left them to their bodies, and returned to her room to rest.

In the dark guest room, Maureen sat on her bed, reaching into her inner pocket to touch the token. It was cold through the cloth.

*What exactly did Aaron give you?*

Maureen thought about the token for a long moment, and answered silently.

*More than I knew.*

* * *

[Night: 1 pass, servant, 02:40. Ward drew 0.1. Mana: 20.1, full.]

*You slept like a stone,* Tally said.

*Good,* Maureen thought back.

During breakfast, Maureen told Denise that she would like to visit the nearest town. The Oserroba heir looked between her father’s rooms and the craters in the garden, already pulled in two directions. “Hastan City is the closest,” she finally said. “You can go there.”

The road leading to Hastan was flanked by fields of stubble, each furrow and clump of earth coated with a layer of white rime. Maureen’s boots rang on the frozen cart ruts, and a crow lifted off from a fence post to complain noisily about her presence all the way to the first houses.

Hastan was not much of a city. It was small and grey, with most roofs made of slate instead of wooden shingle, so the air smelled of coal smoke rather than woodsmoke. The smoke hung heavily in the streets, not rising far in the cold morning air. The market sold mostly root vegetables, with some bread steaming gently under a cloth cover. Somewhere behind one of the high brick walls surrounding a yard, a pig was shrieking.

No one gave Maureen a second look. She was just a plain young man dressed in a travelling robe.

She walked through the town three times, doubling back once through the market, cutting through alleys, pausing at vegetable stalls even though she didn’t have any need for more food.

[Tail: none, by particle sense, 300 m.]

*That is the most thoroughly walked town I have ever seen.*

*Last time I didn’t, I got a brush of powder on my sleeve from a servant boy,* Maureen thought back silently.

The side street was quiet, and the tavern stood at the end with its shutters closed for the day. Maureen knocked on the door, waited a few moments, then knocked again. She kept knocking.

Her knuckles made a dull wooden sound. A sour smell of wine escaped from under the door, mingled with the stale tang of old sawdust. A woman walking along the street with a basket over her arm frowned at the young man hammering away on a closed tavern’s door. She hurried by.

The door opened.

A middle-aged man stood there, the scent of drink heavy on his breath and clothing. His beard was thick, scruffy and unkempt, and he looked at Maureen once without saying anything before turning around and re-entering the tavern. He left the door open.

*His density is exactly as it was last night,* Tally said. *To the grain.*

*He uses drink to cover himself,* Maureen suggested silently.

*Or he drinks. Both columns.*

Maureen followed him inside. Behind her she heard the door swing shut, the latch clicking sharply into place.

The main room was dim and cool, with all the chairs stacked upside down on top of the tables. The great stone hearth was cold and dark, and the wooden floor was gritty with old sawdust. Maureen caught herself holding her breath as she entered and forced herself to exhale slowly.

*No one else in the building, Maureen. Just you and him. By density.*

The man had walked across the room and now stopped beside the cold hearth. He turned around and his eyes settled on Richard’s plain face. They did not move away.

“We haven’t met,” he said. His voice sounded rough from drink. “But I know you, Lady Maureen Olsen.”
