Scarlette's party rode out after the Dark Forest seed squad, and so the whole caravan was forced to creep along. The wheels turned a few times and then squealed in protest before the carriages halted. The horses were content to doze in their harnesses while flies settled on their dung. Scarlette kept returning, his face grim, and the other wizard apprentices were frequently seen climbing into his carriage for whispered conversations.

*Six visits in two days,* Tally announced.

*Counting doors now?* Maureen thought back silently.

*Someone has to keep track.*

Scarlette hadn't been teaching them anything new lately. The five seeds were left to puzzle out their Ancient scripts on their own.

* * *

That afternoon Tally had laid a pale-blue arc across the running deer, and Maureen had drawn her bowstring, loosed an arrow, and dropped the beast herself.

Now at dusk she sat beside the riverbank with Richard, roasting the deer over a small fire. "I thought that deer was sure to get away," Richard said as he carved himself another slice. "And then you shot it, with a single arrow. Poor deer."

*You missed two out of your last ten practice shots,* Tally noted.

*Eight out of ten isn't too bad,* Maureen thought back.

*Eight out of ten is eight out of ten.*

*All right, fine! Eight out of ten is eight out of ten!*

Richard was happily chewing on his meat. Grease ran down his chin. She frowned briefly at the sight, then decided it didn't matter. "Has Scarlette's party returned yet?" she asked.

"Not yet. It's been nearly a day gone. I wonder how deep this feud goes?"

Her Cross Sword lay next to her. The edge of the blade and the heads of the bolts in her sleeve-crossbow had been coated with poison since her encounter with the black-robed youth. There was a faint greasy sheen that ran along the length of the blade, catching the light from the fire whenever the coals flared up.

*That is the bluing agent you used on the hare,* Tally said. *Remember to use the leather glove when handling the weapon.*

*I know. I'm being careful.*

*You are being careful because I nag you about it.*

She scowled but did not respond. Richard was still talking, mostly about Lucy, and he did so with a happy grin, spearing chunks of venison into his mouth between sentences. Neither of them mentioned Aaron, who had been shutting himself up in his carriage like a madman for days now, meditating. The smoke from the burning wood blew low over the water. Behind the trees the light shifted from red to grey as the sun slipped toward the horizon.

* * *

Maureen stood up to make her way back to the carriages, when she heard a hiss from the trees.

[Warning. Warn—]

Tally's message flashed through her head, but her body had already twisted aside before it even finished. The arrow skimmed across her shoulder, and she felt its heat through the cloth of her shirt. It had been aimed directly at her heart, and she felt cold sweat break out all over her scalp.

"Richard!"

Her Cross Sword, made only of plain steel, flashed through the air, intercepting the second arrow and slicing it in half right in front of Richard's face.

"T-thanks," Richard stuttered, swallowing hard.

"Don't mention it." More arrows whistled through the air, and both Maureen and Richard dodged them.

Other seeds began to scream, hit by stray arrows. Maureen and Richard ducked behind one of the carriages. Arrows thunked into the wooden planks above them, and horses began to shriek and pull at their tethers.

*Maureen, you were faster than my sentence there,* Tally said with an almost proud note in its voice.

*Thanks.*

*It's just that I wanted you to know that under pressure I can work quickly.*

*Noted.*

*All the arrows are coming from the trees,* Tally continued in her head. *Four archers at least, judging by the gaps between shafts.*

"Richard, these are Dark Forest seeds." Maureen spoke in a low voice.

"But . . . how?" Richard whispered. "I thought Master Scarlette went after them."

Maureen shook her head.

A rough, furious voice shouted from the other side of the camp. "The black-robed brats have come out to die!" It was the voice of the Third-Class Wizard Apprentice who had been left to guard the camp. "Come on, you curs! We'll send you back to your masters in pieces!"

"Hehe, Berret," laughed a shrill woman's voice from the dark woods. "Forgotten me?"

"What? Emily, you old hag! You're here too? Wasn't it just Derrick?"

*Maureen,* Tally warned, *two sources. One is the apprentice on the camp side, the other is coming from the woods.*

*Which one is stronger?*

*No data.* Tally wouldn't guess.

Spell blasts began to light up the trees as though lanterns were hidden within the wood, their light diffused by the paper-thin bark. The ground shuddered beneath Maureen's knees.

Tally supplied the data. *Scarlette has been gone for almost a full day. One apprentice left behind.*

So that was the Dark Forest plan. Use Scarlette as a decoy to draw off the bulk of the forces while keeping Berret pinned down at the camp. Then move in for the kill with the seeds.

*Yes.* Tally agreed with her analysis. *That is how it reads.*

She looked over at Richard. "It's revenge," she explained. "If they can kill off all the seeds, then Blue Ring and Hader Castle will run short of new blood. Not a bad move, is it?"

The Dark Forest seeds were fighting with more determination than the seeds of the camp. Ambushed and outnumbered, it was no contest. Black shapes began to emerge from the trees, loosing arrows as they came. Then they drew swords and daggers.

"Be careful, Richard!" Maureen shouted.

*Left, leaping.* Maureen thrust her blade upward. The point took a black-robed seed in the throat, lifting him off his feet for a moment before he collapsed to the ground.

*Blade at your throat.* Maureen snapped her sword up, meeting a dagger in silence. Sparks sprayed where the two met. With her free hand she shoved the black-robed seed, knocking him off balance.

Maureen watched the face of the seed change from shock to sneer to frozen mask as her sword's point emerged from the back of his neck.

Maureen pulled her blade free without glancing at the bodies and ran toward quieter ground.

* * *

At the edge of the forest Maureen traded blows with a seed whose left hand was an iron hook. The hook swept the blade away, binding it.

*Hook binds the blade. Let go. Crossbow.*

Maureen stepped back and pressed the catch beneath her sleeve.

The bolt took the seed in the neck. He crumpled to the ground, the wound taking on a bluish tint.

The catch clicked back into place.

The sounds of battle from the camp were suddenly thin, far behind Maureen. Richard was gone. Somewhere in the fighting they had lost each other. Her eyes kept being drawn back to the camp and the sounds of battle there. Tally spoke up.

*You can't see him.*

*And I can't see him. Look to the one who can see you.*

*Fine,* she told it silently.

The chase had carried Maureen deep into the forest. She searched the hook-handed seed's body, keeping the few items he carried that were most valuable and tossing the rest of his possessions into the underbrush. Tally weighed the options for her: *It would be safer to wait near the camp than to run afoul of several more seeds out here in the darkness.* Maureen agreed with it and turned back toward the camp.

Black bark. Wet mould of leaves underfoot. The chill of the night creeping through her sleeves. Her own harsh breathing loud in her ears.

She found an old man seated in a small clearing. His white hair and beard glistened in the darkness. He wore black robes and leaned against a tree, eyes shut, coughing up blood. A Dark Forest wizard apprentice, at least. Far more powerful than any seed. Maureen cursed silently and turned to flee from him.

“Such an unexpected pleasure,” he said without opening his eyes. “I had not thought to meet a little one.”

Maureen threw herself to the right as a fireball exploded from the old apprentice's hand, felling a tree in a rush of flame. The tree went up with a roar, a wave of heat washing over her face. She wiped the sweat from her eyes with the back of her hand.

She just managed to keep dodging more spells. [4s] [7s] [12s] [—] Tally told her, timing the intervals between attacks. Then forty seconds of nothing. He couldn’t aim properly. He couldn’t even stand up. He was burning through all that remained of his strength just to terrorise her. Maureen stopped and turned around.

The old man was a stark contrast to the black trunks of the trees surrounding them. He was lit by the now blazing tree, and dried blood stood out red against his white beard.

Maureen smiled. No seed could possibly avoid the spells of an apprentice unless the apprentice was extremely weak. And the intervals between the spells were increasing. “Respected sir,” she asked. “Do you have strength enough left to cast any more spells?”

There was silence for a long moment. “You are an interesting one,” he finally said. “I think I will—”

Tally was silent. Maureen took that as assent and let fly. Her arrow sprouted suddenly from his chest, pinning him to the tree.

“Respected sir,” Maureen said, “I prefer not to work with anyone stronger than me. You are a wizard apprentice, and I am only a wizard seed.”

Tally gave her the distance at which an arrow would still have sufficient force to kill but where she would also have enough time to react if necessary. Maureen backed up slowly until she reached it. Then she nocked another arrow to her bow, drawing back until it was a full moon. The wood creaked softly by her ear.

“Respected sir,” she said with a cold smile. “Let us continue.” She loosed the string.
