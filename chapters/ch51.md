The mist grew less dense outside the gathering, thinning to a grey haze that smelled of lake water. Hank and three tall bound slaves waited for her on the open ground, all four of them bowing and calling her Mistress as Maureen approached. "Follow me," she said.

The air was cold, and steam rose from the horses' bodies where their sweat evaporated. The new leather armour on the slaves was still stiff, creaking slightly as the big warriors climbed into their saddles. Then Maureen's three new guards followed her to the hollow.

"Wukandima," she said aloud once they reached the hollow.

Two Miles vipers slithered free of the carriage and wound their way up into the tube hanging from her belt. Hank climbed up onto the driver's bench without being asked, sitting alone in the front of the carriage.

Good guards, Tally said.

Good pets, Maureen answered silently.

You called them guards, Tally pointed out.

The three slaves rode three of the five warhorses that Maureen had purchased at the gathering, one brown haired man and a woman flanking the carriage while the other red haired man led the way. The three slaves had already started calling the driver 'Old Hank'.

Maureen pulled back the curtain separating the carriage cabin from the driver's bench. "What are your names?" she asked.

The three warriors glanced at each other, then back to her. "We... have forgotten," the brown haired man admitted.

Maureen nodded, staring at the red haired slave. "From today onwards, your name is Jess." She turned her attention to the brown haired man. Tally's display was showing her the man's scanned physical traits. His shoulders were heavy, his feet steady on the stirrups. She thought about the name quietly, then spoke it aloud. "Bram."

Finally, she studied the woman, whose hands held the reins with practiced ease. Her fingers were thick with calluses, the knuckles worn smooth by old scars. Maureen's hand paused on the curtain for a moment as she remembered Aunt Mary's hands, the red cracks in the skin around her knuckles. "Mary," she started to say.

You already have two Marys on file, Tally's voice reminded her.

"Marla," Maureen said instead.

All three slaves thanked her for their new names. You're good with names, Tally told her.

I named you, Maureen responded silently.

Exactly, Tally's voice sounded smug.

Maureen let go of the curtain and allowed her disguise to fade. Tally automatically closed off the power drain line in her display, logging four people who had seen her real face. Then she turned her attention to organizing her spell models.

* * *

Weeks blurred into frosty dawns, creaking wagon axles and dusky evenings with Marla oiling the blades and Hank stirring the pot. She headed north east towards Macolin Province, part of the Principality of Redbud. Along the way she stopped at wizard gathering halls to share knowledge with the wandering apprentices. Their odd travels added a great deal to her store of information.

At each gathering hall she would use her red haired sellsword illusion to sell off some finished potions. Only a few at a time, never too many.

[Magic stones, loose: ~4,170 → ~4,730. Card: 950.] Tally told her, updating her ledger.

[Days since landing: 720 → 810.]

On occasion some apprentice still tried to follow her out of the halls in the hopes of robbing her, but those attempts ended fatally for all involved.

[Would-be robbers: 9. Survivors: 0.] Tally recorded.

Maureen silently asked Tally to stop keeping track of the attempted robberies, and it agreed to remove it from her display. Of course, just like the Overboard count, Tally still kept the count internally. The attempted robberies ceased becoming events for Maureen; she simply wiped her blade clean on the dead apprentice's cloak before returning to her meal.

The servants took every opportunity to relieve her of every possible chore. If Jess, Bram or Marla saw bandits on the road, they dealt with the threat without bothering Maureen.

At one of the later gathering halls, the dripping cloaks hung around the fire to dry while travellers talked loudly at the next table.

"…dead in his shop. Shelves completely empty…"

"…old Olit…"

"…sellsword did it…"

Found dead in shop. Shelves emptied. Sellsword suspected. Tally noted down the date, comparing it to when Maureen left the hidden market. None of the overheard conversations mentioned a red haired woman.

Wintercold City was a big city, and a busy one. Shops, stalls and vendors packed the streets, and people crowded everywhere. They asked around for the way to Marquis Umas's mansion and were directed down a street. On their way they passed a fur market where animal skins were piled high on tables. Traders haggled in loud voices, and Maureen saw several humanoids she didn't recognise. The mansion walls rose up ahead of her, towering over the roofs of the wealthy houses nearby. Guards in shining iron armour stood at the gate, each armed with a steel tipped spear and an iron sword. Their eyes looked straight ahead, seeing everything without appearing to stare at anything.

"We've come to visit the Marquis," Hank announced as he stepped up to the guard. He handed over the letter, and after a glance at their fine clothing the guard ran into the mansion.

* * *

A short while later, the guard returned, followed by Marquis Umas and his steward. Umas held the letter with both hands, his eyes briefly flickering as he noticed how young Maureen was before he bowed. "Lady Olsen, welcome to my humble home."

The marble floored hall was warmed by braziers, and Maureen's fingers ached as they thawed from their stiffness. She glanced around, taking in the details. Tally counted the guards positioned along the walls, and flagged the pulse of a blood vessel that jumped on Umas's temple. Keep watching that, she told Tally.

"Yes, thank you very much for seeing us," Maureen replied politely as she followed Umas into his study. A report from his scouts lay on the desk, the ink smeared across the page. Dickens's boots were caked with mud from the roads, the brown gloop running almost to his shins.

That smear is shaped like a fist, and still wet, Tally noted. The mud on his boots is also fresh, he has been riding.

Noted. File Dickens's scouts.

"Now tell me what trouble you have run into, that made you feel the need to call upon my teacher's assistance?" Maureen asked bluntly.

Marquis Umas bowed his head, apparently unconcerned by the lack of courtesy title when referring to Adrian.

"Milady, our scouts have risked their lives to bring us word," Dickens explained, his voice grim. "Viscount Feren moves already. He will strike soon."

Marquis Umas slammed a fist down on his desk. "Feren, that ungrateful wolf cub. If it weren't for me, the refugees would have killed him long ago."

The ink pot jumped on the desk. Umas's face went grey, his hands quivering where they lay flat on the wooden surface. "When the refugees came through my lands years ago, I saved Feren from death at their hands. Then I made him a viscount, gave him a position in my fief. All these years he has been plotting to seize it from me, and now he acts."

Umas looked up at Maureen, his eyes intense. "Help me. Crush him."

Maureen nodded, then Umas continued. "But that is not the main problem. The main problem is that a Mystic backs Feren, which is why I called upon your teacher's assistance."

Umas is frightened of this Mystic, Tally noted.

Is he frightened of me too? Maureen asked silently.

Yes. He just hasn't noticed it yet.

Umas sat back in his chair and smoothed his beard, running his fingers along the edges as if to make sure every hair was lying flat against his face. He straightened his collar as well, apparently trying to restore himself to calmness as the sleet tapped against the glass window.

"How strong is this Mystic?" Maureen asked.

"I do not know," Umas admitted, looking embarrassed. Maureen stared at him for a moment without saying anything.

No data, Tally noted.

"Milord," Dickens said. "No one in our camp has seen this Mystic in action. But there are several Mystics living in Wintercold City who claim to have witnessed the same Mystic in battle."

One of the candles on the desk sputtered, the flame dying down for a moment before flaring up again. A blob of wax dripped onto the map, splattering on one of the corners.

Maureen smiled for the first time since she had stepped through the gate.

"Hold a banquet, Marquis," she said. "Invite those Mystics. If you want my help, fine, but first I want to hear from them how strong this Mystic is."

Umas blinked, obviously taken aback by her words, but then he nodded. "Yes, that would be wise."
