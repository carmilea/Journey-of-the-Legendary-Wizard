The laugh died in the thick, sweet air.

It had been forced through the raw, reshaped cords in Jack’s throat. The resulting rasp sounded like a file being drawn across wood. It hurt, leaving her throat sore.

Ten paces of wet leaf-litter separated her from the man who’d promised to kill her.

Maureen stopped smiling. When someone like that made a promise like that, she took them at their word. Beck was an apprentice of the White Bone Tower. He’d paid—dearly—for the right to talk to people like her in that tone. Fools didn’t live long in the black-wizard orders. The ones who could still breathe had earned the right to be arrogant.

Maureen read him like a reagent. He was tall, with chestnut hair that hung lankly in the moist air of the realm. His cheeks were sunken nearly to the bone, and his eyes were narrow, just as cold as hers. She noticed the way his right sleeve hung heavily, looking wrong. It looked as if he kept something bigger than an arm folded inside there.

Tally translated the Ancient Andas numeral glowing on the back of Beck’s hand, and provided the total from its table. *More than twice your total,* it informed her.

*So he’s been busy,* Maureen thought back.

*Busy and still standing,* Tally replied. *Those are the ones I don’t like.*

Beck cocked his head, regarding her with an amused look. The expression a man gets when his dog barks at a passing cart. “That’s what they all say. Word for word. Every one of them, right before . . .” He clicked his tongue in pity. “You could have lived, boy. You just walked the wrong way.”

He held out his palm, flat, toward Maureen. Like an executioner displaying his empty hand to the crowd before he drops his axe. “So lie down and take it, Jack.”

Figures were beginning to appear at the edges of the clearing. Tally identified them by colour as Maureen’s eyes passed over them. [Blue Ring, gold hair. Black robe, red hair. Bone face: Sieg. Mist Labyrinth grey. Hader white, veiled.] [Calibration: all five at or above the top of the column.]

The girl in the veil drew Tally’s attention, as Tally marked the knight-trained gait, straight spine, and set feet. > Partial match: Anna, ship file. Confidence low.

*Anna disappeared the day we reached the Dark Forest,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*People disappear all the time,* Tally said. *Then they turn up again.*

Maureen dismissed the veiled girl from her attention. Beck was what mattered now.

Sieg’s face could not pale, but the apprentice took a half-step back. He was the only one who rocked back on his heels when he saw Jack. It was a half step that Wolf had often taken when walking around her. She remembered. The other four remained standing loose and idle, like men who had come to watch two dogs fight in a pit.

And underneath it all was the second game. At her belt, the stoppered test tube of dark-purple blood shifted against her hip. The sediment within had begun to lean towards the trees behind her back.

*Still there?* she asked Tally.

*Yes,* Tally murmured.

*Good. Let her watch too.*

Beck moved. The earth exploded under his feet, throwing him into the air with a thud that sounded like a sack of grain dropped from an upper loft window. His fist cocked back, high above Maureen’s brow, and began to fall with all the apprentice’s weight behind it.

Tally caught his cheeks filling out as he leapt for her.

> Breath. Prepared. Your fireball dies.

*Let it die then,* Maureen told Tally. *Show me what kills it.*

“Simons-Sado.”

A ball of deep-red fire the size of her fist jumped from her open palm, singeing the skin of her own cheeks as it burned through the air straight for Beck’s chest.

The apprentice smiled as he fell. Then he breathed out, and a spear of white frost erupted from his lips. The cone of frost met the fireball an arm’s length from his body, and both spells annihilated each other with a crack of steam. The particles around them scattered like birds startled from a tree, raising gooseflesh along Maureen’s particle sense with a wave of cold.

*Now we know he does that,* Tally said.

*He built it on the way up,* Maureen answered, narrowing her eyes. *He fights casters for a living.*

*Frost breath, counter, ready before the jump.* Tally filed the data away.

The clearing smelled of wet ash and scorched frost. A thin trail of hissing steam curled away from where the two spells had died on the damp leaves. Beck’s fist hadn’t stopped falling.

* * *

Her feet found their own places in the leaf-mould. The body that Tally had read out for her several days before, every line of it sculpted over the standard, wanted to take this blow squarely. Wanted to prove itself. Maureen Olsen squared her shoulders, prepared to meet the fist with her flesh.

Tally’s voice turned cold, precise. *No. Mass: arm est. 3x human. Tendons alive. Do not block.*

Tally had calculated his crouch against Julo’s recorded blows, and the numbers flashed red before her eyes, superimposed on the descending fist.

*I can—* she started, silently.

*You can’t. Move.*

She muttered the full phrase of the Blood Seal of Thorns under her breath. Red vines, thorned and twisting, erupted from her chest and surrounded her, leaving only her eyes visible. She slid backward across the leaf-litter as though it were made of ice, the armour allowing her to move with a speed and fluidity no ordinary person could match.

[Seal: 0.0 s] began to tick upward at the edge of her vision.

Beck’s fist smashed into the trunk where her head had been an instant before. There was a loud cracking noise like the breaking of a ship’s spar in a hurricane. A burst of white splinters exploded from the tree and drifted down through the clearing like a sudden snowstorm.

The tree groaned, then leaned over and fell with a muffled thud that Maureen felt through the soles of her boots, shaking loose more of the leaf-litter covering the ground. *That was where I would have been standing,* she thought. Her mouth was as dry as chalk.

Someone at the edge of the clearing whistled, low and long. Through the slit in her armour, Maureen caught sight of the gold-haired Blue Ring man’s smile widening. He was interested now.

*Release.* Maureen let go of the armour as Tally ordered, and it sank back into the Seal cradled in her inner pocket. Its glow was brighter than it had been outside the realm, fed by its kill-days. [Seal: 1.8 s. Released.] *Thank you for listening,* Tally said, returning to its normal warm tone. *Don’t get used to it.*

>Beck.
Third-Class, by calibration.
Strength: 9.7
Agility: 9.8
Constitution: 9.8
Spirit: 8.4
Graft: high-energy creature limb
Danger: High

The readout appeared over Beck in her sight.

*Not a lot of spirit for a Third-Class,* Tally commented.

*He sold some of his head to buy that arm. Tough. I’ll fight his head then.*

Beck withdrew his hand from his stump slowly. The arm had grown so much that it was thicker around than a man’s waist. It was pale green, slick and wet, and green filaments crawled over the surface. They knotted together to form the tendons she could see beneath the translucent skin. The tendons pulsed like something alive inside, and they were not human. No human had ever grown this.

The smell of crushed green stems and stagnant marsh water hung in the air. The tendons throbbed with their own pulse, out of time with his heartbeats. The arm had its own life.

Her stomach tightened. She kept her expression fixed.

“You ran instead of standing there like a post,” Beck said. His voice had dropped into a rasp that sounded like coal ground beneath the heel of a boot. It was as if he was holding an iron lump in his mouth. His eyes were reddening at the edges. “Clever boy. Most of them stand.”

His gaze slid over her chest where the armour had been, and he laughed low in his throat. “An enchanted item, too. Lovely. I’ll have that off your corpse.”

“Limb graft,” she answered in Jack’s rasp. She let out a slow breath. “Somebody in your Tower knows their Mutation Studies.”

“Cost me plenty,” Beck replied. The red in his eyes was spreading now, eager and almost proud. “They offered me trinkets like yours, and I said no. I took this. When you’re strong enough, Jack, toys don’t mean anything anymore.”

The fear inside her went cold and hard. She wrapped it with contempt, letting it settle over her like the armour had done. Contempt was useful: it kept her hands steady and her voice flat.

“Pity,” she said. “You’re not strong enough to say the word.”

She opened up on him. Acid Missile first, then a Blast Fireball. Finally an Undead Wind Arrow, as quick as thought.

Beck sidestepped underneath the acid, and blew a frosty breath against the fireball. He slapped the arrow aside with his green arm, as though he’d been practising for this battle every day of his life.

*Three for three,* Tally noted grimly. *He’s trained to fight casters. You won’t be able to land spells clean on him.*

*Then I’ll use something that isn’t a spell,* Maureen told it.

Green threads snaked out from Beck’s arm, latching onto two tree trunks. He pulled, hard, and the clearing became a blur. When it snapped back into focus he was standing right next to her, his fist cocked back so high that its shadow fell across her face.

“Die!” he snarled.

Maureen spoke the Seal’s phrase again. Blood-red tendrils, thin as twine, burst from the leaf-litter around Beck’s feet. They snaked up his legs, wrapped tightly around his calves, and then slithered up to encircle his chest.

[Seal: 0.0 s]

“Useless! Off!”

Beck heaved once, and the vines snapped like wet string. It cost him a moment to tear them loose from his body.

>Bind holds 0.6 s. Throw. [Grenades: hand 11 / pack 54.]

Maureen’s fingers were already at her bandolier. Three small black shells spun through the air toward Beck’s feet. “Kerrash,” she muttered as they left her fingers.

 His expression changed. “Those things—!”

He twisted, trying to get away. Too late.

The first grenade went off, followed immediately by the other two. Fire washed over Beck, and he screamed, a high, raw shriek. Wrapped in the armour, her expression blank, Maureen glided away from him across the ground.

The thorns crackled under the sudden assault of heat. Shrapnel peppered the armour, tapping against the thorns like hailstones. The air filled with the stink of burnt hair and cooked vegetal sap.

[Seal: 3.4 s. Released.]

[Grenades: hand 8 / pack 54.]

* * *

Beck was still standing inside the fire. His robe had been consumed until nothing but rags of flame remained around his legs. On one side of his head, the chestnut brown hair had burned down to the scalp, while on the other it still smouldered. The green arm was moving with a writhing motion that was visible in the smoke.

*Still alive,* observed Tally dispassionately. *That graft is healing him. By sight.*

“Jack!” he screamed, his voice cracking through the iron. “Jack the Ripper!”

He twitched, and his right hand jerked. The filaments of his grafted arm twisted together as they shot out towards her. Every single thread of green fibre merged until there was only one, as thick as a man’s finger.

*Thread. Coming. Acid first, then the blade.*

“Amra-Keis.” A bead of sour-green acid met the thread in mid-air with a hiss. The thread smoked and frayed, and began to sag. It was still flying at Maureen, but much more slowly.

The vial jerked at her hip. In the back-draught of the fire, there was a faint smell of fallen blossoms behind her.

*Behind, by the vial. Blossoms, by the air. She’s choosing your end-of-swing.*

*Then give it to her.*

> Opening in 3.

> 2.

The green thread hanging in the air sagged further. Maureen herself sliced it in two with her own Cross Sword. The blade flashed over her head, in one smooth overhand stroke that carried her whole weight. Her arms whipped down low under her, following through, leaving her back to the shadowy trees behind her. To any watching eye, ‘Jack’ had nothing left. Freya was still unseen in the deep shadow.

*I hate this plan,* Tally said.

*You helped make it,* Maureen told it silently.

*I hate it professionally.*

And then Freya was out of the shadow behind Maureen like a sword drawn from its sheath. The slender Cross Sword was in her fist, dense with black light. The dark elf drove it towards Maureen’s spine.

Maureen’s body didn’t startle or flinch. It had been waiting for this blow for days, and every muscle in her back was already wound tight for the effort. Her heart didn’t even beat faster in anticipation.

She had already begun to turn as Freya emerged. Jack’s long and cold eyes met Freya’s blue-black ones over the black-lit blade of the Cross Sword. The vial hung dark and still at Maureen’s belt.

“Dark elf,” Maureen said in Jack’s rasp. Her voice was as flat and unemotional as a closed ledger. “Finally come out, have you? I’ve been waiting for you a long time.”
