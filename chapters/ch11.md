Maureen sat with Richard for dinner later that night, and when they finished, the two of them washed the bowls together. Then she climbed into her carriage, drawing the curtains closed behind her.

The small lamp's flame was smoky, and the scent of cheap oil hung in the air. The couch rocked gently with each rut that the wheels passed over, and every jolt was accompanied by the creaking of harness outside the wooden slats of the carriage.

*Tally? How are we doing?* she asked silently.

[Optimisation: 10s] lit up before her eyes, and Tally's warm voice counted down from ten to one in her head.

*One.*

Tally sounded pleased with itself, like someone presenting a gift.

*Maureen Olsen, may I transfer the completed Spirit Rune to your memory?* Tally asked.

*Yes.* Her silent answer appeared on another line, before Tally had even finished asking the question.

A sudden swelling headache filled her skull, and she groaned in discomfort. She closed her eyes, rubbing her forehead with two fingers.

There was a building pressure behind her eyes, and a pale blue flicker. After a moment, the pain began to recede from her mind.

*It will be worth it,* Tally noted, apologising in its own way. *Mostly.*

Maureen had planned for this since her first lesson in runes. It only worked because she had learnt the basics so well, and Scarlette kept explaining things and answering questions. It took far longer than generating Silver Light and Basic Swordsmanship had.

The new rune lay over the top of the one that Scarlette had given her, like a tracing of the same drawing but neater and cleaner. It settled into place, like a key sliding into a well-oiled lock.

She ran through both runes side by side, comparing them. Hers was a little stronger, but not by much. Tally's tone was matter-of-fact. *My database can only hold what you have seen and heard. You have done well against the accumulated work of generations of wizards, but you are still only a wizard seed.*

*A little is still a lot when the base is measured in generations,* she replied silently.

Tally's voice was smug. *Remember you said that when I sulk about it later.*

Maureen breathed deeply, letting the air out slowly. She cleared her mind and began to meditate as the caravan continued its journey to the Blue Ring. Recruitment was almost complete; all they had to do now was bring the seeds back.

* * *

Maureen sat on a rock at the water's edge, holding one end of a wet cloth in each hand and drawing it slowly down the length of her Cross Sword. There was a crunching sound behind her and glancing up, she saw Aaron approaching. Quickly she stood up and waited for him.

"Maureen." He smiled weakly, stopping before her. "I've been looking for you."

Tally observed that his smile was using only half his face, while the fingers of his right hand worked at the hem of his left sleeve, rubbing it back and forth so that it was worn threadbare.

*Do not catalogue my friends' grief,* Maureen told it silently.

*I don't have to. I catalogue everything. Their grief is just the part I don't tell you about.*

Aaron had loved Ellena from the start, everyone knew that. But Ellena was a duke's daughter and Carey's aptitude had made him the centre of her attention. There was nothing Aaron could do, he was only grade three, low aptitude. The reeds rattled as the wind blew through them, and Aaron's thumb kept working at the hem of his sleeve.

"We are all wizard seeds," he said finally. "Anthony of Gasha is hosting a gathering tonight. I am spreading the word. This is a rare opportunity for all of us."

"Of course," Maureen said smiling.

"Please tell Richard. I must go now and find the others." Aaron turned and hurried away along the shoreline back towards the camp where the carriages were drawn up behind them.

Maureen hung the sword from her waist, pulled her hood forward over her hair which she had tied back, and crossed the wet shingle to Richard's carriage.

* * *

The gathering began at dusk, and as it grew louder Richard and Maureen headed towards the sounds. Seeds were walking up and down the pathway, in their finery, passing under the swinging lanterns. The young men were handsome, the young women dressed in fine silks, their white shoulders bare. Each one seemed to leave a trail of perfume in the air as they passed.

Richard stared at the bare shoulders of the women and Maureen teased him until his ears went red.

*Seven times and counting,* Tally informed Maureen, letting her know how many times Richard turned to look back at the passing women.

"Aaron and Ellena had words before they left," Richard said as they walked along the path, the sound of voices and laughter coming closer. "Master Scarlette had to step in to calm things down."

Maureen was surprised at this news. They were both from the same principality. Ellena might be cold to everyone else, but she had always been gentle when dealing with Aaron. She opened her mouth to ask Richard what had happened, then closed it again.

Richard shrugged. "It was all Carey's fault," he said.

"Carey?" Maureen repeated softly, breathing the name out rather than speaking it.

A figure stepped out from the darkness onto the pathway in front of them, a grin on his face. "Calling me?"

In just a few days he had transformed himself. His clothes were rich and expensive, several gemstone rings glittered on his fingers, and the scent that rose from him was thick and heavy, like a perfumier's cart in summer.

*Rose oil. Musk,* Tally logged. Two scents that it did not recognise, so they were added to its database. One ring stone matched against a tray that Tally had logged in the Skran treasury. *That's one,* Tally said warmly, sounding ever so slightly smug.

"You shouldn't use my name so casually," Carey said. "I have grade seven aptitude, high aptitude, while you two are only middle aptitude."

"You will apologise for your rudeness earlier," he continued. "And I must admit, Master Scarlette told me to let it go, but we're from the same house so I am feeling generous. You were a maid there anyway. Be my maid, and when I'm a wizard, if I'm in a good mood, maybe I will look after you."

Maureen saw the change on Richard's face. "Carey!" He snarled the name.

Maureen's body became very still. The light from the swinging lanterns lay flat across the hilt of her Cross Sword, the metal gleaming dully at her waist.

She smiled at Carey, and spoke softly. "Carey. You're not a wizard yet."

"True, but some day I will be, and then -"

A flash of silver interrupted him. Maureen's Cross Sword was levelled at his throat.

> Throat. Two fingers' depth is fatal. Half a nail's depth is a lesson.

A pale line appeared along Carey's skin as Maureen adjusted the edge of her sword to half a nail's depth.

He stopped talking, unable to move. His teeth chattered. The lantern light lay starkly on his white freckled face. The rich perfume that was heavy around him suddenly seemed to change character, becoming a sour stink as sweat broke out all over his body. Her expression was cold, unemotional.

"Since you're not a wizard yet, shut your mouth and stop talking nonsense. Understood?"

He did not dare nod.

Slowly Maureen pulled the sword back and sheathed it. A fine thread of red blood marked the tip of the blade, one fat bead of it sliding down the cold steel.

She patted Richard on the shoulder, the boy still stunned into immobility. "We should go," she said, and he walked beside her numbly. They made their way along the well-trampled path back towards the carriages. Behind them there was a dull thump as Carey's legs gave out, followed by a choked sob. Tally flagged both events on her display. Maureen did not turn around.

*Heart rate elevated,* Tally informed her.

*Log it as fine,* she replied silently.

*Logged as fine. Disputed,* Tally responded.

The hand that held the Cross Sword was as steady as ever, and her jaw stayed clenched until they reached the carriages. Her face was turned away from Richard's stunned expression.
