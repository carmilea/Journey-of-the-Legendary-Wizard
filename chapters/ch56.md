The corridor was cold, and the stone arrow slits were furred with frost. The sun was thin in the winter sky, its light lying across the castle stone like a layer of glass. In the glazed window at the end of the corridor she could see a young noblewoman standing poised, looking out into the world. Her dark hair was like smoke from a pine fire, and her eyes were black.

Below her, guards patrolled the manor yard, while the refugees waited in line for the cart to take them out to where they would settle. Tally counted twelve mercenaries and thirty refugees at drill, although it was quick to point out that the spear line on the left was out of step. Jess knelt before her, striking his fist against the breastplate of his armour.

"Milady, Marla and I have hired mercenaries to act as your guard, and we’ve picked out some of the stronger refugees to train," he said.

“It’s all thanks to your mercy, milady,” Jess continued. “Opening Viscount Feren's old lands to the refugees has made all the difference.”

“I only thought that leaving the land empty was a waste,” Maureen replied. “Whoever farms a plot of land for five years may consider themselves the owner. That is my decree.”

The word ‘mercy’ sat uncomfortably in Maureen’s mind. The corner of her mouth twisted slightly as she looked down at Jess.

“I hear you,” she said.

I have logged that as an act of mercy, Tally said.

It’s not mercy, Tally, it’s arithmetic, Maureen replied silently.

That entry is now disputed, Tally said smugly.

Maureen stood at the end of the upper corridor, looking through the glazed window into the manor yard below. The sounds of men drilling in the yard drifted up to her. Her thoughts wandered back over the season.

When she had asked Marquis Umas for leave to live nearby he had given her so much more—he had made her Viscountess Olsen in her own right, giving her Viscount Feren’s title outright. He was trying to win her over, or at the very least warn others through her. Worldly rank and position could never compare with the power of wizardry, but it did make it easier for people to disappear on the back mountain.

Through the winter, Maureen had fed Viscount Feren’s captured soldiers, petty thugs, and other dregs into the Sodara. Several times since then a fruit had ripened, and she had eaten it. Her rune meditation had leapt from crawling to fast.

Tally showed [Third array: 8/12] in her display. In its own warm voice it told her, You are four runes away from completing the Rune Array, and attaining Third-Class status. I must admit that my own estimate was incorrect by almost six years. It is most pleasing to be wrong in this fashion.

Maureen had stayed in the cave on the back mountain for weeks at a time. She had left it heavily trapped. The traps played through her mind in silent thought, one after the other.

Falling anvil trap set.

Confirmed, Tally said, drawing on its records of her setting each trap.

Collapsing wall trap set.

Confirmed.

Spiked pit trap set.

Confirmed.

Dart trap set.

Confirmed.

Poison needle trap set.

Confirmed.

Tally had no eyes in the cave to keep watch. I will just have to worry about it instead, it told her.

Maureen looked out the window to the distant hills. “Soon,” she murmured to herself. “Very soon.”

Her fingers closed over the cold stone window sill, and the frost furred on the stones melted beneath her touch.

“Milady,” said a neat voice behind her. Old Hank, her slave steward and former court treasurer, walked up the corridor, bowing his head. “Your friend Mr. Albert, the well-known Wintercold banker, is waiting for you in the reception room.”

“Thank you, Hank,” Maureen said. “You are always so diligent.” She clapped him on the shoulder before she continued down the corridor.

I like him, Tally said as she walked. His ledgers always balance to the copper.

You like anyone who keeps receipts, Maureen replied silently.

* * *

The reception room was warm. A red carpet muffled her footsteps as she walked across it, dark oil paintings hung on the walls, and a tall glass window framed the view of the misty hills outside.

Albert sat in a sofa, sunk deep into its cushions, which seemed designed to swallow his bulk. He was wheezing slightly in the warm air, which smelled strongly of the liniment he used for his chest.

“Maureen,” he said after they had greeted each other, “as a wizard, not associating with the other apprentices is not a good habit.”

Tally spoke in her head. His breathing is worse than at the banquet.

You noticed? He’s a banker, you know.

No, Tally said. I’m counting his breaths. He’s not the only counter in this room.

Albert smiled. “I have a very small gathering this evening. Just us, Daphne, and the others. Would you join us?”

Maureen looked up at Albert from where she sat opposite him. “Certainly, Albert.” She stood up and held out a hand to help him to his feet. The man puffed slightly as he stood, panting with the effort of moving his massive body.

“Time to go, then,” he said.

Old Hank was waiting by a black carriage. Already dressed in his neatest clothing, he bowed his head as Maureen approached.

“Follow Mr. Albert’s party to Wintercold City, Hank,” Maureen said.

“Yes, Milady,” Old Hank said.

The leather seats of the carriage were cold beneath her, and she pulled a rug over her knees as the wheels jolted over the frozen ruts in the road. Besides eating the fruit, she had spent the last few weeks studying the optimised spell models that Tally had parsed for her.

[Optimised models: +11% power (est.). Dark Wind Ring: no component needed.]

I am rather proud of that last bit, said Tally’s voice. I took out the powder. You don’t need any powder for this one.

That wasn’t powder, that was ground serpent fang. You took out the fang.

Yes, Tally agreed, a little smugly. I took out the fang. See?

Her fingers made the old gesture of scattering powder before her, out of long habit. They found nothing to scatter. The new chant ran whole through her head.

It wasn’t long before her carriage rolled into Wintercold City behind Albert’s party.

* * *

The coal fire popped as tea cooled in thin porcelain cups. Daphne’s needle clicked away, never stopping.

“My lady,” Daphne, Blake and Aubrey said softly as Albert ushered her in. There were strange looks in their eyes and Blake laughed as he shook her hand.

A wave of perfume came off Blake. He had obviously been among the noblewomen again. Maureen kept her face expressionless.

Tally read their looks. They knew about Kane. They did not know about Patton. [Known: Kane. Unknown: Patton.] Good, she thought.

“I found some ghost-face spider silk for you, my lady,” Blake said. “From Madame Jojo. Although, I must say, her skin is still whiter than spider silk.”

There were mentions of scarlet crows flying past, theories on an experiment that no one dared try.

Maureen’s finger tapped against her tea cup, and her eyes wandered to the frosted window as the conversation turned to the nobility and wealthy patrons.

The tea was cold.

[Ghost-face spider silk: Blake. Scarlet crows: sighted. One abandoned experiment: unnamed. Blake's reason: irrelevant.]

Tally placed the afternoon's leads neatly in brackets on her display. It sounded pleased with itself, like any archivist of gossip should after a day such as this.

Maureen watched the frost on the window. I will not be coming to one of these teas again, Tally.

Tally's voice entered her mind. We shall see, Viscountess Olsen. Regardless, I will keep the file open.

“Why don’t we take a trip up to the Grey Dove Settlement?” Aubrey suggested, placing both his hands on his cane.

“Oh, yes!” Albert wheezed. “Capital idea!”

Daphne nodded without looking up from her needlework.

“I’m afraid I must decline,” Blake said, giving a suggestive smile.

Maureen looked up from her tea. “The Grey Dove Settlement? What is that?”

Albert slapped his forehead with a fat hand. “I apologize, my lady. The Grey Dove Settlement is the largest gathering place for wizards in Macolin Province. There are shops, weapon workshops, potion stores... parts of high-energy creatures. All sorts of knowledge to trade.”

I'm hungry for data, Maureen Olsen, Tally said, opening a new line on her display.

You're always hungry, she answered silently.

“That sounds interesting,” Maureen said aloud. “I’ll go.”

“Marvelous!” Albert exclaimed, rubbing his hands together and standing up. “I shall make the plans now. Three days from today, at the beginning of the First Crescent Moon. That should be perfect, eh?”

Blake stood up. He bowed deeply to Maureen before rushing out, a trail of perfume following after him. Albert and Aubrey made teasing comments about Blake's eagerness as he left, but Daphne just snorted without taking her eyes off her sewing.

Old Hank drove her back home in the black carriage. The wheels creaked as they rolled over the frozen grit, leaving the city's lamps behind them.

* * *

Maureen Olsen rode out of the castle gate three days later on a tall brown horse. She wore black leather armour and left Old Hank in charge of the fief. “Support him in my absence,” she told Jess and Marla.

“As you wish, my lady,” they both replied with a bow.

The horse’s hooves crunched against the frosted ground, its breath steaming in the chill air. Her horse's breath steamed white in the still air.

“Viscountess!” shouted Albert, waving an arm.

Maureen nodded to him and Daphne and Aubrey. “We're all here now,” Aubrey said stiffly. “Let us be away.”

Not knowing the way to the Grey Dove Settlement, Maureen let the apprentices lead and rode behind. Tally recorded the entire trip and counted every turn so it could lead Maureen back if necessary.

Finally, they entered a valley choked with grey mist.

The mist felt cold and wet on Maureen's face. The rocks were slick, the black colour standing out starkly against the grey of the fog. The only sound was the breathing of the horses.

Albert muttered a few quiet words then shouted, “I wish to enter the Grey Dove Settlement!” in Ancient Andas. The words boomed through the valley.

There was a flapping of wings in the silence. A flock of crows swooped in from all around and merged into a figure over two metres tall. The figure wore a black robe and had ghastly green eyes. It was followed by a black mist that blew about in the wind.

> Warning: energy radiation source. By sight.

Tally flashed when the crow guardian formed. It added the crows to its list of gatekeepers. Stone statues, a squirrel, an owl, and now crows. Do any of them work for free? it asked.

None. Not one, Maureen thought back silently. Her back straightened and her hand found her rein, pulling it taut. After all the gossip about the crow guardian, it was finally here, and those unnatural green eyes woke her up. Almost gladly.

The crow guardian swept its gaze over Maureen and the apprentices. “Is it you who wish to enter the Grey Dove Settlement?”
