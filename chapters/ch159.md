Maureen stood at the edge of the Pharmacy Department’s cluster, the silver-grey gate at her back. The ground was like iron underneath her boots, frost-hardened earth crunching with every step. The air smelled of cold iron, wet canvas, and campfire smoke. She felt Richard’s ordinary jaw set against her own.

[Realm: day 1. Mana 23. Magic stones, all columns: ~26,200.] Tally listed out in an inner display, pinning up the lines for Maureen to read easily. [Air: abnormal vs outside. By inference. No nose.]

*Breathing easier now, pulse three beats lower.*

*I feel healthier already,* Maureen said silently.

*You felt healthy in the last slaughterhouse too.*

Patrols passed by in fours, wearing the silver-trimmed white robes of the Alliance. A faint glow of ward-light shone from their cuffs, a soft blue radiance in the early morning light. They walked with measured tread, eyes fixed on the treeline. Maureen counted them as they passed, just as she had once counted the Duke’s guards from the scullery door. And they never knew.

[Patrols in view: 4.] Tally painted over them with her precise letters. [Members: Official, all. Captains: Phantom, by comparison to your own scan.]

*They don’t keep this many Official Wizards in the entire Passise at one time,* Tally noted. *They must think this is important.*

*The Alliance obviously agrees that this is a place worth protecting,* Maureen answered silently. *Worth every one of those Officials.*

A captain passed by her, and even with her spirit perception held close, Maureen could feel his density pressing against her like heat leaking out from a stove in the next room. She kept her own aura pressed down, a flat and thin shell around her. Her borrowed face was that of an apprentice wizard, and her aura glowed like an apprentice’s. She let her eyes slide past him.

Others moved through the camp outside the patrols’ order. These were wizards—men and women wearing patched robes, one with an eye covered by a bandage, another with mismatched boots on his feet. They moved in ones and twos, coming into sight from the treeline and disappearing back into it. None of them wore a crest.

Wandering wizards.

At the trestle table by the gate, a male clerk sat at a table with rolled maps and open Alliance crates. He unrolled each wanderer’s hand-drawn map, then kept the map for himself. He weighed the sacks on a large brass scale, then dumped half of the contents of each sack into one of the Alliance crates. No one argued with him, though a few watched the crate the entire time he was filling it.

[Sacks halved: 7.] Tally counted for Maureen. [Maps kept: 7. Maps returned: 0.]

The strongest-looking wanderer had reached for his half a breath too early, then pulled his hand back as a patrol turned in his direction.

“Surprised, Richard?” Ambrose asked, coming up beside Maureen. His white-gold robe brushed the frost-hard earth. He smiled like a gardener showing off his well-tended flowers.

“You fought for every line on the zoning map,” she said in Richard’s low, flat voice. “And now you’ll let strays wander in and pick over what you won?”

“Nobody knows this place yet,” Ambrose replied. “Someone has to walk it first. Who better than men no one will come looking for? We take half of whatever they carry out. Each new path they discover, we have drawn and submitted to the proper authorities.”

He paused, then added, “In return, each receives the basics. Potions, that sort of thing.” He said the next word without any change in his tone, as though it were simply another coin he’d already counted. “It’s humane, really.”

The corner of Maureen’s mouth twitched. It wasn’t a smile. She recognized this trick, having seen it from the other side of the table. Tally already displayed the numbers:

[Wanderers: no spatial items. Half of a small haul.]

*The rest dies with them,* Maureen thought.

*And the maps don’t,* Tally responded.

Maureen nodded silently, watching as the next wanderer stepped up to the long trestle table. At medium confidence, Tally added, *The provided potions are enough to keep a wanderer walking until the Alliance’s zones have been fully mapped. And not much longer.*

*The ration bags on the ship,* Maureen thought.

*Yes,* Tally agreed. *I’ve filed it next to Evans.*

*Yes.*

“An interesting idea, Master Ambrose,” Maureen said aloud.

Ambrose regarded her with pleasure in his eyes, holding them on her for half a beat longer than necessary. He’d seen her catch on to what he was doing. Then he turned to inspect the contents of his crates.

A wanderer passed by close enough for Maureen to smell the blood and wet moss on his sleeves. His eyes remained fixed on the ground as the patrol wizards strode past. For a moment, she was reminded of the thin men from the slums who used to wait outside the Duke’s back gate for scraps.

* * *

The last tent gave way to the edge of the camp. The frost thinned and then abruptly vanished altogether. The growth of the realm began. Fronds grew to knee height, and their undersides were blue, curling away from the tread of her boot. The air smelled of crushed sap and the cold black earth, a rich smell compared to anything beyond the gate.

Tally identified the frond for her. [New. No plate match. Species new today: 4.]

*Four,* Tally repeated.

*Don’t get greedy,* Maureen told her silently.

*I’m not getting greedy, I’m just being precise.*

Maureen crouched down beside Tonk, who was hunched over the frond. He had his notebook balanced on one knee, and the mass of pale gold curls kept falling into his eyes. “Wizard Richard! Look, this one.” He pointed at it eagerly. “When you cut it, the sap goes brown right away.”

Maureen looked at the frond. Its structure was close kin to a bitter fern that they had in the pharmacology shelves. “Its sap is reacting with the air,” she said aloud. “If you want to keep it whole, you’ll need to cut it under oil.”

Tonk scribbled furiously in his book, copying her words verbatim. It wasn’t the first time he’d asked her about the strange flora. One strange plant after another, and Tonk would ask her questions, and the answers would come to her as easily as breathing. She watched him write. His lips moved while he wrote, like Lucy’s had when she listened to the lectures years ago.

“Thank you, Wizard Richard,” Tonk said as he stood. He flushed, bowed his head. “Truly, I don’t know how I’d make sense of any of this without you.” He bowed his head again, then again, as though making sure he got it right.

*Three bows,* Tally noted. *He’s trying to set a new record there.*

*Let him have it,* Maureen told her silently.

“I just talked, that’s all,” Maureen said.

Tonk shook his head. “Sometime, I’d really just like to be half as good as you, Wizard Richard. Half would do.”

For a moment, the warmth in his eyes got through to her. Not many people had looked at her like that. Winnie’s hug back in the corridor of the Dark Forest. The real Richard’s eagerness, years ago now, back on the convoy road.

And then Tally’s display went cold. [Dark-red thread in his density. Grown since the Fourteenth Group. Match: Emilia density knots, Jackman’s bead. Confidence: low-medium.]

Maureen’s hand was about to rise and wave away his thanks. She stopped the gesture, letting her arm fall back to her side. For a moment she tasted scorched honey, felt again the bead leaping from Jackman’s body in the tunnel, the heat behind her eyes.

*Not proof,* Tally said.

*It’s not a coincidence, either,* Maureen replied silently.

*Didn’t say it was,* Tally answered, still cool.

Tonk slipped his notebook back into his robe, and for the briefest instant, the grey light caught the open palm of his right hand. Over it, there appeared a faint dark-red sheen, like a transparent film laid over his skin, and then it was gone as Tonk curled his fingers shut. He didn’t seem to notice.

*Is it hurting him?* Maureen asked Tally silently.

*No data,* Tally replied. *Pulse is fine. Colour is fine. Too fine. Worry logged. Not fact. The Totem is quiet around him. I checked. File opened:* [Tonk: Purgatory note, low-medium, growing.] *Check every time we see him?*

*Check it every time we see him,* Maureen told her.

*Every time,* Tally confirmed.

Maureen said nothing to Tonk about what she’d seen. She did tell him to keep the brown-sapped frond away from his eyes, then nodded once before turning back toward the trees. Tonk waved after her, smiling wide.

Behind her, she could hear him reading her answers back to himself, his voice just loud enough for her to make out if she listened hard. She didn’t turn around.

* * *

The trees here rose more than ten metres into the air, their trunks as thick as the pillars holding up Duke Skran’s great hall. Their bark was ridged so deeply that Maureen could have slid an entire hand into the cracks. A thick layer of moss muffled her footsteps. Beneath its green smell, the air carried a rich scent of musk and old blood.

Tally laid down a pale blue ribbon, leading back toward the camp. [Camp: 1.2 km, behind.] At the edges of her particle sense, large shapes moved. *No wizard density,* Tally noted. *Beasts. Big ones.*

Maureen caught glimpses of them sliding through the spaces between the tree trunks. A stag with antlers that reached higher than any cart, a shape with the bristling back of a boar but larger than a hay wagon. All of them were far too large for their kind, and the woods smelled heavily of them.

Maureen’s hand found the Void Dimensional Pouch and drew Freya’s slender Cross Sword from it. The black-leather grip settled into her hand, and black light crawled along the blade like oil on water. It was the first time she had walked with the sword openly since before the Underworld.

*Oran Olivia led the eastern envoys,* Tally noted once. *Eve was last seen alive. Anyone who knew Freya could know this blade.*

*Only a bear would see it out here,* Maureen thought.

*Bears talk less than elves,* Tally reminded her. *Logged.*

She had chosen steel today deliberately. No Purgatory Fire burned in her fingers, no Necrotic Black Wind twisted around her arm. She wanted to know how much it cost her, in the realm’s terms, to cut flesh with her own arm. Her twenty-three mana points stayed where they were.

A roar shook needles down from the canopy.

A black bear, five or six metres tall, broke from between two trunks and charged straight at her. With each stride the beast took, the ground jumped beneath her boots. The moss shivered with its passage. The bear came on like a loaded cart that had lost its brakes going downhill, and a word from Jing Sheng’s world floated to the surface of her mind, unbidden:

*Tank.*

[Source: none (beast). Mass: est. 2 t, by sight. Charging. 40 m.]

The numbers locked onto the bear’s chest, beginning to drop.

> Feet. Right heel in.

Tally’s line replayed Mr. Jack’s correction from the convoy road, calling out a finger’s width at the right heel. Maureen complied, moving her heel over exactly that much.

Her body fell into the rhythm of the Silver Light. Three long breaths in, one short out. Beneath that rhythm, she felt something she hadn’t felt in a very long time.

Simple happiness. Clean pleasure, at the prospect of a problem she could solve with her own hands.

At the bear’s last stride, Maureen stepped half a pace to the left and sliced once. The slender blade bit deep, opening the beast from its right shoulder down across its chest in a single stroke.

Blood sprayed from the wound, fountaining up in a broad arc. The bear swayed for a moment, then collapsed heavily into the moss. The shock of its fall travelled up through the soles of Maureen’s feet.

The blood steamed in the cool air, filling her nostrils with the sharp tang of iron. She flicked her wrist once, and the red fluid slid away from the black-lit steel, leaving the blade sparkling clean. She almost laughed aloud at the ease of it.

[Realm black bear: ~Middle Knight. By comparison: Jess, Bram, Marla samples; Room 21 constructs.]

*Outside, a bear is a bear,* Tally said.

*Not here.*

[Realm fauna: vs outside.]

*If an ordinary bear was Middle Knight level,* Tally said, *then the things that weren’t ordinary would need their own scale. Start building one?* *Yes,* Maureen told her silently. *Do that.*

*On it.*

Alone beneath the pillar trees, Maureen spoke aloud, using Richard’s low voice. It was amusing to hear. “If even a black bear can come out like this . . . it really is the Ancient Secret Realm.”

She wiped the last smear of red from the blade’s edge and slid the sword back into its scabbard. Farther in among the trees, she saw something large shift between the trunks, then fall still. She turned toward it and walked deeper into the trees.
