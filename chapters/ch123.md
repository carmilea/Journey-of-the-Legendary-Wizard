Maureen adjusted her right heel back, just a finger width, as Mr. Jack had corrected her once. Then she kicked.

The door crashed down flat onto the flagstones inside, the sound rolling through the racks like thunder. Five white-robed apprentices scuttled backwards, hands half-raised to cast.

Behind the lacquer mask, her own breath was warm and close. Dust from the hinges floated in the glyphlight, mixing with the smell of wax seals and dried roots. Underneath, she could smell the fear of the apprentices. Young, sour sweat.

Maureen stepped over the fallen door. “Out,” she said, her Night Owl voice a borrowed throat gone rough from use. “Now.” Most of the apprentices took the advice, shoving each other out a side archway into the night. Three remained. One reached hesitantly for a signal stone on his belt, his eyes darting from Maureen to the rows of racks he’d been ordered to defend, as if his obedience could still save him.

Maureen raised a palm, and Necrotic Black Wind left it. The spell rose like a murky whirlwind of black, swallowing the three apprentices whole. Their robes were stripped away first, followed by their flesh and bones, and their screams were muffled inside the howling of the spell. When the wind fell, a bone dust rattled dryly onto the stone floor, forming grey drifts around the base of the wooden racks.

Maureen let her breath out slowly. The spell had been pleasingly effective. At the same time, something beneath her ribs flinched at how easy it had been.

[Necrotic Black Wind. Formal spell. Built on Dark Wind Ring + Official template. Subjects: negative-energy principles; energy-molecule relations; curved particle diffusion in natural air. Est. 16°, scaled from Room 21 readings + Totem +1.2°. Cost ~3.5.] [Mana: ~11 → ~7.5]

Tally put up the readout she’d been holding in reserve since Maureen engraved the model back in the curtained carriage.

*Estimated,* Maureen thought back silently.

*And I will continue to estimate until someone builds us a testing room out here,* Tally responded. *Preferably with that in writing somewhere.*

Tally also confessed that she’d been keeping a name ready for the new spell. *Coronal Necrotic Vortex Synthesis.*

*No,* Maureen replied silently.

*That makes three now,* Tally said in Maureen’s head. *Basic. Mana. And this one.*

*I’ve grown,* Tally said.

*You’re still sulking.*

Maureen shook out a large black cloth sack, the weave rough against her fingers, and began sweeping the contents of the racks into it. Bundles of roots, jars with stoppered mouths, packets of powder... anything that glowed with preservation glyphs. She held the mouth of the sack over the void pocket at her waist, so that everything she swept in dropped directly through the bag and into the pocket beyond.

Her hands moved quickly and efficiently, her face expressionless behind the mask. Glass clinked against glass, the preserved items still cold from their storage, and dried stems crackled as they were swept into the bag. No matter how much went in, the sack hung slack and empty from her hands. It was a thief’s trick: no one would think to wonder why the bag never filled up.

Tally kept track of every item that entered the pocket, itemising them in the [Bag (Pouch): +materials 31]. If someone did look into the bag, they’d see a greedy apprentice with a sack full of supplies. The pocket was theirs alone.

>Bat. Shadow form. Door.

The word snapped out like a whip, as cold as the stone floor beneath Maureen’s feet. Tally had Bat’s density through the wall before anything appeared in the doorway. His spell was fast, but not as fast as Tally’s particle sense.

The faint glyph-light on the nearest row of racks flickered as a smear of shadow flowed over the fallen door. A draught of air blew in from outside, like a cellar door being thrown open. The dust from the dead apprentices scuttled away from the advancing darkness. The shadow stood up, becoming a man in a bat’s mask. His voice-masking spell cracked slightly under the force of his anger.

“Stop! Damn it, Owl, what do you think you’re doing?”

“Shopping.” Maureen didn’t pause her thieving.

“We came for a gem, not—”

“The job’s the job,” Maureen said. “The rest is the rest. I’d bet Nightingale and Black Bear have their hands in someone’s drawers right now too.”

Tally analysed Bat for her, reading him through the particle sense. Bat’s arms were held exactly where his spells would form, and they weren’t moving at all. The contract’s no-attack clause was the only thing stopping him. Maureen already knew how long that would last.

Maureen tied off the sack and finally looked over to where Bat stood. “And don’t forget, Bat. Tonight you’re on a job. Tonight you don’t belong to the White Tower.”

Every line of Bat’s body went rigid.

*That was cruel,* Tally said.

*It was accurate.* Maureen responded silently.

*Both columns.*

“Very good, Owl,” Bat finally said, his voice so low that it almost didn’t get through his voice-masking enchantment. “Very good.”

Bat folded back into shadow and disappeared through the door. Maureen didn’t watch him go. Tally stored his density and the cadence behind the disguised voice into a new file.

*Bat, White Tower, likely.*

*I might know him again if he walked past without a mask,* Tally said. *Maybe.*

There was a cold iron-bound chest sitting behind the final row of racks. Inside were four low-grade magic stone cards, each bearing the stamp of a white tower. Tally logged [+cards 4] and flagged the stamp as a possible trail.

Maureen dropped the cards through the top of the sack and headed back out into the shouting.

* * *

Across the lawn was a classical hall, elegant in the simplicity of its lines. Built from some pale stone, it was a perfect match for the rest of the base’s architecture. Maureen read the word carved into the lintel of the entranceway and stopped dead.

A library.

Behind her mask, her eyes widened.

After the storehouse, this was like stepping into a completely different night. Fluted columns stood tall and straight beside her, and the single step up had been worn smooth by years of traffic. Lamplight shone yellow from tall, thin windows set high in the walls, and even the alarm bells seemed more muted here, as though a flake or two of snow had fallen on them.

The two apprentices at the doors only had time to raise their hands before she was on them. They never got to finish the syllables of a spell before she broke them with a knight’s bare hands, not expending a single drop of mana.

Inside, shelves of books climbed in tiers, their gilt spines glinting in the light. The ceiling above was painted in an arcane scene, and the ward-light shone soft upon polished wooden boards. The air was thick and heady with the smells of old vellum, burning lamp oil and beeswax polish. The sounds of battle outside were muffled, swallowed into the silence of the library.

In the centre of the room was a lectern, and resting atop it was a thick book bound in silver. Maureen approached, and brushed it with a thread of her spirit power. It was cold to the touch of her mind, like a coin left out in the frost.

First, Tally ran the books-that-eat check. There was no stirring within the pages, no smoke or whisper rising from the binding. Just a preservation ward.

*You’re sure?* Maureen asked silently.

As sure as anyone could be without opening a book.

Tally paused, then began to paint the hall for her. Amber tags appeared over the shelves, rank ordered by halo and spine title. First came the advanced subjects, then pharmacology for the Purple Wood Snake’s tears, then alchemy.

[Reinforcements: est. 38 min, by distance + mount speed. Confidence: low.]

A clock materialised in the corner of Maureen’s sight. Thirty-eight minutes until more troops arrived. That was cutting it close, but it might just be enough time. Tally’s assessment of low confidence was honest. The base was big, and there were a lot of moving parts.

*Record all of it,* Maureen told her silently.

*One spread per glance is still the rule. I’m faster than I was, you’re not. Riffle.*

Maureen riffled through the volumes. Thumb, flick, thumb, flick.

The pages whispered by, their gilt edges warming under her glove. She could feel them turning, smell the ancient vellum. With every spread she turned, a pale blue flicker appeared in the back of her mind as Tally filed away the information.

The shelf counter clicked over to amber numbers. [Spreads: 1,240], then [Spreads: 1,241]. It passed three thousand, and Tally helpfully began to tick off the minutes without being asked.

Maureen’s eyes began to burn. There was grit between her eyelids, from years of dust embedded in the vellum. Her thumb cramped at the joint. Somewhere beyond the base’s walls, another spell cracked, closer than before.

When the lines on the page started to run together, Tally snapped, *Eyes!*

Maureen blinked twice, hard, and the page jumped back into focus.

She finished with the advanced subjects bay by bay, then moved on to pharmacology. Plate after plate of creatures and plants flipped past. She didn’t pause to study any of them, but Tally filed each one away.

Sweat pooled beneath the edge of her mask. The last hour before the lab locked down at Jing Sheng’s bench had been bad enough, but this was worse. At least then she’d been allowed to come back in the morning.

[Reinforcements: est. 6 min.]

Tally wanted her to move on to the alchemy shelves next.

*No,* Maureen told her silently. Tally let her.

“Owl.” A blurred woman’s voice came out of the darkness between the stacks. “Black Bear has the gem. We’re done.”

Nightingale stepped out of the shadows into the soft ward-light. Her nightingale mask was tilted slightly. “Their wizard got word out before he died. Reinforcements are coming.”

Maureen turned two more spreads. “One minute,” she said.

“You don’t have one,” Nightingale replied flatly, not moving from where she stood.

[Spreads: ~11,800. Coverage: advanced stacks ~85%, rest ~30%.]

*Leave the rest,* Tally said. *Some of it’s in me now. Some of it will burn.*

*I hate that,* Maureen answered silently.

*I know,* Tally said. *I’m keeping a list of the spines we didn’t open.*

Maureen ran her glove along the row of unopened books, feeling the raised gilt lettering beneath the soft leather. For a moment she could see Jing Sheng’s notebooks lying on the white bench, never to be read by human eyes again. She backed up to the doorway, and small deep-red Blast Fireballs blossomed one after another in her palm. She lobbed them into the tiers.

The windows blew outward, showering glass over the lawn. The gilt lettering curled and blackened, while the paper itself began to rise in a storm of glowing flakes, like sparks thrown off a forge. Heat rolled out of the hall, beating against her mask. The air filled with the stink of burning vellum.

[Mana: ~7.5 → ~3.5.]

*Now nobody knows what we read,* Tally said. *Only what we took.*

*Like Void Eclipse,* Maureen said silently.

*Like everything that matters,* Tally said.

The roof sagged and crashed down behind them in a thunderous roar of sparks. “Thorough,” was all Nightingale said before turning and leading Maureen away across the darkened lawn toward the wall.

* * *

At the base’s outer wall, Black Bear counted heads—all six present—and the team slipped back the way it had come. By the time the White Tower’s riders reached the gates, they were back in the tavern’s back room.

The room smelled of old ale, gone sour and soaked into the floorboards, mixed with the stink of tallow from the single lamp. Its light guttered, throwing the room’s smoke-blackened beams into stark relief, casting the masks of the team huge across the ceiling. A bear, a bat, a crow, a fox, a nightingale, and an owl.

Black Bear pulled out the pay and placed it on the table—a pouch of stones for each of them, plus a bolt of Wolf-Den Spider Silk, folded into a square. Then he laughed, a deep laugh rough as a millstone grinding grain. “Good night’s work. Nobody dead who mattered.”

Tally weighed the pouch by the sudden drop in Maureen’s hand. Tally tallied the contents silently. *On the silk,* she reported, *I have no plate on file. New material, no data.*

[Magic stones: +~300. Wolf-Den Spider Silk: 1 bolt.]

Bat took his share without looking at anyone but her. Throughout the evening, his mask never turned away from Owl.

Tally began counting again, keeping track of the contract clause’s clock, holding it up beside him in Maureen’s vision. *Let him count,* Maureen said silently.

*That’s my job,* Tally said. *I’m better at it than he is.*

They didn’t make speeches before splitting up. One of the doors banged open, letting in a rush of cold air that smelled like rain. Then the other masks began to file out, one by one, into the dark lanes outside.

* * *

Maureen didn’t go straight home. She looped around the city, taking three wide circuits around the streets and doubling back along canal walls a couple times just to make sure. Once, she cut through an empty market, all its wooden stands shuttered against the night. At each turn, Tally swept all around her with the particle sense.

[Followers: 0 within ~300 m. Rooftops: 0.]

Two streets later, Maureen asked again, and Tally dutifully reported the same thing without complaint.

The cobbles shone with water under the pale ward-light of the wizards’ tract. Somewhere in the distance, a milk cart creaked down the lane. Closer by, the bobbing light of a watchman’s lantern swung into view at the end of the street before turning away.

In a narrow alley, Maureen lifted off the lacquer mask and let it drop into the void pocket. She felt the magic of Mimicry unmake her face and reform it. Night Owl’s broad, heavy-jawed features gave way to the weak jaw and mousy aspects of “Wizard Richard.” Her throat shifted as well, reshaping itself into his flat and low voice.

Ants crept beneath her skin as the bones of her face slid and settled into place. The smell of rain filled the alleyway, mixed with the less pleasant scent of horse dung. Her new mouth tasted strange.

[Mana: ~3.5 → ~3.1]

Tally checked Maureen’s eyes first. *Black,* she reported. *No crimson.*

The gate at 34 Connpa Street warmed to Maureen’s touch and swung open at the brand upon her palm. The household slept deeply as she climbed the stairs to her bedroom. She crossed her own ward, letting the magic of Mimicry peel away as she entered.

The lamp had burned low. The white velvet robe still hung where she’d left it, draped over the chair beside her bed. The quiet of her room pressed against her ears like held breath, in stark contrast to the burning library behind her.

Maureen pulled the pocket from beneath her robe. She drew out the contents and laid them on the rug in neat rows; roots, jars and powders, followed by the four stamped cards, the pay pouch and finally the bolt of silk.

Tally ran the sum for her. [Magic stones, all columns: ~9,900 → ~14,200.]

*And they are marked with the White Tower’s stamp,* Tally added. *Keep them off every counter in Emilia for now.*

Maureen sat back on her heels. A single blood-red token, a mask and one night’s work had earned her a fortune and a library. This Bloodcoat Assassins’ Guild was turning out to be surprisingly useful.

She rubbed her chin, smiling, then laughed softly, the sound strange in her own voice. “Codenames again. Owl now. Jack before that. Jack the Ripper.”

*Jack the Ripper is filed next to Mr. Jack,* Tally replied. *At your request.*

*He corrected my right heel,* Maureen responded silently.

*A finger’s width,* Tally said. *It is still listed under Basic Swordsmanship. You used it on that door tonight.*

Maureen’s throat tightened at the reminder. She remembered the big blond knight’s gruff voice, lecturing her about her feet while he stared down at them from his horse at the camp. She remembered the valley he’d never ridden out of.

She lay down on the rug and closed her eyes.

*I’ll keep the wards fed,* Tally said. *I’ll wake you if anything comes near.*

Maureen slept before the first fingers of dawn’s light touched the garden wall outside her window.
