Maureen felt the serpent’s body beneath her, felt the long sinuous coils of its blackness moving through space. Dark particles swirled around its flattened head, like smoke following a torch through the night. She felt the broken edges of the barrier fizzing past her as she soared. Eve was slumped at the rim of the ritual array, her face grey.

Eve had poured herself into the array.

There was nothing to stop the serpent. It plunged across the cavern, hurtling towards the purple heart suspended in the air above Freya’s coffin.

It was a body length away when the void shivered.

The purple heart pulsed, sending waves out from it that the eye could barely see. The whole cavern blurred, as if seen through the heat haze above Andrew’s forge.

Below the heart, Freya’s eyes snapped open. They were glowing purple. Her face was as still and emotionless as a carved mask. Her lips moved, forming strange syllables.

“A-mon-ro-si-ker...”

Tally added the chant to Maureen’s heard-incantations list—the one that began with Derrick’s Blast Fireball—and supplied some data. Secret-class, rare. Spells like this are never cheap. Through the Mirror Pupil lens Tally painted, Maureen saw six lines of purple power charging along Freya’s body before any of them moved.

[Charging. Purple. Six lines. Mirror.]

Purple beams ripped loose from Freya’s body and whistled across the cavern towards the serpent. The sound they made was like crossbow bolts splitting the air.

The serpent’s scarlet eyes flared as it looked down at the purple beams. Maureen opened Mirror Pupil.

[Crystallisation −2.5. Mana 11.8 → 9.3.]

The beams became veins of purple crystal in mid-flight, then dropped like stones to burst on the stone floor with the sound of dropping glass. The quivering void fell still. A skin of purple crystal formed over the purple heart, and the ache returned to sit behind Maureen’s eyes.

The serpent’s mouth opened wide, a black abyss, and snapped shut around the crystal-sealed heart.

A loud, wet gulp reverberated through the cavern, echoing off the stalactites. There was a bulge in the serpent’s shadow throat that began to make its way slowly downwards before flattening out again. Eve, on her knees before the serpent, watched every inch of the bulge’s journey.

The heart dropped down from the phantom into Maureen’s own chest, pulled by the Minai Totem’s absorption. It settled in beside the Darklight Orb, just beneath her breastbone, the eye’s faint pale light shining above it. She felt the heart hit bottom, like a stone dropped down a dry well.

[Totem: drawing.] [Heart: in.]

Then Tally’s display began to jitter, as though something was causing static. It was the first time it had done so since the rite, and it took a moment for the lines to stabilise on a new line.

[Digesting: Void Mirage heart.]

*You all right, Tally?* Maureen thought silently.

*It’s a bit crowded in here,* Tally said. *Something very big just moved in next door to the Orb. I’m fine. Mostly.*

*Mostly?* Maureen asked.

*Disputed,* said Tally.

“NO—! NO!” Eve screamed. It was a raw sound, devoid of courtly modulations or melodious tones. Her hands clawed at the air where the heart had been suspended.

For a long moment, the cavern fell silent, then the ritual array began to tear itself apart. Violet lines snapped like overstrung wire, throwing off sprays of sparks. Raw energy ripped loose from the array, rampaging through the cavern.

The roof came down. Stalactites, glowing at their tips, cracked free from the ceiling and fell like lamps dropped from a great height. Boulders crashed down among the dark elves, crushing chanters in the middle of their song. Wounded elves shrieked in pain as they were hit by falling rock, and the sweet pale vapour from the Spring of Life abruptly became acrid with the scent of pulverised stone.

Maureen let go of the phantom and fell back into her own body. She floated down through the dust-clouded air until her feet touched the stone floor, then her knees softened, absorbing the impact.

She placed a hand on her throat. There was a feeling of unease in her chest, as though she had just eaten a meal that was too large, but she could feel power humming through her limbs. It would take some time to digest this, she knew.

In the transparent coffin, Freya was pushing herself up onto shaking arms. Her face was deathly white, her breathing shallow. The purple glow had vanished from her eyes. As her gaze focused on Maureen standing in the dust, hatred twisted her features.

The coffin glass misted with Freya’s breath. Beneath the dust there was another smell, the scent of fallen blossoms, the scent of the fern clearing, and Maureen’s pulse didn’t move.

“You,” Freya whispered, blood flaking at the corners of her mouth as she spoke. “The human who wore my blood.”

*She knows you by your grain, not by Richard’s appearance,* Tally said. *Your blood was bound into the shade, which was formed from hers. Her blood remembers it. Not by name though. No one in this cave has that.*

Maureen walked towards the coffin. “The last time I killed you was in the secret realm. This time your plan failed because of me, and still you returned. So, Freya. Die once more.”

Shock turned to panic on Freya’s face. Maureen snapped her fingers and a pale, sharp-tipped claw reached out from the void. It shot across the distance between them and impaled Freya through the chest, front to back. [Pale Hand −1.2. Mana 9.3 → 8.1.]

Freya stared down at the dripping claw protruding from her chest. Her lips moved but no word formed and she fell backward into the coffin. There was a hollow knock of bone striking glass.

*I think Purgatory Fire would be more appropriate than the Erosion Fireball,* Tally suggested. *No ash, no bones, nothing left for anyone to gather her a third time.*

*Good point,* Maureen thought silently.

Black-red flame poured over the coffin, consuming the body until there was nothing left. [Purgatory Fire −2. Mana 8.1 → 6.1.]

Maureen stood watching the conflagration, her arms folded across her chest. The heat washed over her but there was no warmth in it. She didn’t move until the flames had died away and the transparent glass held nothing, not even a blackened smear.

*Twice dead,* Maureen thought to Tally.

*Logged,* her companion replied, *and not coming back. At least, not according to my data.*

* * *

The cavern was a different place now. The platform was split into two sections, the circle of chanters a ring of rubble. The pool still breathed, rising and falling under its film of grit as rock roared down behind her. Edward was where he had fallen on her signal, amid the bodies of the hires. Richard’s illusionary face stared sightlessly at the roof above, his left flank scorched black by Jackman’s lightning.

Seeing her own borrowed face lying amongst the dead made her skin crawl, but she placed her hand flat against Edward’s breastplate and felt the core’s heat through the veilsilver, slow like an animal’s rest. She opened the Void Dimensional Pouch at her waist, and folded him inside.

[Pouch transit: tolerated, 4 tests.]

[Core: Intact]

[Damage: left flank. Lattice scored ~12% (est.).]

Tally said she wanted to give it a bench and a week.

She stood at the edge of the pool and opened the pouch again. Drawing upon the Spring with her spirit, she bade the water rise to meet her. A rope of the milky water rose from the surface, twisting on itself, and spun back towards her, vanishing into the open bag. The cold spray from the water misted her face, sweet against the acrid dust in the air. The water flowed into the pouch without a sound. At her back, the larger rocks were crashing down like a rain of boulders.

*I can’t see what’s in there,* Tally complained.

Maureen watched the waterline as it sank down the wet rock.

[Pool: −1.8 m³ est. by sight.]

[Pouch: Spring water, est. 1.8 m³.]

*If that thing leaks, I’m never forgiving it,* Tally added.

Maureen climbed the cracked platform to where Denise lay, just as the ritual had left her. [Denise: wrist, left, still seeping.]

Maureen knelt over Denise’s still form and dusted some hemostatic powder from her belt kit on the wound. She ripped off a strip of her hem and bound it around the girl’s wrist.

[Denise: pulse 58, rising.]

The Oserroba woman weighed almost nothing as Maureen hefted her over a shoulder. A mass of golden brown hair hung down Maureen’s back, the scent of blossom mixing with the smell of sweat. Maureen rested her fingers on the makeshift bandage for a moment, feeling for dampness.

*She’ll live if you keep walking,* Tally noted. *Nightingale’s sealed spreads will open when the job is done.*

*Both columns.*

*That was my line.*

Tally opened up [Witnesses to serpent: Eve (alive, last seen), elves: est. 9 alive.] and updated Eve’s status to show her density slipping away down a side passage.

*Let her run.*

*She won’t be able to describe you. She saw a black serpent, not a face.*

Tally added another line to the dark elves’ recognition list. Grain of the shade.

*Why?*

*They heard you say “secret realm” and “killed you.”*

*Fine, let it stand.*

“This much noise will bring the whole settlement,” Maureen murmured to no one in particular, then made her exit through the sloping passage before the roof finished coming down.

* * *

The rumbling roar of the cavern’s collapse became more muted behind them as they raced along the passage. Maureen’s boots rang on the wet stone floor, and Denise’s shallow breath was like a tiny spot of warmth against her back.

Tally gave the location of the source ahead first, by grain.

*Jackman. Density low. Uneven. Injured. He survived Eve’s beam.*

*Then he can try to survive me,* Maureen said silently.

*Medium-high he can’t,* Tally pointed out.

They rounded a bend in the sloping tunnel and found the old black wizard huddled against the rock wall. His robes hung about him in ragged tatters. One hand braced against the stone, he dragged himself upward along the slope, taking it one step at a time.

The smell of long-smoked pipes and soured wine rolled off him like fog. He coughed once, a wet cough that brought a spray of blood from his mouth, spattering his silver beard. The sound of his breathing was like a cracked bellows.

He raised his head and Maureen saw his green eyes widen with what might have been relief. “Ah! It is you, Wizard Richard. You—”

Maureen didn’t bother to respond. A Dark Erosion Fireball bloomed from her palm, a purple-black orb that slammed into Jackman, driving him down the tunnel with a stink of vinegar and scorched stone. [Mana 6.1 → 2.7.]

Lying on his back, his chest heaving with painful breaths, he coughed up another glob of blood. “Richard... you... you—”

“Die,” Maureen said.

A huge, pale claw formed out of the darkness before the wizard’s eyes. He flinched away and raised a hand to ward off the attack. “No! Wizard Richard! The Black Wizard Alliance has an oath with that lord! Kill me and you’ve offended that lord! Richard—”

*That lord,* Tally logged. *No further data available on that lord. Jackman, member of the Black Wizard Alliance, the coalition Frans had offered her a seat in.*

*Let the lord remain a blank.*

The Pale Hand closed over Jackman, and he was gone. [Mana 2.7 → 1.5.]

She turned her back on him, then stopped.

Something there. Hidden within the corpse. She felt it like a familiar shadow, just out of reach. It was energy she recognized and yet didn’t. Her spirit perception went down into the body and began searching.

A bead of light leaped from the corpse, dark red, straight for her chest. As soon as she saw it, her hand came up, wrapped in a crackling flame of dark particles, and caught it. [Source: dark-red.] [Purgatory.] [Match: Emilia density knots / Oran Olivia.] [Purgatory note.] [Bloodline: active.] [Totem: drawing.]

Craving burned through Maureen, rising from her core like hunger pangs after the ship. Heat flashed behind her eyes. Scorched honey coated her tongue. The bead pulsed against its cage of black flame.

*Ten,* Tally warned, her voice cold.

*Nine.*

*Eight.*

Maureen narrowed her eyes and opened her palm. The countdown stopped.

The bead sank into her palm and was gone.

A long sigh escaped her lips, and a shudder ran the length of her spine. Her eyes half closed, and her head tilted back, resting against the rough stone wall of the tunnel. Her blood seethed in her veins, like a pot left on the boil.

“Such pure power,” she murmured into the darkness that surrounded her, alone except for Denise’s unconscious form slung over her shoulder. “The Purgatory World. My blood is boiling.”

*That wasn’t you sighing.*

*What?*

*That was the Totem.*

*It felt like me.*

*I know. That’s what worries me.*

Oran Olivia’s file shifted from low to medium priority.

*Correlation. Not a name.*

Maureen hitched Denise higher onto her shoulder and began walking again, moving as quickly as she could manage. Far faster than the slope of the tunnel strictly required. She didn’t look back at the body lying behind her.

* * *

Behind her, the settlement began to stir.

Running feet and a man shouting in Ancient Andas sounded from the corridor. A pale lamp flared into life in a side passage ahead.

*Purgatory Fire?*

*No. Not enough. You’re up to knight strength. One fireball. Then nothing.*

*Agreed.*

Two dark elves cut into the corridor from a side passage. Maureen grabbed the first one by the neck, broke it with a single twist of her hand, then flung him aside. The second one tried to leap back out of reach, but Maureen caught him by the throat and pinned him against the wall etched with silver designs. She tightened her grip until there was a snap. Denise never budged on her shoulder.

The sound of shattering bone seemed small in the stone corridor. The dark purple blood smelled sweetly of fallen blossoms and the low lamps flickered.

At the gate carved from living rock, Maureen turned to face a knot of guards who had begun turning in her direction. A single fireball, deep red, scattered them like leaves before an autumn wind.

[Mana 1.5 → 0.4]

*That’s your floor,* Tally said. *Walk.*

*Richard’s face is holding,* Tally told her as she walked toward the open gate. *The left cheek is a hair late though.*

*I feel it,* Maureen answered silently, reaching up and pushing the errant bit of skin back into place with two fingers. *No need to recast, not yet.*

Maureen walked out of the gate into the starless black sky of the Underworld. The night was absolute. In the distance, the black brush and grass that dotted the landscape gleamed with the light of coals smothered under ash. The air was cold, and tasted of slate left out in the rain. For her eyes, night was no darker than twilight and she and Denise vanished into it without a trace.
