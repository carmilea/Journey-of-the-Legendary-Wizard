The old wizard from the hall was already seated when Maureen walked in. He was sitting slightly apart from Claude, still wearing the same expression of knowing amusement, as if he had held it here just for her since she left. Then Maureen felt it. She couldn’t see anything wrong on the outside, but beneath that ancient exterior was a faint aura. She recognized it now from her trips through the corridors of the Dark Forest. She’d smelled it on the road wanderers she’d killed and on her own skin.

This was no ordinary wizard. This was a black wizard.

Tally rolled out a grain-match over him.

[Grain: black-wizard. Match type: Dark Forest, ship, road wanderers.]

*Same as mine,* Maureen told her silently.

*Yes, same family,* Tally answered. *Not the same animal though.*

Denise hurried over from where she had been standing by the window. As Maureen approached, relief spread across Denise’s face like sunrise.

“Denise,” Maureen said in Richard’s flat voice. She took the seat next to the young woman.

Denise sat down close to Maureen, closer than even manners required. Her knee was almost touching Maureen’s chair. It wasn’t hard for Maureen to read the message. Until this moment, the Oserroba heir had been alone in her house with two Liquefied wizards. She’d been holding her breath the entire time.

Claude lounged back in his chair, letting a lazy smile spread across his face. “Well, Denise, I’d have to say that Wizard Richard here isn’t just any friend of yours.” The words were directed at Denise, but his eyes never left Richard’s face.

*Pulse steady,* Tally noted. *Only one side of his mouth smiling. Trying to make you blush, Maureen.*

*Richard doesn’t blush,* Maureen answered silently.

*Ah, yes. Richard is doing beautifully.*

The corners of Maureen’s mouth twitched upwards ever so slightly. “Wizard Claude.”

Maureen gave him the title and nothing else. His smile waited for a moment, expecting more. Then it gave up.

Claude tapped his forehead with a finger, as if he’d just remembered something. “Forgive me, I nearly forgot. My friend, Jackman.” He let out a light laugh. “He’s Liquefied, you know.”

The laugh carried the boast better than the words did.

Jackman spoke at last. “Wizard Richard.”

The sound was hoarse, rusted and scraping. Like a door that no one had opened in years. *These vocal cords are under-used,* Tally said. *Months by the rasp there, more if we believe the pitch.* [Jackman. Liquefied, by Claude’s word. Label: hearsay.] Tally filed him away, placing him beside Claude. He would serve as her second sample of Liquefied ability.

Maureen nodded. Her face did not change expression. Inside, the sum was cold. Liquefied strength, and a black wizard’s habit of killing layered on top of it. Her hands rested loosely on the arm of her chair only because she ordered them to.

Denise and Claude moved on to family business. An account from the granary, who would be authorized to appoint a new under-steward, which family seal would go on a deed to Oserroba land. Small matters, read off one by one from the stacks of family documents sealed with red wax.

Beneath every small matter was the real argument. Which hand would scrawl across the bottom of the page? Whose signet would be pressed into the wax? Which name would the servants answer to when they came to work in the morning?

Skran’s ledger, but with better candles.

Tally kept count. Claude had interrupted Denise four times. She had interrupted him none. With every item discussed, his seal sat a little closer to the document.

The entire time, Jackman’s sunken green eyes never left Richard’s face. They watched with interest and amusement, but never hostility. Maureen sat quietly, giving back nothing. Like a well that had no bottom, her stillness was deep and unreadable.

[Gaze on you: 41 min.]

[Blink rate low.]

*At medium confidence,* Tally predicted, *he has marked you as a black wizard and is now deciding if that makes you useful or a nuisance.*

*Which do you think?* Maureen asked silently.

*Both, in his shoes. Until it isn’t.*

Denise set her cup down on its saucer so hard that the fine porcelain rang like a bell. “Last night someone crept into this manor. Into the Oserroba house itself. Wizards walking our halls as if it were a market square.”

Her voice climbed, rising in pitch and volume. Through the tall windows behind her head the garden was still frozen beneath its coat of frost, burnt and blackened where the fighting had been, pitted with the purple-black corrosion craters that Maureen had left there.

Claude shrugged and spread his hands wide. “Then we tighten the watch. Better still, every man who walked the patrol last night dies. All of them.”

He smiled, and there was something cold in his smile that the lamplight did not touch. “That should be enough, surely? Satisfied now, Denise?”

[Claude: pulse 70, unchanged. Denise: 118. Hands: shaking.]

*He means it. By every tell I have on him, he means it. But that’s not proof,* Tally said.

Something old and cold turned over in Maureen’s gut. The hollow sound of the Duke’s knights’ boots coming down on Angrey after her silent nod. Power that could turn people into furniture to be moved or broken at will. Her jaw clenched.

*Jaw clench noted,* Tally observed.

*The guards will die for a lie.*

*Possibly.*

*To him they’re the cheapest thing in this room.*

Denise trembled, and Maureen saw it on her face. If she refused Claude’s suggestion then she admitted that she could not protect even her own house. If she accepted, then the blood that was shed would be on her name. Beneath both was a deep well of helplessness that she could not disguise.

The meeting broke up shortly after that. As Jackman followed Claude out of the room, the old wizard glanced back over his shoulder once, his sunken green eyes fixing on “Richard”. Denise murmured a few words to Maureen as she left, too low to hear but with an undercurrent of thanks and apology. She walked out with her shoulders slouched down.

* * *

Maureen heard footsteps die away along the long gallery outside the meeting room. She was alone again amidst the portraits. Oserroba dead stared down at her from the walls, their eyes dark beneath layers of varnish. They had stood in this room arguing over the same seals.

The draught from the high windows ran cold fingers down the back of her neck. Why did it bother her so much? There had been other draughts, in other houses, when it had been just as cold outside. There was something about this one…

Claude. Then, Jackman’s rusty voice echoed in her head. A feeling rose in Maureen that she couldn’t give a name to, but it made her uncomfortable.

*Tally, is there any threat analysis for me?*

[Threat analysis. 1 Jackman: Liquefied, black wizard, unknown kit. 2 Claude: Liquefied, family authority. 3 Claude’s household.]

*Against Jackman only,* Tally said, at full mana [Mana: 20.1], *using Dark Erosion Fireball and the Orb, odds are favourable. Medium confidence. No sample available of how he fights.*

*Then we watch him fight first,* Maureen answered silently.

Claude was up to something, Clair had warned her at the Silver Soul estate’s gate. What was he plotting? Today Claude had sat at his niece’s table with a black wizard, and he had called him friend.

*Yes,* Tally agreed, *but last night Claude lost four men in the garden here. Today he replaced them with a bigger one, but this time not to fight, just sit and watch. Watching is how you price things.*

Maureen looked up at a long-dead Oserroba, staring out of his gilt frame with stern eyes. The gilt was chipping off the corners of the portrait, peeling away from the old wood. One of the old Oserroba’s hands rested upon a book, its cover closed.

Outside the window, below her, the yard was coming to life. Maureen could see men beginning to change the watch. They wore the family livery, and stamped their feet in the frost and blew on their fingers as they waited for their replacements to arrive. No one had told them yet if they would live or die.

*Twelve,* Tally said. *Filing.*

*Why?* Maureen asked silently.

*Because someone should,* Tally answered.

Maureen’s breath fogged the window. She wiped it clear with her sleeve, and Richard’s face stared back at her from the glass.

*Check his eyes,* Maureen ordered.

*Black. No crimson,* Tally reported.

*I have no data on feelings,* Tally said. *Shall I log it as weather?*

*No. Log it as a draught,* Maureen said silently. She turned away from the window and walked back towards her guest room as the servants began to light the lamps along the long gallery behind her.

* * *

The days passed and still nothing came. Maureen had been certain that Claude would react more strongly to the failure in the gardens, that he would send more powerful wizards or maybe even try a second night. However the manor remained calm, as if nothing had ever happened.

The soft tread of the servants, the bell summoning them to meals, and the gradually increasing thickness of the frost on the window panes each morning marked the passing time in the manor. The house ran smoothly, efficiently and normally, everything as it should be.

Except that for Maureen this was the wrong kind of smooth.

Each morning Tally gave her the night’s report.

[Night: 2 passes, servant, 01:20; cat, 03:05. Ward drew 0.1. No sources approach.]

*Again?* Maureen asked silently.

*Again,* Tally answered. *You slept like a stone.*

Maureen checked the ward anyway, three times, running her fingertips just above the silver lines etched into the door frame, feeling each node respond coolly beneath her skin.

*Three,* Tally said. *I was going to ask you to check it three times.*

Claude had stopped visiting Denise in her wing, and at meals he was courteous and distant. Jackman was nowhere to be seen.

On the third morning, Tally spoke. *Claude’s behaviour has changed. Given his silence since the fight in the garden, I predict with medium confidence that he has found a better plan than knives. The most dangerous kind of quiet,* she finished.

She talked to Denise several times over the next few days, strolling the frozen garden paths or sitting in an alcove of the library, and each time she felt Denise’s confusion and tension like a string drawn tight between them.

Before Denise spoke, she would exhale sharply, and touch her own sleeve with her hand. A woman braced for a blow that did not fall. Tally noted the behaviour, logging it as a “tell”. Each time Maureen mentioned Claude, Denise’s pulse rose.

*She feels it too.*

*She lives here. She feels it more.*

“He hasn’t even sent a servant to sneer at me,” Denise said, frowning at the frost creeping across the window panes. “In all the years I’ve known him, he’s never been this polite.”

At night, Maureen lay awake in the Silver Light rhythm, three long breaths followed by one short, as the brazier went out. Edward was stowed in the Void Pouch at her waist, silent and obedient. Her hand would wander to her inner pocket, feeling the empty space where the Seal used to be. The Seal that now beat as Edward’s core.

* * *

“Wizard Richard! Wizard Richard!”

Maureen’s door burst open, and the Oserroba heir ran in without knocking. She left the door wide open, so that cold air poured into the room from the corridor outside. Denise was out of breath, her face flushed from running. The cold air swirled around the brazier, stirring the embers that still glowed among the ashes. This was the first time Maureen had seen her without her armour of poise and self-possession.

“I know what they’re doing. Claude and the rest of them. I’ve found out.”

Denise paused for a moment to catch her breath, then continued in a rush.

“They’re going down to the Underworld to trade with the dark elves. They’ll complete some task for the elves, and in return the elves will allow them access to their Spring of Life. That’s why they haven’t bothered me. That’s what they’re after!”

Tally flashed up the words “dark elves” on Maureen’s display, next to two files: the Stone sold in the fern clearing, and the purple heart that Eve’s elves had carried in a crystal box past the gate.

*We sold them a key,* Maureen said silently to Tally.

*And now someone wants to buy the door,* Tally replied.

Maureen thought about the dark-elf woman at the muster, standing as still as a blade laid flat on the table. Holding the hand of the black-haired girl. The slender sword at her hip suddenly seemed heavier.

“The legend says that the Spring of Life brings the dying back as easily as lifting a cup,” Denise said. The words tumbled out of her mouth, rushed and eager. “If we have it... Father will get up. He’ll be well!”

Her voice broke on the word “Father”. Her hands nervously twisted the fabric of her sleeve, then fell away, only to twist it again a few seconds later. She laughed once, a strange sound that held no humour.

Tally spoke quietly in the back of Maureen’s head. *I have no data on this “Spring of Life”. There is nothing on it in Gregory’s library, and you haven’t turned over any shelves with information on it. All I have to work off is what Denise just told us. And what she said was legend.*

Maureen asked the one question that mattered. “Denise. Is it reliable?”

“It came from someone I placed beside Claude years ago. It can’t be wrong. It can’t.”

There was a finality to her tone, as if she had just slammed a door and bolted it shut. In that finality, Maureen heard an echo - the voice of someone who desperately needed something to be true.

Tally said, *A long-placed piece reports exactly what you most need, in the very week that your enemy goes quiet.*

*The board, again,* Maureen answered silently.

[Match: Gus board pattern. Exact fit = tell.]

*A trap. Medium confidence. Unknown target. Again, we don’t have proof.*

Maureen’s pulse didn’t rise even slightly as she considered that. Tally logged her heart rate at 64 beats per minute. Instead her eyes narrowed.

She’d seen an exact fit before. A job posted in an Emilian attic, with a reward that matched her own commission word for word.

“Is that so,” she said, rubbing her chin slowly. Another pause. “If so... that’s good.”

*You said that like a woman counting exits.*

*I am.*

Denise didn’t appear to notice Maureen’s reaction. Already she was talking about wagons and what they should pack. As she spoke, the frost on the glass behind her captured the grey morning light and held it fast.
