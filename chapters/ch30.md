Maureen Olsen held a test tube filled with bubbling, faintly purple liquid. All that remained was for her to add a solution of Phoenix Orchid Root to boost the activity of her Recovery Potion.

The burner ticked rhythmically, the candle burned with a steady flame, and the glossy green gel sat quietly in the main glass vessel.

Before Maureen could pour the contents of the test tube into the main vessel, the vessel gave a heavy thud and puffed out a cloud of white smoke. The gel within turned an inky pitch black. The stench of burnt hair mixed with the rotten smell of fruit filled the study. The candle’s flame began to gutter as Maureen stood frozen, with the heat from the failing potion flushing her face.

After a long moment, she gently set the test tube back down on the desk. “Failed,” she said to the empty room.

>Causes: [Spirit fluctuation. Melting point misjudged, group 1. Blending agent late, group 2.]

Tally noted in her display. More lines kept scrolling past below.

 *Make it shorter,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*You deviated,* Tally said warmly in her head.

*When?*

Stages two and four lit up on her display. Tally overlaid a simulation of what it predicted would happen if she had not made any mistakes. The dip in her spirit matched with her own meditation log at the exact moment when she had been watching the melt.

Maureen let the heat in her face subside while she sat in silence. Her jaw tightened, and she rested her hands palm down on the cold desk. There was still so much left to learn.

She scraped the black sludge out of the main vessel with a spatula, then scrubbed the glass clean with hot water. The black substance refused to come off, clinging to the container like tar. She eventually got the last bit out, and continued washing until the glass squeaked against her fingers. Then she started a second pack from the beginning. The burner ticked away as it heated. The second pack failed as well, turning black and smelly, with the same puff of white from the mouth of the main vessel.

*Don’t say anything,* Maureen told Tally silently.

Tally waited exactly as long as it took Maureen to scrub the second failure before responding.

*Stage four is improving,* Tally said in her head.

*I said nothing,* Maureen replied silently.

*That was nearly nothing,* Tally noted, just a hint of smugness in its tone.

[Recovery packs: 8/10] read Maureen’s display. She decided to stop for the day before her stubbornness cost her a third pack. She took a short break on her bed, then walked through the black stone corridors to Adrian’s lab.

* * *

Maureen bowed her head. Adrian gave her the Holan grubs, then gestured to the racks of glassware along the bench. “You know what to do, and don’t forget it’s your turn to clean the lab.” He paused, sniffing. “I smell violet-blue leaf and Phoenix Orchid Root on you. You’ve been trying the Recovery Potion?”

Maureen blinked, impressed he could identify them from across the bench. [Violet-blue leaf and Phoenix Orchid Root identified by scent.] Tally logged the information.

*Tally?* Maureen asked silently. [No data on nose] responded the chip.

Adrian’s apprentice Winnie put down the test tube she had been inspecting. “Did it work?” she asked.

Maureen shook her head. “No.”

Adrian’s top apprentice Mace smiled encouragingly at her. “It’s only your first time brewing it. It’s perfectly normal to fail the first time. If you’d like, I can explain some techniques and precautions later?”

“Yes, thank you,” said Maureen.

Mace’s voice was warm and quiet like Adrian’s, and he waited patiently for her reply without pushing. Behind him, his burner continued to tick away.

Maureen walked over to one of the side benches and pulled on a pair of gloves before beginning work. She was still under her agreement with Adrian, so she processed his materials first.

A glass dish full of grey-brown grubs writhing in slime sat in front of her. The smell of rotten eggs and sewage made her stomach turn.

*Maureen, would you like me to find a similar smell in my records?*

*No!*

*But I could help you.*

*I said no!*

*Fine. It will be filed under ‘smell Maureen refused to acknowledge’.*

Maureen grimaced and made a silent promise that once she became a master potion maker, she would never choose such disgusting ingredients. Mace and Winnie laughed good-naturedly when they saw her face. The laughter helped relieve some of the tension in her shoulders. Adrian pushed up his glasses and hid a smile behind his hand. For a moment, it felt less like a laboratory and more like a household.

When Maureen finished her work with the grubs, Adrian came over to her bench while Mace and Winnie worked on the other racks far away. “I know that you’ve only recently become First-Class,” Adrian said in a low voice. “You do not yet know enough.”

Maureen touched the bridge of her nose but said nothing.

>He lowered his voice, Tally noted. Neither Mace nor Winnie turned around to look at them.

Adrian gave her some guidance on her cultivation.

While Mace explained the techniques involved in brewing the Recovery Potion, Tally recorded every word he said. It even asked her to have him repeat the cooling rates. She asked a question, and Tally responded with a smug ‘thank you’.

* * *

Lilith appeared at Maureen’s side the moment she walked out of the lab.

“Hard at work already, huh?” Lilith said with bright eyes as they headed down the corridor. “You’ve been here for barely two months and you’re already brewing.”

“It’s really interesting.”

Lilith looked thoughtful. “Well… I guess it has to be if you’re going to make a career out of it, right? Come to the gathering with me.”

“Hm?”

Lilith rocked on her heels, grinning like someone who had just won a bet. She placed her hands behind her back and stared up at the ceiling.

Maureen frowned. “What gathering?”

“Shhh,” Lilith said, leaning in close to Maureen and speaking in a low volume. “The Potion Apprentices’ Gathering. There are several mentors who teach pharmacology, but you can’t ask them everything, know what I mean? Pharmacology isn’t something that can be learned behind closed doors. You have to trade and exchange with others!”

“When is this gathering?”

“Now,” Lilith said, pointing down the corridor. “Come on!”

*So that explains half of Lilith’s parties,* Tally said in Maureen’s head.

*Log that as unconfirmed.* Maureen responded silently with her own thoughts.

They walked for a long time through the black stone corridors, making several turns before they arrived at a remote hall. The hall was crammed with apprentices, all of whom had the characteristic scent of mixed reagents. A great number of stalls were set up, each displaying an assortment of jars and dried bits and pieces. The hall was alive with the sound of haggling.

Only potion apprentices ever came here. Apprentices of other crafts had no use for pharmacology, and those who wanted finished potions would visit the trading district instead.

Lilith pointed out the main feature of the hall: a great grey cloth that hung from the ceiling in the center of the room. Writing crawled across its surface, composed of particles of energy. “If you have a question about pharmacology, you post it up there and pay however many stones you want for answers. Other people can answer your question and get paid, or you can answer other people’s questions to make some stones.”

The stalls were already busy as apprentices argued over the price of dried roots and jars of pickled things. Voices overlapped into an indecipherable chatter.

Lilith ran off to buy something from one of the stalls, leaving Maureen standing in front of the board. Lines of writing crawled across the grey cloth, formed by faint particles of energy. They tickled at Maureen’s particle sense.

Tally captured the board spread by spread. On Preserving the Physiological Activity of Blue-Blood Frogs at Zero Degrees. Three Methods of Processing Skeck Beetles and Conjectures on the Use of Their Juice. Tally highlighted three posts that weren’t in the library yet. *If only I had come here sooner,* Maureen thought silently.

Maureen sent her own questions to the board with simple wizard energy particles. Then she tried answering a few other people’s questions. Some were about common knowledge, while others were strangely phrased and difficult to solve.

*Two of these read like attempts to harvest a working method,* Tally said.

*Good.* Maureen answered all the safe questions, then moved on. The two that Tally flagged as suspicious she skipped over.

[Magic stones: 100 → 103]

*That was the first stone you earned with your head,* Tally said. The board was now filed under Pharmacology.

* * *

Maureen briefly glanced at some materials from another stall, before turning away.

“Wait, are you sure you don’t want anything here? Nothing good enough for you, friend?” the stall holder called out as Maureen began to walk off.

Maureen turned back, and the young man with the curly blond hair smiled at her. “I’m George.”

“Maureen Olsen.” Maureen nodded back.

George grinned happily at the introduction, then leaned forward over the table. His sleeves still held the scent of pickling brine.

“Have you ever heard of combined spells?” he asked.

“Combined?” Maureen echoed.

“Yeah, it’s when you cast different spells, but at the same time so they merge into something stronger,” George explained. “Anyway, I found this book in one of those ancient wizard ruins, and I think it has some stuff on combined spells.”

A thick old book was produced from beneath the table. The leather cover was cracked, and it had a thin layer of dust coating it.

Maureen skimmed through the pages. “This is all conjecture,” she finally said, “and it gets wilder as it goes along. The last part even needs a cipher to read.”

*This book only contains the spreads you have turned,* Tally said. It flagged the gaps for your review. There is no available data on the cipher’s key.

“Yeah, but these are conjectures from an Official Wizard,” George argued, “and they might be real.”

*The library holds no data relating to combined spells. None. I do not like that.*

Maureen’s fingers still rested on the cracked leather cover. “How much?”

“One advanced paid course.”

“A paid course on that subject is worth at least three hundred stones,” Maureen pointed out. “I do have a course I could trade you, Basic Principles of Pharmacology, but I’d rather hold onto it. I can get something better with it than this book.”

*I know,* Maureen told the chip silently.

*I know you know,* Tally replied smugly, *I’m saying it for the part of you that is tempted.*

“No thanks,” Maureen called back over her shoulder. “Can’t afford it, no money!”

She walked away from the potion apprentice and his stalls of jars, and away from the faint smell of pickling brine. Behind her she heard George call out. “Don’t go, friend!”

Further along the rows of stalls, there was a large gathering of apprentices. They had all packed close together, voices raised over one another as they shouted. The noise from the knot of apprentices rose above the general buzz of the hall.

Maureen had been planning to walk past the crowd, but Tally recognised a voice from the mass of sound before she did. *That’s Lilith. Raised.* She frowned and headed towards the shouting.
