The token lay on the bedspread, glowing a faint wet red, like a coal breathing beneath a thin layer of ash. Its pulse was steady, not hurried. Maureen sat on the edge of the bed in her white robe, watching the token for three pulses before she touched anything else.

[Token: message waiting.]

[Pulse: summons pattern.] Tally placed a small amber line at the edge of her sight. *That’s the same pattern Maffie gave in his notebook for broadcasting to every seat at once. Not a job, a broadcast.*

*You’re sure?*

*One source. Maffie’s.*

Maureen nodded. She let down one thread of her spirit and touched the token with it, as lightly as she would touch a sleeping animal with her fingertip. The token shivered against the thick cotton bedspread and began to pulse more quickly. Dark particles began to drift across the room, moving toward the space above the bed.

The candle’s flame leaned over sideways, drawn to the gathering mass of black particles, then stayed leaning at an angle. Just as Adrian’s projections had formed in the Dark Forest. A cellar chill began to creep along the floorboards, rising to engulf Maureen’s bare feet.

The particles of darkness knitted themselves into the shape of a man dressed head to foot in a grey robe. Only his pale chin showed. In his right hand, held up by the hair, dangled a human head.

Jorran raised the head up to eye level, just as Evans had lifted up Ciri’s head over the ship’s railing. There was a similar air of casualness about the gesture, like he was displaying a particularly fine catch.

“Wizards of the Bloodcoat Guild,” Jorran said. His voice was smooth and reasonable, but there was the promise of menace beneath it. “My name is Jorran. I speak for the Black Wizard Alliance. We have asked to look a little more closely at your guild. Your... figurehead... has said no.” He shook the head once, as if to emphasize the word.

*So.*

[Projection. Band reading: none. Tells: none.]

*A projection doesn’t give me anything to grade, Maureen,* Tally explained. *No density, no pulse, nothing. Just a face and a voice. I’ll file him as* [Jorran: Black Wizard Alliance envoy, by projection only]. *If he ever stands in the same room as us, I’ll take a real sample.*

Jorran continued to speak, his tone still pleasant. “We are not butchers. We welcome friends. Anyone adventurous enough to join a guild of knives desires quick resources, and the Alliance can provide those resources faster and better than any token ever could. Join the Alliance. Think about it.”

His voice began to thin out, then disappeared completely. The image of the man remained, holding up the head for every seat to see.

The dead face turned slowly on its hair, caught in the candlelight. The skin was grey, the jaw slack as it should be in death. There was a smear of dried blood where the neck had been torn away from the body.

Maureen looked at the face for a long time. Nothing moved in her expression.

Tally matched the face to Maffie’s notebook. She had recorded it spread by spread when they were in the vault. Shaved skull, nose broken twice and set crooked, mottling of left jaw due to old burn. [Match: guildmaster, by Maffie description. One source. Confidence: medium.]

*I thought so,* said Maureen silently.

The line from the notebook came back to her. He was merely a figurehead, with nothing to do with the running of things. Each member acted alone. However, he was one of the most powerful members of the guild, at the peak of Solid State, only a hair away from Second-Tier.

*Notebook hearsay. Strongest man on my whole guild page,* Tally added.

Tally pulled up three lines from her data, lining them up neatly. Frans offering her a seat in the Black Wizard Alliance, in the burned field. Jackman shouting that the Alliance had an oath with that lord. And now Jorran.

*Three mouths, one alliance,* said Tally.

*And they want a seat at their table for me,* Maureen answered silently.

*They want every seat there is,* Tally agreed.

Maureen let out a slow breath. Some of the guildmembers would go over to Jorran’s people. The rest would scatter to the winds. Without anyone having to say anything, the Bloodcoat Guild was finished. After tonight, only a madman would take a job off their token.

Her thumb rubbed against the edge of the token and black-red flame licked up at her fingertips. All that was required was a touch of Purgatory Fire to burn it away so completely that not even ash would be left behind. The heat touched the skin of her own hand.

*Wait!* said Tally quickly. *You can’t destroy it, Maureen. It’s a door, remember? If you burn it, you’ll lose your way through. You won’t be able to see Jorran’s next message, or whoever answers the roll from now on.*

Maureen held the flame at her fingertips for another long breath, then pulled it back into her hand.

*Thank you, Maureen,* said Tally.

*It was your argument. I just agreed with it,* Maureen answered silently.

That was the hard part. Agreeing with it. Tally logged that statement for future reference.

Maureen dropped the token back into the void pocket and drew the string tight. The projection had already dissipated into a mass of drifting motes. The candle’s flame straightened itself out again.

She lay back in bed without blowing out the light. She listened to the house. Ike’s slow step on the stairs, the cat hopping up onto the garden wall, frost ticking against the glass. *I’ll take the watch,* Tally said.

* * *

Ambrose stopped her before she had her coat off. “Richard.” One hand went up, as though to physically halt her passage down the corridor. “Wenger of the White Tower wants a word. He’s in my office.”

His voice was even, but his mouth was suddenly tight.

[Ambrose: pulse up. Jaw set.]

*He’s worried for you,* Tally said.

*Or worried for his department.*

*Both columns,* Maureen answered silently.

*So soon?*

*It has been eleven days since Tonk gave his warning.*

Ambrose’s office smelled as it always did, of camphor and old paper. But today there was an extra weight in the air, like the pressure that builds just before a storm breaks. None of the jars rattled in their places on the shelves, but somehow, it seemed like they should be rattling.

Wenger stood at the single narrow window, its frosted glass diffusing the light from outside. His robe was white and wide, his beard trimmed to a neat point, his hair combed flat against his head. He turned at the sound of Maureen’s entrance. His face was as still as the water of a pond. The anger that rolled off him was like the heat radiating out from an open forge.

Tally’s amber lines began to climb . . . and kept climbing.

[Density: new top of file. Above Irina by comparison? No sample data available at this height. Label: Second-Tier, hearsay, Karl.]

*I need a new column again!* Tally said.

*His anger is leaking out as radiation, Maureen. The way yours did in the courtyard of the Jadeleaf Ring. Any wizard along the corridor will be able to feel it through the door.*

*He knows he’s doing it?*

*I have no data on whether he can stop himself or not.*

“Wizard Richard,” Wenger said. His voice was quiet and courteous, like a man who was certain that courtesy would be returned. “I would like to ask you a few things.”

“Ask,” Maureen said, sitting herself down in the chair across from him without being invited to do so.

Wenger wanted to know what Gus had wanted when he came looking for her. The library. The offer. What he’d said. How many times he’d come.

Wenger’s questions were blunt, like a merchant asking the price of grain.

Maureen could see it in the Mirror Pupil lens Tally provided her. A fine net of pale threads swept over her skin, like cold water cascading down her back. *He’s checking to make sure you don’t lie,* Tally said. *Don’t lie to him.* That much Maureen did not need to be told.

*Give me the rhythm.*

Tally fell into the familiar cadence of the Silver Light, three long breaths and one short. It was the same rhythm that had carried her through the interview after the trial. Maureen answered on the out-breath.

“He came to the free library. He offered me a place in the Tower. A title, a timeline.”

She let a beat pass before she continued.

“I said no.”

*Pulse down two. Jaw loose. Breathe out longer.*

Maureen didn’t realize that her jaw had clamped shut until Tally told her to relax it.

Wenger watched her as she spoke. His face was calm and impassive, giving away none of his thoughts. “In your discussions with him, did you notice anything amiss? Anything strange or out of place?”

“No.” Another breath, in and out. “He was polite. He seemed sure I would say yes.” She thought about the white tower embroidered on Gus’s robe, the way he’d stood with his weight forward on the balls of his feet, ready to move in any direction. “He was exactly what he looked like.”

The weave flowed over her, sliding down from her face to her breastbone, and touched the place where the Totem lay coiled deep within her. Heat stirred behind her ribs.

Tally dampened it down. [Damping: −0.2. Mana 23.0 → 22.8] *He’s brushing up against the edge of the Totem without realizing it. Hold.*

Maureen did. She could sense Tally watching her eyes too.

*They’re black. And they stay black.*

*Not a single fleck of crimson.*

Maureen kept her gaze level, fixed on the old wizard’s neatly trimmed beard.

“Master Wenger,” Ambrose said from his desk. His voice had an edge. “Is my group leader cleared now?”

Wenger ignored him. Instead, he looked at Maureen for a long, thoughtful moment, as if she were a horse he’d been told was sound, but didn’t quite believe. Then the head of the High Ring White Tower turned and left the room without a word. The pressure went out the door with him.

The jars lining the shelves did not move. The frost on the window did not melt. But the air grew lighter somehow, as though someone had opened a door to let in the cold grey light outside.

Ambrose let out a slow breath. “Don’t worry about him, Richard. Gus wasn’t just the High Ring’s bright boy, he was Wenger’s own disciple. Of course he’s turning over stones.”

[Wenger: density on file. Second-Tier, hearsay. Comparison: none possible.]

*The listening weave’s structure is on file too,* Tally added. *Next time, I’ll spot it before it touches your skin.*

In the corridor outside, between two beeswax lamps, Maureen’s knees gave once, just a single small movement, like they had outside the interview room after the trial. She stopped walking for a second, putting her hand against the cold stone wall to steady herself, then continued along the corridor. Tally noticed the momentary weakness, but said nothing.

After they had walked another dozen steps, Tally spoke again. *You didn’t tell a single falsehood in there.*

Maureen didn’t answer aloud. *No,* she agreed silently, *I didn’t tell a single whole truth either.*

*That’s not a lie according to his spell’s rules. I checked. You may not have told a single whole truth, but you also left him unsatisfied.*

*And what will he do with that?* Maureen asked.

*I have no data,* Tally replied. *Only that men like that—men that strong—do not like to leave loose ends dangling.*

*Neither do I,* Maureen answered.

* * *

The last pine crate was nailed shut, and the Fourteenth Potion Group’s workroom fell silent for the first time in weeks. It was a moment of triumph. The rows of potion tubes stood quietly in their straw, like a legion of tin soldiers ready for battle. They stood in ranks of green, amber and pale purple, while the benches they stood on were stained from the potion mix, as were the hands of all the pharmacists who had worked on them.

[Quota: complete.]

[Crates: 41.]

Tally spoke up. *We are two days ahead of schedule according to my calculations. Viska has earned his tea.*

Maureen didn’t say anything out loud, but she silently agreed. *He has.* Then a figure came into the workroom. Ambrose walked the length of the pine crates, picking up a tube here and there to hold under one of the beeswax lamps, peering at the contents. Finally, he nodded.

“The entrance to the ancient realm opens in three days,” he said, turning to look at Maureen.

“So soon?”

Ambrose sighed. “I had expected at least a month before we would be ready. That tells you how hungry the great ones are to get their hands on what is within.” Then his voice rose with energy. “In three days the Pharmacy Department will enter the ancient realm. Go and get ready, Richard.”

Beside her Maureen saw Viska rubbing the back of his head, looking at the crates with a pleased grin. Someone at the far bench let out a whoop of triumph that was quickly shushed by the others.

*Three days is enough time to pack for the trip,* Tally noted. *It is not enough time to finish the upgrade on Edward. Will you take him as is?*

*Where I go he goes,* Maureen answered silently.

*I thought you might say that.*

* * *

“Now, Ike,” Maureen said to the steward in Richard’s flat, expressionless voice. “You have your orders. Run the house while I am gone. All letters that arrive must be held for me. No letters addressed to Wizard Richard are to be opened until I return. No visitors are allowed past the gate while I am away. Not a one. Understand?”

“I understand, Master,” Ike said, bowing so low that his face was even with Maureen’s boots.

“Good. For some weeks.”

“Yes, Master.”

Maureen took an afternoon to pack everything she needed into the void pocket. Magic stone cards, potions, glassware, materials, even spare clothing.

“Manas,” Maureen said when they were back behind the coal bay. She pressed down the flush knob, and the wall slid open again to reveal the energy pool beyond. Edward still lay within, but the frame of his blade-arm remained nothing more than lines scored into the veilsilver.

Before doing anything else with Edward, Tally checked once again to make certain that the Rental Centre’s ward-knot still ended at Gregory’s second ring. Then she logged Edward as he went into the pouch. [Edward: lattice scoring 7% → 4%. Upgrade: blade-arm frame, design stage. Pouch transit: tolerated, 6 tests. Core: intact.]

* * *

The Alliance launching yard was white with frost when Maureen and the others arrived just before dawn. There, waiting for them, was a bird large enough to fill the entire yard. Its body was larger than a house, its blue feathers shading to white. The curved blade of its beak shimmered with the colour of fire.

Tally measured it with the length of the yard, then again as it shook out its wings. [Wingspan: est. 30-40 m. By sight.] *This is larger than any Night-Crown Eagles you have seen at the Preserve,* Tally noted silently. *They would look like sparrows next to this creature. I would very much like to know what this eats.*

*Me too,* Maureen answered silently.

*I thought you might,* Tally replied.

The pharmacists lashed their department’s crates to the bird’s back with thick, tarred ropes. When they were finished, each one scrambled up and settled into place between the crates, perched on the warm, creaking feathers.

“Depart!” shouted the lead wizard from his place near the front.

The bird beat its wings, sending a cloud of white frost whirling off the flagstones in a blinding storm. It rose slowly into the air, circled the yard twice as it climbed, then began to rise steadily until Emilia’s rooftops became a blur beneath them.

High above, the winds roared past them with sufficient force to tear a man from his seat and send him screaming to the ground. They did not feel it though, as the gales broke against their shielding fields like waves against glass.

Beneath them, the plains stretched away like a great white sheet, broken here and there by the glittering lines of irrigation channels that wound across the landscape like threads of silver.

[Wind: strong, by sound on the field. Field holding.]

A thin blue ribbon stretched out towards the horizon.

*Log it,* Maureen said silently.

*Logged,* Tally responded. *I have also logged it as you flying. And I have logged it as you smiling.*

For a long moment, Maureen’s whole face changed, Richard’s borrowed mouth opened and she let out a long breath that the wind snatched away. “So this is what it’s like,” she murmured.

*I have filed that under joy, unconfirmed,* Tally said quietly.

Maureen didn’t argue the point.

The bird settled down onto the trampled and frosted ground and the pharmacists slid off its back, their legs stiff from the ride. Maureen turned around to look at her surroundings. In front of her was a great silver-grey gate rising several tens of metres into the air and stretching just as far in either direction. The gate’s surface rippled slowly, like a deep pool standing on end, as if it opened onto another world entirely. A line of frost crept up to the gate’s base, then stopped abruptly, leaving a clean dividing line between the white ground and the metal surface.

Alliance guards in shining silver armour patrolled before the gate in pairs, faint energy humming from their plate in a high-pitched whine. Their faces were stern beneath their open helms. Somewhere in the columns of apprentices gathered behind them, Tally caught sight of a faded green leaf on a white robe. Jadeleaf. Joanna’s people.

*I have no data at all on what lies beyond that gate,* Tally said. *I am looking forward to that more than I should.*

*We’re here,* Maureen answered silently.
