“My people have a job for you here in the Shadow Mountains,” Eve said from her seat at the end of the room. Her face was bathed in the pale light of the lamps that burned around the hall. A smile played across her lips, as if she were the host offering wine to a guest. “There are packs of Dark Wind Wolves in the mountains nearby, little more than a day’s walk away. We request that you deal with them. All of them, if you can manage it.”

The stone benches were cold beneath Maureen’s robe, the chill seeping up through the fabric and into her thighs. The pale lamps illuminated the hall without providing any warmth. Eve’s attendants stood around the edges of the room, black against the black stone, motionless and silent like candlesticks. But not a single dark elf watched their mistress. All of them focused their attention on the group of wizards in the centre of the hall.

“The wolves raid our people frequently.” Eve pursed her lips, and her voice grew soft. “They are cunning creatures and very cruel. Their darkness is a cousin to ours. We are children of the deep both, and our blades slide off each other like oil on water. For you, this should be simple work.”

*Stated without proof,* Tally noted.

*Your own school might have as much trouble sliding into one of those wolves as Eve does. Try fire first and use your darkness only if that doesn’t work.*

Maureen nodded slightly. *Do you believe anything she says?*

*I believe her mouth. I’m still waiting for the rest of her to show up.*

Eve sat as still as the statues around her. She kept her hands folded neatly upon her knee. Her smile stayed fixed in place, a lid clamped firmly atop a pot that desperately wanted to boil. The pulse in her throat beat slowly, regularly. By sight alone, it was steady and calm, but Maureen knew better than to trust what she could not feel with her own senses.

The benches shifted and murmured around her. The hired wizards asked careful questions, looking for anything they might have missed, trying to find an excuse to turn back.

“How large are the wolf packs?” one wizard asked.

“Can you provide us with elves who know the mountains?” asked another.

“What do the wolves do at night?”

Eve answered each question smoothly, her smile never faltering.

“And if they come in the dark?”

“Then you will be glad you are wizards,” she said.

*Six questions, six answers. No numbers at all,* Tally noted in Maureen’s head.

*None in the answer,* Maureen agreed silently.

*Keep that column open for the moment, just in case.*

*Already did.*

Near the front of the meeting hall, Claude turned his seal ring around on his finger. Two rows over, Denise’s hand drifted to her sleeve, then paused there. The old tell. Maureen watched the girl force herself to pull her hand from her sleeve and place it on her knee.

Eve let the silence stretch on a little while longer, her red lips holding their gentle smile immobile. “No more questions? None at all?” she asked in a coaxing voice, as though she would be deeply hurt if she was left with nothing.

The hired wizards glanced at each other, sitting in their crowded benches. They shook their heads.

Jackman rose slowly to his feet. It was the first time the old black wizard had spoken since entering the hall, but he merely said, “Then we go,” in his hoarse, scraping voice.

It was at Jackman’s command that the hired wizards stood up and filed out of the chamber, though Claude was the one paying them.

*And they stood for him, not for Claude, who held the purse,* Tally noted.

Maureen filed that observation away, next to the night when Tally’s watch had logged a dense source with Jackman’s particle signature moving across to Eve’s quarters and back again.

The wizards filed out of the meeting hall, through another cold stone passage that smelled of fallen blossoms and wet rock. The dark-elf attendants’ eyes followed the procession of wizards the entire way, measuring them like a farmer eyeing a pen of sheep he was thinking of buying. The hired wizards set off in loose groups of threes and fours toward the Shadow Mountains. Maureen, her face still disguised in Richard’s plain features, walked alongside Denise’s people, her hands tucked into her sleeves.

* * *

It took a day of walking across the grey scree to reach the foot of the Shadow Mountains, close by the dark-elf settlement just as Eve had promised. They made camp just below the first black slopes.

The fire spat resin sparks up into the sky, but there were no stars for them to meet. On the slopes above the camp, the black pines stood crouched like men poised to spring. The chill came down off the stone, smelling of wet needles.

Claude sat beside the fire for a moment, letting his eyes move around the ring of wizards, taking them all in. “These wolves are cruel, and they think. Be careless and you’ll pay for it.”

Jackman stood at the edge of the firelight, his arms crossed over his chest. He said nothing. It was as clear to Maureen as though he’d spoken: Claude was the face that had hired them; Jackman was content to let him wear it.

Denise called her own hired wizards together a short distance away. From what Eve had told her, she briefed them loudly on the wolves’ tactics. They came low to the ground and hunted in pairs, went straight for the throat, and dragged off the wounded to be devoured later. Three young women sat close beside her: Rita, who laughed too loudly; Glorette, who kept her braid wound tight around her head; and Rachel, who was quietest of all, sitting nearest the dark.

Tally noted whom Denise spoke to first and longest: her own hired wizards, and those three more than any others. Denise’s pulse stayed steady as she did it, her voice pitched just loud enough for the rest of the camp to overhear.

Maureen drew the obvious conclusion from this behaviour. Every one of her wizards who lived through this would be another bit of leverage against Claude when the time came.

*The same arithmetic you used yourself,* Tally said silently.

*It beats being nice,* Maureen answered silently.

*It’s rarely as good,* Tally replied.

Half amused, Maureen made certain that Richard’s borrowed mouth didn’t smile at that. She recognized the sound of someone counting her assets out loud while pretending it was caring. She’d done the same thing herself, back on the ship.

Denise’s eyes sought out Richard through the sparks of the fire. “Wizard Richard, I know that wolves are nothing to you. But humour me. Watch yourself.”

The night’s sounds settled down into a background of snoring and the tick-tick of cooling stones. Maureen unrolled her bedroll, lay down without a sword at her hip, since Freya’s blade was folded away in the pouch, and closed her eyes. She knew Tally wouldn’t.

While Maureen slept, Tally watched. She tracked several sources of dense dark particles moving through the scrub brush beyond the firelight. They moved low to the ground. They moved in pairs. They were working their way up, against the wind. As each pair slid closer, Tally logged them.

Maureen woke with a jolt from the mana that snapped through her body. The readout was already burning behind her eyes:

[Sources: 14+, by particle sense. Closing. 200 m.]

She came up in one motion, hands already cold, and before she could even draw breath to shout, the wolves broke from the dark and into the circle of bedrolls.

“Help me!”

“No, no, don’t, don’t—”

One of the wolves bowled over a wizard near the fire, the wet weight of it sending the man sprawling. Blood ran black in the orange light of the fire. The air suddenly stank of soaked fur, copper, and scattered embers that hissed against the frost.

>Biggest one. Left. Low. Coming for you.

 In the time it took Maureen to turn her head Tally continued.

*Use the Orb,* Tally said. *We can use their own particles. No catalogue. Nothing that will read like Jack’s or Night Owl’s work. Just their own stuff, sucked out of the night air around us.*

Maureen gave silent consent.

Dark particles bled from the night air and began to spin on themselves in a tiny vortex. A small black whirlwind danced on the ground in front of Maureen, and when she breathed, “Go,” beneath her breath, it obeyed, sweeping forward and dragging the charging wolf into its spinning heart.

[Orb routing: −0.5. Mana 20.1 → 19.6.]

The whirlwind closed around the howling wolf, tearing at it from all sides at once. In a few heartbeats the howl choked off and the wolf fell to the frost, stripped of fur and life by the spinning particles.

Only then did Maureen get a good look at one of these creatures called Dark Wind Wolves. It was a massive beast, standing two or three metres at the shoulder. Thick joints ran through its legs and its body was a bunch of muscle and bone. Its fur had been as dense and black as the night, now steaming away in the whirlwind. Its eyes still held a dim light behind their green irises, as if something behind them had been thinking up until a moment ago.

[Dark Wind Wolf. Underground creature. 2-3 m. Hunts in coordinated pairs. Source: Oserroba archive + sight.]

*They will come back for their wounded,* Tally said. *Medium confidence.*

[Paid.]

*Jackman watched the whirlwind for 2.1 seconds,* Tally said. *To him it would have looked like an unfamiliar darkness working beside Richard.*

*Log it as paid,* Maureen thought silently.

One of the pair began to close on Rachel’s flank. Maureen responded with a pair of dark-red Blast Fireballs, chant reduced to just the name of the spell. The fireballs exploded against the wolves and both went down burning. [Mana 19.6 → 17.4.] *Fire bit them clean,* Tally noted.

The other wizards finally found their feet and spells began to light up the camp from one end to the other. The Dark Wind Wolves lost ground quickly, howling back and forth between one another before melting back into shadows which flowed away towards the mountains.

In the sudden quiet after the last of the howls had faded, the night seemed even more enormous. The resin fire continued to spit and pop, while steam rose from the scorched fur of the fallen wolves. Somewhere within the ring of bedrolls lay a wizard, trying to sob quietly through his teeth.

“Damned animals!” Claude spat out.

Denise said nothing beside her uncle, but her mouth had become a thin line, the corners going white.

*How many?* Maureen asked silently as the sky began to brighten to grey.

*Eight,* Tally responded.

*How many of them bought potions off Richard on the way down?* Maureen asked.

*Two.*

*Log them. Don’t show them on my display.*

*Logged. Not shown.*

The bodies of the dead wizards lay in a row, covered by their own cloaks. Even as Maureen watched, a rime began to form on the cloth. Her jaw set. Rita, Glorette and Rachel were alive, huddled near the ashes of the fire. Rita wasn’t laughing.

* * *

Claude and Denise led the sweeps, using the method Eve had instructed them in. They would drive up the gullies toward the dens, then the wolves would be flushed out into the more open scree. The wizards would kill what came out. Day followed day in a blur of sweat and scree, boots sliding on loose rock, the acrid musk and stale stink of old bone that poured from each den’s mouth as they drove the beasts out, the wind whining down the gullies at all hours.

On the second night, as Tally had forecast, the wolves came back for their wounded. They were low, in pairs, creeping through the darkness.

*Logged as correct,* Tally said quietly.

Maureen gave her the point silently. This time the wizards were awake and waiting. The pair of Dark Wind Wolves that slipped through the darkness toward the injured, never made it to the bedrolls. They died in a crossfire of spells before they got within ten paces.

*Blast Fireballs, with the chant shortened,* Tally noted quietly. *Totem amplification +1.2° on each. Mana topped up again overnight. Nothing that looks like Richard’s black fire.*

Maureen did the work like it was bench work in the Lab. Aim, cast, step forward, check the body from a sword’s length. She kept it simple, using plain dark-red fire. Purgatory Fire and the new black wind remained folded inside her, like letters she had no intention of posting.

They found the wolf king near a high den. He was larger than any of the others, with grey fur at his muzzle. Together the wizards brought him down in a storm of spells.

The wolf gave a long, mournful howl as he fell. The sound rolled around the mountains and continued to echo from the cliffs after the body had hit the stone. It sounded as if the mountain itself were finishing the wolf’s song.

Tally counted the last echoes, making sure there were none left.

[Dark Wind Wolves sighted today: 0.]

*How many are there left?* Maureen asked silently.

*We do not have data outside of visual range. There are not many left,* Tally replied.

The quiet that came after was the quiet of something that was nearly gone. Maureen stood for a moment, feeling it around her. Thinking about the Skoll hounds in the valley, and how much or how little it would weigh in an elf’s ledger.

The wizards swept the slopes around the mountains one last time, then turned back. They reached the dark-elf settlement by dusk. Eve met them with a delight that she made no attempt to disguise.

“Thank you, all of you,” she said. “Rest now. Tomorrow we will talk about your reward.”

She clapped her hands once, and the maidservants came in to direct the wizards to their rooms.

*Tally, did you see where Eve looked when she said “all of you”?*

*Of course. She looked at Denise and Claude most of the time. At Denise the longest.*

Maureen filed that away without comment.

The maidservants led the way down the corridors. Their feet made no sound on the stone floor. The air in the corridor smelled of blossom and cool water.

* * *

Maureen lay on the stretched hide and stared up at the black stone above her, counting the seams. The pale lamp burned with a steady light, not a flicker or a jump. In the silence of the dark-elf guest room, her breathing sounded loud to her ears.

[Dense source. Grain: Jackman. Moving toward Eve’s quarters.]

Tally’s night log ticked across her display.

Three minutes later: [Dense source. Grain: Claude. Same route.]

*Jackman first,* Tally said quietly. *Claude is following him.*

*Where are they going?*

*Both lines end at Eve’s door.*

The two human grains sat down beside Eve’s dark-elf grain. They were all together in one room.

Eve, Claude, Jackman. Three in one room, at night.

Tally’s voice dropped even further. *Something’s coming up.*

Maureen tensed. A moment later, there was movement through the particle sense, like a great cold weight passing under the floor. It was broad and deep and slow, more like a river than anything else. It might have been four feet beneath them, or it might have been four hundred miles.

The hair rose on Maureen’s arms. For a moment, as she sensed that river flowing, she felt as if the floor had dropped away beneath the bed, leaving it suspended in space. Her teeth felt cold, but the flame of the lamp didn’t stir.

Then the sensation was gone.

[Pulse: 1. Duration: ~4 s. Direction: everywhere at once.]

Maureen sat up in bed. *What was that?*

*It matches Nightingale’s account of the Styx Pact. The river that the strong can feel.*

[Styx Pact: probable. Sworn by Eve, Claude, Jackman. Terms: no data.]

Maureen sat very still on the edge of the bed, her palms flat on the hide.

Tally set the river beside two files: Nightingale’s account from their meeting around the stone table in the shuttered villa, and the file on Adrian’s contract.

*Eve, Claude, Jackman,* Maureen thought silently to Tally.

*Yes,* came Tally’s voice, *grain analysis only. What they swore, I cannot hear through the stone.*

*They bound themselves together,* Maureen thought, remembering the moment when she had refused to let that great cold river flow over her at Nightingale’s table. Her fingers crept up to her throat, feeling the thin cold collar of her own bindings.

*The pact was sworn this night, before the talk of a reward tomorrow, and only Eve, Claude, and Jackman were present. None of the hired wizards. Based on that information, I project with low to medium confidence that whatever binds them will protect no other.*

*All right. Keep all three sources logged until dawn.*

*I never meant to stop.*

Toward the small hours, the three sources separated and returned to their own rooms. Tally noted each one’s return in the logs.

Maureen didn’t lie down again. She remained seated on the edge of the hide bed until the flame of the lamp sputtered low, listening to the silence of the night. It didn’t feel empty anymore.
