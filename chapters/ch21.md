One lantern swinging from its hook.

Wet timber and tar filling the air with their scent.

The deck creaking above, in time with each slow breath of the great tortoise beneath the ship's hull.

Richard walked close beside her in the narrow corridor. "Maureen, this was Aaron's first kill, wasn't it?" He kept his voice low.

Aaron's record appeared before her eyes.

His thumb, wearing a hole in the hem of his sleeve.

The night in the woods when he had wept, as she and Richard struggled to hold him down.

No kills logged against his name.

"Yes, Richard," she said. "Aaron's father brought him up to be a polite, refined nobleman."

She saw it again.

Lucy's hand clapped to her mouth.

Alice taking a step back.

Richard's expression changing.

Her own raised eyebrow.

"Carey was one of us, Maureen," Richard said. "Not some stranger."

She smiled but said nothing.

There was a small pang inside at Richard's softness and she kept it hidden behind the smile. Her hands remained loose at her sides as the lantern's light swung back and forth across the wooden planks of the corridor.

*Is that a weakness here?* Tally asked in its warm voice.

*I haven't decided yet,* she told Tally silently. *And I don't want you deciding for me either.*

*Very well. We will leave that question open. Unlogged.*

After a few more words with Richard, Maureen left him and made her way back into her cabin, hooking the bell cord across the door.

* * *

The fights for the black cloth ration bags got worse. The seeds were using whatever means they had to get their hands on the food.

Maureen Olsen was crouched down waiting for her ration when there was the sound of a catch clacking somewhere behind her. Tally flashed > Left. High. Rail. and Maureen dropped flat onto the deck. A crossbow bolt hummed past her ear, the breeze from its passing ruffling her hair, and thunked into the wooden plank she had just been leaning against. She felt her breath catch once.

Later Tally told her that it matched no fletching in its records, but that Rheas's men did have crossbows.

*That proves nothing,* she replied silently.

*Correct. Nothing. But worth remembering.*

The wooden planks remained a dark red colour no matter how many times the buckets of water and scrub brushes were run over them. A coppery smell hung in the air over the brine and tar, a smell she remembered from Angrey's beating.

Within days, dozens of seeds had died. The black armoured knights tipped the bodies over the rail without a word.

Gulls followed the ship now. Screaming and diving they would drop and snatch up whatever went over the side.

[Overboard: 34] Tally informed her.

*Can you please stop telling me that?* she asked it silently.

[Confirmed.]

The number disappeared from her display.

*Tally still keeping track though right?*

*Yes.*

She and Richard had forced out the two seeds occupying the cabin on her other side. Lucy and Alice had moved into the cabin together. Aaron didn't speak much now. Beneath his black robe he was a killer, disposing of every seed who tried to rob them of their rations. He made no secret of it either. His thumb no longer flicked out to wipe the blade on his sleeve hem. Instead he would clean it on the robes of the men he had just killed.

[Kill ranking. Group. 1 Aaron. 2 Maureen. 3 Richard.] Tally told her and she looked at him for a long moment.

* * *

"Food's getting harder to get," Maureen said.

Richard nodded. "Too risky to fight over now. Only the big gangs or the strong individual seeds dare try."

Lucy and Alice sat on the edge of the bunk, nodding in agreement, eyes sliding to Aaron then quickly away.

There was a knock at the door, three taps in quick succession.

Instantly every hand in the cabin went to the hilt of a Cross Sword.

"Who's there?" Richard yelled.

[Voice match: Anthony] Tally told Maureen a fraction of a second before Richard said it. *Four sets of boots outside.* Maureen kept her hand on her hilt.

Anthony entered followed by three youths, all holding Cross Swords, all looking wary.

"Walking alone isn't safe anymore," Anthony said, "Every seed that dies means a bigger share for us." His once white shirt was grey around the cuffs from days of wear and the smell of soap had long since vanished from him.

Anthony stood just inside the door, a tallow lamp smoking between them, its oily stink mingling with the musty scent of the damp boards that creaked beneath nine people sitting knee to knee.

"We need to unite. Alone we have almost no chance of getting food." No one answered him and the bell cord swayed gently by the door.

Anthony smiled as if he'd expected no answer, "It is Anna's idea. It requires many capable fighters."

Maureen could hear the whispering of Tally in her head. *Anna has joined with Anthony's band now he brings some of his following to Anna's.*

*Then he needs us more than ever.*

Maureen tilted her head slightly, regarding Anthony thoughtfully, "And what would we be?"

Anthony leaned in quickly, "We're friends, so of course we would be equals. All of us are equal."

Tally flagged a half beat pause before 'all of us' and Maureen noticed the way Anthony's thumb rubbed across the gold thread at his cuff.

*Cost of joining Anna? Log this?*

Maureen looked around the cabin. Richard nodded first then Aaron, Lucy and Alice. "We'll come and meet your Anna."

* * *

Anna's cabin was packed and hot with bodies and candle smoke. A single timber beam ran low over the heads of those inside. Anna's own seeds were crowded shoulder to shoulder around one bunk, like court officials arranged in a semi-circle around a throne. Maureen and the others squeezed into the space by the door behind Anthony.

In the centre of the circle on the bunk was Anna, a pale skinned girl with chestnut hair and a pale metal bracelet around her wrist. She regarded Maureen's group with a sweeping gaze, taking their measure.

Tally pulled up its hearsay file. *Ice beauty, trained as a knight.* As it read Maureen looked at Anna, seeing her straight back and feet planted firmly apart, like Jack's men ready for action. *Holds herself like a knight. The hearsay is accurate?*

Tally agreed. *If she draws steel I will upgrade the tag to Knight Trained.*

"These are my friends," Anthony said smiling, "I told them they could join us."

Anna glanced at Maureen's group once more before calling out. "Cromwell!"

A burly youth stepped out from the press of bodies around Anna and drew his broadsword. The tip of the weapon scraped against the timber beam overhead as he brought it up in the confined space.

"I'm Cromwell," the boy said. "Listen, if any of you can last thirty breaths against me, then, the girl first maybe," he looked directly at Maureen, "I'll let you join us too."

Tally helpfully overlaid a pale blue line from Maureen's own right hand across to the timber bulkhead next to Cromwell's neck. > Blue edge. No nick.

Maureen's wrist flicked and the Cross Sword seemed to leap from her fingers in a silver streak that ended with a 'thunk' sound as the point bit into the wooden bulkhead pinning Cromwell's collar a finger's width from his neck. Before Cromwell could react to the blade's sudden appearance, Maureen closed the distance separating them. She whipped her hilt free and the flat of her blade, never the poisoned edge, rested against Cromwell's throat.

Silence stretched for long seconds. The tip of the broadsword dropped to touch the floor as Cromwell's face became blotched and dark.

The corner of Maureen's mouth lifted slightly in amusement.

"If this was some kind of test, I think we've passed."

Anthony and his three followers were frozen in place while Anna's eyes widened in surprise, Tally overlaying [Anna: touched bracelet. Pulse at throat visible: rising, then settling.] Then just like that Anna's calm demeanour was back, as if it had never been disrupted.

"Quite right," she said without inflection. "You are strong enough to join with us." Then she turned away dismissively, her chestnut hair catching the candlelight.

*So,* Maureen thought, *the bracelet is the enchanted item rumoured to exist. Huh.*

Tally obligingly moved the tag from hearsay to probable.

Maureen slid her Cross Sword back into its sheath and checked the edge. It hadn't even touched Cromwell's skin. Then she laughed silently.

* * *

The next day on the open deck, Anna and Rheas faced each other across the stained planks. The tortoise's shell was rising ahead, and Emily was watching them from the upper level. Rheas tilted his head slightly as he looked them over. "These are your former white-robe seeds?" He scanned the group, his eyes coming to a halt on Maureen and remaining fixed there.

"You have plenty of people," Anna said, coolly.

"Only strength matters," Rheas replied with a laugh.

Tally noted the blond youth standing beside him again.

*Rheas holds the crossbow in a way that indicates familiarity with having fired it at people.*

The weak black robes stood around the edges of the open deck, in the wind that blew off the grey sea. They were thin and hollow-cheeked, but they watched intently. "Begin," Anna said.

Rheas grinned, a large and proud young man, his bright golden hair almost glowing. "Listen you weak trash," he said loudly, "from today all food on this ship is ours. You're not allowed on deck."

"Why?" shouted one of the thin black robe seeds standing at the edge of the deck.

A bolt from Rheas's crossbow took the boy through the heart and threw him backwards onto the planks. Maureen's hands stayed where they were, by her sides.

[Lethal: heart. One shot.]

Rheas raised the crossbow to the skies. "Anyone else object?"

The weak black robe seeds filed below deck in bitter silence. Before Rheas even opened his mouth, Tally's display had changed to [13 / 13 / 1]. Rheas looked around, then spoke, "The left thirteen are mine. The right thirteen are yours. We split the last one."

"Agreed," Anna said.

Within Anna's bloc, the food was apportioned according to strength, and Maureen's group got a good share of it, roughly what they had once been fighting for.

"Uniting, then expelling the rest..." Maureen said softly. "What a fine idea."

She was thinking about the body still on the planks with its bolt through the heart. She went back to her cabin.

After that the days were calm, broken only by those desperate black robe seeds who tried to take rations by force, and ended up being slain.

One morning there were only eleven of the twenty-seven groups of knights standing out on deck. There were cries of concern from the black robe seeds.

From above, Emily sneered down, "Have you forgotten Lord Evans's rules of the game? There are enough rations to feed two hundred people, or one hundred, or fifty, or thirty, or none at all."

While Emily was still sneering, Tally told her [11 groups ≈ 33 meals.]

[Alliance ≈ 100.]

"We won't have enough for both sides," someone said.

Anna's bloc and Rheas's bloc turned to watch each other, silently calculating.

*Tally, how likely is the split to hold?* she asked silently.

*There is no data on Rheas's sense of honour.*

Maureen shifted her weight forward onto the balls of her feet.
