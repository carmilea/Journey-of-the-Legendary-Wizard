Only eleven groups of black armoured knights stood on deck with their sacks of food. Far too few. There had been over a hundred seeds.

Anna's side and Rheas's side faced each other across the open deck, neither side moving.

The deck rose and fell beneath her boots, the salt wind blew in her face, the planks beneath her feet were damp and the smell of baking bread came from the knights' large sacks. Hunger twisted in Maureen's belly.

"Eleven groups' worth of food," Anna said coolly. "Something has to give."

Rheas smiled. "Of course Anna, we'll split it evenly. We follow the old rules, don't we?"

His hands held out wide, showing that he held nothing, Rheas nodded encouragingly. His smile never faltered and Anna's shoulders eased down a fraction. Tally flagged up for Maureen, Rheas shifting his weight to the back foot, the cloaks lifting behind him.

Bows. Twelve. He's lying.

Maureen had already grabbed Richard and pulled them both behind the line of knights before Rheas yelled, "Attack!"

The black robe seeds surged forward as crossbows cracked, bolts punching into Anna's side of the seeds, dropping many of them screaming onto the wooden decking.

"That's for always having to share food with you former white robe trash." Rheas sneered at the writhing figures on the deck. "You're a bitch Anna and now I'm going to teach you a lesson."

He raised his hand and yelled, "Hila!"

A ruby red stone set in a ring on Rheas's hand glowed bright as the sun and a sea of fire roared from his hand towards Anna's forces. Anna raised her wrist, shouting "Usta" and a torrent of icy green light met the fiery tide.

[Item: ring, red stone.] [Spell cast: Hila.] [Source: item, not caster.]

[Item: bracelet, green gem.] [Source: item, not caster.]

[Spell cast: Usta.] [Addition to heard spell list.]

Tally reported.

Maureen saw the two items meeting each other, the red tide of fire crashing into the icy torrent of green. Two dogs fighting, neither one hers so she didn't have to face them head on. Planks blistered where the emerald light passed over them, steam rising in a hiss that stank of scorched wood pitch. One cheek burned hotly, while the other went bitterly cold. The entire deck erupted into a maelstrom of killing.

[Blade, right low.]

Maureen slipped the sword thrust, her shoulder hitting the seed and knocking him off balance as she drove her sword into his neck.

> Richard. Behind him. Club.

Tally flashed across her display, the line terse and urgent. Maureen Olsen shouted a warning, even as she threw her Cross Sword with all the strength her lean body could muster. The silver blade took the man creeping up behind Richard square in the chest, lifting him off the deck and pinning him to the cabin wall behind. [Warning: major threat, 30° left.]

Maureen's reflexes had her twisting clumsily away from a blade she hadn't been paying attention to. She snatched at a plain sword lying on the deck, turning awkwardly backhand, and taking the man's head off. A coppery smell filled her nostrils. The borrowed sword's hilt was sticky under her fingers. Smoke from the flaring fireballs stung her eyes and her vision was blurred by the shouts and screams that came from every direction around her. Tally spoke in its own warm voice, breaking into her consciousness.

That one was close. Your left side is slow right after a throw.

I know. File it for practice. She wrenched her own Cross Sword free from the man she'd pinned to the wall, dropping the common blade she had used to kill the second assassin. By the time she reached Richard, Aaron, Lucy and Alice again, Lucy was demanding anxiously what they should do.

The weakest members of their group were now streaming towards the knights who were handing out food. Old grudges between white-robes and black-robes were being settled in the crush of bodies trying to get close enough to receive the bounty that the knights offered. The knights themselves stood still in the centre of the maelstrom. Their black plate armour was unmarked by the melee. Sacks lay at their feet, while smoke drifted past their visors.

Food? Maureen asked silently.

You've already stored two days' worth in your cabin, Tally replied in its own warm voice. There are eleven groups here, approximately a hundred fighters in all. Now that the weaker ones have joined the fray, it may not be worthwhile. You could grab one of those bags on the way though.

No. Everyone out, Maureen responded.

Fine...as you wish. Tally sounded sulky.

"Go! We're pulling back!" Maureen shouted to her group, turning and leading them below deck. Down here the screams and shouts were muffled, coming through the timber of the deck above. The smell of burnt pitch hung about Maureen's sleeve, while Lucy's breathing came in harsh gasps behind her.

* * *

The five of them squeezed into her narrow cabin, as the screams, thuds and sounds of bodies hitting the floor came through the planks above.

Maureen's fingers found the Blood Seal of Thorns concealed beneath her robe, and rested there for a moment. Her trump card, concealed until the last possible instant.

Nine point five seconds tops, Tally reminded her. After that comes the dull ache behind the ribs. Don't forget that. That is your last card, not your next.

I know, Maureen answered silently.

Of course you do, Tally replied. But I know you know. But it said it anyway, so she didn't have to.

When the noise began to die away, Richard and Aaron slipped out to see what was happening. Aaron came back white faced, and Richard was sick in the slop bucket. Maureen told them it was all right now, then closed her eyes. That night, as she lay on her bunk, the smell of coppery blood coming through the cracks in the timber was thicker than ever.

Over the next few days both factions fell apart. The knights changed the ration count every day, and no one knew if their group would be left out tomorrow. Anna and Rheas still had a few followers each, but the bloc was disintegrating fast.

It held as long as the number held still, Tally commented.

Evans made it that way, Maureen answered silently. He was a First-Tier Wizard through and through, a black robe with blood on his hands, but he knew how to play a game.

"Anna and Rheas are both hurt," Aaron said, leaning against the wall for support. "Anna badly. Nearly had her enchanted item pinched too."

Something strange flickered in Aaron's eyes as he said this.

"An enchanted item?" Lucy and Alice gasped, envy etched on their faces.

Aaron's thumb worried at the hem of his sleeve.

[Aaron: pupils widen at 'enchanted item'.] Tally reminded her what hung beneath her robe.

I saw, Maureen answered silently.

"Where's Richard?" she asked aloud.

"Gone out," Lucy said worriedly. "He said he wanted to..."

Maureen frowned, pressing the Blood Seal of Thorns further into its pocket. Then she went to look for him.

* * *

Dim corridors, doors hanging loose, a guttering lamp in an alcove. Her boots stuck slightly to the wooden planks in places. She found him around the corner clutching his arm, which was bleeding from a cut, standing over the body of a black-robe. "He jumped out at me," Richard told her, his voice shaking. "He fought like crazy to kill me."

Maureen nodded and dug out some hemostatic powder for him. Tally told her the cut wasn't deep and was clean with no blue, so it wasn't her poison. [Hemostatic powder: 54% remaining.]

"Why do you think he fought so hard?" she asked Richard.

Richard glanced down at the body again. "I found some bodies in his room, all mangled up. I think if he'd killed me, he would have eaten me." Tally counted the remains through the open door for her, four. So she only had to look once.

Her face betrayed nothing. "Then he knew," she said. "We won't let any of us go out alone anymore." Richard sighed heavily at the body of the seed he had slain before they moved on.

Ahead of them came strange sounds from one of the cabins. Richard nodded at her and drew his Cross Sword, hacking open the door while Maureen stood beside him, blade at the ready.

The stink rolled out of the cabin as the door fell away. Rotten timbers, old blood, unwashed bodies mingled with the smell of freshly splintered wood lying across the floor.

A girl crouched on the floor, her hair matted and her clothing torn, holding the arm of a dead boy. Her eyes flicked between Richard and Maureen. "Don't hit Brother Kevin," she stammered, hugging her knees tightly to her chest. "Please don't take our food..."

She started sobbing again, rocking back and forth. "Nise will be good. Nise will do what she's told. I'm Nise. I'm good." She crawled over to the bunk and sat with her legs tucked under her, posing for whoever was standing in the doorway.

Maureen felt cold and hot at the same time. Her stomach turned and her face did not. Cold ran up her spine. This is what waits for any girl who stops being strong...

Tally spoke softly, not offering opinion or emotion, just the facts as it saw them. Teeth marks on the table, the chairs, even the frame of the bed. There was no food anywhere. Two bodies' worth of smell.

"She's gone mad," Maureen said. The girl's eyes were empty. Starving, too afraid to leave, her mind had snapped leaving only the instinctive need to eat.

Richard's lips were pressed together so hard they were white. His hands were clenched into fists at his sides.

Maureen patted him on the shoulder. "Come on, Richard, we should go."

Richard didn't move from where he stood.

"I can't leave her like this," he sighed.

Do you wish to prevent him from doing this? Tally asked softly.

No, Maureen thought silently.

Tally fell silent, presenting a simple breath counter on Maureen's display. Maureen counted her breaths against Tally's timer.

Richard stepped forward, and in one swift stroke, Nise was dead. He gently lifted Kevin's body and placed it on the bunk next to Nise. The two seeds lay with their arms around each other, as though asleep. Richard walked out of the cabin, and Maureen followed him.

Richard placed the two seeds together. Logged, Tally said quietly inside Maureen's head.

Keep that one, Maureen thought.

On the walk back to her cabin Maureen's throat refused to open. Richard's blade swung forgotten by his side, the blood still wet on it. Unwiped, it dripped occasionally, the sound loud in the lower corridors. His steps were heavy beside her. In the cabin Richard's face was grey and he wouldn't say a word no matter what the others asked.

* * *

News travelled fast among the seeds, and the rumours were not pleasant. Stronger seeds were taking the food from weaker ones, leaving them to die of hunger. It was also whispered that some had killed the weak and eaten the corpses. Some seeds, like Nise, were dying in agony of hunger. Girls who had used their bodies to gain more food were still eaten by the stronger seeds.

In the corridor Maureen overheard one seed laughing that it was a good deal keeping the pretty girls on scraps, some sport now and a good meal later. Richard didn't speak after that and Aaron just smiled. The smile was different since Aaron had killed Carey.

Do you want the odds on yourself? Tally asked.

No, Maureen answered.

Yes, she amended.

You are strong enough not to be taken. You are not strong enough to sleep, Tally said bluntly.

That night before she slept Maureen checked the bell cord across her door twice, armed the crossbow cuff and wound another loop of her cloak around her wrist. In the morning the food bag was light in her hand, most of the hard biscuits gone, the fist of hunger under her ribs a constant companion.

Lucy hasn't touched her ration for two days, Tally said.

They're afraid that if they run out of food they will be dropped overboard, or used as bait to attract fish, or traded to other seeds for food, Tally told her.

Maureen placed the shares of food in front of Lucy and Alice herself.

That will do more than any speech, Tally said.

"We have at most two days of food left. If we can't take any today on deck..." Maureen didn't need to finish the sentence. Immediately Lucy and Alice agreed with her.

They left the cabin together, all of them watching every door they passed as they climbed towards the deck.

* * *

The water was a mass of glare, and the planks beneath Maureen's feet were wet with the spray. The bronze bell seemed to gleam dully in the light. Her stomach was empty but her hands were steady.

The black-armoured knights stood around the bell in fifteen groups, more than before. Across the open deck from the bell, the other black-robed seeds who still lived waited in twos and threes, all of them hungry and hard-eyed, all of them survivors. Tally counted the knight groups and displayed the ready breath counter on Maureen's display. Start on the bell, she told it silently.

One of the knights raised his weapon and struck the bell. As Maureen began to gather herself to run, one of the nearby seeds shouted out, his voice shaking. "What . . . what is that?"

Maureen turned her head, looking at what had caught the seed's eye. Blue-skinned monsters, heavily muscled and standing over two men high, fins jutting from their backs, were running across the waves towards the ship. Water sprayed in great sheets from each stride they took, the sea itself heaving and breaking around their charging forms. The deck shuddered beneath her boots as the tortoise's breathing stirred its massive body.

[Warning: unknown lifeforms, by sight. Bearing ahead, closing.]

Count them, Maureen told Tally silently.

Too fast to count cleanly, Tally replied in its own voice, short and unhappy with the admission.

A man appeared in midair a short distance away, middle-aged and dressed in a tailcoat. He was surrounded by halos of dark-red fire particles, which flared in the sunlight. "Evans, get out here!" he roared.

Every seed on the deck froze where they stood. The black-armoured knights remained motionless as fence posts, and the man's voice rolled across the deck.

[Energy radiation source: NEW.]

No match on file, Tally said quietly.

Maureen remembered Evans sending word that he would repay the Blue Ring wizards who'd wounded him.
