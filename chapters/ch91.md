For the first time since she had become Jack, there was silence. No screams on the wind, no footsteps but her own, no voices crying out to her for help or pleading for death. There was nothing but the drip of water, and the sound of her breathing echoing back to her from the grey stone walls that were beading with damp mineral sweat. She drew a deep breath and let it out slowly. Then she turned her attention to setting up a defensive perimeter.

First she laid down a line of wards across the mouth of the cave, then she walked them along the sides. As she completed each line of the runes that Gregory had given her, it sank into the stone leaving a faint silver line behind. Tally monitored every line, checking it against the patterns that Gregory had provided as she laid it down.

When Maureen had finished laying all of the wards, she walked the entire pattern a second time.

*You've checked it twice now,* Tally noted in its warm voice.

*I know. You were about to ask me to,* she responded silently.

*I was going to ask you three times,* Tally said with a smug note in its voice.

Maureen Olsen drew Freya's slender sword from its scabbard and the black light crawled up the blade like oil on water. She swung the blade at a boulder, slicing the top off in one stroke and the rock fell away leaving an empty seat behind as smooth as if it had been carved from a block of soap. She seated herself crosslegged on the boulder, the cold of the stone coming through her trousers. Then she drew out the loot and sorted it into piles, based on what could wait. The stone cards could wait, the rare roots could wait, everything on the dark elves' list could wait. Four things could not wait.

The two fruits called Skoll sat in her bag at her waist, warm to the touch. They were a deep purple-red colour and pulsed faintly like the glow from the Seal before she had broken it. She remembered the hounds tearing at the shades that screamed in terror and knew exactly what they had been fed.

Tally recited the method for consuming the Skoll fruit to her from Gregory's entry. They could not be eaten, but a fresh shallow cut needed to be made at the sternum, the fruit laid upon the wound, and the power would be absorbed through the brow. A pale-blue line appeared on her display over her breastbone and Tally's voice gave her instructions that the cut should be no deeper than a fingernail.

She pulled her shirt aside at the sternum, just below where the faint pale eye remained marked upon her skin where the Seal had left it years ago. She pressed the tip of the blade against her skin, just over the line Tally had painted for her, and drew it along her breastbone. Blood welled up in a thin red bead.

*Very calm about me cutting myself open,* Maureen thought.

*Calm about the depth. Not so calm about you,* Tally answered.

Maureen put the first fruit upon the cut, and it split open with a sharp wet sound like a seed bursting. The flesh collapsed into a writhing knot of purple-red mist that clung to her chest. The scent of scorched honey and singed fur, like the refining flame, filled her nostrils. The mist felt solid against her skin, as though a live creature caged behind her hands was trying to break free. Maureen kept every muscle in her body perfectly still.

The mist began to draw itself out from the knot, becoming finer and finer strands of purple-red that crawled up her throat. They spread out across her jaw and over Jack's borrowed cheeks before drawing back together to converge upon the point between her eyebrows. A pool of coolness settled there, like water from a spring cupped in her palm. The ache of all those days of killing drained away from the back of her skull. For the first time in a week, her shoulders relaxed.

[Spirit: rising.]

[Spirit: rising.]

The alerts scrolled across her vision, but no number followed them.

*How much?* Maureen asked silently.

*Not yet. It doesn't want to give you a number then have to take it back,* Tally answered.

Hours passed in the grey light that never changed. The cut sealed itself to a thin pink line. Somewhere in those hours she slept sitting up, the sword resting across her knees. When it was done, Tally displayed the results:

>[Spirit: 7.6 → 9.8.]

>[+2.2]

Beside it, it displayed the second numeral on the back of her hand:

>[Exit: 9 days.]

On the third day after the wound, Maureen brewed the green potion, mixing the reversal note from Gregory's Mimicry entry and a dye stripping solvent from the pharmacology database she had stored with her. She used a small fire built from the dry wood she had gathered for cooking, carefully measuring the ingredients into one of the test tubes from her belt kit. She didn't dare let a drop touch her until Tally ran a simulation.

The potion would undo the face, the throat, and the dye, but it wouldn't undo the haircut. *Hair grows,* Maureen thought silently.

*Slowly,* Tally told her.

Two days after the first fruit, Maureen reopened the pink line and laid the second fruit upon it. Once again, the mist rose, the threads writhed, and the cool touched her brow. All were thinner this time.

>[Spirit: 9.8 → 11.2.]
>[+1.4]

Tally displayed the two gains side by side, then called up the rainbow fruit on the Skran road. *First doses are most effective,* it said. *Some things never change.* *So then I was still just a slum girl eating fruit,* Maureen thought.

*A very expensive slum girl,* Tally noted.

On the next day, Maureen uncorked the vial of Heart-Eater powder. The dust smelled of old books and pepper. Using Tally's analysis of the Totem as a guide, stroke by stroke, she worked the dust into the rune lines already drawn in her body.

>[Minai: 4% → 41%.]

The ghost-faced spider eggs were a pale grey colour, cold as pebbles from the bottom of a river. Each one had a single faint marking on its shell, like a tiny face, forever screaming. They rustled like paper as they broke beneath her fingers, dissolving into the lines.

>[Minai: 41% → 69%.]

[Materials: 2 of 4 used.]

[Remaining: Silver-Trace Ink (trial reward), Demon-Face Flower.]

Tally sounded pleased that the pharmacology had paid for itself...again.

* * *

The sky hadn't changed in nine days. It was still an unbroken lid of flat grey, and looking up at it for too long made your chest feel tight.

A small yellow bird landed on the green vine which trailed over the lip of stone at the mouth of the cave. It hopped along the vine, stopping to cock its head every so often, its eyes like two bright black pebbles.

It was the first bright colour Maureen had seen in days. It looked harsh against the wet grey of the stone slope, like a drop of yellow paint splattered on a dirty dishrag. It was also the first bird she'd seen anywhere on this realm's slopes, where nothing ever sang.

The oriole's next hop took it across the ward line. There was a flash of grey light as the power spread out from under the stone, forming a ring around the little bird before it could open its wings to flee.

[Ward: tripped. Bird, by sight.]

[First intruder on this realm in the last nine days. Mass approximately one ounce. Or, roughly the weight of a spoon.]

Maureen spoke a few syllables of the line-tongue, feeling Tally pull her vowel back further in her throat. The ring of grey light flickered and faded.

Before the oriole even realized it was still alive, Maureen had it in her hand. It lay perfectly rigid in her palm, too terrified to struggle.

The little bird weighed almost nothing at all. Its feathers were dry and warm to the touch. Its heart beat fast against her skin, like the rapid tapping of a fingertip on a table.

“Little thing,” Maureen said, laughing in Jack’s rasp.

She spread her fingers, letting the creature go.

“Fly.”

There was a flash of yellow, and then there was nothing.

* * *

*You are smiling. I am logging this.*

*Tally, log that as a bird, please.*

[Maureen Olsen. Third-Class Wizard Apprentice. Strength 10.4. Agility 10.2. Constitution 10.6. Spirit 11.2. Mana 11.2. Minai Totem: 69%.]

Snap.

The pale blue panel unfolded in front of her eyes.

Body . . .

Tally ran through it line by line, comparing her stats against the inheritance standards.

Strength . . . met.

Agility . . . met.

Constitution . . . met.

Spirit . . . past ten by 1.2.

Mana . . . past ten by 1.2.

The gap that had been a mountain back in Gregory’s library was gone.

Maureen stood and stretched, feeling her spine pop as her back arched. Her body felt light . . . and dense at the same time. Like a sword that had finally been tempered all the way through.

*The mountain is behind you now,* Tally said.

*There’s always another mountain.*

*Yes, but this one is behind you.*

The second numeral on her hand read 1, sitting next to her total of 12 points.

[Exit: 1 day.]

Tally laid a blue route over the stitched-together collection of fourteen muster maps, leading back to where they’d first arrived.

Maureen unpicked the wards, removing them line by line so not even a trace of their existence would remain in the stone, then slung her pack over her shoulders and headed down the ridge.

* * *

She crossed paths with more apprentices, again and again. A pair on the ridge just ahead of hers, both robed in Hader white. A lone black-robe crossing a stream in the valley below. Each time, both sides paused for long moments, hands resting on the hilts of their swords, before turning and backing away without a word. Tally kept the tally for her.

[Sightings: 4. Contact: 0.]

*We can all see the same number on our own hands,* it pointed out. *Nine out of ten is dead already. No reason to kill any more of them.*

But she found that her own hand was resting on her hilt each time, before she was even aware of doing it. There was no more need, but the reflex remained. This was the realm’s lesson, burned into every surviving arm.

Eventually she made her way well away from the trail, dropping down into a hollow where a narrow stream ran over pale stones. She looked both ways up the banks, staying perfectly still for a long moment, listening.

[Witnesses: 0, by sight and sound.] [I cannot see behind that rock.]

Maureen turned to check behind the rock, as well. “That’s enough, Jack,” she said, her voice low and rasping—the voice she would not have to use again. “Time to put Jack the Ripper back in the box. Time I was myself again.”

She reached into her pocket and pulled out the green vial. It felt smooth and cool in her hand. She squeezed and the glass shattered with a pop, releasing a cloud of green smoke that rolled upward, swallowing her whole.

It smelled like crushed stems and vinegar, stung her eyes until they streamed, and felt cool and damp against her skin, like a river fog. Tally ran the pale-blue muscle map backwards across her face and throat, the lines it had built unraveling one by one, each released with its own call.

There was a wet squelching inside the green fog, as the cartilage slid back beneath her cheekbones, her jaw drawing in with an ache like biting into ice.

Then suddenly all the lines gave way together, and her throat opened up with a cough. The cough came out in her own voice. Higher pitched than Jack’s, not as rough or deep.

*There you are,* said Tally.

The green fog began to break up, drifting away on the current. She knelt down and plunged her head under the water. The cold shocked her for a moment, then her scalp tingled as the dye let go. Brown tendrils spun away from her head, curling in the current, clouding the crystal water in slow-motion plumes that thinned as they went until they vanished completely over the pale stones.

In the still pool behind the flat rock a face looked up at her. Black hair, cropped raggedly where she’d sawn it off with the knife, framed a pale face with black eyes. Not Jack. Not the fourteen-year-old who’d stood in the basin of the Dark Forest. This face was older, more defined, a little strange.

She reached out a hand and touched her nose. It was a striking face, one that would make someone look twice, even if they didn’t know why. It was her face.

“Maureen,” she said, trying it out. Then she laughed quietly to herself. “Maureen Olsen. This is me.”

[Hair: black. Length: cropped.]

*Jack had short hair too,* pointed out Tally.

*So do I now.*

*Half the knights in the Dark Forest had short hair.*

Tally logged the fact anyway, marking it a risk.

She took out the black robe she’d worn in the Dark Forest and pulled it on over her head. Then she stuffed the singed grey cloak back under the stream stones where no one would find it. The robe smelled like dye and someone else’s old sweat. The same thick wool Emily had handed out years ago. After nine days as Jack the robe felt more like a uniform she’d forgotten she owned.

* * *

The clearing was already filled with people when she reached it at dusk. They were spread wide across the arrival point, but the orders had drawn themselves into tight knots of colour. She joined the outer edge of the black robes.

No one talked. Every hand hovered near a weapon. A cough from the other side of the clearing sounded as loud as a pot dropped on the stone floor in the sweet, heavy air.

Tally identified Bevis’s fiery red hair near the front of the black robes. His eyes passed over Maureen Olsen’s face without stopping. *He’s never seen this face,* she told Tally silently.

*Nor have you, until an hour ago at the stream.*

She sat out the night with her back against a tree, unmoving. At dawn the numeral beside her points had burned down to nothing.

[Exit: today.]

The air rippled in the middle of the clearing. The grey of the trial realm hazed open to reveal a gate that tore itself into existence with a sound like cloth ripping. Everyone knew what it was.

Maureen Olsen ran a dull check on the Seal through the cloth.

[Seal: glow jagged. Recharge: none.]

Tally had told her it didn’t climb any more. She kept her hand off the cloth.

No words passed between them, but it was understood who would go first. Maureen Olsen waited for the strongest to pass by, then stepped into the line somewhere in the middle. She kept her expression blank and walked at an average pace.

It was like a film of cold water washing over her skin as she went through. Then sunlight hit her face. Real sunlight. Warmth. The smell of trampled grass.

Outside, nothing had changed. The gathering ground lay ahead exactly as they’d left it. The leaders of the orders still hung suspended in the air, each wrapped in their own haze of coloured light.

Tally counted the survivors as they emerged from the gate. [Survivors: ~55] Men and women of every order, all with weapons in hand. Tally tagged each source of aura above as a high-band emitter, but it couldn’t give her anything finer than that.

Jason turned his heavy beard towards Augurit. He didn’t even try to hide the sneer. “Augurit. Your Dark Forest came out a little thin this time, didn’t they?”

Augurit’s normally mild face had gone grey and set. “Over six hundred went in, Jason.” His voice was quiet, almost apologetic. “Count what’s come out before you laugh.”

She watched the leaders’ eyes move over the knots of people below, mentally ticking them off. The sum was far worse than nine in ten. Alka’s easy Blue Ring smile, the one Tally had filed beside Frey Olivia’s, was stiff at the corners.

Then Jason’s voice exploded in the air. “Varo! Where’s Varo? Why hasn’t he come out? My granddaughter’s barely married to him!”

She kept her eyes on the grass. Varo’s last scream, trapped inside the black wind, was still on file. His worm was now powder, worked into the lines under her skin.

Tally flagged Jason’s knuckles turning white on the head of his cane. That was his tell, four times in under a minute.

“And her,” Jason said, his voice dropping in volume. “She’s gone too. Dead in there. This is going to be trouble...”

The veiled girl, Anna, Tally supplied. Trouble for a man like Jason meant someone above him. Her pulse jumped once. Tally logged the spike and said nothing else.

Jason’s face darkened until it was as black as a storm cloud. Particles of fire surged out from his body, swirling around him so quickly that the air began to shake with their passage. The flames twisted tighter and tighter around his body, until they became a tornado of searing heat that roared on his skin.

The heat rolled across the field, reaching her even from this distance. The grass flattened in a widening circle around him, and the apprentices closer to him stumbled backwards with their arms raised to shield their faces. She took one ordinary step back, along with everyone else.
