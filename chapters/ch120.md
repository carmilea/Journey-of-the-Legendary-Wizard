The courtyard fell silent after Maffie. Slow drips from the carvings fell in soft ticking, like rain drops falling into puddles. The frost still clung to the dusty leaf-carved columns. Maureen could feel her knuckles tingling where her fire-wrapped fists had broken through Maffie’s ice. She could hear her heart beating in her ears, but it sounded slow, because she wanted it to be slow.

Ace dragged himself out from behind a column, still putting as little weight on his left leg as possible. “Rich…Richard… Wizard Richard, I… I…” He tried to give her a smile that was somehow worse than if he’d been crying.

Tally laid an amber outline over Ace’s favoured left leg, and the shake in his hands. *He’s got nothing left. You know that.*

*Yes, I know.*

*I’m just saying it so that it’s on record that you know.*

Maureen looked at him once, and said nothing. A ball of black-red flame ignited within him, and Ace screamed, rolling across the marble and batting at himself with his hands. A few breaths later, there was nothing left on the stone floor, not even ash.

[Mana: 6.5 → ~4.5]

*And your pulse didn’t move at all this time either. Just like with Lauren. I’m writing this down, for both of us.*

The air smelled like scorched honey and sulphur. All six of the Black Spirit Society apprentices knelt in a line, and they all flinched at the same time. Like a herd of deer, when one catches the scent of the wolf. One of them gave a small, dry retch.

The thin apprentice broke first. “Mercy… honoured Wizard, please, we only did what he said…” Next to him, another of the men’s knees buckled, and he sat down hard on the cold stones.

Maureen looked at them carefully, as though she were considering their merits at a market stall. Purple-red sashes around their waists, the colour Maffie favoured. Knees of their trousers still wet from where they’d knelt on the dripping frost, hands that had collected the debts of others. She looked at them and felt nothing she could find.

She raised her hand. A tongue of black-red flame flashed into existence above her palm, enough for all six of them. Her fingers twitched closed, but then stopped, half open.

Tally spoke before her fingers closed over the flame.

*They all looked to the east door when you mentioned Maffie. People look towards where the money is. All of them did.*

*So?*

*So they can walk you there.*

*I said walk.*

The flame retreated back into her palm.

“Ri—Wizard Richard?” Joanna asked from the edge of the scorched oval, as if she hadn’t quite caught up to the situation yet.

Maureen turned Richard’s plain face towards the girl, then used his low flat voice to reply. “Joanna. I’m stepping out for a while. I’ll be back.”

She turned away without another word and walked to the gate. Behind her, the Black Spirit Society men scrambled to their feet and fell in line without being told. The alternative had just been reduced to ash on the marble floor.

In a blink, the courtyard behind her emptied. Only the shattered flagstones and the loose shutter that banged against the wall remained to show that anything had happened. The pile of magic stones lay just where Maffie had scattered them, three hundred individual points of light among the splintered wood of the smashed box. The Jadeleaf apprentices were still standing there, looking at each other with no idea what had just happened.

* * *

They crossed the wizard tract with the six men walking ahead. It was like a funeral procession. Mortals on the street knew better than to get in the way of something like this. They stepped aside and kept stepping, giving the processional party three full paces of clearance before they dared step back into place. The iron studs on the door of the society’s base were furred with rime.

There were fifteen more men inside the long room. Most sat around a table, rolling dice and drinking from glasses that smelled of spilled spirits. They rose to their feet when they saw who was walking behind their fellows, but not one of them reached for a weapon or even moved to block the doorway.

Maureen paused in the entryway, taking her baseline. Every pulse at a throat was visible, every shift of weight outlined by the movement of clothing. *I have them now,* Tally said. *All twenty-one. You can start whenever you like.*

The heavyset man with the ring of keys shifted uncomfortably under Maureen’s gaze. “Where does Maffie keep what’s his?” she asked.

“The cellar, Wizard,” he said. “Everything’s down in the cellar, I swear it.”

*That one word made him sweat,* Tally noted. *If he was going to lie, that’s where I’d look.*

Maureen didn’t look at the man’s face, though. He was standing with his back to the black slate hearth at the far end of the long room. She watched his feet carefully. His right heel was turning away from the hearth, like a man who didn’t want to stand on something. *No,* she corrected Tally. *He told me it was the cellar because he wanted me to be looking there.* She walked past the man, heading toward the hearth. Behind the slab of black slate, Tally’s all-around sense detected a cold knot of density.

*Fine,* Tally admitted grudgingly. *You got me there.*

The frost ferns had spread across the plasterwork surrounding the hearth, delicate as lace. Through Tally’s lens, the fine patterns of ice became filaments of gold, with a hard knot of ice-school density at every node where they crossed.

*Maffie tied his ward the way he fought,* Tally commented. *Tight, cold, and rushed. The left side is slacker than the right.*

Maureen nodded, focusing her attention on the left side of the weave. That was where she could find the most slack to start picking the thing apart, thread by thread. [Mana: ~4.5 → ~4.0]

The black slate swung inward easily under her grip. The niche behind it was filled with stacks of stone cards, leather bags filled with loose stones, a small velvet pouch, and several jars of ice-school materials that chilled her glove when she touched them.

She fanned the cards out across the flagstones. They were cold, thin, and each one was stamped with some kind of marking. She caught herself leaning over them, like the little slum girl in the Skran treasury. The scent of lamp oil was still in her nose.

[Low-grade cards: 9 (×1,000). Loose: ~650. Superior: 1. Magic stones, all columns: ~150 → ~9,900]

*Best day of my accounting life!* Tally declared.

*You said that last time,* Maureen replied silently.

*And the time before that, and the time before that. But it keeps being true!*

Maureen smiled and set the cards aside. Underneath them, she found a blood-red token and Maffie’s leather notebook.

[Item: token. Material: no match. Pulse: 1 per 40 s.]

The token wasn’t metal or wood. It felt slightly warm against her particle sense, throbbing with a faint pulse every forty seconds.

*Hey, Maureen.* Tally spoke up. *I’d like to see every spread of this thing before you stick anything into the Void Dimensional Pouch on your waist.*

*All his handwriting? Why?*

*Because men write down what they’re afraid of.*

*Fair enough.* Maureen turned every page under Tally’s pale-blue flicker, then tipped the entire niche—velvet pouch and all—into the grey Void Dimensional Pouch at her waist. From the heavyset man’s desk, she took the Society’s roster book. Tally quickly compared it against her density count.

[Roster: 23. Present: 21. Missing: 2, named.]

“Downstairs,” Maureen said to the twenty-one men. “All of you.”

They complied, probably thinking that she was sending them to some kind of prison. Maureen followed them to the top of the steps, then sent a single spread of black-red Purgatory Fire down into the darkness below.

[Mana: ~4.0 → ~2.0]

The conflagration rolled down the steps and filled the cellar from wall to wall.

The sounds from below stopped very abruptly. A wave of heat washed up the stairs to where Maureen stood at the top, and there was nothing left to smell. She tossed the roster book down into the black-red light, then shut the door to the cellar from the hallway above. The townhouse fell silent, and no mark remained upon it.

Before dusk, she found the two missing names. One was staying in a room above a chandler’s shop; the other was sitting at a gaming table. Each man died under a knight’s strength and Freya’s slender sword, without a single spell cast.

*Logged. Not shown.*

Maureen turned and walked away from the Black Spirit Society’s base, leaving it as quiet and empty as the day she’d first arrived. Tally filed away the last entry in her ledger with a flourish.

Black Spirit Society: closed

*You’re getting low on mana,* Tally warned. [Mana: ~2.0] *Isn’t exactly what I’d consider comfortable, especially when you’re about to eat dinner with a house that just saw you murder one of its own Official Wizards.*

Maureen nodded as she walked back toward the Jadeleaf Ring, keeping time with the Silver Light rhythm. Three long breaths, one short.

* * *

She re-entered the Ring through the same gate she had left by, just as the light outside finally failed. The hall smelled of tallow and cold mutton fat, the candle flames flickering and leaning sideways with the wind blowing down from the high windows. Senge rose halfway out of his chair to greet her, his grey-white hair combed flat and neat over the unnatural flush of his face. He gave her the formal bow between wizards.

“Wizard Richard,” he said, his voice hoarse. “This time… thank you for stepping in.”

Tally read him. The flush on his cheekbones, his shallow breathing. His density was thinner than her baseline from the first day at the Ring. *A man of his age does not breathe like that if he can help it,* Tally noted.

Maureen returned the bow, equal to equal, before she sat down. Senge did not ask where she’d been all afternoon, and she didn’t tell him. The other apprentices around the table kept their eyes carefully off “Richard”, and one even dropped a spoon, turning white as the clatter sounded loud in the quiet hall. At the other end of the long table, Christine, the blonde Jadeleaf apprentice who used to sneer at “Richard”, sat with her eyes locked on her plate.

Dinner was thin soup, some black bread, and a joint of mutton carved with suspicious care. The slices were cut thinly to make the meat stretch around the table. Throughout the meal, Senge kept raising a white silk cloth to his mouth, and each time it came away red.

*Six times in an hour.*

*Weeks. Not months, unless something better than what the Ring has in store gets to him. Medium confidence. I’d like to be wrong though.*

*I know you would Tally.*

*About this sort of thing anyway.*

Maureen’s throat closed for half a breath, and then she filed the sound. Like she had once filed Old John’s cough, in the shack in the slum. It was a sound to count, not respond to.

An Official Wizard who hadn’t climbed in years, and at his age? He wouldn’t get up from a wound like that. Not from Maffie’s hand.

*With the upkeep on the Totem running at less than her recovery rate, the ~2.0 should be back by morning. It shouldn’t cost you a drop to maintain Richard’s face until then. You can do that much without effort, can’t you?*

*Yes, Tally.*

Maureen kept her jaw loose throughout dinner, just to be sure.

After the plates were taken away, Senge offered Maureen a bed. “The east rooms are aired, Wizard Richard. If you’d stay the night?”

“I have my own house, Steward.” She rose from the table as did the others. The apprentices scattered to their rooms while Senge made his way slowly up the stairs to his chambers. Maureen took the garden path which ran out to the gate.

* * *

Frost crunched beneath her boots. Ahead of her a flowering tree stood stark against the rime frost. No spring thaw had yet melted this corner of Emilia, and there was no way that the tree should be in full bloom. This was the Jadeleaf Ring’s own doing, one of its plant-school crafts. The tree’s pale petals shimmered blue in the ward-light. Their sweet scent carried in the air, but the warmth of its perfume felt wrong in the chill that could bite the skin.

Tally found Joanna before Maureen’s eyes did. There was a glow on Tally’s sense, like a coal buried in ash. *Your seed is behind the flowering tree,* she told Maureen. *And so is the girl wrapped around it.*

Maureen halted on the path. “Joanna. Come out.”

Nothing moved for a long moment, then Joanna stepped out from behind the flowering tree. She bowed low. “Wizard Richard.”

Joanna’s lips moved several times without sound, trying to start, then failing. The red flush that Maffie’s slap had left was now just a blotch on the side of her face. Her hands kept twisting at the bottom hem of her robe. Tally noted the pulse fluttering at 140 beats per minute in the base of her throat.

“What is it?” Maureen asked.

Joanna laughed at herself, a small and rueful sound. “We have a request, Wizard Richard. I’d ask that you hear us out.”

The girl explained quickly, speaking aloud this time. Every three years, the Emilia Wizard Academy Alliance held a tournament. It was called the Academy Ranking Tournament, and the results would determine each member academy’s share of resources for the following three years. Only Official Wizards could fight in the tournament. The Steward could not stand, let alone fight. “So we would ask you to fight in our name, as the Jadeleaf Ring’s honorary professor.”

*Official Wizards only. Every three years. Resource shares. Joanna has rehearsed this,* Tally said. *She timed that speech perfectly. Her breath ran out right at the last word.*

*Yes, I noticed,* Maureen told Tally silently.

Maureen let the silence sit for a moment before she responded. “I spared you because you said you could put me inside the Alliance. Now you want me fighting for your rank? Does that seem right to you?”

Joanna’s breath caught in her throat. She stood still, as if she’d reached into her purse in front of a seller and found it empty.

“But, Wizard Richard,” Joanna stammered, “it was an accident. I never thought the Steward would be—”

Maureen shook her head. “I’m sorry, Joanna.” It was an honest refusal, nothing more.

“Wait. Please, wait.”

Joanna pulled a black bundle out of her purse. The bundle was humming with wizard energy, the Ring’s treasures kept through all these years.

“All of it, for one fight. Please.”

Joanna’s hands were clumsy on the knot that held the bundle closed. As she finally got the knot loose, the contents spilled across the stone table. A few things gave off heat, like a banked stove left to smoulder overnight. One was a stone with a silver shimmer. The rest were small jars that clinked together.

Joanna pointed them out as fast as she could. “Burning Flame Grass. It’s a strong aid for anyone with a fire talent. Bright Silver Fusion Stone. It helps a wizard sense the elemental particles around her. It makes them sharper.” She continued down the line.

Tally identified each item by plate from Gregory’s pharmacology, keeping it to one line apiece. *Good,* Tally said. *All good. None of them what you’re waiting for.*

Maureen’s eyes stopped moving.

She picked up a fist-sized crystal. A tiny purple snake lay inside, its ruby eyes glittering in the ward-light. The snake looked perfectly formed, down to the smallest scale, as if it had been carved from purple stone rather than made of crystal. The crystal was cold, even through Maureen’s glove.

Her hand went very still.

Maureen turned the crystal toward the ward-light, narrowing her eyes. The pale petals stirred slightly where they lay on the stone table, tickling her wrist.

Tally fell silent.

The seal was wizard-made, designed for preservation. There was no tamper ward.

[Purple Wood Snake. Wizard-made. Non-venomous. Pharmacology: body / field harmonisation.]

Match found.

[plate + Void Eclipse layer-2 list]

*Say it,* Maureen told her.

*One of three,* Tally said. *Two of three, if you include the stone. Your hand has gone very still.*

*Log that as nothing,* Maureen ordered.

*Logged as everything.* [Void Eclipse, layer 2: 2 of 3. Missing: Necro Flower.] *How to draw tears from a snake sealed in crystal. No data.*

Joanna followed where Maureen was looking. “A Steward of ours who knew pharmacology almost died to get that. None of us since has known what it is for.”

Maureen finally looked up at Joanna.

She was beautiful. That was a simple fact. Just as much a fact was the desperation in Joanna’s eyes.

Maureen remembered this girl stepping in front of Maffie’s ice, telling her to run.

Tally did not push. Instead, the old entry reappeared before Maureen.

>Twice now someone has stepped between you and danger.

*It is a trade,* Maureen silently told Tally.

*It is a fair trade,* Tally said. *I will write “fair” into the record. You may write in the other word yourself if you wish.*

“Who do you usually face?” Maureen asked aloud. “How strong are they?”

“Ordinary Official Wizards,” Joanna answered aloud. Her voice was quick and eager. “About the Steward’s level. We’re weak. We’re nearly out of the Alliance. We only want to keep our place, the same as before.”

“Fine,” Maureen said. “I accept.”

Joanna’s voice cracked as she spoke.

“Thank you, Lord M— Wizard Richard. Professor Richard.”

All the breath seemed to leave Joanna at once. She rested a hand on the round stone table to steady herself. One of the flowering tree’s pale petals stuck to her knuckles, but she didn’t notice.

Maureen regarded her through her Richard face. “Do not thank me. The snake matters to me. This is an equal exchange.” The corner of the wizard’s mouth lifted in what might have been a smile. Maureen slid the crystal into the Void Dimensional Pouch and gave Joanna a street address for the morning of the tournament.

34 Connpa Street.

Then she turned and started back down the path toward the garden gate.

Without turning around, she called out, “Joanna!”

Maureen tossed something over her shoulder. There was a faint greenish glow as the object arced through the air and Joanna caught it. The glow washed over Joanna’s hand for a moment, leaving it clean and well rested.

“A gift for the Jadeleaf Ring,” Maureen said.

Joanna studied the vial in her hand while Tally identified its particle density.

[Muster healing potions: 3 → 2]

It was one of Augurit’s three muster healing potions. The same potion that had been sitting in Maureen’s inner fold since the trial.

*Dark Forest issue,* Tally noted. *It will leave a faint trail.*

*Let it,* Maureen silently told her.

When Maureen reached the hedge, she passed Christine standing in shadow. The younger girl’s eyes were locked on the glowing vial in her sister’s hand.

Behind Maureen, Joanna called out, “Christine!”

Christine’s voice floated back, thin and fierce. “I’ll become an Official Wizard. I will!”

The sound of running steps faded into the night’s darkness.

Maureen had caught a glimpse of Christine’s heel, slightly turned out. She’d seen the younger girl bite her lip as she drew in a sharp breath before speaking.

Tally read the body language. *Envy. That much was obvious. There is something else beneath that.*

*What?* Maureen asked.

*Not a crush, before you ask.*

*I wasn’t going to ask.*

*You were going to.*

*No, I wasn’t.*

*Yes, you were.*

Maureen reached the gate and let it swing shut behind her. She stood for a moment in the chilly Emilia night, then began walking down the street toward Connpa Street and the bedroom ward waiting for her there. She owed no one.
