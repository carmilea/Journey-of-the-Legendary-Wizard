The carriage rolled and jolted along the dry scrubland road. Weak sunlight shone on the map that lay across Maureen’s knees.

Four magic stones bought her this map in the Trading District. The Dark Forest and surrounding principalities were rendered in fine detail.

Route? Tally asked. It overlaid its data on the parchment.

[Redbud: NE, ~3 months. 27 provinces. Macolin: SE Redbud.]

Maureen traced a line from the Dark Forest to Redbud, then down to Macolin. The wizard orders controlled the individual Passise principalities in all but name. They pulled the strings to keep the resources flowing their way while allowing the royal houses to maintain some voice in the running of things. More a partnership than a leash.

Macolin Province first then, the Court of Marquis Umas. She could sell off some of her potions en route at the various wizard gathering points Tally had flagged up during its research. She hadn’t managed to sell much of her stock in the Dark Forest.

Gathering points highlighted for route. Tally displayed the relevant data over the map.

You could have just memorised that and saved me four stones, she thought silently.

I did memorise it. You paid four stones for the paper it was printed on.

She grunted and looked back at the crates of stoppered potions strapped to the rear of the carriage behind the bench. I wonder how much I can get for those outside?

Her mind felt clear and sharp. Almost content.

* * *

She was still calculating potion prices when faint chanting drifted out from the trees to the left of the road.

Incantations, by sound: 3+. Left treeline. Tally matched one fragment of the incantations against its heard-spell list.

Simons. Blast Fireball. More incantations reached Maureen’s ears before there was a hiss in the air from a crossbow bolt. Then the forest erupted with ice spikes, black mist and flame.

Tally read the thin colours of the spells. Held back. Take it.

The force of the blast knocked Maureen off the bench and into the trees. Bark exploded inches from her head, coppery blood filling her mouth. She rolled over into the brush and lay still, her heart beating loud in her ears.

[Damage: ribs bruised, left forearm scorched.]

Stay down, Tally said.

Wasn’t planning on going anywhere else, Maureen answered silently.

Black robed apprentices appeared between the trees, walking towards her.

“Held back because Young Master Jay wants you alive,” one of the apprentices said as he approached. “Saved the carriage too, cost a potion-maker’s fortune. Pity we won’t be able to use it ourselves.”

“Simon,” the pimple faced leader of the group called out. “Take Fess and the other two, grab the brat. Walton and I will hold the carriage back a few paces.”

Six. Spreading. Two to the carriage. Footsteps crunched closer through the dead leaves.

Now.

“Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa.”

Red thorns snapped around her body, drawing into place to form an armour, blood red light spreading from where she lay. Thin vines burst through the dead leaf litter, binding all six apprentices where they stood.

Armour gleaming in the midday sun, Maureen Olsen rose from the brush and walked towards them.

“So it's Jay.”

The pimple faced man shrieked “Enchanted item! It's an enchanted item!”

“You have a good eye.”

Who leads them? Maureen asked Tally silently.

>Pimples. Centre.

How many seconds?

>One. Two. Three. Four.

Maureen Olsen snapped her fingers.

The thin vines tightened with a wet crunching sound, reducing five black robed apprentices to vegetable pulp in less than a heartbeat. There was no sound from any of the apprentices as they died, but the air suddenly smelled of iron and sap. The vines that bound the leader remained wrapped around his legs alone.

Seven seconds. Release.

The armour fell away from Maureen Olsen and the vines binding her dropped away, still pinning the leader’s legs to where he stood. [Release: 6.8 s. Glow: dimmer.]

No data on reserve, Tally said.

Maureen Olsen regarded the pimple faced apprentice with a cool expression. “I think you know what I'm going to ask.” she said softly.

“No! I don't know anything! Honest!” the apprentice babbled, his eyes flicking eastwards.

>Eyes east.

“Where is he?”

“He's just ahead! There's a clearing! He's waiting there!”

Maureen Olsen nodded once, her lips moving silently before a deep red Blast Fireball erupted from her hand to explode against the apprentice's head. [Mana: 5 → 3.5] Jay is east, Tally said, ignoring the kill.

Maureen Olsen dragged the remains of the pimple faced apprentice into the ditch beside the road, then kicked earth over the corpse. The smell of burnt hair was sickening, and her bruised ribs caught with every breath.

* * *

Maureen Olsen tied the carriage behind a large fold in the featureless grey rock, then crept through the thorny scrub, the longbow clutched in one hand. The clearing was just ahead, and through the brush she could see the pit cut into the raw earth. The lip of the pit held faintly glowing sigils, and the scent of rose water was evident on the air.

>Apprentices: 14. Third-Class: 3, by calibration.

Tally noted Jay's presence amongst them, dressed in fine silks, as well as Wolf, recognising the burly man's yellow beard and small eyes.

“Why aren't they back yet?” Wolf asked. “Simon's group should be here by now.”

Jay snorted. “If they can't deal with one potion maker who can't even fight, then we don't need them. They'll be back.”

“But why aren't any from the family doing anything? Why does it have to fall to us?”

“Enough, Wolf!” Jay snarled. “Grandfather Barok and the others are too busy being full wizards to do anything about the bitch! So I had to sneak out and hire these apprentices instead! There's two or three Third-Class among them!”

Maureen Olsen, Adrian turned down Barok when his projection visited the lab.

Yes, Maureen Olsen thought silently.

One group of apprentices dragged a large cage into the clearing, filled with small black snakes, their fangs almost invisible. The cage was placed near the lip of the pit, and Jay laughed at the sight of the slithering creatures.

“Are the pain and permanence spells fixed in the pit?” he asked.

One of the other apprentices nodded. “Yeah, we checked. Whoever falls in there won't be able to move a muscle, and they'll feel every bite... well, a lot more than once anyway. By the time they die, they'll have felt each bite dozens of times over.”

>Pain amplification. Permanence (immobilising).

>Black-thread horned-fang snakes: 40+, by sight.

Tally matched the sigils against its library entries, while counting the snakes. The black-thread horned-fang snakes writhed and hissed as they filled the cage, and Maureen Olsen's fingers turned white as she gripped the longbow, an arrow nocked.

* * *

“Young Master Jay,” Wolf called out from beside him, “You are mighty beyond measure! Those who would offend you must lack eyes! They will die for nothing...”

Maureen Olsen drew the longbow, and Tally traced a pale blue arc through the air between the trees and Wolf's throat. Wind from the left.

Mid-word.

[Arrows: 22 → 21.]

The arrow took Wolf through the throat, and he gurgled loudly, his eyes widening with shock before he collapsed heavily to the ground.

He died flattering his master. Tally's voice was warm and dry in her head.

A fitting last word, Maureen responded silently.

She lowered the longbow and stepped out of the cover of the trees. “So you wanted me dead that badly, Jay?” She called out across the clearing.

Jay gaped at her, then demanded, “Where’s Simon and his group?”

“What do you think?”

The scent of rose water mixed with the smell of freshly turned earth and the musk of the snakes hung in the air as Maureen walked towards Jay.

“You fool!” Jay shouted, “You should have run when you had the chance! Apprehend her! Wound her badly!”

Maureen spoke softly. “Then I guess I’ll have to try something new on you all.”

There was movement all around the clearing, lips moving silently, and Maureen felt her particle sense thicken, the air feeling heavy before a storm.

Up.

Rank Zero spells blanketed the ground, and trees and soil erupted into the air. Maureen leapt through the flying dust cloud, speaking the full phrase of the Seal's power. [Seal: 0.0 s] Blood red armour formed over her body, thorny vines covering her from head to toe, only her eyes visible through the mass of plant matter.

High in the air, wrapped in blood red armour, Maureen flung black powder from her palm. She began to chant in the language of Ancient Andas.

“Endless wind, surging darkness, let me show your might;

Tear apart every offender before me!

Dark Wind Ring!”

Tally ran the combined model simultaneously with Maureen’s chanting, instructing her to pull half a beat early on the Field’s wrap. Maureen followed Tally’s instructions.

The black powder evaporated into nothing. A tornado of darkness roared down from the sky, and the apprentices were stripped of their clothes and flesh, reduced to yellowed and cracked bone. Even the energy halos around their bodies barely slowed the force of the spell. The wind shifted, becoming gentle, and lowered Maureen to the ground. Dust settled onto the bones and torn up earth. It was a bit more than she expected.

[Dark Wind Ring: Rank Zero combined spell. Hand of Negative Energy + Mantik Death-Aura Field. Subjects: Conversion Theory of Energetics; The Dolato Conjecture; Misuan’s Course in Classification. Est. 8°, scaled from room readings.]

[Mana: 0.5]

The three surviving Third-Class apprentices ran for their lives.

Maureen pointed a finger, and vines burst out from the ground. They wrapped around the fleeing apprentices, holding them firmly in place. Their energy halos resisted the plants for a breath, but that was all it took.

“You wanted to kill me. I’m just going to ask you to die instead.”

The vines obeyed, strangling the lives from the apprentices.

Too close, Tally said.

[Release: 8.6 s.]

I know. I counted too, Maureen responded silently.

Jay fell from the vines and collapsed to his knees, his body shaking from mana exhaustion. He’d spent everything in the barrage.

Maureen stepped forward and placed the flat of the Cross Sword against Jay’s throat. A blue edge warning glowed in her eye. “Long time no see, Jay.”

“W-What was that! Some kind of enchanted item! That wasn’t even a proper spell!” Jay stammered as he held up his hands in supplication. “You can’t do this to me! I’m of direct-line Olivia blood!”

“Yes, I’ve made one mistake here already,” said Maureen, pressing the Cross Sword harder against Jay’s throat. “I don’t want to make it twice.”

“Before, I didn’t know what the rules were. I didn’t know about the wizard world.” Maureen patted Jay’s cheek as she spoke softly to him. “But now? Now I know. Now I’ve learned, and now I’m leaving the Dark Forest. So you understand what I mean, right?”

Her voice never lost its softness, nor did it rise in volume. It remained very calm, as though she were having a friendly conversation with an old acquaintance. The colour gradually faded out from Jay’s face.

* * *

Her hands were still trembling after two uses of the Seal. Stage it as an underground race raid.

Its rumour file and Barok's coalition news suggested those raids would hit outposts. Tally listed off what might give her away. The stripped bones, the vine-crushed pulp, and the pit sigils.

Then we hide them.

She bound Jay with his own silk sash, and smashed the sigil anchor stones until both arrays went dark. She also tore apart the pit edges with her knightly strength, making it look as though the pit had been destroyed by brute force.

Maureen raked the bones and pulp into a gully, then caved the bank over them. Her ribs burned with every pull.

Tally provided the faint blue sheen on her edge before her eyes, [Coating: confirmed.]

She cut Jay deep, until he couldn't move any more, then threw him into the pit.

"Goodbye, Jay." She said, tossing the cage into the hole after him. It broke on the bottom, and the snakes rushed towards his blood. He screamed for her to save him.

She walked away without looking back, and his screams echoed off the rock.

[Screams: ceasing… 0.]

Confirmed.

Thanks, Tally.

You're welcome. You left no enemies alive behind you this time.

She let out her breath in a rush, climbing up onto the driver's bench. There was still dirt from the gully beneath her fingernails, but the black horse stamped impatiently at the rock fold. The carriage was unharmed, but her hands trembled as they gripped the reins.

Rest, Maureen. You've used the Seal twice in one day. Its glow is already beginning to dim. That's a legitimate cause for concern if you intend to continue on the road. Tally's voice sounded warm inside her head, concerned.

I'll rest at dusk. I promise. She answered silently.

The road fell silent below a mountain peak, not a sound of a bird to be heard. Maureen's hand dropped to the Cross Sword at her waist.

A sound like a giant forge bellows rolled down the mountainside. She craned her neck and saw a figure standing atop the peak, balanced precariously upon the head of a phantom serpent. Then it was gone.

"Aaron…" She whispered.

The faint smell of sulphur and fire smoke drifted down the mountain. [Odour: sulphur, fire smoke. Match: Fasol serpent, close.] Tally said.

Close is not certain. It added in its own voice.

Aaron saw everything?

If he was there, he would have seen the armour.

Maureen smiled.

Smile unknown, Tally said. Cannot read smile.

Neither can I, she replied silently, flicking the reins.
