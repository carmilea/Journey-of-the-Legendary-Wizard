The air inside the closed carriage was hot. Sweat beaded on Maureen's scalp, her skin flushed red with heat. She could feel the couch getting soaked underneath her.

[Please do not move, Maureen Olsen. Whatever this is, it is coming out of you, not going into you.]

[Not going anywhere. I promise.] she replied silently.

After a long while, Maureen gasped for air, her body springing up off the couch.

Her clothes were sticking to her now, the front soaked in some black filth that was coating her skin like tar.

"Ugh. So disgusting!" She pinched her nose shut as the rotten stench filled the carriage. She climbed down.

[For the record, I have no nose, and I am grateful,] Tally said.

[What is this stuff Tally?] she asked as she looked at the filth that was dripping down her legs.

[No data Maureen Olsen. All I know is that it is leaving your body. That's all.]

It was dusk outside now and the camp fires had water heated up already. Maureen set up the large tarp used for covering the baggage behind her carriage. She carried bucket after bucket of the steaming water and began washing herself clean.

She scrubbed hard until her skin was raw red. Black water pooled in the dirt, then as more buckets poured over her, the water ran grey before finally turning clear. The cold air felt good against her clean skin.

[So this must be what they meant by 'cleansing the marrow' in Jing Sheng's novels. I wonder if I will live forever now Tally?] She asked as she dried her body with a rough towel.

[No data on immortality Maureen Olsen, but at least your hands smell better.]

[Scan. Now.]

[Maureen Olsen. Quasi-Knight. Strength 1.6, Agility 1.7, Constitution 1.7]

[More than double, Maureen Olsen. First doses are always the most potent. No second miracle with that fruit, sorry.]

[Fine.] Maureen flexed her arm, watching the tendons shift under the skin of her forearm. The new force rippling through her muscles, she allowed herself a smile.

* * *

Determined to feed the database, Maureen tried chewing and tasting every plant she saw along the road, during each stop the convoy made. Tally would analyse each one for her.

Bitter. Mild Astringent. Logged. Entry two hundred and something.

[You've already got this one, spit it out please]

Fussy eater.

[Goat. Maureen Olsen.]

Her fingers stained green from the sap of the leaves and plants she sampled, grit from the road between her teeth. The constant creaking of the harness was deafening by the end of each day. They stopped in several principalities as Scarlette recruited more wizard seeds under his agreements with the local nobility. Each time they stopped, only a single seed was delivered.

Tally kept count for Maureen.

Four stops.

Four seeds.

His reward increases with each delivered seed, Maureen thought silently.

Scarlette's face soured with each stop and he rode in silence. His mouth was a tight line beneath his white beard.

* * *

The streets were paved in the duke's seat and far busier than in Skran. The air was thick with the smells of cooking and roasting meats, the cries of peddlers and market criers. The weaponsmith's stall smelled of oiled leather and steel.

Maureen walked alone in the streets, coin from the strongbox clinking in her purse. She openly bought a Cross Sword from the weaponsmith, testing the balance in her hands.

Then she leaned in close to the man. "I would also like a sleeve crossbow," she said quietly.

The man hesitated, glancing at her.

[Eyes: darting to the street, pupils dilated] Send a porter. "I'll get it myself thank you," she said, her expression unmoving.

Maureen ignored Tally's advice and bought the sleeve crossbow herself. She kept her face straight as she paid for it, then carefully threaded the clockwork catch under her cuff, her fingers moving slowly and steadily. The spring clicked into place as she released it.

At the ironmonger, she bought a grappling claw and a length of rope. As the man counted out her change, Tally spoke up. [He has cut your change by a sliver. Tell him or do not, your decision. It has been counted.]

Maureen held out her palm expectantly. The ironmonger frowned, but added the final copper coin to her stack before handing it over.

She visited the apothecary last, buying medicines—including a hemostatic—and several antidotes. The apothecary kept his poisons locked in a cabinet behind the counter, and Maureen picked out several small glass vials, each filled with a different toxic substance.

The tinker had set out a polished brass mirror on his stall, and Maureen's reflection stared back at her, yellowed and warped by the curved metal. There was a line of darkness visible along the parting of her hair.

[Pigment change noted. Hair roots and the first finger-width of the shaft are measurably darker. Cause: no data]

Was this from the purge? she thought.

[Tally does not guess.]

Maureen frowned, then purchased a plain hood from a clothier and tied back her hair before pulling it up over her head. Then she turned and made her way back.

* * *

Richard greeted her enthusiastically when she returned to the convoy camp, and he examined her new Cross Sword with interest. It was the only thing that he noticed that was new. Maureen returned his own sword with her thanks, and then slipped away to the privacy of her carriage. Underneath the seat was an ironbound strongbox, and in her head Tally closed the books on the Skran column for another day. 2,788 gold spent so far, 212 remaining in the box. In the light of a lamp, with a half dozen cold glass vials resting on her knee, she carefully emptied their contents one by one into a single small vial. A mixed poison, its effects would be unpredictable.

What will this do? she asked Tally silently.

No data available, it responded.

You don't know?

[I only know what you know, what you see and what you touch. You're not tasting this one.]

No, I'm not planning on it.

Tally was silent as Maureen labeled the vial with the Ancient Fusiform lettering. [Nice handwriting.]

Maureen stoppered the vial, then sealed it with wax using fingers that didn't quiver at all, even as the thick tallow smoke stung her eyes. She carefully stowed the crossbow, claw, rope, and vials within easy reach. Caution felt good, like a warm blanket.

* * *

Just over a month since she'd started practicing breathing, Maureen drilled Basic Swordsmanship. The blade hummed dully as it sliced through the air, so clean that it made the skin prickle nearby. Tally kept flagging errors now and then, but Maureen was getting the basics down pretty well. [Tally, you are becoming bored of me.] [Maureen Olsen. Lower Knight. Strength 3.9, Agility 4.2, Constitution 4.0.]

Without prompting, Tally displayed Richard's stats next to hers. [Richard. Lower Knight. Strength 3.6, Agility 3.2, Constitution 3.4. Silver Light is superior to his family's method of breathing.]

[Even if he uses his Knight Secret Arts, I can beat him?] [Yes, but don't.]

Maureen wiped away the sweat beading on her brow and glanced across the little glade. The grey, papery wasp nest buzzed quietly next to the white wild daisies in the grass.

Carey walked up with a grin. "Maureen, could you do me a favor?"

"What?"

"I'm trying to get some wild daisies for Ellena, but there are too many horsetail wasps. Could you pull down their nest and run with it?"

She frowned, looking from Carey back to the nest. "Carey, those things hurt when they sting you. It hurts a lot. You can even die if you're not careful."

[Tally, do you know anything about their venom?]

[No data available.

Maureen remembered the time Old John had gotten stung by one. His hand had puffed up for days.

"You won't kill anyone with Master Scarlette here," Carey said. "It just hurts, that's all."

"I don't want to."

Carey sneered. "You knights aren't as tough as you think. When I'm a wizard, I'll outstrip all of you."

"Carey," Richard said as he walked over, "you shouldn't—"

"Shut your mouth, Richard," Carey snapped. "We're not at the Duke's house anymore. I'm not a pathetic gardener, so stop treating me like one."

[Tally, analyze Carey's status.] [Carey is shifting his weight on his heels. His vocal pitch increased by one note when Richard arrived. All signs of loud, but harmless, posturing.

Richard flushed and grasped the hilt of his sword. Carey laughed. "Want to draw steel? Come on, then. Do it."

The former gardener turned his attention to Maureen.

"You are an idiot, Maureen Olsen. A fool wasting your days in practicing a sword. What use can it possibly be?"

Maureen's eyes narrowed and she became still. Her voice came out sounding light, but the smile on her lips was cold.

"Carey, you're saying my sword practice is useless?"

[Mark Carey for strike.]

[The loose fold of his tunic at the shoulder. One palm's width left of his neck. Target height at your hilt.]

Cloth. Not flesh.

[No blood. Nothing for Master Scarlette to punish.]

Good.

"Well? Is it or isn't it?" Maureen asked as Carey opened his mouth to speak.

"Yes, it is."

Maureen drew and threw. Silver flashed in the sun. The blade thunked into the tree's rough bark, pinning Carey's loose shoulder flap against it. He hung at a slant with his toes dangling off the ground.

Carey's face went white and his lips trembled. He couldn't speak. Maureen's pulse hadn't even risen with the effort of her throw.

Maureen walked up to the dangling Carey. "Is my sword practice still useless now?"

Richard laughed with relief. "That was a fine throw, Maureen."

She shrugged, then effortlessly made her way up the trunk with a few leaps of her legs, sitting on a branch that put her at eye level with Carey.

"Well, Carey? Still want me to help you?"

"No," Carey whispered. "I was wrong."

Maureen patted the pale boy gently on the cheek with her fingertips. "Your mouth stinks, Carey. Learn to keep it shut from now on. Understand?" Carey trembled but said nothing.

Maureen jumped down lightly. "Leave him up there for a while, Richard. Let him learn the lesson." She brushed her hands together to rid them of imaginary dirt. "Come on. Let's sit for a while. It's hot today."

Richard chuckled and nodded as he helped her find a comfortable place to sit in the soft grass.

So, Tally said quietly as Maureen sat beside Richard, you enjoyed that.

I didn't enjoy it. I just taught him a lesson.

You enjoyed teaching him a lesson.

Maureen didn't deny that.
