It had been several days on the road when Maureen drove out of the Dark Forest's borderlands and onto the open farmland. The huge sails of a windmill creaked as they turned round. The fields were divided into long strips of green and gold by hedgerows or fences. Farmers worked, bent double, in the long rows. A farmer's dog ran along beside the carriage wheels, barking.

Maureen's hands relaxed their grip on the reins. The sounds of ordinary humans going about their business were the first she had heard in over two years. It felt like a dream.

[Model analysed. Transfer?] Tally said.

*Yes,* she replied silently. There was a familiar building up of pressure behind her eyes.

She snapped her fingers, and a tiny bead of sour green shot from her finger. It hit a fence post and began to hiss, sending up a thread of smoke.

*Show off,* Tally said.

*I wasn't doing anything. You did all the work. I'm just showing off for you.*

*What about the lines and the totems in that combination-spell book? Can you make anything of them?*

[Negative. Unable to analyse. Database records suggest: Ancient Era.]

*What does it say about the Ancient Era?*

[The Ancient Era saw the rise of wizard kind, with schools numbering in the thousands, each with their own speciality. These wizards conquered plane after plane, plundered the resources, split up, and planted colonies. Then there was a catastrophe, the details unknown. All powerful wizards disappeared from the planes. Many great spells were lost. A revival came a thousand years later, but this was followed by a war between wizards, the cause of which is not recorded, that nearly destroyed the wizards' lineage again.]

All that history, crammed into a few lines. All those spells, all those wonders, forgotten. All those notebooks Jing Sheng left behind, sitting on the white bench, never to be read by anyone.

Her hands tightened on the reins again.

*Long-term task. First priority. Ancient Era lines and totems.*

[Task created: Ancient Era lines and totems. Priority 1.]

*It will need more than one book.*

*Then we'll have to go looking for them.*

* * *

She wore her disguise now, the illusion of brown hair and a ring of yellow beard about her face. There was also a scar on her cheek, and a set of battle-worn leather armour over her robe. Her real Cross Sword lay beside her on the seat, the plain steel weapon resting loose in its sheath.

*It was a good job. I made it myself, you know,* Tally said in her head with its warm voice. *Simulated the spell from a speculative idea in a library miscellany, then matched it to your own illusion trace.*

[Disguise: holding. Mana drain: 0.1 / hour.]

 *Remember the illusion covers your face but not your voice,* Tally warned her.

Maureen made a low grunting noise aloud in the empty carriage.

*That's worse,* Tally told her.

*No it's not,* she replied silently.

The town ahead was called Hale City, according to her map, the closest human city to the Dark Forest. Among ordinary people, the face of a mercenary was the simplest to assume.

A high wall rose above the low buildings of the town, their roofs a patchwork of timber. Even before they reached the gates, the stink of rot and sewage reached the carriage. Maureen's shoulders were tight beneath the fake leather armour, and she kept her chin down as the gate came nearer.

At the gates, four guards wearing rusted mail were shaking down a carter for the city entry tax. They looked up as Maureen approached, and with a cringe bowed to her and waved her through without a word.

[Guards: 4. Extortion: routine.]

*The beard works,* Tally said.

*Don't get attached,* she told it silently.

*It's already attached,* Tally replied smugly.

Beyond the gate, the streets were lined with low patched timber buildings. Rubbish was heaped in the gutters, and a steady stream of sewage ran down the centre of the lane. The people who hurried past looked thin and tired.

*Two. Behind. Since the gate.* Tally had tracked them in shop windows and by their steps.

*I know,* she told it silently. *Fat sheep, lone mercenary with a carriage.*

At the best inn in Hale City she rasped out her request for the best room and the best food. The young man nodded, glancing at the two men who loitered on the street outside before turning his attention to the carriage. "Is that your carriage outside? I can have it put in the stable if you want?"

"Don't worry about it," Maureen told him.

Before she went up to her room she pulled the cork from the test tube inside the carriage, letting the Miles vipers slide out from under the seat.

Once behind the barred door, she let the illusion drop. The hot tub tasted slightly of the iron pipe that fed it. Her body was fine after the long ride, but her head was tired. She strung the bell cord across the door and armed the sleeve crossbow. *You know I cannot watch while you sleep,* Tally said.

Maureen nodded as she sank into the hot water. *That's why I left the vipers to watch the carriage.*

* * *

After an early breakfast she raised the disguise again and went down to the back yard. The carriage still stood where it had been left. The black horse snorted white steam into the cold air. Underneath the piles of dung and straw was a sweet rotting smell. Beside the carriage wheels were two small puddles of rotting fluid and one or two scraps of cloth. [Remains: 2. Probably.] *Probably is enough,* she said silently.

Maureen held out the test tube. "Wukandima!" she said, Tally correcting her tongue on the second syllable. Four or five tiny black threads whipped out from under the carriage seat, coiling inside the test tube before they could strike. Tiny black snakes, Miles vipers from Adrian's laboratory. Small, fast, and with a venom that even an apprentice had to worry about.

Maureen corked the test tube. The glass was cold against her fingers. Inside the tube the tiny serpents were knotted together motionless, like black thread.

*Good guards,* Tally noted.

*Good pets,* Maureen corrected.

*You said guards.*

She sneered at the cloth scraps by the wheels. "Want my carriage? Laughable," she said as she drove out of Hale City.

* * *

As the carriage rolled along the road, Maureen considered her situation. She'd passed gathering points near the Dark Forest, but sold nothing there. No sense in taking chances. Now, she was far enough away to be safe.

The forest around her was quiet. Birds called in the trees, and squirrels darted across the track as the wheels rolled over the soft leaf mould.

With Tally's help Maureen adjusted her illusion. Red curls tumbled to her shoulders. Her skin was fair, and she had the looks of a striking young woman sellsword. A Cross Sword hung at her hip.

*Why a woman this time?* Tally asked.

*I want to be underestimated where I'm going.*

*You're planning something.*

*I'm planning on being a fat sheep.*

Tally helped Maureen select a hollow near the still grey-green lake, hidden from the path. After tethering the horse there, Maureen released two of the vipers inside the carriage before strapping the tube with the rest of them to her belt. The box with the potions went on her back.

The straps bit into her shoulders. From inside the box came the faintest clink of glass against glass as the potion vials shifted against their straw wrapping.

"I am a wizard apprentice," Maureen said in Ancient Andas when she reached the lake shore. "I wish to enter the market and exchange resources."

Nothing happened. The wind rippled the water, but that was all. Maureen stood there, presumably looking like a madwoman holding an invisible conversation.

[Waiting: 4 min.]

*Patience is data,* Tally pointed out.

*I'm being patient,* Maureen replied silently.

A grey squirrel hopped onto a rock. "Well now," it said, its dark eyes darting quickly here and there. "I thought you were just another mercenary. Imagine my surprise when you turn out to be an apprentice."

"I want to enter the market," Maureen said.

"Yes, but it costs half a magic stone," the squirrel responded.

Maureen tossed it a whole magic stone. The squirrel chomped off the top half and swallowed before tossing the bottom half back towards Maureen.

She caught the half stone. [Magic stones: half returned.]

"Follow me," the squirrel instructed, hopping along until they arrived at a high cliff overlooking the lake. The squirrel leapt, disappearing over the edge of the cliff. Its bushy tail was the last thing Maureen saw, a wisp of grey against the brightening sky.

The wind coming off the lake roared in Maureen's ears. She could feel the drop swimming below her feet, and she held her breath.

[Drop calculation: lake below. No spikes in sight. Unable to see under water.]

*Is that a no?* she asked Tally.

[No data.]

Maureen gripped her Cross Sword and jumped. Her vision blurred as the world fell away from her.

The mists parted, revealing a collection of crooked rooftops, all reaching different heights. A market lay beyond, with people coming and going.

Her particle sense flared to life, highlighting every single person at once.

[Energy radiation sources: multiple]

Tally's alert repeated on itself as Maureen walked into the market. Everyone here was an apprentice.

Damp mist washed over Maureen's face, cooling her skin. The air smelled like smoke, herbs, and wet stones.

"Lots of people don't like coming in this way," the squirrel said, rolling its eyes. "The builder has a nasty sense of humour. Once they had people jumping down here, only to discover that there were stone spikes waiting for them." With that, the squirrel exploded into a cloud of purple smoke. Maureen smiled and continued into the market.
