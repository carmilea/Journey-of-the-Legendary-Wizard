“As you can see from the specimen before us,” the Dark Forest wizard said as he tapped the blueblood frog pinned to the tray before him, “the principle of neural linearity is clearly visible. Watch closely.”

The wizard waved his hand, speaking another spell word. Suddenly a magnified image of the frog’s internal organs appeared over the teaching hall. They were rendered in glowing colours, making them easily visible against the dark stone of the walls.

Around her Maureen heard the scratching of quills on paper and the creaking of wooden benches as students shifted to get a better look at the floating organs. Her own hands remained still on the desk before her.

The lecture ended with a bell and the Dark Forest wizard transformed into a black smoke that writhed and twisted in mid-air, disappearing in the middle of a sentence. A groan of disappointment rose from the hall.

Well, that was interesting, Tally said. I’ve filed away the frog’s organs and the images under Anatomy. [Anatomy: complete. Basic subjects: closed.]

Good, Maureen thought back. How many of them do you think got all that?

Tally counted the number of faces still busily copying down what was written on the large chalkboard at the front of the hall. Eleven, possibly.

Be generous, Maureen told it silently.

I was being generous, Tally retorted.

Maureen sat back and thought about everything she had learned so far. This was certainly a lot farther than any of Scarlette’s carriage lessons had ever gone. It was almost impossible to believe that she had learned so much in such a short time. Thanks to Tally’s help, it was all organised into categories and subcategories which made everything fit nicely in place inside her head.

“Maureen, please help,” Richard pleaded as he slid across the bench towards her. “That wizard went way too fast, and I didn’t catch any of it.”

Maureen smiled at him. “Haven’t I been helping you this whole time?” They had decided to go to the public courses together.

Do you want me to play back my summary now? Tally asked.

Maureen nodded to Richard then turned to Aaron and Lucy who were leaning over to hear what she had to say. “Basically the lecture covered…” She gave a shortened version of Tally’s summary, leaving out all the extra detail.

You left out my best diagram, Tally grumbled.

Richard needed to hear it without all the diagrams, Maureen answered back silently.

“Thanks, Maureen,” Richard said rubbing his forehead. “I don’t know what I would do without you.” Lucy was furiously copying down every word Maureen had said, her lips moving soundlessly with each syllable.

The three of them left together while Maureen remained on the pathway outside the black stone tiered lecture hall. She stood there for a moment, thoughtfully rubbing her chin. With all the basic subjects now completed, she had a much better understanding of both her pharmacology subjects that she was paying to attend. It was probably time to try her hand at brewing.

* * *

The arcades were packed with apprentices. Maureen passed stalls with blades of all types, short swords, daggers, throwing axes and Cross Swords laid out on cloth covered tables. Underneath the arches of the trading district, the smell of sweat mixed with the heavy smoke of tallow candles was thick in the air. There were more stalls selling odd trinkets and knick-knacks. Every so often she would pass by one of the few tables selling finished potions and the crowds would be noticeably thicker.

Tally counted the purchasers, separating them by robe and purse before displaying [Est. mark-up: 3x materials] on Maureen's display.

So that’s where the money is, Maureen said silently.

That’s the queue for the money, Tally retorted.

Maureen could feel her pulse quickening as she walked through the crowds. She was hungry… for income.

Tally named it first. Hunger. Maureen wasn’t going to argue with it though.

She left the tables with finished potions on display and followed the painted signboards above the arcade arches until she found the shop she was looking for, wedged between two other shops’ signs. As she approached, an old man was suddenly at her elbow. He made no sound to indicate his approach, he just appeared beside her, beaming a welcoming smile. His hair and beard were pure white, his face lined with wrinkles but still looking kindly. His eyes, small and darting, were shrewd as they ran over her.

[Energy radiation source. Close.]

The warning only flashed once the old man was in view.

Too close, Tally said, offended. He got too close.

Tally can only see what I see, Maureen reminded it silently.

That’s no comfort to me.

“Ah! Welcome, welcome!” The old man said, “I’m Gandolf. If you’re after quality and range, then my shop beats Mary’s General Store and Baffet’s Hut hands down.”

The shop was narrow and cramped with stock, rows of shelves lined with jars that were all stoppered with a cork. A lamp sat on the counter, sputtering and hissing, filling the air with the smells of burning tallow and dried herbs. Outside, Maureen could hear the click of magic stones against wooden counters.

Mary? Tally said, flagging the name.

Aunt Mary’s cracked red knuckles passed through Maureen’s mind for half a breath before Tally spoke again.

Different Mary. Common name.

I know, Maureen told it silently.

Just being thorough.

Maureen turned back to the old man. “I’m after an apothecary’s tools.”

“So! An apothecary are you?” Gandolf gasped, one hand clutching at his chest. “What is your name, child, and your mentor?”

“Maureen Olsen. Apprentice to Adrian.”

The old man’s body went rigid as soon as Adrian’s name had been mentioned. He stared at her as though seeing something new.

“Adrian...tsk, tsk,” the old man shook his head. “Even across the whole Passise Subcontinent, Wizard Adrian has quite a name.”

With a flourish, Gandolf raised his hands in the air. “But what do I see? An old Third-Class like me knows good stock when he sees it.”

Maureen studied the old man with her particle sense, finding him dense, heavy, almost like wet stone in the way that his particles packed together. Tally filed away the information, adding Gandolf under the category of Third-Class Wizard Apprentices. Next to it was a collection of labelled samples, including Maureen, Mace and Winnie.

He’s denser than any apprentice I have on file, Tally said. Against Wizards, no data.

Keep him at the top of the column, Maureen said silently to Tally.

The old man disappeared from view for several seconds before reappearing behind the counter with a full brewing set laid out. A new apprentice-grade set.

Maureen picked up each beaker, test tube and glass rod, holding them up to the lamp so she could examine the quality of the glass. None had bubbles or chips. She nodded.

“Do you have the basic formulas?”

“Of course.” The old man dug under the counter for a minute before coming out with three dirty black scrolls. “Here we are. Stamina Potion, Recovery Potion and the dangerous Berserk Blood Potion.”

Maureen nodded.

“All three?” the old man asked. Maureen nodded again. “I’ll take all three and ten portions of ingredients for each one please.”

The old man nodded, wrapping up the formulas and ingredients in a bundle of thick black cloth. “Everything together comes to twelve magic stones.”

Maureen checked the bundle before handing over her magic stones. The old man didn’t bother trying to negotiate with her.

[Magic stones: 112 → 100.]

[Days since landing: 60.]

You’re not even trying to bargain them down, Tally said.

“By the way dear,” Gandolf called out as she was leaving. “If you need any more supplies come here first and I’ll give you discounts on what you need. If you bring me some finished potions too... Adrian’s, Mace’s, even Winnie’s... or your own if you make any, I might be able to reward you even more generously.”

“Is that how you do justice to that kindly face of yours?” Maureen asked.

Gandolf spread his hands wide and shrugged. “Business is business.”

On the way out Tally spotted a jar filled with what looked like fanged black wolf eyes sitting on a stall. Plan six is unblocked! Plan six is unblocked! Plan six...

“What are those?”

“Eyes,” the black robe apprentice said with a yawn.

“Wolf eyes?”

“Yeah.”

“Do you know where they come from?”

The black robed apprentice shrugged. “I buy them from other traders.”

Maureen sighed. Two wolf eyes, a redwood nut and a handful of crystal grapes later she was on her way out again.

[Plan six: restarted.]

[Magic stones: 99.]

No new data on where the wolves live though, just where their eyes are sold.

* * *

The brass key turned stiffly in the lock. As the lights woke up by themselves, Tally muttered that it was still offended by them. Maureen carefully unwrapped the glassware from the black cloth and spent a long while setting up her own bench in the study. She tapped one of the beakers experimentally and it rang with a thin clear note. In the candle’s reflection she could see the curve of the glass doubled. Her fingertips rested against the cool rim of the beaker, and for a moment Maureen was back in Jing Sheng’s white walled lab. Tally remained quiet for a while before asking if she wanted the racks spaced the same way as last time.

Yes.

“I have my own bench now,” Maureen said softly to the empty room.

She spread the three black scrolls out on the desk and broke the masking spells with a fine thread of energy particles. As she unrolled each scroll Tally recorded the formulas spread by spread, a pale blue flicker behind Maureen’s eyes.

> [Recovery Potion formula: sorting and analysis. ETA 35h 00m.]

Why only Recovery? she asked silently.

One hard problem at a time Maureen. You taught me that.

Maureen carefully opened up the bundle of ingredients and began sorting them into their labelled storage. There were enough ingredients to make ten portions each of the three potions.

* * *

The next few days in the Library District were a blur. Maureen worked the free stacks from the opening bell to closing, paging through every book under the cold light of the Glow Stones. She carefully removed each book from the shelf and riffled it open so that Tally could record the pages as she flicked through them. Every glance of her eyes gave Tally another spread of data to store away.

The air smelled of dry paper and old glue. The sound of rustling pages and occasional coughing from readers echoed between the shelves. The Dark Forest apprentice librarians paid her no attention; she was just another bookworm with dusty sleeves.

The knowledge was free but vast, and not organised in any way that helped Maureen. The restricted and paid shelves remained firmly behind their iron grilles. A shelf counter ticked over on her display as Maureen made her way through the books, silently telling Tally, I’ve become a library goat. I eat everything I can find.

No. Wrong animal Maureen. More like a magpie. You’re collecting and hoarding all the shiny bits you can find. Except for one thing. No magpie has ever kept their nest as tidy as you keep your data.

Tidy is kind of the point, Tally.

Fair enough, log it as a magpie then.

From the free stacks Maureen was able to learn all the precautions of brewing. The warnings, the timings, and all the little mistakes that could ruin a batch or burn the brewer.

On the last day of searching, Maureen closed the final book and muttered to herself, “Almost done.”

> [Recovery Potion: analysis complete.]

> [Simulation plan built from database.]

A stream of pale blue data flowed across her vision.

She watched the simulation twice without saying anything.

>I’ll be counting with you Maureen.

Back home she took a long, hot bath before sliding into bed. Her sleep was deep and dreamless, and she woke up feeling clear headed and alert. She picked up the violet-blue leaf and gazed at its perfect blue surface broken only by the purple stems. She placed the silver knife carefully against the leaf’s edge and made a small incision. The knife never wavered, though her held breath marked how much this meant to her. Her planned actions played over in her head.

First, purify the leaf’s juices.

Then, apply the cold-oxygen treatment.

Tally started to count softly in time with her hands, and the first step in the simulation lit up at the edge of Maureen’s sight. The quiet room was broken by the faint pops of corrosion and the hiss of oxygen escaping the glass. The candle flame flickered beside the container of liquid.
