The little line in the corner of her display was Tally’s idea, and it would not go away.

[Trap vapour: active. Window: ~3 h left]

I’ll remind you every quarter hour.

Stop that.

Very well, every twenty minutes then.

[Next reminder: 20 min] A second small line appeared at the bottom of her display.

Tally was very pleased with itself. Maureen wasn’t.

The slave market was far wider than any street outside. The grey fog held the lamp smoke low over wet cobbles. The air smelt of wet straw, unwashed bodies and rust.

Patrol... four men, full plate, steel spears… patrol…

A second later, Tally had the information on display for her.

A squad of four men, each man worth almost a full knight on his own… armoured boots clanked on the cobbles as they walked.

Each exit marked on her display.

You’re not watching the gate any more.

And I’m watching the guards now.

A short, middle-aged man dressed richly came up to her hurriedly. He bowed, fawning. “Have you come to buy, mistress? We have strong men for estate work, gentle women who can -”

“I want high-grade goods.” A deep-red fireball rose from her hand, floating on her open palm.

Engels’s rule. Against another. Keep it on your palm, do not point it.

Yes Tally, I know how to read a rule.

And you know how to bend one too.

The attendant stammered an invitation then stumbled ahead. Maureen let the fireball gutter out and followed at an even pace.

[Mana: 5 → 4.0]

The room they entered tried hard to impress. A thick fur rug covered most of the floor and a brass lamp hung down from the ceiling. It was richly decorated, as was the large chair that sat behind a desk, carved with many twists and flourishes. Maureen sat as the attendant disappeared into the back of the room.

A short and thickset man strode out from the back, laughing. His full beard was thick and heavy, reaching almost to his chest. “Ah! Young wizard. How nice of you to visit us.” He used the common term for apprentices and wizards. “You must be quite the talent, if you are already able to cast at such a young age. I apologise for the clumsiness of my servant.”

The bearded man thumped his chest. “I am Julo, one of the slave merchants here. I am glad that you have come to buy.”

The scent of pipe smoke and mutton fat came from Julo’s beard. His voice was loud and hearty, filling the room to all corners.

[Julo. Upper Knight, peak. Strength 9.8 / Agility 9.7 / Constitution 9.7. Danger: strong.]

Maureen’s shoulders set at Tally’s display, but her expression remained unchanging.

He is stronger than you are, came Tally’s voice. Warmer, more impish than before. He is more agile than you are, and has greater constitution. In fact, he outclasses you in every body column, but you still have the other columns.

Which ones? Maureen asked silently.

Spells, poison, and me, Tally replied as Maureen opened her mouth to respond to Julo.

And remember, while your face is borrowed, your name is your own.

“Julo, I’m Maureen,” she said. The bearded slaver waved her outside to see the stock.

* * *

Maureen followed Julo around the ordinary rope pens of slaves, turning several corners and passing into a section that was better guarded by Julo’s burly men. The slaves here were clearly worth more.

Six corners, Tally noted, I can keep track of the way back for you. Maureen nodded as she followed Julo, and they passed into a section where the straw was drier and the lamps burned brighter. The scent of unwashed bodies and rust was the same.

Now there were cages instead of rope pens, and the slaves within had only a scrap or two of cloth covering their modesty. Strong men and slender women peered through the bars at the passing potential buyers.

“These are special, my dear,” Julo said, stopping before a large wooden cage. He reached through the bars to slap one girl on the arm. “Trained in the arts of pleasure, untouched. Each one. Like the handsome young men two cages along. All trained for the bed of any rich buyer.”

Maureen’s throat tightened at the thought. She forced her face to remain impassive, looking at the cage as if it were a shelf of goods for sale.

Untouched, Tally said. Two have callused feet, one from dancing, one from walking. One has the posture of a noblewoman, but that could be learned. That one refuses to drink, her cup of water untouched. Shall I continue?

No, Maureen thought back. Just log that and move on.

Very well, Tally responded, filing the information under an unfamiliar tag.

It made sense, really. These slaves couldn’t have been acquired by honourable means. Women, even slave women, were a commodity that could be traded for resources. Slavers and their apprentices wouldn’t ignore such an opportunity. Besides, even if these women had been sold willingly, who knew what they had been through before coming to this market?

Julo winked at her, slapping another girl’s thigh. “Come now, my dear, take a few with you. To pass the time . . .”

Maureen’s hand rested on the strings of her pouch for just a moment too long before she released them.

“No, I’m not shopping for companions,” she said. “I need strong servants, at least Middle Knight.”

Julo eyed her thoughtfully for a moment, then led her forward, past the ordinary rope pens and stained canvas roofs. Here, in the high-grade section of the slave market, iron and timber cages held the wares. Lamplight gleamed off the bars, illuminating drier straw on the floors and more substantial wooden guard posts. The fog drifted between the bars, a ghostly curtain, as if the slaves were kept in cages within cages. A smell of sweat, old blood, and iron hung in the air.

“Here we are, my dear,” said Julo, gesturing to a line of large iron cages. Tall men, all scarred and bearing themselves with a fierce pride, stood or sat inside. “All Middle Knights, all with some memories removed by wizard experiments. Once you’ve bought and bound them, they’ll be loyal to the death.”

A chain scraped against the bars somewhere as one of the large men shifted his weight.

Tally highlighted each man’s head with a small box of text, showing [Middle Knight 6.1 / 5.8 / 6.4] hovering over one, [Middle Knight 6.6 / 6.2 / 6.5] over another, and so on down the line. It sorted them by strength, highlighting the top three for Maureen’s convenience.

Among Tally’s top three was a woman with a shaved head, scarred forearms, and callused palms. You sure about that? Maureen asked silently.

Calluses don’t lie, people do, Tally responded.

The woman with the shaved head met Maureen’s eyes, and didn’t look away. Maureen pointed at her and the two men who topped Tally’s list. “Those three.”

“Excellent choice!” said Julo, clapping his hands. Burly guards pulled the three slaves from their cages and led them off to wait for payment and binding.

* * *

“One other,” Maureen said.

Julo frowned. “What kind?”

“A steward. A man, if possible, but not necessary. One who’s had a good deal of experience dealing with money.”

Julo shook his head. “Rare. Those go very fast. Other slaves might die on you, but the one who deals with your money keeps your affairs in order.” He eyed Maureen speculatively.

She raised an eyebrow and tossed Olit's stone bag in her hand. The magic stones inside clicked together like a handful of dice.

“Julo,” she said, “I have magic stones.”

“Ah,” the slave merchant said with a smile, rubbing his beard. “I’ve always had a weakness for magic stones.” He tapped his forehead and muttered to himself, then slapped his hand against his head. “I have one. A man about fifty years old, probably another ten or fifteen years of productive service left. He’s been with me for years, and I can’t sell him because most nobility and wizards don’t want to pay for only ten to fifteen years’ service. But he was once court treasurer for some principality, so he should know all he needs to. Will you see him?”

Maureen nodded.

Julo led her along the row of slavers’ tents at the back of the high-grade section. The tents had wet canvas sagging between poles, a single lamp hissing on a pole beside each tent, straw trampled into the mud around the entrances, and the cold fog creeping under the flaps. Julo halted before one of the tents and bellowed, “Hank! Old Hank! Come out here! Your future mistress is here!”

A man stepped out of the tent, his long silver hair combed flat against his head. He wore a coat that looked as though it had once been thick, but had been brushed until it was thin. The cuffs were carefully mended, the darns almost invisible. His eyes were sharp, and he seemed to be weighing Maureen before she could weigh him. Tally noted the careful darns in the cuffs and the ink stain on his right middle finger.

“What is the value lost over ten years on a cart purchased for a hundred gold?” Maureen asked.

“Ten gold per year, which equates to nothing at the end,” Hank answered promptly.

“What is the amount due on a loan of a hundred gold lent at the rate of ten in the hundred a year, compounded, for three years?”

“At the end of the first year, a hundred and ten gold would be due,” Hank answered. “At the end of the second year, one hundred and twenty-one gold. At the end of the third year, one hundred and thirty-three gold and one tenth.”

Tally displayed both answers before Hank had even finished giving them, and grumbled silently that it had competition again.

A steward who can count will leave you more time for the interesting work, Maureen told Tally silently.

But I’ll still be counting him, came Tally’s silent response.

For another breath, Maureen was looking at the white bench with columns of numbers balancing each other. Then she turned back to Hank. “That will do.”

She paid the man from Olit’s superior stones, sixty for each of the fighters and forty for Hank. Two superior and twenty ordinary. [Magic stones: 824 → 604]

* * *

Once inside the tent, Julo showed her the dormant binding array that each slave carried, as well as his house’s mark. Then he gave her the Ancient Andas command word that would complete the array, using her own mana.

Before she cast, Tally read the array through Maureen’s particle sense.

>Consistency check: no secondary anchor. Loyalty routes to caster only.

>This is a check only. There might still be a secondary anchor that cannot be detected.

Maureen sealed all four arrays herself. One by one, she cast the command word, completing the arrays. Each slave screamed once when their array activated, then fell silent.

Tally also kept a count of the screams.

>Four.

Then Tally displayed her mana remaining.

[Mana: 4.0 → 2.0]

After the last scream, the only sound in the tent was the hissing from the lamp. A cold stillness descended on Maureen, one that had settled deep within her.

The four slaves followed Maureen out of the tent with deep respect in their eyes. Hank called her Mistress.

* * *

Julo bellowed after her. "Don't forget to get yourself a few women, lass! Just for amusement. I'll give you a good discount!"

Maureen smiled and kept walking. The fog swallowed Julo's loud voice before she reached the market gate.

She made good on her promise, acquiring the clothing, armour, weaponry and daily necessities for the four new 'people'. Afterwards, she made a circuit through the stalls, looking for materials of her own.

[Magic stones: 604 → 574]

>You now own four people.

Four contracts.

>Both columns.

"Hank, take the others and wait for me outside," Maureen ordered.

"Yes, Mistress," Hank bowed before he questioned her further. "What will you be doing?"

Maureen's eyes shifted towards Olit's shop, hidden somewhere up ahead.

"Nothing much. It is time to attend to some business." Her smile was cold.

The four bowed without another word, disappearing into the fog. Maureen's footsteps sounded alone on the wet cobbles.

Tally brought forward the trap clock.

The potion should have done its work by now.

It was time to deal with it.
