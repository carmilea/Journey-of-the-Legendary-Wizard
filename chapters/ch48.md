The grey fog blurred the edges of the gabled houses. The lamps' lights diffused into hazy yellow haloes. It reminded her of the old picture books of London that Jing Sheng had loved as a child.

Maureen stopped when she reached the signpost at the crossroads. Just as she was about to read it, a small owl landed on top of it. It was dressed in a black round hat and wore a red bow tie.

"Hello, young wizard apprentice." The owl spoke in Ancient Andas.

Maureen nodded back.

Tally analysed the positions of the owl's tongue while it spoke. Its accent is better than yours, it told her.

Thank you very much, Tally. It's had a lot longer to practise than I have though.

The owl tilted its head. "Would you like me to guide you in? Half a magic stone please."

Maureen tossed the half magic stone to the owl. It was all she had left from the squirrel's change.

The owl caught the stone, swallowed it with a jerk of its throat, and a thin wisp of black smoke drifted away from its feathers before it settled down again.

[Construct: No data on make.]

It must have been made by a wizard then.

That is your job.

"Wizard Engels founded this place," the owl said, flying slowly ahead of her. "It's not large, but it has a weapon workshop, auction house, fur shop, apothecaries and other amenities. Wizard Engels also instituted the rule that no one may cast a spell against another inside the settlement."

[Rule filed. Consequences unknown. Applies to all.]

So, if they want to kill me they will have to use their hands.

Or friends.

As the owl gave her the quick tour, Maureen caught glimpses of tanned hides hanging outside the fur shop, and the rhythmic sound of a hammer ringing in the weapon workshop. Finally, they arrived at a wooden post. The owl landed and doffed its hat with one wing before bowing and flying away.

"That was an interesting little thing," Maureen said with a smile.

Jars and rolled skins were arranged along the walls. A young man, sharp eyed, sat on a stool beside a back wall shelf.

Maureen had decided to try Olit's shop first. It was one of the more well known apothecary shops in this area, so she felt that the prices should be fair. The owner hurried out as soon as he spotted her.

He had a kindly face lined by time, and green hair that reached his waist. His robe carried the odour of lamp oil and dried herbs.

"I am not here to buy," Maureen said flatly as she placed a black wooden box onto the counter. "I am here to sell." The box was filled with her finished potions.

Olit's brow furrowed as he opened the lid and stared inside. He mumbled a few words while waving a magnifying glass over the box. The crystal on top of the magnifying glass glowed softly after that. Then the old apprentice studied the vials filled with coloured liquids. Each vial was wrapped in straw and tied with twine. The light from the hanging lamp illuminated each vial as Olit raised them up. Maureen kept her face bright and eager while the old man studied the merchandise. Her hands rested quietly on the counter.

"This is the best quality I have seen in years," Olit said after a long time. "Twenty Berserk Blood, eighteen Stamina, eighteen Recovery, and a few other things. Seven hundred and eighty-one magic stones."

"I only have a little interest in Pharmacology," Maureen replied.

He gestured to several rope pens that were erected in the middle of the clearing. They were half hidden by large pieces of stained canvas that hung from poles.
