“Maureen…” Lilith said quietly as they walked down the black-stone path towards the dormitory district. “Julie and I have always clashed, but I never thought that she would go this far. Thank you.”

“It was nothing,” Maureen answered, rubbing the side of her nose with a finger. Lilith smiled to herself before bidding Maureen goodbye. They went their separate ways.

*Lilith didn’t laugh behind a hand this time,* Tally observed.

*Yes,* Maureen replied silently. *File that and hush.*

The sound of Maureen’s footsteps echoed back at her from the empty corridor lined with identical doors, each marked with its brass lock. The key clicked in her lock, and Maureen entered her rooms.

She sank into the hot water, letting the heat wash over her until the sharp chemical smell was gone from her fingers. Afterwards, she lay on top of her bed.

*Tally?* Maureen asked silently. *Update?*

[Task created. Database updated. Spirit curve recalculated. Simulation corrected.]

Maureen sat up immediately and headed straight for her bench. She brewed from one portion and then waited tensely for the results. It failed, and she poured the ruined liquid out of the tube.

*Reason one: Insufficient reagents. Reason two: Incorrect reagents. Reason three: Improper sequence.* Tally listed each reason methodically as Maureen sat at her workbench.

*You’re enjoying this, aren’t you?* Maureen said silently.

*Yes,* Tally said smugly. *I’m enjoying the data.*

* * *

For days, Maureen shuttled back and forth between the dormitory, lab and the trading district. Tally kept track of the number of failures for Maureen to see on her display. Each failure taught Tally something, but Maureen's shoulders remained hunched up as her sleep became shorter and shorter. The burner ticked past midnight, and her fingertips were stained a faint green from the herbal extracts.

"Did you know that Mace took half a year to make his first potion?" Lilith said one evening as they ate dinner together. "I mean, he's Adrian's top apprentice, but apparently his family threw a lot of resources into him." She laughed behind one hand. "That's what I heard."

When she finally visited Gandolf, he laid out a black cloth bundle in front of her before she even spoke. "Ten standard portions of Recovery Potion materials, already prepared for you."

Maureen slid a single magic stone over to him. Gandolf scooped it up with lightning fast hands. "Ah, young miss, young miss, early brewing is so costly in materials. You would be surprised how many apprentices give up on pharmacology because of this initial setback."

[Ledger updated.]

*You have twenty-nine failures before you should be concerned about your progress,* Tally said.

*Great. That’s comforting,* Maureen thought back.

*Comfort was never the objective. Accuracy was the goal,* Tally responded.

The bundle was heavier than it looked, and the smell of crushed leaves came through the cloth wrapping.

"Since we're old acquaintances, Gandolf, don't you think that we deserve a discount?" Maureen asked.

The elderly man coughed and stammered, then Maureen just laughed, grabbed the bundle and waved as she left.

* * *

Adrian showed the apprentices a snake the size of a human thumb, but with a dangerously thin and sharp horn protruding from its head. Its colours changed constantly, as did its body structure, depending on the temperature. Its venom was terrible, he said. This was the Canes Black Rope Snake.

As Adrian brought the creature near to the burner, the scales on its back began to shift and slide, moving from a deep black to a rusty brown. The apprentices leaned back from the bench, putting some distance between themselves and the snake.

After the lesson, Adrian had Maureen stay behind while he pushed up his glasses and regarded her. "How goes the Recovery Potion?"

She hesitated a moment, trying to decide on a response. "It keeps failing," she said. "I'm not sure why." Maureen kept her hands flat on the edge of the stone bench. The surface was cold under her fingers. She didn’t move them while she talked to Adrian. She had noticed that people were more likely to believe what she said if her body language remained still.

Adrian wasn’t surprised by her admission. "The road of the apothecary is a difficult one. Creating your first finished potion is an enormous challenge. Once you cross that hurdle, everything after will be much easier."

Tally filed away the lecture in its entirety. The snake's horn, shifting colours, and terrible venom. Adrian’s unconcerned reaction was noted. Her pace hadn't tipped him off yet.

*Not yet,* Tally amended quietly.

Maureen didn’t respond with words, but just bowed her head before Adrian dismissed her to continue her other work. Winnie approached quietly as she left. "How are you doing?" the kind girl asked. Mace gave Maureen an encouraging smile as well.

Lilith called out from outside the lab as soon as Maureen stepped into the black-stone corridor. The pale girl gathered her courage and walked up to Maureen, holding out a thick book. "This is a potioneer's notebook. I got it for you. It took me a lot of work, but I thought that maybe it would help with the Recovery Potion." Lilith looked up at Maureen and waited anxiously for a response.

Maureen smiled at the gift and accepted it. "Thank you very much, Lilith." Maureen said. Lilith flushed slightly, then stole a glance before running off to her other duties.

*Colour increase in Lilith's face noted. Stolen glance recorded,* Tally reported.

*Log that as unconfirmed, please.* Maureen thought back.

*Unconfirmed for weeks, now. Turn the pages slowly tonight,* it ordered.

Maureen cradled the book in both hands. The cover was worn leather that had softened at the corners with age. She counted up how much she now owed for it.

* * *

The first page was full of notes and the next two had diagrams. The real problems were in the next few pages and she read them carefully. A real apothecary saw the problems far deeper than she did.

Tally re-ran the model when she turned each page and the number ticked up in her display. When she reached the last page, the number stopped.

[New simulation plan generated. Est. success: 87%.]

*Maureen, that is the best number I have ever given you,* Tally said.

*Don't jinx it,* Maureen thought silently back.

*Jinxes are not real.* Tally said.

*I know, but just don't say anything, will you?* Maureen thought.

Data, images and tables flashed past her eyes as her mind processed everything it needed to know. The blue light faded from her eyes, she closed the book and she knew that she could do it.

Her breathing was even as she worked. Her hands moved by themselves while her mind stood back by one step watching carefully. The violet-blue leaf was slit open by a small knife, then the faintly blue sap was drained into a beaker. The sap was purified using standard techniques, before being treated with cold-oxygen. Frost formed on the glass beaker, creeping up the sides as the sap was chilled by the treatment. The sap smelled like grass and metal under her nose. In the last step, the test tube filled with the green jelly like substance was topped off with the solution of Phoenix Orchid Root added drop by drop.

[Drops: 1]

[Drops: 2]

[Drops: 3]

One bracketed line for each drop, the numbers marched across her display, like a clock, never wavering.

The gel began to shudder, becoming less viscous. It started to thin. Then it became a green liquid, but a liquid with a misty cloudiness to it.

Elementary Recovery Potion

A healing potion, it sped up the rate of recovery from injuries or illnesses. In some cases, it could even mean the difference between life and death. Every apprentice wanted more of the stuff than there was to go around.

Her display fell silent for a single beat. Then Tally's warm voice sounded in her head. *Maureen Olsen,* Tally said, its voice quieter than she had ever heard it.

Her body caught up to the moment after that. Sweat ran down her back while the blood drained out of her face. Her knees threatened to give way, so she gripped her bench tightly. A laugh escaped her lips and she allowed it, secure in the knowledge that the door was locked.

*Did you record my state and actions?* She asked Tally silently.

*Yes, Maureen Olsen, every second of it.*

*Name it the Brewing State, and file it under Pharmacology please.*

*Affirmative, Maureen Olsen.*

Maureen could have sworn that there was a smug flourish in Tally's voice. Once she was cleaned up, she sat down on her bed, the small vial of Elementary Recovery Potion clutched in her hand. Suddenly, she went cold as she remembered Adrian's eyes of golden fire and what wizards were supposed to be capable of.

Mace's first potion appeared on her display. The most advanced apprentice in Adrian's charge, Mace was blessed with a high aptitude and had the resources of his family behind him. Six months of effort was all it took for him to brew his first potion. Beside it, Tally displayed her own few months of effort. The difference between the two was calculated.

*I don't know how to explain that, do you Tally?*

*No.*

*Anyone who did try would probably find you though, right?*

*Yes, Maureen Olsen.*

*I do not think I should show anyone right now. Not until I know more about this.* Maureen let out a long breath.

*Slowly. Slowly.*

* * *

It was weeks before she ventured outside the dormitory, the lab or Gandolf's store. She met Richard and the others occasionally, picked up materials as needed and asked Adrian and Mace ever more probing questions about the Recovery Potion. Finally one night, late, she ran through a full brew with Tally keeping silent all the way. She barely needed the chip for anything. Her own hands knew what to do now.

Carefully she worked a black stone loose from the wall behind the desk in her study. She placed the vials of potion in the cavity and covered them with cloth, leaving the Seal and the stones on her body outside.

[Cache: 12.]

*Tally, please rank those vials by their recorded deviations.*

The chip did not take long to rank the vials.

*The vial for Adrian is the worst, Tally?*

*Yes, Maureen Olsen.*

*The worst one then.* The best one stayed hidden in the wall.

Maureen carried the vial with her to Adrian's study. He was sitting in his high backed chair, reading a thick black book that was burning with red flames that didn't touch him. He looked over his glasses as she entered. "Another problem?"

He spotted the vial in her hand and put the book down. A mouth dripping drool and filled with fangs opened up beside him and swallowed the book whole.

The drool hissed on the flagstones, and the smell of hot paper wafted in the air.

Maureen took a deep breath before she played up her excitement. "Mentor, I did it! I brewed a Recovery Potion." Adrian walked over and gently plucked the vial out from her hand. He unstoppered the vial and sniffed it, then frowned.

His lips moved, but no sounds came out. The lip shapes were recorded, but Tally had no data to compare to what Adrian summoned. A thin black smoke dove into the vial, and the contents churned with its descent. As the smoke withdrew, a swarm of tiny black insects flew out from the potion.

Adrian snapped his fingers, and the tiny black bugs disappeared.

"Hmph, the purity isn't quite there yet, the separation is off, and your timing is poor, but this is indeed a Recovery Potion." Adrian looked up at Maureen with a quiet smile. "Congratulations, Olsen."

Maureen beamed an eager and excited smile right back at her mentor. Beneath that performance, she felt her ribs finally relax.
