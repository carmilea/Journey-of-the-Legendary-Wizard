They topped the last switchback and the garrison rose out of the mountainside. It was a collection of tall, connected buildings built of bluestone, squatting side-by-side so closely they almost looked like a single structure. The bluestone walls were thicker than the length of a cart, and a faint dampness from the mountains beaded on them. It smelled of snow from the peaks above, the cold coming off them sharp enough to cut through Maureen Olsen’s thick robe despite the late afternoon sun.

Tally counted the wards for her by the tell-tale glints of particles along the parapet. [Wards: 9 visible, by particle sense.] *Enough to keep things out,* she thought silently back at it.

*Or in,* Tally responded. *Wards don’t care which way they work.*

Olivia land, Adrian had said. And the Olivias kept long memories. She let her shoulders fall loose as she held the reins, keeping her eyes moving between the huge stone gate, the two towers that flanked it and the strip of roadway that led back down the mountain.

Daniel swung down from his horse first, his light cross armour gleaming in the sunlight against the dark grey of the stonework, his sun-bright hair shining. He grinned up at the wall as if it was a festival in his honour, rather than an armed garrison. “Not bad at all, is it?” He cupped his hands to his mouth and bellowed, “We’re the wizards sent to hold this garrison!”

He whipped his arms up over his head, launching a Blast Fireball into the sky. The fireball rose high into the grey sky, burning bright before it exploded in a shower of sparks that could have been thrown off the anvil of a smith.

Tally logged the colour of Daniel’s fireball against her own Simons-Sado catalogue and informed Maureen that it was the same catalogue, but that her heat signature was better.

*Don’t be petty,* she told Tally silently.

*I am not being petty,* Tally responded. *I’m just being precise.*

One of Daniel’s fireball sparks drifted onto Lise’s hood and the girl made a sharp little noise of disgust as she brushed it away. Hasen wasn’t even looking at the wall. He was looking at Lise.

Someone yelled from up on the wall and one of the guards ran along the parapet out of sight. There was a long groan of iron pins, and the great stone gate began to grind open, moving in short, heavy jerks. A party came out to meet them, led by an old man with silver hair and a lined face that looked as though kindness had carved it. He bowed low to each of them, his hands clasped across his chest.

“Doug, of the Olivia wizard family. Welcome. Truly, you are very welcome here.”

Tally read the pulse beating in the old man’s throat, measuring it by sight alone. Steady beat. Practised. [Tells: none. Not proof.] *Practised at what?* Maureen asked silently.

*No data,* Tally responded. *Bowing, possibly.*

But the old man’s greeting seemed the same for all of them. His warmth was measured out evenly, like loaves of bread. Only his eyes lingered on Maureen for half a beat longer than they did on the others, before they moved on.

The six Dark Forest Middle Knights who’d escorted the wagon handed it over at the gate, watered their horses at a stone trough, then turned around and headed back down the road without a word to the Olivia men. It was still the same afternoon when they left.

[Escort: 6 out. 0 in.] Tally counted them out. The last Forest faces on file. Tally logged them as such.

The apprentices followed Doug through the gate without speaking. Behind them came the grinding of stone on stone as the gate closed again. There was the sound of the great wooden bar sliding into its brackets.

* * *

Inside, the garrison was run on Olivia money. Servants scurried around with armfuls of fresh linen, an Olivia quartermaster walked with a chain of keys hanging from his belt. One by one Daniel, Hasen and Lise were led away to their quarters, but no one beckoned to her.

The hall began to empty around her, the last of the footsteps fading down the corridors. A distant door opened and shut, and the air was left smelling of tallow smoke and wet wool and mutton roasting in some unseen kitchen. She sat down at the long black table and waited.

She rested her hands flat on the cold marble, remaining perfectly still. The cold worked its way into her skin, the black stone sucking the warmth out of her palms. It rose through her wrists like an ache.

The big hearth in front of her hadn’t seen flame for days, and the ash within was grey and settled. She waited, not moving.

Tally ran a clock in the upper right corner of her display, counting the seconds she had been waiting.

[Waiting: 14 min.]

*It took four minutes to find rooms for the others,* Tally said. *Deliberate.*

*I can count,* she told it silently.

*I count faster,* Tally told her smugly.

A servant crossed the far end of the hall, carrying a stack of plates. He didn’t look at her, but his avoidance was worse than if he’d been staring.

Then another man came in through the servants’ archway. He was young, dressed in silk, with two retainers half a step behind him. Maureen raised her head.

“Has nobody arranged a room for me?” she asked mildly.

The young man’s name was Shuel, and his Olivia rank was evident in the way he walked, dressed, and spoke. His clothes were fine silks, and he wore a pin bearing the Olivia family sigil at his collar. The retainers behind him did nothing until he stopped moving, and then they stopped as well.

Tally calibrated him immediately:

[Second-Class, upper column, by calibration. Knight body: Middle.]

A second mark ringed the sigil on his collar:

[Olivia coiled sigil.]

“You?” Shuel laughed through his nose. “A room? You’re the one who crossed Young Master Frey. And you sit there expecting the Olivia family to make up a bed for you? Honestly—”

The tall man, golden-haired, with a mild smile that never moved. Eleven black robes behind him. The Central District Hall. And Jay’s bones in a gully far from here.

Her face didn’t change. Her eyes didn’t shift. Her lips didn’t tighten. Her breathing didn’t change.

*Frey is Third-Class,* Tally said. *Top of the column. Jay’s brother. Whatever he knows, he did not learn from a contract.*

*I remember,* Maureen told it silently.

Maureen raised an eyebrow. Then she laughed softly. “How interesting.”

The laugh sounded out of place in the cold, still hall. One of the retainers shifted his feet. Colour rose in Shuel’s neck. She did not flush, plead or bluster. Instead, her fingers found the corner of the table.

Tally painted a ghost arc from her hand to Shuel’s face. It was pale blue, and that was all it said.

*Mouth. Not eye.*

Maureen closed her hand. There was a dry cracking noise as the corner of the black marble tabletop broke free. She hadn’t cast a spell, hadn’t summoned any Silver Light. The chip of stone lay in her hand, and she flicked her wrist, throwing it at Shuel.

It hit Shuel square in the mouth, and he staggered back several steps. His lip split open, and blood welled down his chin, soaking into the fine silk of his collar.

The two retainers froze where they stood. Maureen brushed bits of black marble dust off her palm. Her breathing hadn’t even quickened. She noticed that, and didn’t like noticing it.

[Pulse: 71. Unchanged.]

*Writing that one down for both of us, just like I did with Lauren.*

*I know.*

“You—!” Shuel’s hand came up, blood flecking his teeth. Black particles began to gather in the Olivia man’s palm.

Before he could do anything else, Doug strode in through the main door. “Enough, Shuel! What have you been doing all this time? No room ready for Miss Olsen? Have you lost your wits?”

*He timed that very well,* Tally noted. *Came in just as Shuel was getting ready to start something. Might have been standing outside the door, listening.*

*Or he’s quick.*

*Doors don’t make men quick.*

*Fair enough.*

The Olivia elder turned to Maureen, bowing slightly and holding out his hands in a gesture of apology. “Miss Olsen, I am so sorry for this little mishap. Everything is ready for you now. Please, allow me to show you the way.”

‘Mishap’. The word slid out effortlessly, like oil. He never even glanced at the broken table. Maureen nodded calmly, rose from her chair, and followed Doug out of the room. Her back was loose and relaxed, her ears open for any sudden noises.

As she stepped into the corridor, a voice came after her. “Maureen Olsen! Wait until Young Master Frey gets back. Do you think he sat on his hands when he heard you’d crawled back to the Forest?” A fist slammed down onto marble, making a dull thudding sound.

[Slurred speech.]

*Frey has moved. No other data available.*

[Frey: move, unknown.]

Maureen didn’t turn around.

* * *

The room had obviously been made up in haste, but it was livable: clean linens, brazier lit, door bolted. Whatever game Doug was playing, he wasn’t willing to openly slight her.

The smell of new straw mingled with the dampness of the stone walls. The corners of the mattress were tucked in too sharply, as if someone had folded them while being watched. Maureen brought her things inside from the carriage. The black loot bag, the crates of glassware, the Sodara trunk wrapped in cloth, slung over her shoulders like a felled sapling, and the padded crates of grenades. The Seal, the Token, the cards, the bronze book, and Adrian’s box of dark gold remained in their usual places on her body.

[Grenades: hand 8 / pack 26.]

[Room: trunk, loot bag, glassware.]

*Now there is something worth robbing.*

Tally logged each load of items that came through the door, and then warned her with its own dry voice. Maureen nodded and warded the room. She traced the lines of Gregory’s rune with her fingertips, the same pattern she had used at the mouth of the cave in the realm. Silver threads sank into the bluestone walls, the stone floor, and the wooden doorframe.

The room fell quiet, not abruptly or noisily, but in the way that cellars are quiet. The clattering sounds from the yard beyond became a muffled murmur. A low humming sound settled into the stone, more felt than heard.

[Ward: holding. Mana: 11.2 → 10.6.]

It cost Maureen six tenths of her mana to cast the spell, but only once. The ward appeared in Tally’s vision as a faint line of silver running along the doorframe. Looking didn’t cost anything, so her mana remained steady at 10.6.

*Check it again,* she told Tally silently.

*Yes, I was just going to suggest you do that three times,* Tally replied.

An old joke between them.

Maureen felt her shoulders drop a notch as she watched the last glints of silver sink into the bluestone walls. For the first time since the gate, she felt a little bit safer.

In the dining hall, under the glow of tallow lights, with platters of mutton, loaves of black bread, and pitchers of sour ale set out on the long benches, Daniel was already waiting for her. He had taken off his cross armour and waved her over to sit beside him on his bench. Lise and Hasen sat at the far end of the table, Lise leaning in toward the empty place beside her that Daniel had vacated.

Daniel poured her a mug of sour ale before she sat down. He laughed at nothing too loudly, and then pushed the better cut of mutton to her side of the table. He’d been like this ever since the Forest, and Maureen still wasn’t sure if it was a gift or a hook.

“I like knowing strong people,” Daniel said, his face cheerfully flushed from the ale. “Can’t hurt up here, can it?”

Maureen smiled an answer that was neither yes nor no.

Daniel: tells none. Not proof, Tally read through Maureen’s eyes, drawing a line in front of them. Friendly. Unconfirmed. This is the second Daniel on file.

Daniel leaned closer to Maureen. “Raids come quick in these mountains, Maureen. Let’s swap brands. If I get in trouble, I call you. If you get in trouble, you call me. Simple as that.”

*Brands are a channel,* Tally objected, its voice cold. *Your face flows through them. They might tell your direction. No data on Olivia brands.*

*If I refuse now, the entire table will notice.*

*Yes, a corpse gets noticed too.*

Lise had paused with a half-chewed mouthful of mutton. Hasen’s eyes had snapped up from his plate to watch them intently. On the table between the loaf of black bread and the pitcher of ale, Daniel’s hand lay open and relaxed, waiting for her decision.

Maureen held out her wrist. For a moment, their wrists touched, and a cold sting shot through her skin where it met Daniel’s brand. When she looked down, there was a new mark barely visible beneath Adrian’s brand.

[Brands: 2. Adrian. Daniel.]

*Another risk you willingly accept.*

Back in her room, with her wards set, and the brazier lit—too late—against the chill damp of the bluestone walls, Maureen’s wrist still itched under her sleeve. She rubbed at it with her thumb in the dark, watching the faint silver glow of her doorway until she finally fell asleep.

* * *

The yard was furred with frost when morning came. Maureen’s breath smoked as she made her way across to the planning room, and the rich smell from the kitchens couldn’t quite cover the scent of wet ash in the grate.

The apprentices were gathering around the map table again, its surface marked with black lines showing the raid routes, and Daniel was in the process of dividing the walls up into watches. Maureen didn’t pull up a stool.

“I’ve business of my own to attend to,” she said. “From here on, this garrison is yours.”

Daniel just stared at her, charcoal still in his hand. “You’re leaving? Now?” he asked. “Maureen, you can’t just—”

His face showed disbelief, but underneath that was something else, busy doing sums. Hasen looked at her once before dropping his eyes to stare at the map. No one else said a word.

Tally caught the jump of the pulse at Daniel’s throat. [Tells: surprise. Not proof.]

*Surprise can be genuine,* Tally told her. *So can being annoyed at having your plans spoiled.*

Maureen Olsen turned on her heel and walked out. None of them said a word against her. She was the strongest apprentice in the garrison, and that was reason enough for silence.

She didn’t take any more than she needed: the bandolier and the padded pack, the belt kit, and the things on her body. The rest could stay safe behind the wards in her room. The carriage and its two horses could remain in the yard. [Grenades: hand 8 / pack 26, carried.] Tally told her as she buckled on the bandolier. [Left behind: trunk, loot bag, glassware. Room: warded.]

*A warded room is still a room. Someone else holds the keys.*

*I know, Tally.*

Maureen Olsen walked through the gate on foot. The guards glanced at her but didn’t ask where she was going.

The gate ground shut behind her. Her boots crunched on the frost that rimed the flagstones. The wind blew down from the peaks ahead, cold and clean, untainted by tallow or the stench of Olivias.

A faint blue ribbon bloomed in the air ahead of her, dancing over the slope. Three other routes flickered into existence beside it, then faded one by one. The blue ribbon solidified, pointing southwest.

[Proan Canyon: 92%.]

*That’s the direction to Aaron,* Tally said.

*Aaron meant the realm,* she replied silently.

*They are still filed side by side,* Tally told her.

She hadn’t come here to defend walls. She had come for the legacy of the ancient wizard. For the first time since the Forest, the tightness in Maureen’s chest eased. She took a deep breath, feeling the air fill her lungs, expand them, and pull free with a long, smooth exhale.

*Just us,* she thought.

*It has always been just us,* Tally responded.

Maureen Olsen stepped off the road onto the ribbon and began walking southwest. She didn’t look back.
