Maureen landed well clear of Beck, dropping into a crouch, Freya’s slender Cross Sword clenched tightly in her fist. Beck fell to his knees in the churned earth. Already, the green flesh of his grafted arm was crawling back, sealing the wound where she had forced the shells in.

>3.

>2.

Tally’s countdown was cold and flat, hanging across her vision.

*I can count,* Maureen thought back at it.

>1.

 In Maureen’s inner pocket, the Blood Seal of Thorns lay pressed against her ribs. It was cooler than she had ever felt it, even through the cloth of her pocket. She had caught a glimpse of its glow as it returned, and it was cracked, uneven. The dull ache behind her ribs had settled in to stay.

Thump.

The sound came from somewhere inside Beck’s chest. Muffled, like the slam of a door deep in the cellar of a house.

Beck’s eyes opened wide, the red irises standing out sharply against the white of his sclera. For half a beat, he looked more surprised than angry.

Then he burst into flame. Shards of Hillan exploded outward from inside him, and for an instant the flash of fire was brilliant white at its core. When it dissipated there was nothing left of Beck. Not a hand. Not a boot.

The watching apprentices flinched away from the spinning iron that shot out of the smoke. Shards of Hillan hissed where they landed, sinking into the wet grass. The air stank of hot metal and burnt meat. A bank of smoke drifted lazily across a shallow crater, and there was nothing in it.

Even through the smoke and iron and ash, Maureen could smell a fading sweetness. Fallen blossoms. Dark-purple blood, soaking into the trampled ferns. It was all that remained of Freya in this clearing, and even that scent was growing thin.

There was a faint pull, coming through the glyph etched into the back of her hand. Beck’s whole total. Reaching for her. She refused it with her will, as she had learned to do, and it unravelled. A pale wisp of it drifted away, towards the northeast. Towards the heart of the valley.

Beck was gone.

Ash in the crater, and the numeral on the back of Maureen’s hand still read 12.

*Your total didn’t move either,* Tally said, sounding smug. *That one went northeast too. Like all the others you’ve ever turned down.*

*File that bearing with the rest,* she thought back silently.

Tally gave an impish laugh. *It already has its own shelf. All of them do.*

The clearing fell silent. In that silence, someone standing at the edge of the crowd swallowed. The thick, wet sound was audible over the distance.

Maureen turned toward the sound. “What?” she asked the crowd, her voice rasping like Jack’s.

Who was it who had shouted “Jack!” as she drove home the final shell? She wanted to know who had seen her.

*On that single word you just spoke, your pitch went up half a tone,* Tally warned her. *You’re getting dangerously close to your natural voice.*

She pressed down on her reshaped vocal cords, forcing the rasp back into her throat. The rasp returned to her voice.

*Good,* Tally said. *Back where it belongs. You had me worried for a moment.*

Sieg flinched away from her, as if she’d raised a hand to strike him. His blood-red robe quivered in the sudden breeze.

“N-nothing,” he said quickly. “It was nothing.” His smooth white bone face turned first one way, then the other, his eyes darting around the edges of the torn clearing.

Maureen’s gaze never left his face. “It’s you, Sieg. The White Bone Tower.”

This was the second time Sieg had watched Jack kill someone up close and personal, and the apprentice’s hunched shoulders suggested that he hadn’t forgotten the first occasion any more than he had this one.

Tally began counting faces for Maureen. *Nine watching, seven gone pale. One swallowed. That was Sieg, so he counts twice.*

*He’s afraid of me,* Maureen thought.

*You bet he is,* Tally replied. *Sensibly so, I might add.*

* * *

There was an awkward silence among the elite apprentices. They stood in a loose half-circle facing her, with enough space between each of them to draw a blade through. Each one’s particles pressed against her sense individually, like the heat from separate stoves in a single room. Behind them, the ordinary apprentices kept their distance without being told. They retreated from the elite apprentices a step at a time, like lesser dogs giving ground to bigger ones as they fought over a carcass.

Tally hung tags over all the apprentices, pulled from its files of the first day’s watching. It listed faction colours, calibration bands, all of them higher than Tally would normally expect for apprentices. The veiled Hader girl still had her low-confidence match to Anna, but Maureen silently dismissed it again. Tally ignored her wishes and kept the tag anyway.

“Never thought our Dark Forest had someone like you hiding in it,” said the red-haired apprentice eventually. The other apprentices made no move to stop him from stepping out of their half-circle formation, which told Maureen even more about the order amongst them than any formal introductions would have done. His deep eyes fixed themselves on Jack’s face. “My name’s Bevis. I expect you’ve heard it.”

Maureen gave him a small nod, but nothing more. She would keep Jack’s voice for when she needed it.

Tally dug up the name from its gossip archives. Richard had said it twice during the old group’s dinners together, with a note of envy in his voice. Anthony had mentioned it once.

*The star,* Maureen thought silently.

*The star, by hearsay,* Tally agreed.

For a moment, Maureen was back in the stone alcove beneath the smoking oil lamp, listening to Richard’s voice rise half a tone as he rolled a drink around in his cup. He was telling everyone what Bevis had achieved that week.

He didn’t ask Jack’s name. He didn’t ask what Jack looked like under the hood. Or who Jack’s mentor was. Instead, the red-headed apprentice smiled. “Welcome,” he said.

With a single word, he made Jack one of the most powerful apprentices in the realm.

*He didn’t even ask who you are. I like him for that. I trust him less for it.*

Maureen agreed with both sentiments silently.

Over Bevis’s shoulder, the gold-haired Blue Ring apprentice exchanged a glance with the Mist Labyrinth apprentice. Neither of them said anything against Bevis’s welcome, but Maureen could read them well enough. The Blue Ring youth’s jaw had set, becoming a hard line in his clean-shaven face. The fog that perpetually clung to the Mist Labyrinth apprentice’s sleeves seemed to tighten around his fingers, obscuring them. The madman had died at Jack’s hands, and now both apprentices were doing the sums.

The introductions were brief. Bevis did them himself. “Durin of the Blue Ring. Carter of the Mist Labyrinth.” There were a few more, their names whisking past too quickly to catch, before he moved on. He didn’t introduce Sieg - it was obvious that Jack already knew him.

[Bevis: black robe, red hair] Tally added to her display. [Durin: Blue Ring, gold hair. Carter: Mist Labyrinth.]

*Durin wears Patton’s colours.*

*I’d noticed,* Maureen replied silently.

Bevis’s eyes flicked once to the slender sword in her hand, the black light running along its edge like a trail of oil. Freya’s blade. Then his eyes moved away, as though he hadn’t seen anything worth questioning.

The circle broke up then. Durin and Carter moved off in separate directions, Sieg and the others disappearing into the smoke-dragged ferns, and Bevis fell into step beside Maureen.

“Walk with me, Jack,” he said. “There’s something you ought to see.”

* * *

The ground sloped gently down towards the heart of the valley. Underfoot, it changed from dark and damp to pale and dry, crunching softly at each step. The grass was grey at the tips, and the air grew sweeter with every step, like steam from a pot of boiling sugar.

 His manner was easy, as though they had known each other for years. He talked as they walked, telling her what he had been able to piece together of this realm. The fluctuations of wizard-energy were strongest in the centre of the valley.

“Not many of us can make it this far,” he said conversationally. “Most turn back. Those who don’t - well, they stop being a problem.”

Tally analysed him as he talked, reading his body language. His hands moved smoothly, his stance was relaxed, and the pulse beating in his throat - if there was one - was hidden by the high collar of his robe. No tells observed, it said. Either honest or practised. Not proof of either.

A body lay beside the path, one of the Dark Forest apprentices reduced to a shrivelled form within his robes, as though he had been a sheet of paper left too close to a fire. There was no wound on his body that Maureen could see. Bevis stepped over the corpse without even glancing down.

Tally added the body to its count of the dry dead.

*Keep counting,* Maureen told it.

*Never stopped,* it replied.

“Here,” Bevis said, stopping and pointing ahead. A wall of thick white fog filled the heart of the valley, so dense that you couldn’t see more than an arm’s length into it. The fog seemed to suck sound from the air like dry sand sucks up water. Their footsteps fell silent at its boundary. The fog was motionless in the still air, not a wisp of breeze to disturb it. Where it brushed her lips it was cold and faintly sweet.

“That’s it,” Bevis said. “That’s where it all leads.” They’d thrown everything they had against it. Scrying. Illusions. Sending some summoned thing to walk right into the fog, then waiting for it to come back. Nothing had worked. Every probe they sent in came back empty. Empty of data.

Bevis’s mouth twisted. “No families behind any of us. So we cut each other’s throats for a thing we can’t even name.” He laughed once, a sharp bark of amusement directed more at himself than anything else.

Maureen reached out with her particle sense, letting her awareness extend forward until it met the white fog. It slid off without catching anything, like a hand on wet glass.

She kept her eyes on the fog as if she were just looking at it, and did not speak aloud. *Tally, give me everything you have,* she told her bonded friend. *Give me every record you have from before. Give me everything you can match.*

*I cannot see into this,* Tally told her. *I cannot see past the edge of that fog. But we can match conditions. The dry dead. The refused totals drifting northeast since your first days here. The flow of particles running backward. The sweetness of the air.*

And there was one more link that Tally added to the chain, so quietly that Maureen almost missed it. The glow of the Seal. It brightened on each day when people died near it.

Tally matched all of it against a single entry from Gregory’s library.

[Skoll Soul-Hunting Fruit. Otherworld plant. Ripens on wizard energy radiation and young souls. Official Wizard grade. Match: by conditions, not by sight.]

Maureen’s heart jumped hard against her ribs once, like someone pounding a fist against a door. Jack’s face did nothing at all.

*Keep your face,* Tally said.

Maureen kept it.

*Good,* Tally said. *Your pulse is 104. Your face says 70.*

Bevis turned toward her, eyes deep in shadow and the top of his face glowing faintly in the light, a question half-formed on his lips. Did you spot something I missed? Then he saw the flat, expressionless mask of Jack’s face, and the question froze. He shut his mouth and turned back to look at the fog bank.

Underneath the Mimicry, Maureen held the borrowed cheek muscles perfectly still. The faint ant-crawl sensation of the spell ran across her jawline, and she let it go without moving a muscle.

“Jack,” Bevis said with a smile. “We’re both Dark Forest. That ought to count for a bit more than the rest, eh? Whatever happens in here . . . well, let’s not go for each other.”

“Of course,” Maureen rasped. She nodded agreement. They were close enough equals that either one would be a deadly foe, and neither trusted the other enough to make any promises beyond that.

*One fewer rival, not one more friend,* Tally noted.

*I know the difference,* Maureen thought back silently.

*I know you do,* Tally replied. *I was just saying it for the file.*

Bevis smiled again and nodded, then walked off up the slope. Maureen watched until his bright red hair faded into the grey haze. A small frown crossed Jack’s face, but only after Bevis was gone.

For two apprentices of the Dark Forest, a promise not to stick knives in one another was about as close as professional courtesy got. Neither had turned his back on the other first—Bevis had only done so when it cost him nothing.

* * *

Maureen Olsen stood alone at the edge of the white fog. The silence was complete. She could hear her own breath. The sweet air clung to her throat, making it hard to swallow. The grey light lay flat against the slope. Somewhere far off she could hear the dull thudding sound of fighting. Her hand reached for her inner pocket before she had made the conscious decision to move it.

*The Seal fit the conditions as well,* Tally said quietly.

*The Seal was feeding,* Maureen responded silently.

*It was at least near the feeding,* Tally corrected. *I can’t tell which of the two it is from here.*

Through the cloth, the Seal felt cooler than it had before. Its cracked glow had a sullen look. Beneath her shirt, the pale eye lying against her breastbone felt cold. She did not say anything more about it.

She considered the entry again. Fruit that could raise even an Official Wizard’s spirit toward advancement? If the others knew what was ripening in that white fog, they would go mad for it.

Tally noted the status line for her: [Spirit: 7.6. Inheritance standard: 10.]

In the same breath, Tally reminded her of the other part of the entry. It promised to lift even an Official Wizard’s spirit, but it also mentioned hounds.

Even if one disregarded every other possible rival in the realm, the fruit was still nearly impossible for any apprentice to obtain. Whatever guarded it was no apprentice’s quarry.

*Well, then, we make ready for the hounds,* she said silently to Tally, without speaking a word aloud.

*I’ve started a list,* Tally told her. *You’re not going to like the first item on it.*

*What?*

*A stone we don’t have.*

“Time to make some preparations,” she said under her breath in Jack’s rasping voice. Alone where no one could hear her, one corner of the borrowed mouth quirked upward.

The hunger returned. The same hunger that had driven her up every rung, the same hunger that had led her to this moment, but now it was slower, more patient. Her mind was already working, considering the bait, arrays, and the weight of shells in her pack.

She turned her back on the white fog and began walking up the slope, away from it and into the trees. Jack the Ripper slipped out of sight as if he’d never been there.

From the rise, Tally drew her attention to a man standing on the ridge across the valley. Carter of the Mist Labyrinth. She knew him by the fog that clung to his sleeves. His [Carter, by sight] tag identified him. He stood with his arms crossed and his chin down, watching. *Let him watch for the moment,* she told Tally silently.

Somewhere behind her, muffled by distance, came the dull thud of a blast followed by a scream that climbed the scale before it was suddenly cut off. In the secret realm, the killing continued.
