It hadn't changed by so much as a hair.

The flat grey haze hung in the air like breath on an old mirror, obscuring the sky without providing cover. There was no sun and no wind. The air was cold and tasted of dust from pulverized stone. Her boots made no sound as she walked across the pale gritty ground. She glanced down at her robe to make sure it was straight, then looked up ahead.

Tally overlaid the Pupil-Sight frames recorded by the Filth-Star Slate on top of her vision. The recorded images were grainy, thin-coloured, and tilted back and forth slightly, reflecting the way the Slate had been carried by its short legs. Seven amber points marked traps along the route, and one red mark indicated the end of the line far ahead, where the Slate had died in the middle of the courtyard. Maureen paused to glance around.

*You sure this is the same stone?* she asked Tally silently.

*Same lichen, same crack, eleven steps,* Tally replied.

*You're sure about the lichen?* Maureen asked.

*Yes, quite sure. Lichen is very individual.*

*Comforting,* Maureen told it.

*I try,* Tally replied, sounding smug with its data.

So Maureen walked in the footsteps of the dead scout. She placed her boots exactly where the small red-and-white shoes of the Slate had stepped in the recorded image, and Tally counted every step in her head in a metronomic monotone.

At each amber trap marker, Maureen studied the ground beneath her. The real terrain looked perfectly innocent. A flagstone just a shade too clean, a grey stone where no lichen grew. As she drew near the fourth amber point, the fine hairs on her forearms began to rise. She kept her breath on the Silver Light rhythm, three long breaths followed by one short, and didn't look down twice.

After passing the seventh amber marker, Tally spoke up in her head. *Beyond the red data point I have nothing at all. The Slate never made it further. No data.*

*I'll treat everything past there as live then,* Maureen answered silently.

*Good,* said Tally.

The manor began to take shape in the haze, emerging like a tombstone from the fog. Grey walls of weathered stone rose before her, their defensive spells shimmering ever so slightly like oil on water. Dead ivy hung from the roof in places, and the grey tiles of the roof were covered in lichen. In the recorded Pupil-Sight images from the Slate, the manor had been a blurred thing in the background. Now the actual stonework was sharp and detailed, and the ghost image slid over it, falling half a finger out of true.

The gate creaked like a rusted hinge as she pushed it open, a dry, lingering sound that echoed across the empty courtyard. Maureen looked down at the cracked flagstones and black scorch marks, beneath which lay the Devil Vine floor waiting to trip her. She smiled. "This time," she said to the ground, "you can't stop me."

Tally had shown her once:

> Desiccant: 1

> Green vials: 4

> Void Servant materials:

> white-sand powder

> black-vapour wood

> black-marked snakeskin

She still remembered. The desiccant had been built against the vine reactions Tally had recorded through the Slate. The green had been Bina's alchemy.

The Seal stayed in her pocket, its light nearly dark.

Withdrawing one hand from her wide sleeve, Maureen took out a tube filled with a boiling grey-white potion. The glass remained cool against her fingertips. The liquid inside never stopped bubbling. It was like holding a tube of boiling water, but not. Using her thumb, she worked the stopper loose.

The liquid leapt from the mouth of the tube, turning into wisps of white smoke as it hit the air. For a second the smoke drifted, a few feet off the ground. Then it dropped, all at once. As if grabbed by unseen hands, it pulled itself into the soil between the flagstones.

The entire courtyard began to bubble. A thick glugging sound came from under the stones. Below that, there was a thin wailing. High-pitched and almost too faint to hear, it seemed to come from many places at once.

Maureen stood still on the step before the gate, letting it scream.

Tally timed the boiling of the courtyard:

Boil: 41 s

Soil: grey-white

Movement below: ceased

When the boiling stopped, the courtyard lay grey-white, like a desert. The Devil Vines lay in a tangled heap on the ground. Their leaves were curled and shrivelled, as if every last drop of moisture had been sucked out of them. They smelled like dry sap and scorch. When Maureen poked at them with the toe of her boot, they crumbled.

*Desiccation complete,* Tally said.

*You brewed that against a recording of a floor. I would like that noted.*

*Noted,* she told it silently. *By you.*

*Twice,* Tally said.

* * *

Maureen turned her attention to the manor itself.

She took out her materials and laid them out on the grey soil in front of her, in a line. First came the bottle of powder, which looked like very fine white sand. Next came the block of wood, dark and oozing black vapour. Last came the snakeskin, writhing with its shifting black markings.

The air was thick and bitter with the smell of burnt resin and cold ash. The snakeskin felt papery and dry, like a discarded onion husk. The powder inside the bottle was shifting slightly, though nothing was disturbing it. There was a faint hiss from the bottle, just audible above the crackle of the grey-white soil under her boots. Like old snow, Maureen thought.

"Right," she murmured. "Near enough ready."

She closed her eyes for a moment, stilled her mind, and went through the casting gestures in her head. Then she began to chant, her voice low, in the line-tongue.

*That's not Ancient Andas,* Tally pointed out.

*I know,* Maureen told it silently.

*It's one of the languages that came out of Patton's book with the sigil. You need to bring your vowels further back in your throat, or you'll mispronounce them.*

*I know,* Maureen repeated.

*Well, I know you know, but you're still doing it wrong.*

Tally continued to point out her mistakes as Maureen chanted on, ignoring it. The wood rose off the soil, hung for a moment in midair, and then burst with a sharp crack into a great cloud of black smoke, which rolled over the white powder and the snakeskin, swallowing them both.

The cloud began to pulse, swelling and shrinking like a giant lung. There were muffled roars from deep inside it, and the grey air began to lean towards it, making Maureen's robe hem tug at her ankles. She stood, chanting, her voice low and even between her clenched teeth.

The black smoke began to thin. A huge phantom shape emerged from within it: the torso of a human, but inhumanly muscled. The face was ugly and twisted with viciousness, the hair standing straight up in the air like sharp steel needles, and from the forehead jutted two great bull horns. In one clawlike hand it clutched a long steel trident, the tines glittering in the air. Below the waist was the body of a giant snake, which writhed with black smoke. Its coils dragged along the grey soil with a dry rasp, leaving scales of smoke, like steam rising from wet wool.

Nothing about the creature was stone. Everything about it was muscle and slither. The trident's tines shone bright and wicked in the haze.

It looked at the dead courtyard surrounding it in open disbelief, then croaked something hoarsely. It turned its gaze to Maureen, and the disbelief hardened into hostility.

Maureen felt that hostility spike in her particle sense, hot and savage. She heard Tally's voice turn cold. *Hostile. Bind now. Second syllable low.*

Maureen's face darkened. "Vashtur kemmaron!" she snapped. Tally nudged her tongue back, and the second syllable came out low, just right.

The word lashed out, a crackling whip of command. The Void Servant screamed as if it had been set aflame, every scale shivering on its body. It thrashed around on the ground, great coils of smoke snapping into the air and flinging up sprays of grit. When the shuddering stopped, it lowered its horned head. There was no more fire in its eyes. Only fear.

Maureen felt the command sitting between them now, like a short leash in her fist.

She gave the Void Servant no softer word. She simply pointed at the manor. "Kemmaron, ithá!"

The creature obeyed instantly, breaking down into smoke. It poured across the courtyard and sank straight down into the manor's walls. The defensive glyphs carved into the plain, ancient stone woke as one, glowing with a strange and sickly light. They flickered hard for a moment, then began going dark one by one like lamps pinched at their wicks.

Tally kept count. [Glyphs dark: 1 / 23.]

[Glyphs dark: 12 / 23.]

[Glyphs dark: 23 / 23.]

Maureen chuckled under her breath. Without a Void Servant, dismantling those defences would have taken days. The line-tongue was an odd language to use for casting, but in its own limited field, it was incredibly efficient.

Tally was a little sulky about the whole affair.

*Hugh would be so flattered,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*Hugh would eat it,* Tally said.

* * *

Maureen pushed open the door of the manor, and was instantly struck by the air. It was thick, dead, and still. It smelled stale, like it had been left alone and untouched for ten thousand years. She took a shuttered lantern out of her kit and opened its shutter. A bright white wedge cut through the drifting dust. Everything in sight was covered by a thick layer of dust. It lay on the floor like grey fur. The footprints she was leaving behind her were the first in centuries. She could tell because the edges were crisp, and no other prints were visible in the dust. The air was so dry it caught in her throat as she breathed, and it smelled of old wood that was rotting away to softness. Beneath the wood’s scent there was something sweetish, rotted long ago.

Then Maureen froze. There, just ahead of her, a man faded into view. He was translucent, and he was glowing faintly. It was the spirit imprint that had appeared during the Slate’s visit. His features were blurred, but his smile shone through. "Inheritor," he said. "You've come."

*No data on what he carries,* Tally said, its voice low in her head.

*Neither do I,* Maureen answered silently.

"I am so glad," the imprint said. "Now that you are the Inheritor, let me explain—" Maureen already had a green vial in her hand. She whipped off the cork and flung the contents straight at him. The imprint broke off with a cry of, "Ah! You! You—"

The green mist curled around the imprint like frost forming on a windowpane. His light flickered like a candle in a strong wind, and his outline began to smear and twist. His face filled with pain.

Maureen's hand held the empty vial without a tremor. Tally began to count her breaths. One. Two. Three. Four.

All within the Silver Light rhythm.

"This is your house," Maureen said flatly to the imprint. "I don't know what you have planned for me. What else you've left waiting for me. So, just to be safe, I think you should go first." The imprint's outline was almost gone now, just a few last traces of his face still visible. "If you mean me harm, then you've earned it. If you don't, well, you're just an imprint. Losing you won't cost me anything. The real you died a long time ago."

The imprint laughed, a broken and wondering laugh. "Heh... heh... this... this is what a wizard should be... Maybe Teacher... wanted me to become someone like you..."

"Teacher... I'm sorry... I was always sorry..."

And then, one last time:

"Teacher..."

It burst into thousands upon thousands of specks of light. Tiny points that floated on the air, dancing and shimmering, then winking out one by one until the beam of her lantern picked up nothing but dust motes. Her thumb stayed pressed against the cool glass of the empty vial. She narrowed her eyes at the spot where he had been, not moving for another long breath.

*Logged.*

*Was I wrong?*

*No data on 'wrong'. You walked into no traps. No traps set off. Thumb still on glass.*

*Oh.*

She lifted her thumb from the vial, then tucked it away again. Then she looked around the floor. It had been built as a place for him to retreat to. Nothing was lacking. There was an area for planting, a room for conducting experiments, a storage room for his materials, a greenhouse, cultivation beds. Every aspect of a wizard's working life was represented here.

The jars in the materials room were full of shrivelled brown crusts, their preservation glyphs still glowing faintly on their lids. The glass panes in the greenhouse were grey with dust, and the soil in the planting beds had crumbled into cinder. Everything smelled like dried paper.

*All materials scanned are spent,* Tally noted. *Their potency is depleted. The preservation spells slowed time within, but they couldn't stop it. Nothing on this floor is worth bringing out.*

In one corner of the greenhouse, Maureen found a small potted plant that looked rather uninteresting. But it had been a rare and precious herb, dried to a brown twist. "Huh," she muttered. "Really..."

*A pot like that would have fetched...*

*I don't want to know.* Tally obediently kept the number off her display, but continued in a hurt tone, *It was a good figure, you know.*

Maureen turned away from the dead herbs, leaving them behind. She climbed to the second floor, lantern swinging. The stairs groaned beneath her weight, and a thin sift of dust fell from the railing as her sleeve brushed past it.

* * *

The second floor had fewer doors, but the rooms beyond were bigger. Maureen hesitated, then picked the largest and pushed inside.

Immediately, the sigil in her palm glowed with a clear light, bright and sharp as moonlight.

Silver lines rose up out of the walls, the floor, and the ceiling. They flickered in and out of brilliance, like moonlight reflecting off a moving body of water. The entire room seemed to glow from within, lit by its own power. Her lantern was a dim yellow smudge next to the silvery effulgence. She had the sudden sensation of having stepped through some barrier into another dimension, one she wasn't familiar with.

Maureen put a hand out, trying to touch the nearest line. They rippled around her fingers like water disturbed by a pebble, then closed up again seamlessly behind them. She felt... nothing. They were there, but she could not touch them.

Tally spoke up. *None of this matches any frames I hold,* it said. *The Slate didn't get this far, so I don't have any data on this room. I'm recording all the lines, just in case.*

Maureen nodded, her pulse slowing again. The excitement bubbled up, but she clamped down on it. She peered past the shimmering light, making out rows of bookshelves lined with dusty books. Ahead of her stood a black desk, strewn with objects as if someone had been sitting at it, only to rise with the intention of returning immediately.

A crystal ball sat atop the desk. Maureen hesitated for a moment, thinking, then reached out and placed her hand on top of it.

A faint cold seeped into her palm. The ball woke up with a black glow that spread across the desktop.

Something came through her hand from the crystal, but it wasn't anything she could understand. It was a sense of perfect fit, like a stream of milk being poured into a glass of water. There was no resistance, no separation between the two. No seams. She removed her hand slowly, standing and staring at the crystal.

Tally finally broke the silence. *I set the ball against my records for the Sunset Canyon crystal and Hegel's cross-star orb,* it said. *The darkness channel matches yours, but 'fit' is not a unit I have on record.*

*It felt like milk poured into water,* Maureen told it silently.

*Then I'll log it as milk,* Tally said.

*Is this thing going to eat me?* Maureen asked silently.

*Not so far. No data on what it does next.*

Some kind of testing device, Maureen decided. She turned her attention to the two books resting on the desk. One was a simple notebook, bound in leather and looking vaguely like a diary. The other was bound in bronze, with silver veins running through it. They glowed faintly beneath her moonlight sigil. She reached for the notebook first, flipping it open. Tally dutifully recorded the spread as she turned each page.

"I'm sorry, Teacher," the first line read. "You put so much of yourself into me, and still I can't inherit what is yours."

The Teacher had called himself a lucky man, the apprentice wrote, and would never speak of his past. But he had taken the apprentice as his only disciple, and gathered the resources needed to build the Minai Totem, to adapt to the bloodline of a Child of Purgatory.

He had wiped out countless wizard organisations on the Passise Subcontinent, killed a Third-Tier Wizard, a Fourth-Tier Human-Faced Spider Fiend of the Underworld, a Fourth-Tier tiger-headed whale of the Endless Sea, and numerous other high-energy creatures.

Tally started a new column down the side of Maureen's sight, listing the dead. *Hearsay,* it noted next to every kill. *One source, loyal source.*

The apprentice's Teacher had opened a dimensional gate to the Purgatory World and dragged out a Child of Purgatory. "Such a gift," the apprentice wrote. His body could not absorb its blood. He could not even absorb the blood of the Black Mando, a subspecies of the Child of Purgatory Pankora.

He did not have the ability to fully grasp the Minai Totem, and his body was unsuited for the road that his Teacher walked. "Even if I can kill Official Wizards," he wrote, "what does this mean? I cannot walk your road. None of these things have meaning for me. I am sorry, Teacher."

Page after page was filled with anxiety and apology, the pen pressed so hard into the paper that it furrowed the surface. The last page was almost a scrawl, letters torn and forced together, lines slanting off the edge of the page as though written by someone shaking.

*You can stop reading now, Maureen,* Tally said quietly. *I have the page.*

Maureen kept going until she hit the end anyway. A disciple given everything, and unable to carry any of it. She thought of Adrian, his eyes fixed on her, saying that he didn't sell his apprentices. She thought of Jing Sheng's notebooks, lying open on the white bench, the words that no one would ever read again. Her throat grew tight, and she swallowed until it eased.

She set the diary down. "Well," she said softly. "That's interesting."

He'd been kind, and perhaps too sensitive for his own good. He had not been able to inherit what he wanted, and died for it, leaving all of this behind. Even if he'd puffed up his Teacher, the man couldn't have been much less than what the apprentice described. Maureen's nerves prickled with excitement, held down firmly by caution.

She let out a long breath, then reached for the bronze book. Before her fingers could touch it, her palm burned and the sigil flared. The book lifted itself off the desk, floating in midair as it slowly opened.

A voice came out of the book. It was vast and ancient, as cold as the moon. It was like a great hall filled with the dead, all speaking at once. Dust trembled on the shelves from the force of it. The silver lines running through the walls, ceiling, and floor pulsed with each word.

"What is a wizard? Take a bloodline's power and become a warlock? Brand runes and become a Rune Mage? Call creatures from other worlds to fight beside you? Meditate on alien energies until they rot you into a monster? Or study spells and crawl slowly upward?" It paused. "No. None of these. Branches. What is left after the true road fails."

*Here is another competitor,* Tally said with feeling. *Louder than the testing room.*

"Knowledge and power: that is a wizard! Learn endless knowledge, walk endless worlds, cross endless dimensions; that is the first part. Take every source of power, bloodline, curses, alien energies, sound waves, illusions, runes, and make it your own; that is the root. Knowledge is the means. Only power is eternal!"

*Bloodline.* Tally counted them off, one by one as the voice said each one. Curses. Alien energies. Sound waves. Illusions. Runes. Tally organised them into a column on Maureen's display, all six powers stacked up in a nice neat row. Maureen liked columns. She liked this one.

"Our forebears, numberless, left us the most orthodox road: the Path of the Ancient Wizards! We pursue power, but no power binds us, because we have the Minai Totem! With our own bodies as the medium we inscribe the secret rune lines, ignite them with the essence blood of bloodline creatures, and on that foundation absorb, learn and devour every power without ever being bound."

Maureen's pulse climbed. Tally helpfully displayed the number [Pulse: 118] in the corner of her vision, but didn't say anything else.

She stood, stunned, as she realized what the voice was saying. With the Minai Totem as her foundation, she could take bloodline, curses, sound waves, soul, illusions, runes, combined spells, strange arrays and spell torrents. She could make use of everything and conquer it.

Every other road offered to her so far had been a cage, built by someone else for her to step into. This wasn't a cage. Her hands stayed perfectly still on the edge of the desk, her breath held. "The Path of the Ancient Wizards," she whispered.
