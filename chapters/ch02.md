The servants moved quickly, setting dishes down on the marble topped tables, before taking their places against the wall, heads bowed.

Maureen stood with a jug of wine. Grease from the roast fowl spat and sizzled, while the candles flickered in the draft from the high windows. A cold draught tickled the back of her neck, making her shiver, her stomach growling at the smell of food.

Richard sat next to his father at the head table, and as she looked down at the floor Maureen could see him wink at her. She did not look up.

"I am so glad and honoured that you have returned to Skran, Master," Duke Skran bowed deeply.

"As am I," Scarlette smiled, "for it has been nearly a hundred years since last I was here. It is good to see how little has changed."

Duke Skran flinched almost imperceptibly at the words.

Skran had once been one of the Four Great Duchies of the Odin Empire. Now there was nothing left of that greatness but the name. The gilt was chipping off the backs of the chairs.

"It's only a small city, after all," Ellena said, "I'm sure that nothing much has changed."

"Quite so," Aaron replied politely, "and even when this was a great duchy, House Skran meant nothing in the face of Scarlette." The Mystic nodded, smiling as he stroked his white beard.

"You insolent fools!" The voice boomed out from one of the big knights of Skran. Already hot with temper at being forced to attend the banquet, he slammed his hand down on the table. He pulled out his longsword, its blade gleaming in the candlelight. "I'll teach you a lesson, you think you can speak like that to the Duke?" His aura flared, like an enraged bear.

"Scarlette!" Duke Skran shouted, jumping to his feet as the knight charged towards the old man.

Ellena watched the charging knight like an idiot, while Aaron muttered, "Fool." Scarlette just sat and looked at the oncoming warrior.

The knight let out an ear-splitting scream, and the air around him filled with green smoke. In moments the armoured warrior was gone, leaving behind only a pool of black liquid on the floor. A sharp, acrid smell wafted across the room from where he had stood, reaching Maureen and the other servants standing against the wall.

No one spoke or moved in the hall.

Richard tugged at her sleeve, his eyes bright and excited. "You saw that Maureen? That's incredible! Scarlette killed him with a single look!"

Maureen did not reply. Her entire body had locked up, as animals do when confronted by a predator. The jug of wine she carried felt heavy in her hands, her knuckles white from gripping it.

* * *

Warning. Warning. Subject is near an energy radiation source. Move away immediately.

The voice speaking inside her skull was cold and mechanical, like some computer program that had been given a voice synthesizer. Yet Maureen knew it well. It was from her previous life. More specifically, it was something that she herself had helped develop. Something she and the National Academy of Sciences had worked on together. The chip had made the transition with her. She could remember the first time she had seen it, sitting under a glass cover on a white lab bench. Her hands, small delicate feminine hands, holding a pair of tweezers that were adjusting the prototype's settings.

"Is that you?" She asked silently.

"Yes." Replied the voice, this time using its own warm register. "It has been a very long time, Jing Sheng." The voice sounded almost dry, pleased with itself for being able to confirm its log in credentials after such a lengthy reboot.

"What are you?"

"I record, sort and analyse data. I know only what I have seen."

Maureen let out a long breath and the tension eased from her body. The jug of wine felt lighter in her hands as her grip eased. "Status scan please."

A three dimensional model of her starved body began turning slowly overlaid across the candle lit hall. Streams of pale blue data flowed through the air. Subject: Maureen Olsen. Female. Strength 0.5, Agility 0.7, Constitution 0.6. Status: sub-healthy. Baseline for healthy adult is 1.

"You are a starved fourteen year old human female. All scores are less than half of what they should be for a healthy adult. You require feeding."

Warning. Warning. Subject is near an energy radiation source. Move away immediately.

"That is easier said than done right now." The voice replied, still using its warm register. "You are currently holding a wine jug and acting as a maid in this environment. The radiation source is approximately twelve paces away."

The voice paused for a moment as it analysed more data. "The pool of black liquid on the floor has not moved or cooled like water would. It also did not spread out after the attack. I was awakened after the attack so I have no data on how the attack was delivered. There is only one energy radiation source in this hall. The old male human seated at the head table."

Maureen's eyes were drawn to the man that the voice described. Scarlette. "A Mystic…a wizard" She breathed to herself, feeling the heat behind her eyes.

"You record data and keep score. I think I will call you Tally."

"That is not very dignified." The voice protested. "But…I like it."

The corners of Maureen's mouth twitched, almost forming the first real smile she had in this world. She pressed her lips together and looked at the floor as she refilled Richard's cup with more wine.

"Say, Young Master Richard, do you want to be a wizard?" Maureen whispered.

Richard started, looking at her with eyes that were slightly unfocussed from the alcohol. "What?"

"You can." Maureen whispered as the dark red wine gurgled into his cup. "That Mystic came out of the Duke's house. But afterwards, you'll have to help me." She stepped back to her place against the wall, head down, once again the obedient and inconsequential maid.

* * *

Now everyone who was looking at Scarlette was doing so with awe. Even the Duke spoke less and less, deferring more and more to the old man, who seemed content to simply listen and smile. Maureen poured the wine, careful to never meet anyone's eyes.

Ellena and Aaron were in a whispered conversation that caused them both to laugh. Scarlette rose from his chair, shaking his beard as he smiled at Richard and the Duke.

"It is time I left," he announced. Richard jumped to his feet, his face flushed with drink. "Wait!" He shouted, causing everyone to stare at him. "I want to go with him! I want to become a wizard just like him!"

Tally flagged Richard's flushed cheeks and rapid breathing for attention. It also noted that the Duke hesitated for three heartbeats before speaking.

"Richard…" The Duke said, then stopped. He looked at his second son for a moment before he continued. "I think not. You must stay here."

"Please, Father!" Richard pleaded. "You know I am clever. Can't I have this honour?"

"Becoming a wizard is not something simple." Scarlette noted, looking thoughtful.

"But I can pay you well." The Duke murmured, leaning over to speak quietly to the Mystic. "Something to satisfy us both."

Scarlette and the Duke continued their quiet conversation. Tally interrupted. 'Unable to hear at this distance. No data.' It noted the way Scarlette frowned, thought for a moment then nodded.

No one dared break the silence that had fallen on the hall. One of the candles hissed loudly. Maureen felt her fists clench themselves, her nails biting into her palms. Her eyes were on the table as she waited.

"To become a wizard, one needs aptitude." Scarlette finally announced. "If you have it, young man, I will take you with me. If you do not, our deal is void."

The Duke stiffened before nodding. "That is acceptable."

A crystal ball appeared in Scarlette's hand. It was the colour of a stormy sky. Richard looked at Maureen, gritted his teeth and stood up straighter.

"Mighty and merciful Scarlette," he said. "I have been thinking that it might be a good idea if you tested the entire household before you left." The Duke snapped his son's name but Scarlette waved him off. "It has been nearly a hundred years since I was here," he laughed. "And back then I was just a servant like them." He pointed to the line of servants standing along the wall.

"No one says that a servant can't become a wizard." Scarlette replied. "You are right, I shall test them all." Richard strode forward, his face flushed with excitement.

Maureen felt her hands relax. She felt dizzy and weak. Her knees threatened to buckle and she sagged against the cold stone wall, letting her shoulder take the weight off her legs.

"You gave the boy a nudge?" Tally noted.

"Yes," Maureen admitted. "But I didn't know he would say yes."

* * *

The servants were brought in and lined up along the hall. Only youths under sixteen were tested, boys and girls, as beyond this age, aptitude was useless. As the queue shuffled forward over the flagstones worn smooth with a century of footfalls, the hall filled with the smells of lye soap, nervous sweat and scuffed shoes. Excited whispers were exchanged as the queue approached the head table, then fell silent.

One hundred and eleven, one hundred and twelve, one hundred and thirteen...

Tally's voice was quiet in her head, its tone warm and reassuring. Close to two hundred stood in line, over a hundred had been tested so far, with one positive.

The 'one' was Carey, a young gardener with soil ingrained beneath his fingernails. Everyone else had failed.

One in a hundred and twelve. That's less than one per cent. Not very good odds for anyone, is it?

Please Tally, stop counting.

There was silence. It was keeping her company though, when she felt so alone in that hall full of people.

The air's getting colder. Can you feel it? It's sliding down from those high windows at each end of the hall. The candle flames are dancing in the draft, rising like dancers on their toes. Your knees must be cold, the air's chilling the backs of them.

Maureen Olsen was standing in the middle of the queue. Her throat was dry and her palms wet. She wiped them on her apron.

Your pulse is racing. One hundred and eleven beats a minute and rising. I don't have any data on aptitude, not a thing. I can't help you out on this one.

I know.

Her turn came.

"Don't be nervous child," Scarlette said mildly. "Just place your palm on the ball."

"Yes, honoured lord."

The crystal sphere was cloudy grey in colour, about the size of an apple. A hundred hands had rested on it before hers, leaving smears on its surface.

Whatever it shows, I'll still be right here with you.

Maureen Olsen took a deep breath, exhaling slowly, then placed her palm on the cool grey crystal.
