For days now, Denise had been everywhere at once. She crossed the manor corridors with her jaw set and steps quick, and Maureen had watched the worry eat into her from across dinner tables, down stairwells.

Maureen stood amongst the shelves now, a silver hard-bound book open in her hands. The pages lay spread while Tally worked to file them away. The metal binding felt cold as a coin against her fingers and it smelled of glue over a century old.

Denise burst through the doors to the library. She almost skidded to a stop in front of Maureen, a flush high on her cheeks. “They said yes. My uncles. They finally said yes, Richard. I’m going with them.”

“Yes?”

Denise nodded quickly. “If I go down to the Underworld with Claude’s party then I can help finish up the trade negotiations with the dark elves. If I do that, then I get to use the Spring of Life too. I can bring some back... and father can mend. It will take time, but he’ll be all right.”

The joy on her face was real enough. It filled the cold room like a lit stove, and for a moment Maureen almost let it warm her. Tally laid a quiet readout over the girl.

[Pulse up. Pupils wide. Hand to sleeve: 2. Voice drops at “agreed”.]

*You’ve paid for that,* Maureen said silently.

*Yes. Something big though. Her signet ring has been missing from her left hand since yesterday. By sight.*

Maureen nodded and slid the silver book back into its slot. There was no hurry in her movements. Instead she smiled with Richard’s plain mouth. “Congratulations, Denise. Truly.” The smile didn’t quite reach her eyes.

The chill of the library floor seeped through Maureen’s boot soles. White light, filtered by the frost coating the windows, fell across the silver-bound spines of books.

Maureen looked down at Denise’s left hand, where the signet ring had been. She drew her own conclusion: Claude had not been bought with gratitude alone. Denise had given up a large chunk of her power in the family.

*You’re going to tell her?* Tally asked.

*No,* Maureen thought silently. *An heir who has just sold off part of their house doesn’t want their guard doing the sums in front of them.*

Tally sighed. *Tact, logged. Under protest.*

Denise reached up absently to tuck a stray strand of golden-brown hair behind her ear. It was such a soft shade of brown that it bordered on blonde. She seemed to be thinking of something. “Claude and the others are heading over to father now,” she said. “They’re going to explain why I’m going to be away for a while. I want you there, Richard.”

There was the faintest thread of need in Denise’s voice. It was the voice of someone who wanted one person in that room to not be family.

Maureen paused, thinking about it for the length of a breath. Then she nodded once. “Lead the way.”

As they headed out of the library, Tally noted the number of shelves for later reference. *Eleven bays. Just mentioning it. In case you were wondering.*

*I know. You always do.*

* * *

Lawrence lay propped up on pillows, swaddled in white silk bedclothes. He looked like something that had been left outside to dry. His grey hair was brittle as straw. His skin had been drawn so tightly against his bones that his knuckles looked carved into the back of his hands, the flesh stretched flat between. His eyes were a dull, sunken yellow.

Except when they moved, he would have passed for a corpse.

*Seven breaths a minute,* Tally told her without being asked. *Weaker than during our first visit.* And her mental voice softened a fraction. *His density has decreased since then too. A wizard of the Phantom-State, burning down like a lamp with no more oil in its reservoir.*

Claude stood bent over the bed, talking to his brother in a low, reasonable voice. Jackman stood apart from them, staring moodily out the window. The mountains were going dark under a flat lid of grey cloud, and he seemed more interested in watching that than in whatever was happening inside the sickroom. The smell of camphor and old sweat hung in the air. There was a single lamp in the room, turned low.

Maureen felt the hairs rise on the back of her neck. The old man’s back might have been turned, but the black-wizard grain of him sat in the room like cold air creeping in under a door. She kept her own aura pressed firmly into her chest.

Denise rushed to the bedside. She stroked both hands across her father’s face, as if trying to warm it up. “Father?” she said.

Lawrence turned his head toward his daughter with some effort. “Is it true? Claude says you’re going down with him. Into the Underworld.”

The girl’s eyes reddened, but she spoke without letting her voice bend. “It’s true, Father. I’ll bring the Spring of Life back. I’ll mend you. I promise.”

Her father’s lips moved as if to speak, then stopped. Something went dim in his expression. He sighed deeply and put one hand on Denise’s hair.

[Openings: 1, abandoned.]

*What did he mean to say there?* Maureen asked silently.

*No data,* Tally replied, *but I think it had something to do with the trip, based on where his eyes were looking.*

Claude stood up straight and adjusted his tailcoat. He smiled mildly at his brother. “She’ll be under my eye every step, brother. You have my word.”

The reassurance settled on the bed like a hand on a shoulder, a hand that never lifted away.

“Denise,” Lawrence said, “see that you take care. Be cautious at all times. The Underworld is a place of shadows and horrors. You mustn’t . . .” His voice faded into low murmuring. Then, halfway through his quiet instructions to his daughter, his gaze slid past her to Richard. His mouth opened again, but he didn’t speak, and finally he closed it.

[Openings: 2, abandoned.]

*Was that for Denise or for me?* Maureen asked silently.

*The second time he looked at you,* Tally answered. *So not a confession, but a tell.*

The two unfinished sentences seemed to lodge themselves beneath Maureen’s breastbone, like stones she’d swallowed. A father who wanted to warn his daughter, and who wanted to live himself. Who couldn’t choose between the two.

Tally kept a counter over Jackman’s back. [Impatience: weight shifts 9 in 4 min.] As the old wizard’s green eyes shifted to look at the bed, Maureen dropped her gaze to the floorboards.

*Good,* Tally said. *Let him think Richard is bored too.*

At last Claude nodded fractionally. The visit was over. They filed out into the corridor, then scattered toward their own rooms.

* * *

Denise turned on her heel a few steps away from her father’s door. In the guttering light of the sconces, her eyes were bright. “Richard, will you come with me?” she asked. “Down there? To the dark-elf task?”

 “I know it’s not what you signed for. But I’ll pay over the top of it.” She paused to consider, then made her first offer. “Any who help complete the task may use the Spring of Life. For a wizard, that’s worth more than a vault of gemstones.”

Then she lowered her voice slightly, her tone almost sly. “And you wanted access to our archive. You told me so. Come down with me and it’s yours to read. All of it.”

Maureen felt an old hunger lift its head inside her. It was the same hunger that had rifled through the Skran treasury, that had flipped through the stacks of tomes in the Dark Forest. Ten generations’ worth of notes, jottings, and magically preserved spells from an entire family of wizards. Offered to her across a long, draughty corridor.

[Original contract + Spring of Life (no data) + Oserroba archive]

*The use of the Spring of Life is based entirely upon Denise’s word,* Tally said. *One source. Eager source.*

Tally began listing the risks as well, starting with the cold hard facts. Freya’s slender sword hung at Maureen’s hip, beneath her robe. Ani’s eyes had gone to it four times during their conversation over the Bloodline Seal Stone. The dark elves knew that blade.

Maureen’s fingers found the black-leather grip beneath her cloak. *Pouch,* she thought silently. The sword vanished from her side, sliding into the Void Dimensional Pouch. [Freya’s Cross Sword → Pouch.]

*Dark elves, Eve, and the key we sold them,* Tally continued. *There is also Jackman, who watched Richard in the sickroom.*

[Probability of trap: medium. Target unknown.]

Then Tally ran through what they were taking with them into the Underworld. *We are taking into the Underworld our spells, the Orb, knight strength, and Edward folded away in the pouch. But no blade.*

Maureen’s thumb pressed against the empty place where the sword normally hung at her belt. It felt wrong to have the cloak hanging there without its weight, just like it had been wrong to feel the empty inner pocket ever since the Seal became Edward’s core.

*Then we’ll go armed,* Maureen said silently.

*We’ll go together,* Tally corrected. *I am putting that in a separate column.*

*Write it into a new contract?*

A second scroll would bind Maureen just as much as Denise. Besides, Tally herself had pointed out how precarious it was to try and lean on contracts.

*I’ll log them as verbal, witnessed by me,* Tally said.

Maureen nodded once, then spoke aloud to Denise in Richard’s flat voice. “Yes. I’ll come.”

Maureen’s curiosity was aroused by the prospect of the legend, and opportunities like this did not present themselves twice. An archive like that—

Denise’s face broke open with relief. She gave a full formal wizard’s bow, hand to breast, and held it for a fraction of a breath longer than courtesy demanded.

Maureen turned and began walking back down the corridor. She absentmindedly rubbed her nose with one knuckle.

*That makes eight times on file,* Tally noted. *You always rub your nose when you win.*

Maureen did not reply, which was answer enough.

* * *

There was a long, dry groan from the archive door. The smell that rolled out of it was paper and mouse and cold wax, the smell of a place that no one has bothered to tidy up for a hundred years.

Chests were stacked in piles to the curve of the vaulted cellar; leaning racks held scrolls tied with rotting ribbons; bound ledgers lay scattered where some distant ancestor had dropped them. There was no order to anything.

Tally painted a pale-blue progress bar along the nearest shelf, like a line of high tide, and put a counter in the corner of Maureen’s sight, reading 0. *Best-looking room I’ve seen all month,* she said.

Maureen put the lamp on an upturned chest and began. Unroll, turn, a spread at a glance, roll it back, the next one. Tally filed each page as it passed before Maureen’s eyes.

The parchment cracked along the folds like old bread. The seals crumbled to grit under her thumb, red dust falling unnoticed to the floor. The inks changed every few decades—first iron-gall, brown as dried blood, then lamp-black, and finally a violet that had faded over time to a grey as insubstantial as a ghost.

Hours flowed into one another. Maureen’s shoulders eased for the first time since Emilia. The silence and the rhythm of turning pages were as close to the white bench as this house was likely to get.

*Library goat,* Maureen thought silently.

*I prefer magpie, actually,* Tally said. *Besides, I have a filing system. And the filing system is the really important part.*

Day after day, Maureen came down with the first light of dawn and climbed out after dark. It took days. Chest by chest, rack by rack.

Tally ran a search for Spring of Life as they went. *Here’s what I’ve got so far:* [Mentions: legend and song only. Method: no data.] *Either no one in this family ever got anywhere near it, or they never wrote anything down about it.*

On a scrap of vellum the Spring was “the white water under the dark”. Maureen read it twice before she let Tally have it.

Tally flagged up a run of entries which looked like they might have something to do with Claude’s branch of the family for more scrutiny, as well as a gap in the household accounts where the ledgers suddenly stopped for a decade, then started again in another hand.

*Later,* Maureen told her silently.

*Always later,* Tally teased.

*And I always do it,* Maureen answered.

*Well, you are good at that,* Tally admitted, and left the flags burning amber for attention.

On the last evening, Maureen finally put down a yellowed scroll of sheepskin and pressed two fingers to the bridge of her nose. *Tally?*

[Documents: 13,524. Categories: 87. New subfolders: 156.]

Tally gave her the figures.

Underneath that Tally drew a blue graph with peaks and troughs, rising and falling over the days. Each peak and trough was labelled with the hour, and how many documents Maureen had turned in that time. *Meals,* Tally explained. *See how it dips at regular intervals? That’s when you’ve eaten. I logged those too.*

Beside the graph was a short list. [Unopened: 3 chests, east alcove, rusted shut.]

*Everything in there is still no data.*

Maureen almost smiled at the curve of the graph. There was still the Spring to come and the Underworld itself, and that would be on top of all this. Even so, the house had already paid her better than any job she’d had yet.

She snuffed the lamp and made her way up the cellar stairs in the dark. The dust still drifted lazily behind her, although she couldn’t see it anymore.

* * *

Back in her room Maureen trimmed the lamp and squared away a stack of wizard paper. It was as dry and crisp as fallen autumn leaves, but just right for working out an idea on the structure of a new spell by hand. Tally had the first few lines of the lattice ready for her in pale blue, waiting patiently for her pen to touch the page. Before the nib touched the paper, the sorcery brand on her forearm flared to life with a faint, ashy grey light.

There was a sharp popping sound and the entire stack of paper burst into flame. In a second the papers were gone, leaving a small curl of black ash on her desk where they had been.

Maureen’s shoulders snapped taut with the noise and smell of scorched paper filling the small room. Her eyes locked on the grey glow still flickering on her skin.

[Brand: Clair.]

[Signal: incoming.]

*Read it.*

*It’s in the ash. Give me a second.*

Maureen scowled at the flakes of black ash that lay where her paper had been. If Clair’s brand had activated, that meant that Clair had something to say.
