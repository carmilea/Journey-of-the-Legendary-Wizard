Maureen walked away from the treasury, past the motionless guards.

"Lady Maureen, your room is this way," Duke Skran's butler said as he walked ahead of her. He was silver haired, with an impassive face, his hair combed flat to his head. His tailcoat and trousers were impeccably pressed and he wore round spectacles, giving him the look of a correct and quiet man. He led Maureen along a cold corridor at the back of the manor house, the girl's breath forming faintly in the cold draught.

The smell of the room was not of tallow candles, it was beeswax. Maureen stood on the threshold, just inside the doorway, unwilling to step further onto the white rug with slum feet.

The bath was copper rimmed, and as Maureen sank into the hot water, she saw the edges of the rose petals darken where they touched the water. Her ribs showed through the water, her fingers wrinkled from their long soak.

In the heat of the water Maureen stayed very still, as though someone might come in and take it away.

Tally?

How long have I got left in here?

3 hours 25 minutes, 14 seconds for the breathing technique.

2 hours 46 minutes, 17 seconds for the swordsmanship.

Thank you, Tally.

The water temperature will decrease by one degree every eleven minutes. That is eleven minutes and 18 seconds

Tally, please don't time my bath for me.

I can't help it, that's why I'm called Tally.

Maureen dried herself off and slipped into a robe. There was a knock at the door, and a servant stood there with the Duke's gifts. Fine cloth, a brooch and a pair of slippers. As he listed them, Tally kept a quiet running total of what the girl would owe if she accepted them. 12 gold, 8 silver, 3 copper. That would go into 'the Skran column', as Tally thought of it, of the ledger.

"I thank the Duke for his generosity," Maureen said to the servant, "but I cannot accept them." She stood there, dressed only in her robe, and declined each gift. Then she shut the door. The servant stood there for a moment before walking away.

Column unchanged, Tally announced. Well done.

Maureen's shoulders relaxed, and she crawled under the thick quilts. They were heavy and warm and she was tired. She emptied her head, and slept.

* * *

The candle had burned to a stub, and grey dawn was visible at the window when Maureen woke. The manor was silent, and the flagstones were cold outside the coverlet.

Maureen Olsen? Tally's warm voice roused her from her sleep. Maureen Olsen, wake up. I have finished both techniques. You can name them now.

Tally had finished the swordsmanship first, but held off telling her until it had also finished the breathing technique. It made no sense to wake her twice.

The Olsen-Tally Synthesis of Four Hundred and Seventy-Nine Schools, Tally suggested proudly.

The Silver Light Breathing Technique and Basic Swordsmanship. Maureen responded silently.

Basic? Tally sounded offended. I read four hundred and seventy-nine manuals for basic?

Nobody ever looks twice at a technique called 'Basic', Maureen said dryly in her mind. The chip sniffed and gave in. Fine. I'll take Silver Light then.

Transfer both techniques to my memory, please. Maureen ordered.

This will hurt. Short version: A Lot, then Not At All. 5...4...

Tally began counting down quietly inside Maureen's head. 3...2...1...

Maureen's skull swelled with the information. Countless body movements, lines of text. She clenched the quilt, holding on to Tally's count.

It passed. She rubbed her brow and breathed out deeply. The forms and key points were in her head, each detail as clear as if she'd memorized it herself.

Sleep was an impossibility. Maureen sat up, cross-legged on the cold flagstones and wearing only her robe. She began to train her Silver Light.

"Three long breaths. One short." Tally counted out loud. Maureen's fingertips pressed against her sternum, feeling for the rush of air. "Short. Shorter. There."

The candle flickered and died. The chill of the flagstones seeped through the robe. Her slow, regular breathing was the loudest sound in the manor house. After a while, something like a current of air formed in her body. It flowed through her without stopping.

"See it?" Tally asked as Maureen's breath caught in her throat. "Not sure." Maureen answered honestly. "I think I imagined it." "I don't imagine things. Blood flow to the abdomen, up nine percent." The pale blue energy confirmed Tally's report, flowing behind Maureen's eyes. For the first time, this body did what she wanted it to do.

In the grey dawn, there was warmth low in her belly. Maureen rested a palm over the spot, testing it for heat like you would a stove.

* * *

* * *

Knocking on her door, Richard called her name. "Maureen? Father and Master Scarlette are waiting outside."

Behind him, as they walked through the corridor, Maureen asked for a scan. The three-dimensional model appeared again, rotating to show the incoming streams of energy in pale blue.

Strength 0.6

Agility 0.7

Constitution 0.6

Status: Sub-Healthy

One night of exercise increased her strength by a tenth. From a very low floor, Tally noted. Don't get vain.

I'm never vain, Maureen protested. I'm a scientist.

"Uh huh. You smiled."

The current of warmth continued to flow in her belly as she walked. The body was weak, but the method was strong.

"Come on," she said to her dawdling friend, "let us meet Master Scarlette."

* * *

The morning light shone at the manor gate, as a line of carriages waited in the cobbled yard before the gates. Harnesses creaked, horse sweat mixed with dung on the cobblestones, and beyond the gates crowds lined the street. Beyond the city walls, columns of coal smoke drifted lazily into the sky.

Scarlette stood before the Duke of Skran, taking his leave. Maureen bowed politely to the both of them, exchanging a few words with each.

While Scarlette stood there watching, the Duke gestured to two of the manor porters who picked up an iron-bound strongbox between them with a grunt of effort, and stowed it under the seat of Maureen's carriage.

"Some travel money, for Richard's companion upon the road," the Duke said with a warm and loud voice.

Maureen Olsen, you have just been given almost three thousand gold, Tally noted in her head. That is a large sum of money. I have created a Skran column in my ledger to keep track of it.

I know exactly what that money costs, Maureen replied silently.

Tally's smug note was the last word on the subject. Refusing the gift in front of Scarlette would be an insult to the Duke. So Maureen bowed again, hands still at her sides.

Before she could get in, the Duke held Richard back by the gate, speaking to him quietly. After his father's talk, Richard made his way to her carriage, his eyes red and sad from holding back tears. Without a word he climbed inside and sat beside her.

His eyelids are swollen, Tally observed. He is catching his breath before he speaks. There is a disturbance in his breathing when he exhales.

Leave him be for now, Maureen told it silently, and Tally fell quiet.

"What did the Duke say?" she asked. Tally had no data.

She didn't press Richard. Tally noted the question without any answer and stored it.

"Let us go," the Mystic called out. The convoy began to move. Duke Skran stood tall and proud and unmoving as he watched them pull away. The crowds outside the walls parted and pointed.

The carriage moved slowly at first, bouncing up and down as it crossed the cobblestones. A fine powdery dust came through the cloths covering the windows, mingling with the smell of horse sweat and dung. There were other smells too. Coal smoke, Tally noted. Bearing south-east. That would be the slum.

Tally fell quiet for a while after that, keeping silent as Maureen rode with her thoughts. Her throat felt tight as she thought about Grandpa's grave. Andrew and Mary. She said nothing aloud though. The moment was here. She was leaving Skran City, she was on the way to the wizard's road.

She lifted the lid off the box when they were well out onto the wide highway. The coin glittered in the morning light.

Yes, Tally noted, there are three thousand gold pieces in the strongbox.

Maureen hesitated for a moment, then put the lid back on the box. She took none of the money with her.

* * *

The carriages came to a halt. A tall man clad in black armour approached Maureen's window. "The master wishes you to join him now."

Maureen nodded. Twenty-four armoured knights were riding with Scarlette. All wore black armour, all of the same height and build, all sat on their horses with the same gait. They had been trained by the same school. Maureen had heard that these were Upper Knights at least.

As she approached the carriage of the wizard, she told Tally, Stop raising the alarm about this one. He's a known source now. Just keep your cold alert up if we get new sources or spikes.

"Energy source. Known source. No shouting about him. As agreed." Tally responded.

The inside of Scarlette's carriage was cramped and the once luxurious velvet seats were worn smooth and shiny. There was a smell of old man and ink in the air. Ellena sat with her back straight as ever, while Aaron sat with proud stillness.

Richard and Carey joined them a short time later. The former gardener murmured a quiet "my lady" as he seated himself, his knee bouncing nervously up and down as he looked around. Richard sniffed.

When they were all seated Scarlette turned to the three new wizard seeds. "Congratulations," he said, his voice mild. "It is surprising that such a backwater as little Skran should produce not one but three wizard seeds."

"Thank you, honoured lord," Maureen replied with a bow of her head. "We are honoured to be your disciples."

Scarlette shook his head. "No, Maureen, you are mistaken." Carey and Richard exchanged a quick glance at the interruption, then there was a beat of silence as Carey's knee stilled. "I am a member of the Blue Ring, child. Part of my duties include recruiting young wizard seeds for our ranks. Which wizard becomes your mentor is dependent on you."

Scarlette continued in the same mild voice. "Nor am I a wizard myself. I am only a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice."

Tally caught the slightest pause before 'Third-Class'. What's a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice? Maureen asked silently.

No data, Tally replied, equally silently, right into her mind.

So neither of them had any clue how low down the ranks this 'Third-Class' might be.

"Honoured lord?" Richard asked, still sniffing. "What is a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice?"

Scarlette smiled at him. "You will know, once you have studied some of the wizard's knowledge."

Maureen kept her hands folded demurely in her lap and her face polite as she rapidly re-evaluated all her options now she apparently didn't have a patron. Dust from the road blew past the window and outside she could hear the creaking of the black-clad riders' leather, oiled to keep it supple.

"Every morning," Scarlette said, turning back to the three of them, "you will join Aaron and Ellena in my carriage to learn Ancient Pati, Ancient Andas and Ancient Fusiform Script."

He smiled at them all, white hair and beard gleaming in the sunlight. "We have many more principalities to visit, and more young wizard seeds to recruit, before we make our way to Sunset Canyon for you to meet other Blue Ring apprentices, then on to the Blue Ring itself."

For a moment there was stillness within the carriage, then Maureen bowed her head along with Richard and Carey.

Dependent on her. Maureen thought silently. She considered Scarlette's words. Tally spoke in her head, its voice warm with a hint of smugness. Good. Then it depends on us.

Yes, it does, Maureen replied silently.

She sat back against the worn velvet of the seats. Cold and determined now. No patron. Well, then she would have to be better than any of them.
