Maureen returned to her workbench from Ambrose’s office. Without pausing she began work on the large order of standard potions the Fourteenth Potion Group was assigned. She let her hands work while what Ambrose had told her remained lodged firmly behind her breastbone. Adrian almost killed, Mace used as a vessel for Hader Castle, the Dark Forest’s base taken.

The Brewing State came easily. Maureen took a step back in her mind from her hands as the reduction began to thicken, listening to the glass ticking as it cooled. Beneath it all, the news sat in her chest like a stone she had swallowed.

Tally didn’t need to be asked to update on Adrian’s condition. *His brand on your wrist is still reading warm when I sense it.* She paused for a moment before adding, *Daniel’s mark beneath it is still cold though.*

*I know. Keep checking please.*

*I will, every morning and every time you look at it.*

Viska put a cup of his signature smoke and mint tea at her elbow.

“Group leader?” He rubbed the back of his head. “Are we really supposed to fill this entire order this month?”

“We’re getting through it.” Maureen told him how many batches they would be doing tomorrow, but said nothing about the East.

Around them the other pharmacists were corking flasks and packing them into crates. Someone laughed at the far bench and the sound seemed to come from a very long way away.

*We are ahead of schedule according to Ambrose’s sheet. We are also ahead of my forecast which I find annoying.*

*We will be further ahead tomorrow.*

At dusk she wished them goodnight, her Richard voice flat and unemotional, and Viska called “group leader” after her as she left the Alliance tower.

* * *

The gate responded to her palm brand with a faint warming sensation. It closed behind her, not like a door so much as a boundary between a world of breeze and one of still air. There was frost on the gravel drive, a thin layer that crunched under her boots. Lamplight spilled out the windows of 34 Connpa Street, lying in yellow squares on the gravel.

Ike was waiting for her on the stone steps.

“Master,” he said bowing, his eyes never rising above her collarbone. The cook and a groundsman were going about their business in the hall without fuss or bother.

“Mushroom soup tonight Master. Fresh mushrooms this morning. Veal and a fruit platter for dessert.” Ike told her the menu in his slow careful voice. “And the slaves you asked for have been purchased and await you in the stable yard if you wish to see them first?”

There were three of them standing in a line on the cold cobbles, dressed in new grey wool that was probably itching, their breaths smoking in the evening air. Behind them horses shuffled and stomped in their stalls and the stable yard smelled of straw and dung and the oil from the lanterns.

All three were from the same Emilian house that had provided Maureen’s first five slaves. A man with grey stubble on his cheeks stood at one end, his hands red from the cold and the dirt ground into his knuckles. In the middle was a woman with square hands made rough by years of pulling curry combs through horse hair. At the other end of the line was a lanky boy whose Adam’s apple bounced in his throat as he swallowed. Each of them had a binding array from the same house dormant inside of them, waiting for an owner to provide mana and speak the seal word that would activate it.

Tally read out the arrays one after another from Maureen’s particle sense.

[Secondary anchor: none. A check, not proof.]

[Secondary anchor: none. A check, not proof.]

[Secondary anchor: none. A check, not proof.]

*I know what a check is worth,* Maureen responded silently.

She stepped up to each of them in turn, placing two fingers against their brows and speaking the Ancient Andas word that would trigger the arrays. Each array sucked up mana from Maureen before it activated, and with each activation came a cry of pain as the array bound its victim’s soul. [Mana: 16 → 14.5]

The boy turned his cry of pain into a choked sob, his eyes squeezing tight and his hands gripping his own wrists. The woman clenched her own hands tight until the knuckles were white. Over all of this, Richard’s face was cool, calm and bland.

*A household is five,* Maureen told Tally silently. *A payroll is eight.*

*A ledger is eight,* Tally corrected. *And I am delighted by this turn of events. Household, Payroll, Ledger…* Tally repeated, filing all three under the column marked “home”.

“These answer to you, Ike,” she said aloud. “And the upstairs remains closed to all of them.”

“Yes, Master,” Ike said, leading the three away towards the stable loft.

The dining hall smelled of soup, of wet earth and butter. The broth was thick and creamy, swirling with mushrooms that had been picked fresh from the garden only an hour before. The veal was cooked pink in the centre, and laid beside it were fans cut from winter pears. As she ate, the scent of beeswax filled the air, burning cleanly in the wall sconces. In every other house along Connpa Street, the sconces burned tallow.

Halfway through her soup, her left hand drifted to the inner pocket of her robe. It was an automatic reflex, developed over years of having the Blood Seal of Thorns ride against her ribs. Her fingers found only cloth. The Seal was now the core of the puppet down in the cellar chamber.

*Fourth time today,* Tally commented.

*Habit,* Maureen responded silently.

*Then I shall log it as such. Once you stop reaching for it, I shall stop counting.*

Maureen finished her veal without really tasting it. Ambrose’s news still sat where it had sat all afternoon.

She climbed the stairs to her bedroom and shut the door on her household.

* * *

The ward hummed quietly as it sealed itself. Maureen let Mimicry go. Richard’s face slid off of hers like a wet rag. The light from the single beeswax lamp showed her pale skin and cropped black hair reflected in the darkened window’s glass.

Maureen pulled the blood red Bloodcoat token out from its pouch and turned it over on the stone table. Last night came back whole in her memory. The attic, the Black Bear cut in half, Gus boasting at the foot of the oak.

The token smelled slightly of copper. The cold of the stone leeched into her fingers but the token felt warm with her particle sense. Like a coal buried under ash.

Then it flared a dull blood red. Red particles rose from the surface of the token and wrote a line in the cold air.

Nightingale requests meeting this evening at a shuttered villa on the quiet edge of the city.

The letters remained in the air for a moment, suspended, before they unravelled like thread pulled from the hem of a garment.

Tally was not idle while Maureen read the message. In an instant, the two dead tokens were checked. A pale blue line of particles appeared above the fading red.

[Gus token: dark. Black Bear token: dark. Pulse: yours only.]

*The Tower?* Maureen asked silently.

*Medium confidence that this is a legitimate guild seat. Low confidence that it is a friendly seat. After last time, we should assume it to be just another job board.*

Maureen’s jaw had already set before she’d made any conscious decision about the situation. Last night, a posted job had been the bait. Her own desire was written upon it. Her pulse sat higher than she liked.

*We go.*

*Why?*

*Because I say so.*

*Well… yes, but why?*

*I need to know if I have friends or foes inside the guild seats. It isn’t enough to worry about the outside. We have to worry about the inside too. We need to know what they want with us.*

*Are you sure?*

*Yes.*

*Fine. If we must. But under my conditions.*

*What?*

*Night Owl’s rules. You count out an exit route before you ever knock on the door. You test the gate wards before you touch the gate. They are still running Emilia under Wenger’s seal. Searching for us. Night Owl walking around at night is exactly what they would be looking for.*

*Then we will be careful.*

*I’ll hold you to that.*

Maureen prepared herself for the excursion. She wore the plain black wool robe. The full-face lacquer mask retrieved from Maffie’s vault helped hide her features. Freya’s slender sword was sent into the robe’s pouch. Finally, Mimicry was cast to replicate Night Owl’s broad face, thick jaw, and perpetually dragged-down mouth. [Mana: 14.5 → 14.1.]

Tally double checked to make sure there wasn’t a hint of crimson peering out through the eyeholes. [Eyes: black.] Then she had Maureen speak a single word just to test the voice.

*Speak lower. Cartwheel, don’t rasp. Not Jack.*

The voice ground in her throat like iron rims rolling over gravel. The left side of her new face settled down a heartbeat late, just like Jack’s always had.

Maureen left through the garden wall instead of the gate. Tally started counting the turns that pointed towards the edge of the city.

* * *

The wards on the villa’s gate were old and slack, without an alarm thread woven through them. As Tally ran through the weaves via Maureen’s particle sense, she had the exit route in mind before Maureen knocked.

*Back window to kitchen yard. Two low walls. Nineteen turns back to the house.*

The inside of the villa smelled like long dead fires and mice. Dust sheets covered all the furniture, turning chairs and settees into pale hills. There was only one source of light— a tallow candle resting on the stone table.

Maureen pushed the door open. Nightingale rose from the stone table with a bit too much haste.

“Owl. You came.”

“You called.” Maureen said in her grinding voice. “Talk.”

Tally immediately began to take stock of the room, running through it by density and breathing, then presenting her findings.

[Sources: 1. No others within range.]

Nightingale was alone. At least, as far as Tally could sense.

Nightingale looked away for a moment, her mask turning so that the candlelight was blocked from the lenses. Her gloved fingers worried at each other, and the candle’s flame leaned towards the cracked shutter.

The edge of Maureen’s vision flickered with a line.

[Nightingale: breathing quickened. Not proof.]

Her breathing had increased, a sign of anxiety. The way one might breathe if asking a favor they couldn’t afford to have refused.

Nightingale spoke first of the dead. “Black Bear’s team. Every one of them. Except your seat.”

Maureen nodded.

“The night you were attacked, I pulsed the guild’s roll through their tokens. Owl, only your token answered, besides mine.”

Nightingale did not ask how Owl had survived. Tally noted this lack of inquiry. It mirrored Bevis’s approach. He’d never asked for a name or a face, either. Nightingale knew Owl had survived. Nothing more.

“Now,” she said, “I want to trade.”

Nightingale opened her hand and a translucent membrane swelled up from her palm. A collection of glowing books drifted and turned in its white depths.

“You’ve been hunting enchantment and necromancy. These are yours if you help me.” The books floated beneath the membrane like fish swimming under the ice. Their cold light played across the dust sheets, glinting off the lacquer of Maureen’s mask.

Tally said, *Nightingale has read your commissions on the Bloodcoat Assassins’ Guild board. Every seat in the guild can do so.*

*Then she knows what I want.* Maureen thought back silently.

*She also knows what you’ll pay for them,* Tally replied. *Try not to look so hungry.*

*I’m not hungry.*

*Your pulse is disagreeing with you, Maureen. Ninety-one beats per minute.*

“And for them?”

Nightingale drew a deep, long breath before she spoke. “Soon I go back to my family. I want you to come with me, and keep me alive there for a while.”

Family.

Maureen’s files held records on the Olivias and the Walkers. The Olivias had kept receipts on her for years. The Walkers had a hundred and fifty years of service. Under the mask Maureen’s mouth was a straight line.

She did not answer immediately. Instead, she held up two fingers in a crooked “come hither” gesture. The books slid out from their translucent prison and into her hands. She opened the first one, peering at the pages by candlelight.

Nightingale waited patiently, unperturbed by the delay. Maureen was not surprised by this reaction. Tally recorded only what Maureen could see of the books’ spreads. The portions that remained sealed appeared as dark blanks on Maureen’s display. [Gaps covered: 4 of 7. Sealed: 3. Animation core: partial.] Tally assessed the books at medium confidence. There was enough here to build the puppet in stages, but not enough to do so in a single pass.

The sealed pages were colder than the rest beneath Maureen’s thumb. Their ink evaded her eyes, slipping away like oil on water.

When Maureen closed the final book, Nightingale spoke. “Those are the best and most complete texts my access allows me to provide. There are very few others I could even ask.”

“How long?” Maureen asked. “And against whom?”

“A season at most,” said Nightingale. “In my own house. The danger is inside my family, not from the Tower. I will give you their name when we begin.” Maureen considered the job. Difficult, certainly, but not foolish.

“Done,” she said.

Nightingale drew a breath to respond, but Maureen wasn’t finished. “And advanced alchemy on top. Those staged builds will cost me months.”

Nightingale was quiet for three heartbeats, then she said, “Agreed.”

Nightingale’s magically flattened voice cracked a little as she spoke. “This is a private job. Thank you, Night Owl.” Her tone grew more serious. “The underground is a wolf in every alley now. The rest of the guild has gone to ground. They’re taking no work at all.”

A breeze fluttered through the shutter. The candle flame guttered and both their shadows danced across the dusty sheets.

“Let’s swear it on the Styx,” said Nightingale.

*The Styx?* Tally said.

Nightingale’s voice took on an oratorical tone, as though reciting memorized text. “The Styx is the river that carries every life from birth to death. Those with strength can feel the water on their skin, hear its current in their ears. A contract sworn upon the Styx cannot be broken. Ordinary contracts are pliable things—they stretch and bend as we ourselves grow and change. A Styx Pact is forever. The River demands its due.”

Tally logged the information, attributing it to Nightingale. [One source.] Beside it, Tally set Adrian’s contract, the one that had compelled him to teach Maureen the false path for so long.

*Ordinary contracts have ways around them,* Tally said to Maureen silently. *This one claims it has none. You cannot test that claim. I don’t want you to be the test for it.*

*I agree,* said Maureen, also silently. There were no words of argument, no internal debate. It had been a very long time since that had happened.

Maureen shook her head. “No.” Her mouth turned up just a fraction beneath her mask. It was the closest thing to a smile that she could manage. “I’ve no wish to carry the Styx on my back.”

“Which wizard does?” said Nightingale. “Even the white robes turn grey at the word.”

“An ordinary contract, then.”

Nightingale nodded. She made a grasping motion with her hand, as though clutching at empty air. A scroll formed in her fingers, its parchment a deep purple that flickered with red flames. She recited the final terms of the contract, and a brand of light burned from her forehead and sank into the parchment.

The flames gave off heat but not warmth. The lacquer mask reflected their light, appearing as two small, red points. Maureen kept those points far away from her own eyes.

When Nightingale finished, Maureen read each line of the contract. Then she nodded, and her own brand of light rose from her brow, sinking into the parchment. The contract burned in the air, shrinking until it was gone. A new sensation settled at Maureen’s throat—thin and cold. It rested beside the other three collars she already wore.

[Bindings: 3 → 4]

*Another collar?* said Maureen.

*Technically, yes,* said Tally. *This one is thin, and will become thinner as you grow. At least, according to Nightingale. And, according to me, everything about you is growing.*

“I’ll send word when it starts,” said Nightingale, pushing open the shutter. She slipped out the window and disappeared over the rooftops.

The shutter swung in the breeze, knocking once against the wall. Then the candle flame went out, and the room was dust and darkness and the smell of dead, cold ash.

* * *

Maureen left by the route that Tally counted for her: through the kitchen yard, up and over two short walls, and then out into the streets again. They were busier now, closer to the city’s centre.

Lamplight gleamed in broken patches on the frosted cobblestones. Late crowds of people still wandered, their furs and woollen cloaks wrapped tightly against the chill air. Somewhere ahead of her, in the direction of the gambling quarter, dice rattled and a man shouted in excitement.

Maureen slipped her mask and robe back into the pouch as she entered an alley’s dark mouth. She then cast her Mimicry spell, transforming back to Richard’s weak jaw and flat voice. [Mana: 14.1 → 13.7.]

*Tally, check my eyes, please.*

*Black,* confirmed Tally. *Try saying a word.*

Maureen murmured a word under her breath.

*Flat,* said Tally. *It sounds flat. You’re Richard again. Welcome back to nobody.*

Tally kept count of robed patrols standing at the intersections ahead, and painted a pale blue line down the street for Maureen to follow. The line kept her two streets away from each patrol— three patrols in total, their white tower emblems stitched on their sleeves. Wenger’s searchers.

One patrol crossed the end of her street anyway. Their density brushed her senses, like a bucket of cold water splashed over her body. She maintained her tired clerk’s gait, a man simply walking home late at night, and the patrol didn’t turn to look at her.

She raised her hand to call over a horn-scale carriage that waited idling at a street corner. “34 Connpa Street,” she told the driver, a mortal man with a hunched posture and fleece collar wrapped around his face. He wasn’t curious enough to look back at her as he snapped the reins and the carriage began to roll.

The horses’ breath steamed in the chill night air, smelling faintly of hot iron. Their scales clacked against each other as they moved in the cold, and the carriage’s lacquer seat was chill through her robe. For the first time in the day, she felt her shoulders relax, dropping away from her ears.

The wheels rolled as she reviewed it all. The Necro Flower rested in its black stone box, the Dark Element Energy Stone was hers, and she had the Purple Wood Snake crystal. Enchantment and necromancy were in hand. Alchemy was next.

Now she could think about rank again.

Tally laid out the next rung for her. [Void Eclipse, layer 2: 3 of 3 in hand. Tears: method no data.]

*Spirit 16 against the Ancient road’s line of 18 for Phantom State.* *Layer two should get you there,* Tally said. *Medium confidence.*

*Then it’s the archive,* Maureen thought silently.

*Archive costs points. We can’t afford it.*

*Then we’ll have to spend some. This is important.*

*I love it when you say we when talking about money.*

With the Necro Flower, Void Eclipse would reach its second layer, and then she should meet the standard for a Phantom-State Wizard. Maureen leaned back against the cold lacquer carriage seat. She was looking forward to it, she realized. The carriage turned into Emilia’s wizards’ tract. Her gate answered her palm with its faint warmth, and then the ward closed behind her with its quiet hum.
