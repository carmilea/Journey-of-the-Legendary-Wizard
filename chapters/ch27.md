Tally had organised things differently when it had put the method into Maureen's memory and it was laid out far more clearly than the mass of crammed data she had received from the crystal ball. The method was fixed in her memory, as though branded there, not subject to the vagaries of recall.

Well what do you expect when you let me tidy up first? Tally said smugly.

Fair point. Maureen responded silently and it sounded smugly pleased with itself.

Maureen sat on the bed, cross-legged, facing the open door of the washroom, and began meditating on Spirit Runes using the new method. Her breathing slowed until the rise and fall of her chest was a long, almost imperceptible movement. The stone walls breathed cold through the blanket that covered her legs and the window shifted from black to grey. In the small mirror of the washroom, motes of purple light flickered on her forehead, while her black eyes watched them come and go.

She came out of her meditation feeling clear-headed and full of energy. She stretched and told Tally to analyse the state she had just been in.

Tally displayed its results for her:

[Brainwaves: abnormal. Energy radiation received: abnormal. Meditation speed: +]

Tally also displayed two side-by-side pale blue curves, one representing her brainwave activity before the use of the new method, the other after. The second curve climbed far more steeply than the first.

Will it always be this fast? Maureen thought at Tally.

No data, Tally replied in its warm voice, but with a trace of ruefulness. One sample only. If you did it again tomorrow, then I would have two samples.

Maureen mentally turned over the information in the same way she would examine the results of an experiment in the lab. The new method appeared to drive not just the promotion, but also the rune meditation itself.

A cold chime interrupted her thoughts and a line of yellow alarm text appeared across her display:

[Adrian. This morning.]

I need another five minutes, she pleaded silently.

No, Tally responded, smugly. In your entire life, there was never any time when you took an extra five minutes.

She sighed and got up from the bed, once more washing in the cold water of the washroom. The black stone walls were sweating chilly moisture. Tally overlaid her sight with the map that Winnie had shown her during the tour, a pale blue line stretching from her dormitory room in the Dark Forest to the dining hall, and then on to the lab.

The dining hall was filled with long tables and the smell of roasting animal fats and burning tallow. Black robed students sat in groups at the tables eating and talking in low voices. Maureen ate roast goose liver and white bread. The bread stuck in her throat as she remembered Carey, lying dead on the ship. She tried to swallow the lump in her throat, but it was impossible. Tally remained silent about Carey, just recording the food as an entry in its food database, a faint pale blue flicker behind her eyes. Maureen forced herself to eat every last crumb.

* * *

"Mentor." Maureen bowed slightly to Adrian as she entered his laboratory.

Winnie was there, and two other people she didn’t know.

The sun shone in through one window high on the wall. It shone on rows of glassware racks, lighting them up from above. A flask bubbled gently on top of a burner which gave out a regular ticking noise. The air was filled with the bitter taste of boiled herbs mixed with black stone dust. A young man with curly golden hair was speaking quietly to Adrian using the strange terminology of reagents. A slender girl with pale skin stood listening intently, occasionally glancing sideways at Maureen.

“This is Mace.” Adrian smiled and stopped talking to Mace. “And this is Lilith.” The young man smiled at her, his eyes warm and gentle. His voice was quiet and gentle too. “Hello.” Lilith said nothing, just looked her up and down.

>Mace

>Age: 17-18

>Hair: Curly golden

>Eyes: Warm

>[Energy radiation source]

Tally displayed.

Lilith is measuring you, Tally's voice said in Maureen's head.

I'm measuring her too, Maureen thought back silently.

Adrian regarded her with an amused smile. “Did reading the method make you unwell?”

Maureen decided to tell him the truth. “It felt like my head was swollen, foggy and heavy.”

“That is perfectly normal,” Adrian said.

Honesty costs nothing here, and buys trust for what comes next, Tally said in her head.

I know, Maureen thought back.

“I suggest you attend the free public courses available in the Teaching District,” Adrian said. “Once you are First-Class I will take you under my tutelage for one of the paid advanced subjects.”

“Mentor?” Maureen said quietly. “I already know a lot of the basics. I’m close to First-Class Wizard Apprentice.”

“Oh?” Adrian said with a surprised note in his voice.

Maureen told him about the journey, Evans had brought their batch of seeds, and Scarlette had taught them on the way, leaving out Derrick, the Seal and the scroll. As she talked Tally kept a running list in Maureen’s sight.

[Derrick: withheld.]

[Seal: withheld.]

[Pharmacology: withheld.]

So if she needed to repeat any part of her story it would be consistent. Her voice remained even, her palms stayed dry and her hands were folded on top of her robe where the Seal lay hidden.

Tally noted that as Maureen spoke of Evans, [Adrian: frown, eyes drop.] and then provided the information that he did know of Evans and what he knew he did not like.

“Evans...” Adrian said, “Maureen your journey here can’t have been a peaceful one.”

Maureen just smiled wryly.

Adrian still decided to send her to the Teaching District, whatever this Blue Ring apprentice had taught her may not have been complete. She agreed that was a sensible plan.

Adrian turned back to Mace and they began discussing potions at length, Winnie adding a line here and there. Maureen understood none of it but Tally was making a note of every word promising to sort it all out later.

[Potions, Mace: Reagents, boiling points.]

Lilith is not understanding anything either. You have an ally here, Tally said.

Mace’s gentle quiet voice continued, talking about reagents, boiling points, and other things Maureen didn’t understand. She caught Lilith’s eye and they exchanged rueful looks.

* * *

They bade farewell to Adrian together, and in the cold black stone corridor outside his laboratory, Lilith's smile turned brilliant as she held out a hand.

"Hello Maureen, I'm Lilith."

"Hi." Maureen responded cautiously "You're new as well?"

"Oh yes. Pharmacology is very difficult, Senior Mace is a real alchemy genius, you know. Mentor Adrian thinks he will be his heir." She giggled, then continued. "The other potion-master mentors think he is a genius too."

Alchemy again? Tally noted, Hegel had called it lost, this was twice now. [Adrian: Unknown, Alchemy] Tally filed that with Hegel's pregnant pause in its memory.

"Oh." Was all Maureen could add to that, she rubbed her nose. The air in the corridor was cold, the torch in its iron bracket was smoking slightly, and there was the sound of a distant door banging shut far below them. The closed door to Adrian's laboratory was still giving off a faint herb like smell.

Lilith's eyes brightened for a moment "Say, are you doing anything soon? There is going to be a gathering, it should be most interesting."

"Sure." Maureen agreed.

"Great! I will let you know when." Lilith said her robe whispering slightly as she walked away down the corridor. Maureen watched her go with an amused half smile.

Are we keeping a social calendar now? Tally asked as Lilith disappeared around a corner.

Yes, make a note of it as an errand. Maureen said silently.

[Errands: Gathering. Lilith. Social.] Tally protested at the classification before filing the information away.

* * *

Tally organised all the lab discussions into its Pharmacology category. Then, unprompted, it gave her an update. Thirty-seven new entries. It said happily, pleased with itself for discovering something new. Most of them still have no data on what they mean though.

You sound like a magpie with a full nest. Maureen told it silently.

A magpie does not keep its nest so orderly and empty of detritus. Tally said.

Maureen considered the new knowledge while Tally continued processing information. It wasn't enough to just meditate the runes, she needed to get to First-Class soon. For one thing, it was obvious that there was some serious advanced knowledge that she needed to learn, but more pressingly, her own safety depended on it.

Every wizard and apprentice was a source of energy pollution, their bodies were constantly radiating energy. That radiation was harmful to wizard seeds until the body's first conversion by wizard energy at First-Class. Tally provided what information it had on the topic.

[Host: mild energy radiation, rising since arrival.]

Today then. Maureen responded silently.

With her jaw set in determination, Maureen sat down cross legged on her bed, positioning herself so that she was facing the mirror in the washroom. The dormitory room was perfectly still and silent, the only sound being her own faint breathing. The cold black stone walls absorbed every other noise. Tally displayed [Spirit Runes: 11/12] at the very edge of her sight, and in her head she could hear Tally's own voice whispering a count as she breathed. One... Two... Three....

In the mirror in front of her, points of light began to form on her forehead. They grew brighter and brighter, before they began tracing an eerie new rune. The twelfth.

More runes sprang from her forehead in the mirror, dancing around her head in a circle, each one glowing with a faint light.

Converging, they formed the strange glowing Rune Array on her forehead. It shone for a second, before the glow faded and it sank back into her skin.

Tally flashed [12/12 → Rune Array.] and then, in a very formal voice, said Congratulations, First-Class Wizard Apprentice Olsen.

Maureen hadn't expected to complete it so soon. The lessons Scarlette had taught her on the road, combined with the life or death pressure on the ship, had built the rest of the required knowledge. She felt warmth radiating through her bones, her skin prickling as her body adjusted to the changes.

Suddenly she became aware of the energy particles in the room. They were everywhere, drifting and surging around the still air, flowing around and through her bed, brushing past the window.

I can see what you feel! Tally exclaimed. New channel!

Just like that, Tally opened up a particle log where Maureen could see it, and began counting the particles.

Exhilaration overwhelmed Maureen, closing her throat as her heart raced. Alone in the still room, she laughed aloud at the miracle she had just achieved, and grinned at Tally once before she turned back to the particle display, marvelling at the world suddenly laid bare before her.

* * *

Adrian was sitting alone at his bench, his glasses pushed up on his forehead as he rubbed his eyes. A thick cloud of the particles she could now sense hung around him. The old wizard looked exhausted.

“Is there anything else you need?” he asked wearily.

Then Adrian froze, looking up at her. “This energy, this aura…”

[Adrian: dark circles, slow speech, unknown cause.]

I have no data on the cause of his fatigue, Tally said.

“Mentor,” she said quietly. “I have become a First-Class Wizard Apprentice.”

Adrian paused for a moment, staring into her eyes with his own keen gaze. “Yes, I can see that,” he said, pulling down his glasses and adjusting them. “Congratulations.”

Congratulations! Me too! Tally exclaimed.

Thank you, Maureen thought at it, then bowed her head to Adrian, her expression composed and calm. The particles stirred around her as she straightened.
