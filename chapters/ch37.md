The smell of roast fat from a dozen different principalities' dishes hung in the air. It mixed with the smoke from the tallow candles and filled Maureen's nostrils with an interesting blend of aromas. The black-robes were scattered through the Dark Forest restaurant, sitting at long tables and eating with their heads bent low. Their voices were kept even lower. A stemmed glass of dark wine was placed beside her hand.

Anthony's face brightened. "Ah, I wasn't expecting a yes so soon. You've made quite the name for yourself as an apothecary, I heard your potion sales are very steady."

"Mhm." Maureen laced her fingers together. "What sort of missions is the team looking at?"

"Suitable ones are hard to come by right now." Anthony looked around, then leaned forward. "Rumour has it that the Blue Ring and Hader Castle, the white-wizard orders, are planning a bit of revenge. The underground races have been going out raiding as well. Hell Fiends, Bloodthirsty Spider Fiends, dark elves." When he mentioned the Blue Ring, Maureen's stomach tightened. She kept both her hands flat on the table, one on either side of the glass.

Hearsay. Tally filed the Blue Ring under Eric, Ciri and Scarlette's oath over the Seal.

Scarlette isn't likely to come looking for the Seal inside a snake's belly. Maureen replied silently to the chip.

No data on where Scarlette is searching. Tally responded.

"Several outposts have been hit," Anthony continued in a whisper. "It wasn't just the apprentices this time. Everyone was there, knights included. All dead."

Maureen tapped the tabletop slowly. First one finger, then another, and another, as the bad news stacked up.

"Apprentices have also been vanishing. Traces point to some sort of giant snake swallowing them whole. Our team is being assembled to find and kill this snake. It's been sighted in a mountain range near the Dark Forest. It doesn't sound too dangerous, but we could use your help."

Anthony's thumb was rubbing the gold thread of his white shirt cuff. Trying to persuade her or not being completely honest? Tally couldn't tell from its file on Anthony. Both possibilities were listed with high probabilities.

Keep both options open. Maureen told the chip silently.

Yes, I will. Tally promised.

"It's an Academy issued mission." Anthony said. "Seven magic stones plus academic knowledge in several subjects. Magic stones are always better when you have more of them."

There was a long pause before Maureen smiled. "Fine, Anthony. Let me know when and where we meet."

"The Trading District Hall. Two days."

Anthony stood up and left. The mission would go well with her addition. Maureen raised her glass in a mock toast to him, then put it back down untouched. She'd never fought using wizardry before. Not once. She paid and left.

* * *

At the long stone bench, Adrian nodded without objection to her request for a few days' leave.

"You are a Second-Class Apprentice now, Olsen." He reminded her. "Going out on a mission will do you good. Just be careful."

Tally told her that Adrian looked at her for a moment longer than usual. It didn't have any data on why he did that.

Does he like me? Is he happy to have me as his apprentice?

That is also unconfirmed. Tally responded.

"Yes, Mentor." Maureen bowed her head. Behind her mentor, the burner ticked, and the test tube burned with a heatless black flame.

* * *

In the trading district, Maureen purchased a longbow made of laminated horn. She also picked up a quiver full of arrows. The bow was heavier than her old hunting bow had been. The chip loaded up its archery record from the road and the camp fight. In that fight, she had hit eight out of ten shots.

Every miss taught me more than any hit. Tally commented. I hope we don't need to learn those lessons on this trip.

[Arrows: 24] Tally displayed as it automatically deducted the price from the stone ledger. The new bow smelled vaguely of hide glue, and when she pulled it all the way back the leather tab bit into the tips of her fingers.

Maureen spent both days in the testing room. She cast her spells again and again, while running Basic Swordsmanship against targets formed of flowing black light.

Tally drilled her casting state over and over. The chip timed every spell chant until the range of times shrank down and held steady.

When Tally announced the drill was over, Maureen silently asked for one more round.

Tally gave her three.

The still air in the room let the sweat on Maureen's neck cool down. She shot arrow after arrow into the flowing black light targets. When Tally's marks clustered, she paused to steady her mind for the road ahead.

* * *

"Where is this Maureen? We've been waiting for ages!" A flaxen haired youth dressed in fine silks complained.

"Aye, Young Master Jay is here already." A burly yellow bearded youth growled.

Anthony stood amidst several other youths. He smiled wryly. "The agreed hour hasn't come yet, Young Master Jay, Wolf."

Then Anthony caught sight of Maureen and waved. "Maureen! Over here!"

Maureen made her way over to where four young men were standing. The flaxen haired youth's silk robe wafted a hint of rose water over the smoky scent of the lamps. The yellow bearded man looked her up and down.

"This is Sim, my follower." Said Anthony. The curly haired youth nodded without saying anything.

"And this is Jay, direct-line heir of the Olivia Family, grade 7, high aptitude." Anthony gestured towards the flaxen haired youth, who nodded arrogantly.

"This is Wolf, Jay's follower. A strong Second-Class Apprentice and Upper Knight." Concluded Anthony as he gestured towards the bearded man.

Upper Knight, close to hers, Tally commented.

Jay, direct-line heir of the Olivia Family, grade 7, high aptitude. Hearsay, family name attached. No apprentice rank given. Tally filed away.

Wolf had just opened his mouth to complain about the girl when Anthony interrupted him. "Everyone, this is Maureen. An apothecary." Wolf shut his mouth at that. Jay only snorted coldly when he saw her.

An apothecary is worth more than your face to him. Tally said.

Good. Maureen said silently.

Maureen stood calm but bristling, and considered what the name Olivia meant. One of the seven powerful wizard families of the Dark Forest. A full wizard family behind Jay. Anthony was playing a game here, bringing an apothecary and an Olivia heir together on his errand to raise his own standing. She gave Anthony a knowing smile that made him shift uncomfortably. That was enough though.

"Maureen?" A familiar voice called out.

Aaron walked up to her. He looked thinner and more haggard than before, but there was still a faint smile on his lips. It took effort for him to smile, she could see that. Still, it was good to see him again.

"Hello Aaron." Maureen said as they shook hands.

"It's been a long time." Said Aaron, his voice sounding like dry leaves in the wind.

"Yes, it has." Replied Maureen, his palm felt dry and light as paper against hers. Her throat ached slightly as she let go of his hand.

Dry voice, effortful smile, thumb at sleeve hem. Tally reported, saying nothing further. She had asked Tally to look and it hadn't found anything more that it could name.

"I wanted Richard and Lucy to come too, but unfortunately, Lucy and Alice were helping their mentor with an experiment." Anthony explained. "Richard was worried about Lucy so he went to help too. Only Aaron was free."

With all of them here, the six set off towards the exit.

* * *

The black flagstones underfoot were dotted by beads of moisture from the damp air. The chill of the gate seemed to roll out of the stone and into their faces. The two statues came to life, crimson eyes flashing into existence.

"What is the password?" Demanded the lizard headed statue, its voice a deep rumble. "Speak quickly, or the great Lord Hugh will eat you in one bite!" It lifted its huge steel trident and flicked its long slobbering tongue out, its scarlet tongue almost reaching the apprentices.

Hugh-or-Iman tag closed. The lizard head was Hugh, so the silent one was Iman. Solved! Tally said, its voice bright and satisfied.

Two years for one name? Maureen replied silently.

Worth it. Tally answered her.

Spit from Hugh's tongue hissed where it hit the cold wet flagstones. The sharp steel tines of its trident hovered a body's length above Maureen's head. The six apprentices bowed their heads, but without fear. These weren't normal statues or golems, they were life forms created with wizardry, as powerful as any full wizard. They had been made to guard the Dark Forest and would kill anyone who tried to enter without permission.

"Honoured Lord Hugh," Anthony called out. "The password is: Endless darkness; the dark falls from the endless point and shrouds the whole world!"

Password recorded, word for word. Tally announced, placing the sentence on a line on its display.

Keep that one safe. Maureen told Tally silently.

Already have. It assured her.

The two statues glanced at each other in a look of helplessness, before the lizard headed statue turned back to face the apprentices. "That is the correct password." It announced, before it and its partner turned around to face each other, ignoring the apprentices as though they were no longer there. "T-told you they were too clever." The lizard headed statue muttered. "Haven't eaten an apprentice in ages. N-next time we don't ask. They come back, we p-pretend they never said it, and eat them in one bite." Its partner Iman didn't reply, just cackled. Wakaka.

The back of Maureen's neck prickled, and the six apprentices started moving faster as they passed under the archway and out the other side.

The air outside the Dark Forest was different. A weak sun shone through a thin layer of cloud overhead, and Maureen raised a hand to shield her eyes as she tilted her head back. For a second she left her face upturned, feeling the warmth on her skin. She could almost feel Tally smiling as it displayed [Days since landing: ~680] and told her that her pupils were shrinking, while informing her it had no baseline for how warm her skin should be in sunlight.

Log it as sun please Tally.

Logged.

Maureen blinked as her eyes began watering from the light, and breathed deeply. The air smelled different too. Instead of incense over rot and damp the air carried the scent of grass and the wind that blew over an open land. The horse under her was different too, the flank of the black animal warm beneath her calves.

Maureen turned in the saddle and glanced back once at the black stone walls of the castle she'd spent nearly two years in. As she did so she let out a long breath.

Up ahead the land stretched out before them, and Maureen took the folded mission map from her saddlebag and unfolded it. Each member of the team had one, they were expensive and the information they contained valuable. All the routes and paths that had been discovered were clearly marked on the map and Tally quickly captured each marking Maureen's eyes crossed as she examined the map. Her friend then overlaid the route on the land ahead on its display.

The disappearances were southeast of the Dark Forest, 586 km away. Maureen frowned, tracing the route with her finger, turning the mission over in her head.

586 km... That's what? 9 horse days?

At the pace we're travelling for this mission? About a week.

A week then. Maureen said silently, and Tally began counting down.
