The windows were all dark again, and the frost that had been growing on the garden wall since the sun set was now as thick as felt. Inside, the hall smelled like banked coals and Ike’s beeswax furniture polish. Nobody moved, because Ike and everyone else in the household had long since gone to their beds.

Maureen crossed the hall. *The house is clear,* Tally reported in her head. *Eight sleeping lights. Stable boy curled up near the horses. Nothing new touching the wards.*

*Thanks,* Maureen responded silently. *Don’t bother counting the cat.*

*Already counted. On the ledger,* Tally said. *You can’t blame me for being thorough.*

Maureen let herself into her bedroom, shutting the door behind her. She stood still for a moment, feeling the ward close over the door like a lid on a jar. Then she let go the Mimicry. Richard’s weak jaw slid away beneath her fingers, and the black glass in the window returned her image, the short black hair cropped close around her ears, and the pale skin almost translucent in the night light.

She sat down on the edge of the bed, still wearing her boots, and asked the question silently. Tally projected the readout in front of Maureen’s eyes. [Maureen Olsen. First-Tier Wizard (Ancient Wizard). Strength 14.1, Agility 13.8, Constitution 14.0. Spirit 16.1. Mana 16.1.] [Tally. Anchors: Minai Totem, Darklight Orb. Status: settled.]

Tally highlighted the two lines that had moved since the rite. Constitution was now up by a tenth, at 14.0, and Spirit up by the same amount, to 16.1.

*Point one,* Maureen thought silently.

*It’s progress!* Tally said.

*Rounding error.*

*My rounding error. I’m very proud of it.*

Weeks of Void Eclipse, every night, had bought Maureen that tenth of a point. Her fingers drummed out the Silver Light count on her knee. Four beats, pause, four more. Her face was perfectly still, because it was not her face that was impatient. That lived in her hands.

At this pace, it would take years to reach the eighteen that the Ancient road demanded for Phantom State. The only way to shortcut it was with Tally’s plan for Void Eclipse’s second layer, and she already had all three materials. She just couldn’t open the seal on the snake’s crystal.

The gap lay out before her. The Dark Element Energy Stone in hand. The Necro Flower in hand. The Purple Wood Snake tears locked inside their wizard-made crystal. The harvesting method, no data.

*The Alliance archive is the most likely place to find it,* Tally said. *It will cost contribution points. A lot of them.*

Maureen pulled off her boots and folded her legs on the bed. She closed her eyes and meditated anyway, until the window began to turn grey with the dawn. Meditation was the foundation of everything else, and a tenth was still a tenth.

Tally kept watch on the wards. She counted Maureen’s breaths in a whisper all night long.

* * *

As Richard, Maureen drove the Fourteenth Group through Ambrose’s large expedition order ahead of schedule. Benches full from first bell to last, leaving nothing but a completed job behind.

Hot glass, mint and ash filled the air. The racked flasks clicked together as they were moved around the ash benches. Pine crates stamped with their destination in the East rose towards the beeswax lamps next to the door. Viska’s cup of smoke-and-mint tea left a trail of steam wherever he went, like a little ghost following him around the room.

“You will be acting group leader while I’m on leave,” Maureen told him on the fourth evening.

Viska’s hand tightened around his cup, slamming it down on his workbench before he absently rubbed the back of his head. “Leave? Now? With half the department keeping an eye on the White Tower?”

“Now,” said Maureen.

Tally assessed the hand at his neck, and his quick, steamy breaths. Concern for Richard, not suspicion. Medium confidence.

*Fine. Log it. We’ll check later.* Maureen thought silently.

She left the room. It tugged at her, the desire to stay and see the work she had started through to completion. There used to be times when she would leave Jing Sheng’s white bench with a dirty beaker sitting unattended, and the thing would bother her all evening. She double-checked the seal on the final crate with her own thumb before she let it go.

In his camphor-scented office, Ambrose listened to her request, pen poised in mid-air. “You delivered early so you could take leave?” he asked when she finished.

“Yes.”

He raised an eyebrow. “That is a new way of asking for time off.”

Maureen waited as he approved the leave, then with two fingers he touched a sheet of conjured particles to credit the group their delivery bonus.

Tally flagged the timing. Wenger’s people were scouring Emilia right now, and Richard was about to disappear. Unlikely anyone would connect the two events, low confidence, but someone was bound to notice the shape of it.

*Let them notice a pharmacist going on leave.* Maureen thought silently.

*Logged. On leave. Disputed,* Tally replied.

Tally ran the numbers as the credit hit the account.

[Contribution: ~60 + leader stipend ~160 + order bonus ~300 = ~520.]

*I’ve never seen it that fat before. Give me a moment to admire it,* Tally said.

The archive ran away from the door, spreading in lamplit aisles. Shelf after shelf of books and scrolls sat under the steady light of beeswax candles. The air smelled of old glue and camphor. In the silence, the only sound was the occasional turning of a page by one of the other readers. Each reader sat in their own pool of yellow light, alone with the words on the page.

A brass counter-plate read her palm brand at the entry. Every book, scroll, and tome she selected appeared in the plate’s polished face as a number, ticking down. The cost of her reading time was displayed in front of her the entire time.

Tally ran a counter down the spines of the books, a pale blue number appearing over each one. The books were sorted by usefulness: first accounts of Phantom-State thresholds, then texts on how to reduce the risks of transitioning, then general pharmacology. Maureen turned the pages, spread by spread, giving each spread a single glance before Tally filed it away with a faint blue flicker.

With each withdrawal, the counter-plate ticked down by another point, and Tally sighed. *Points are for spending,* Maureen told her silently.

*I know,* Tally replied. *I just like to see them disappear slowly.*

The accounts agreed with one another. A breakthrough into Phantom State was a loud and visible event that sent pressure rippling through the floor. It took eighteen Spirit to open the way onto the Ancient road. The Alliance had its own sealed chambers, which is what the careful used when they could.

Then Tally halted the shelf’s counter. *There. Page forty-one.* A preservation-crystal archive entry describing how to open a wizard-made seal without spoiling the contents, and how serpent tears were drawn.

*You sound smug,* Maureen thought.

*No, I’m being precise. Smugly.*

Maureen was happy. Quietly and completely, the scientist in her satisfied by a productive shelf. She lost track of time until an attendant began trimming the lamps around her, and the aisles began to fade into darkness one by one.

On the way out, Tally closed the column.

[Contribution: ~520 → ~70.]

*That was a good night,* she said. *Also a funeral.*

* * *

That night, behind her bedroom ward and with Mimicry down, Maureen knelt on the floorboards. She laid out the three materials for the second layer of Void Eclipse on a dark cloth, positioning them as she would reagents on an alchemy bench.

The Purple Wood Snake crystal was the size of her fist, with a faint violet tinge in the lamplight. Within the crystal, a small snake was coiled in a tight ball, perfectly still, like a thought held in abeyance. The crystal felt cold enough that her fingers ached from the contact. The Necro Flower waited patiently in its black stone box. The Dark Element Energy Stone sat beside them, matte and black, almost drinking up the light from the lamp that shone on it.

Tally painted Maureen’s view with the page-forty-one steps, rendering them as a list of pale-blue lines along the edge of her vision.

[Step 1: Check ward. Step 2: Prepare the vessel. Step 3: Examine the seal. . . .]

First, Tally checked the ward. Everything was perfect. The golden filaments were sound, each knot fed with mana, no slack to be found anywhere.

*Are you going to check the ward three times?* Maureen asked silently.

*No need,* Tally responded. *Twice will suffice this evening.*

With Maureen’s consent, Tally laid the Mirror Pupil lens over her sight. The world washed into pale reds. The seal stood out clearly, a fine knotted weave, with three particular knots bearing the major part of the load— just as the archive entry described.

Maureen reached out with threads of her own mana, cutting each of the three knots in the order listed in the entry. The crystal broke, snapping with the sound of ice fracturing across a pond’s surface. First a clean line, then a webwork of cracks, and finally the shell opened without a single shard falling into the liquid within.

The tears ran out like thick, violet oil. They were cold enough that they fogged the glass Maureen held underneath. The smell was sharp, like river stone and something bitter just behind it. She caught every drop of the viscous fluid in the best vessel from the pouch’s stock of glassware.

[Tears: 9 drops. Spoilage: none observed.]

*What should I do with the empty shell?* Tally asked.

*Keep it for me. Please.*

[Shell stored. Label: Void Eclipse souvenir.]

*It’s not a souvenir,* Maureen responded silently.

Tally kept the label anyway.

Maureen opened the black stone box. The Necro Flower unfolded in the light of the single lamp, as if some long-dead hand were unclenching its fingers. The petals were grey-white, curling back away from a dark heart at the center of the flower. The smell of rot and cellar earth wafted up from the flower, cold and dead. The flame in the lamp bent toward the flower, as if it were starving for air.

Maureen sat with the flower in the rhythm of the Void Eclipse, drinking in the tears as they dripped down her throat. She inhaled the petals as they broke down into a cloud of grey motes, and she held the Dark Element Energy Stone against her breastbone as the darkness within it poured out into her.

*May I hold a spell lattice through the Darklight Orb?* Tally asked.

*Consent granted,* Maureen thought.

*Holding lattice, first layer,* Tally said.

Maureen threaded the second layer through the first, which Tally maintained with a steady hand through the Darklight Orb.

*Hold,* Maureen said silently.

*Holding,* Tally replied.

In the corner of her display, Maureen saw the tick of another small cost.

First, there was the cold. Then, the heat. Black-red motes swam upward behind Maureen’s eyes, while a pressure began to build in her skull, like the breath held too long beneath the surface of a still pond. It wouldn’t let go until . . .

It did.

Tally’s readout settled down.

[Void Eclipse: layer 1 → 2. Spirit 16.1 → 18.0. Mana max 18.0. Threshold reached.]

*Well done, Maureen Olsen,* Tally said quietly, as if she were back at the very beginning, the first time Maureen had made a potion right.

Tally began listing off what the third layer would require, based on what she had seen in the Void Eclipse pages.

*Later,* Maureen told her silently.

*You always say later,* Tally responded.

*Well, later always comes, didn’t it?*

* * *

This morning, when Maureen left for the Alliance, Ike and the whole household were lined up on the frosted steps to see her off. Wearing Richard’s face, she said, “I’ll be gone a while this time. Ike, everything in the house is in your hands.”

Ike’s expression did not change by so much as a muscle. “Yes, Master.”

His stooped bow was just like every other morning, his eyes stopping precisely at her collarbone. In the chill air, their breaths condensed into smoky trails. Behind him, the stable boy shifted awkwardly from foot to foot on the icy flagstones.

>Household: 8. Coal-bay wall: closed. Rental Centre ward-knot: unchanged.

Then Tally’s voice, silent in Maureen’s head. *The house will be dull without us.*

* * *

The Alliance had sealed chambers that could be rented out by the hour. Maureen got one from the counter, where a young Wizard Apprentice was on duty. He didn’t glance up as he handed over a heavy stone key. “Just settle up when you come out, Wizard Richard,” he said, flipping a page in his ledger.

The chamber was empty and made of bare grey stone. The cold was biting enough that Maureen could see her breath condense in the air. The Alliance’s rune arrays formed a ring around the walls and ceiling, visible only as the faintest lines. There was still a smell of chalk and old incense in the air—the scent of countless wizards who came before and sat very still while they worked their magic.

She’d chosen this place because of the noise the breakthrough would make. Even in the quiet of the villa, the whole street would have felt the shock. Wenger’s searchers would have been knocking on her door within the hour.

Even here, Maureen drew her own silver runes just inside the Alliance’s grey lines, forming a second ring within their first. Whatever happened in here would stay in here.

Tally double-checked the weave of both rings—her silver over the Alliance’s grey.

*Tight?* Maureen asked silently.

*Yes,* Tally replied in her head. *First check or third?*

*Third. You did the other two while I was still drawing them.*

Sitting cross-legged on the cold grey stone, Maureen thought of Peaceridge and the rite that had made her an Official Wizard: Tally’s readout breaking off in the middle of a word, the display suddenly going dark. The empty corner of her mind where the countdowns used to run. Her throat tightened before she even realized she was going to speak.

*Will you go dark again?*

*I don’t know,* Tally answered. It was warm in her head.

*Last time . . .*

The blood had rebuilt her then as well, but every account in the archive had only spoken of the wizard being reborn. *Medium confidence,* Tally said.

*Medium?*

*Yes. If I go quiet, it doesn’t mean I’m gone. I’ll come back. I did last time.*

*Stay anyway.*

*Every second.*

Maureen let out the breath she’d been holding. *Tally,* she ordered silently, *watch my state at all times. Record every reading of the advancement to Phantom State.*

[Monitoring.]

[Recording: all.]

Maureen closed her eyes and began the Void Eclipse rhythm. As she started to chant the words, the two rings of runes around her began to glow faintly—grey and silver.

Particles began to pour in from the walls around her. Darkness and fire streamed towards her like smoke pouring out of an oven left closed for too long. The chamber grew cold, then hot, and the grey stone beneath her knees felt like it was humming.

[Spirit 18.4 . . .]

[19.2 . . .]

Each number was a stone Maureen could stand on, each one bringing her closer to her goal.

[20.1. Rune Arrays: settling. Bloodline: active.]

Her bones ached as if they were being lengthened, pulled apart by some invisible force. Her blood ran hot enough that she could feel it in her fingertips, and a pressure began to build behind her sternum, pushing outwards like a fist trying to break free of its confinement.

Tally kept both rings well fed with mana, and calculated the tremors running out through the floor. By density she logged the pulse of the guard apprentice outside, spiking as the stone beneath his counter began to shake.

A dull boom ran through the floor, shaking the chamber. A serpent’s hiss coiled in the darkness around her. The pressure in her chest broke open into lightness, and Maureen could feel her body beginning to resonate with the particles, its edges thinning like smoke around a lit lamp.

For a moment the world seemed to pause, resting not upon her but around her. Her body weighed nothing, yet filled the entire chamber. The cold of the grey stone walls was something that she remembered more than felt.

Before Maureen had even touched the door, Tally detected her Mimicry beginning to fail, Richard’s jaw broadening slightly, the left side falling just a fraction of a second behind the right, as Jack’s used to. Maureen recast the Mimicry (−0.4), and Tally dampened the heat from the Pankora through the Orb (−0.2). *I can’t do much about the eyes,* Tally said, *but I think I can quiet the serpent a little.*

The chamber’s door swung open, revealing an ordinary room beyond. Maureen walked out of the chamber and over to the counter before dropping a small black cloth bag upon it. “The chamber fee,” she said evenly. “Two hundred and seventy-five magic stones.”

Without thinking, the apprentice looked up into her eyes. They were bottomless pits of flame, a fire burning deep beneath their surface. He quickly dropped his gaze, stepping back away from the counter as he did so. “Yes, of course, I’ll just record that...” He whispered to himself, “A powerful wizard... ”

Tally read the apprentice’s pulse at 140 beats per minute. His eyes were fixed on the ledger, unwilling to look back up at the wizard standing in front of him. Based on the apprentice’s emotional state and his physical reaction to Maureen, Tally predicted with medium confidence that the apprentice would only remember Richard as a powerful wizard, and nothing more. Tally then turned her attention to the ledger. [Magic stones: ~8,500 → ~8,225.]

*Worth it?* Tally asked.

*Unanimous.*

Maureen walked down the corridor slowly, her aura muted, nearly absent, while a serpent’s hiss trailed behind her like the whisper of dead leaves in autumn. A faint rune array danced in her eyes, a single silver line of magic forming the shape of a serpent, before gradually fading away. Once, unnoticed by any eyes, she briefly rested her hand against the cold wall for support.

Tally projected the new scan in front of Maureen’s eyes.

[Maureen Olsen. Phantom-State Wizard (Ancient Wizard). Strength 15.2, Agility 14.9, Constitution 15.3. Spirit 20.1. Mana 20.1.]

She then placed the data beside Barok’s sample, using the ladder overlay to compare the two.

*You’re standing right on Barok’s rung now,* she said. *And yours has a lot more rungs above it.*

*Don’t get poetic on me,* Maureen thought back.

*I’m being accurate.*

*It just sounds poetic because you’re happy.*

* * *

She stayed in Emilia for half a month, taking the time to learn how her new body worked from the inside. Each night behind her bedroom ward she tested all of her spells until each one came to her hand without a second thought. Then every morning Tally would report to her from the active watch.

[Night: 3 passes. Cat, 02:10. Ward drew 0.1.]

*You slept through the cat again,* Tally said.

*Very well.*

The guild token remained quiet. *You’ll need to recast Night Owl’s face before you wear it again. With all the changes.*

Tally recalibrated every spell cost against Mana 20.1, finding them all unchanged despite the spells striking harder and more efficiently. *That’s free money!* Tally proclaimed.

*It’s an estimate,* Maureen replied.

*Fair enough. But I still like it.*

Maureen moved through the house with a lighter step than before. She had to consciously remind herself to set her feet down with a little more weight as she descended the stairs so that Ike would hear his master approaching. He was in the middle of polishing the banister when she finally descended into the cellar, past the black heap in the coal bay. “Manas,” she whispered, pressing the small flush knob on the wall beside it.

The wall slid aside to reveal the hidden chamber beyond.

As always, Tally double-checked that the Rental Centre ward-knot did not extend past Gregory’s second ring before Maureen stepped through.

*All clear,* Tally said.

Within, the particles were going wild. The elemental energy pool churned with a conflagration of strange energies—greens turning to ambers, then bruise-violet, crackling like fat in a pan. At its centre lay the faceless veilsilver puppet, unmoving, a sharp smell like struck flint hanging in the air above it.

“I am a Phantom-State wizard now,” Maureen said out loud as she stood alone at the edge of the energy pool. “So now I can finish you.”

And then, just barely audible, she continued. “I have spent so much on you, that it hurts to think about it. Don’t let me down.”

*About nine thousand one hundred stones for the array,* Tally provided. *Plus the Seal on top of that, which no amount of stones could purchase.*

*Don’t say it, Tally,* Maureen thought silently back at her companion.

*But you told me, years ago on the road, to count every stone you spent, and you never told me to stop,* Tally answered.

Maureen reached into the inner pocket of her robe where the Blood Seal of Thorns usually rested, feeling only the soft cloth that had met her fingers every day for the past half a month. The Blood Seal of Thorns was not in there anymore. It was down here, in the frame’s chest.

She took a deep breath. Then she stood at the edge of the energy pool and formed the casting gestures. She began to chant the Ancient Andas activation words she had memorized from the construct spreads. Immediately, the particles grew even more restless, and the pool began to shift colours, rippling with the movement of the energies.

Waves of colour began to flow up the walls of the room, staining the palms of Maureen’s hands with a faint greenish light before changing again to a blood red. The crackles of the churning energies became sharper, snapping like breaking branches. The air pressed against her eardrums, just as it did during her breakthrough behind her bedroom ward.

As the chant continued, Tally called out the core’s resonance.

*The Seal’s array is awakening,* Tally said.

*That’s the same ember-red pattern I logged the first time those vines grew over your hand on the road to the sea,* Tally continued.

*I know that pattern.*

*So do I,* Maureen thought back.

In the centre of the energy pool, the motionless puppet suddenly shuddered.

A pair of blood-red eyes opened up in its faceless head. Maureen felt it looking back at her, just like the Seal was doing.
