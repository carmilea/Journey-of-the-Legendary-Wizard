The ambush had not gone well for this particular group of Mist Labyrinth apprentices. Two or three of them lay scattered around the craters, torn apart by the blasts. The last one stood shaking, staring at the cracked pendant in his fist. It was smoking slightly, an enchanted item he'd picked up somewhere within the realm by sheer dumb luck. Just enough of the blast had been swallowed to keep him alive.

The smells of burnt Hillan and wet earth hung in the air, faintly sweetened by the pervasive flavour of the realm. The apprentice's fog halo was trailing away from him in tatters, like wet paper. Maureen tossed the black shell from hand to hand. It was cool to the touch, heavy as a river stone, and slapped softly against her palm every time it arced upwards.

"My, my," Jack's low rasping voice said. "Aren't you lucky. I wasn't expecting anyone to walk away from that." She paused a beat, letting the shell continue to toss. "Doesn't mean you have to keep on walking."

The apprentice's mouth opened and closed, forming half words. "You...what...you..."

He broke and ran, and Maureen let the grenade fall to rest in her palm before she flung the Cross Sword with her other hand. The blade flashed silver in the flat grey light, sank between the boy's shoulders and pinned him against a tree trunk. His head lolled sideways, then stayed there.

*You were holding a grenade,* Tally pointed out conversationally.

*I didn't think it worth wasting seven stones of Hillan on a boy who'd lost his nerve,* Maureen replied silently.

*Fair point, I suppose.* Tally's voice was smugly thoughtful. *I've never heard you price out a death quite so economically. I shall make note of the saving.*

Maureen absently rubbed at the bridge of her nose, a nose that wasn't hers, and walked over to tug the Cross Sword free. There was a wet scrape as the metal came loose, and the boy's body slumped to the ground. Her hands were steady, her breathing unaltered by the effort. She noticed their steadiness the way one might notice the weather, and moved past it without further thought. It was a fact, to be noticed and then moved beyond.

The glyph on the back of her hand gave a faint tug, the dead apprentice's point reaching out through her skin to touch her. Over the last several days, she had learned that the tug could be refused, if one wanted. She refused it with an act of will, no different than the way one might close a fist.

The unclaimed point untangled itself from the body. It formed a thin, pale wisp which blew away to the north-east. *Three so far this week, all heading in the same direction,* Tally said. *Logged bearing.*

*Log it and leave it,* she told it silently.

*Logged,* Tally said, though not left.

She watched the wisp thread its way between the grey tree trunks, like breath condensing in the cold air of morning. A high score would mean two things: Adrian asking about it with kind eyes, and everyone else asking with knives. Five wasn't high enough for anyone to remember.

Maureen went through the bodies quickly. Most days were running that way now. Sometimes they ambushed her, sometimes she ambushed them. Either way, it always ended with the others dead and her kneeling beside the bodies, searching their pouches for supplies.

*Low-grade magic stone cards, four. Worth approximately 1,000 each.*

Maureen pulled a bundle of rare roots out of one dead man's pack, along with a sealed jar. Any apprentice would have killed for those roots. She dropped them into the black ash at the edge of the blast crater.

*You're going to leave those?* Tally asked.

*Weight is speed,* Maureen thought back.

*Bandolier nineteen, pack seventy. Shall I move five over?*

*I'll do it at the next stop,* she thought back.

*You said that at the last stop,* Tally said.

Several days ago, she'd told Tally to take the kill count off of her display, just as she'd done with Overboard and Remains. Tally kept count anyway, somewhere out of sight.

The straps of the pack settled over her shoulders, the padded iron shells inside shifting with her steps. Nineteen shells on the bandolier, seventy in the pack. A normal person would have staggered under the weight, but her knight's body barely felt it.

Maureen tightened the bandolier across her chest, wiped the sword on the robe of a dead boy, then walked off deeper into the trees.

* * *

The ridge path was quiet enough that she could hear the needles give under her boots. Nothing sang anywhere on the slope. A Blue Ring apprentice lay across the path. He was still dressed in his blue-green robes. There was a hole punched straight through him, front to back, as round as a fist. The edges of the wound were cooked black.

Before she crouched down, the smell reached her. It wasn't grenade, it wasn't fireball. It was something else, older, hotter. She could feel the hairs on the back of her neck standing up before her mind even identified it.

[Odour: sulphur, fire-smoke. Match: Fasol serpent, close.]

*Same match certainty as the phantom serpent beneath the peak of the mountain, the day Aaron stood on its head.*

Aaron, coming out of the rubble with a smile that went all the way up to his eyes.

Maureen didn't touch the body. She rose without a word and walked on down the path. *Keep Aaron's file open,* she told Tally silently.

*Open,* it responded. *By sight alone, the weapon that made that wound is approximately the size of a hand. On what it is, I have no data.*

* * *

Before she entered the clearing Maureen’s hand automatically went to the inner pocket to find the Seal. *There,* she thought. *Brighter this morning again.*

*By sight only. It brightens on days when people die near it. Correlation, not cause.*

*Well, I hope that’s not the cause. That’s a horrible correlation.*

*I didn’t arrange it.*

Through the cloth, the Seal felt warmer than it had in months. Almost like a living thing. The faint, pale eye that lay against her breastbone prickled. She removed her hand and continued walking.

The sound came to her first, a tearing sound, wet and brutal. As she came to the end of the trees and entered another clearing, she found the source. A tall shape with two heads was crouched over several corpses of apprentices, the heads alternating as they ripped at the dead bodies.

Long ropes of thick yellow drool hung from the jaws of both heads, running down the bodies beneath them. With each bite the sound of a wet sack being torn open echoed in the stillness of the trampled clearing. The stink of opened bellies rose from the clearing, thick enough to taste.

Tally filed away the image for later reference, next to the two-headed monster that the construct in Room 21 had raised for her once upon a time. *Same silhouette,* Tally noted. *But no smell. File match: Hell Fiend.*

*Hell Fiend. Underground race. Thick hide. Slow wits. Weak against sorcery. Small fry down there. Came in with the dark elves. Hearsay. Jason’s ‘damn dark elves’ at the gathering. You glanced at them. Lost interest.*

Both heads rose as one, and the fiend turned from its meal, roaring deep in its throat. It charged Maureen, leaving the corpses behind, and the ground thudded with each step it took toward her.

“You got lucky with those,” she said to it, glancing at the corpses. “That’s done now.” She held out her hand, palm up. “Simons-Sado,” her lips formed, and deep-red fireballs streamed from her palm, one after another. The reworked version, quicker than in the book.

*Left head. Eyes.*

The words came to Maureen terse and cold, even as the mana line dropped with each cast. [Mana: 7.6 → 6.5 → 5.4 → 4.3]

One ball of red after another exploded beneath the uniform grey sky, and Maureen could feel the heat slap against her cheeks. The roar became a shriek, the shriek became a gurgle, and the gurgle became nothing. The air was suddenly filled with the smoke of burning fat, hanging low over everything in the sugary atmosphere of the realm.

When the smoke thinned, chunks of purple-black hide were twitching on the trampled and scorched leaf litter. Maureen was not surprised, and turned to go.

Then she stopped short, making a small sound in her throat.

Two figures were standing in the parting smoke. On the ground was a young man, dressed in the white robes of an apprentice from Hader Castle. His skin was very pale, his cheekbones high, his mouth thin-lipped, and his expression cold and contemptuous. Floating above him on a treetop, as light as a bird, was another figure, also dressed in apprentice’s robes, but these were blood-red. His face was smooth and fine, made of white bone.

*These are my two “avoid” entries,* Tally said. *You are walking toward both of them.*

*They walked toward me,* Maureen replied silently.

*Technically true,* Tally said smugly. *But I hate it.*

Maureen’s palms grew cold within their gloves. It was the first time she had felt real fear during this trial, and she noticed that fear with the same detachment that she used to note everything else. Chunks of the Hell Fiend drifted down between the three of them like black flakes of ash. No one moved for a long moment.

“Who are you?” Maureen asked.

The two apprentices talked over her as if she were a piece of furniture.

The bone-faced apprentice cocked his head. “That fireball was reworked. Whoever did that is no ordinary wizard.”

The pale man snorted. “And you think he’s in our league?”

The bone-face shrugged, the red robe rustling with the movement. “Him? The spell is good. He’s not. Not by a long shot.”

The pale man’s voice became flat and emotionless. “Then kill him. He’s dead anyway.”

“Him,” the bone-face had said. “He.” Jack’s face and Jack’s low rasp were still holding, and these two had instantly dismissed her as male and inferior without a second glance. In spite of her fear, Maureen was a little pleased.

*Twelve for both, est.,* Tally said, counting in shells, the only currency that held any weight here. *I’d rather you didn’t.*

*So would I, but it looks like they’ve already made up their minds for me.*

The bone-face remained where he was, perched on the tree limb. The pale man turned to Maureen, clearly enjoying himself.

“Listen, apprentice. Bad luck, really. You might have lived. But you ran into us, so go and—”

* * *

Two black shells arced over the pale man’s shoulder before he could finish the sentence. His face went blank with surprise.

“Kerrash,” Maureen said under her breath.

The blast blew apart the air in the clearing, spraying fire and spinning shards of Hillan. Trees shook off sheets of bark like snakes shedding skin. Maureen’s teeth rattled from the shock. For a heartbeat, the world was reduced to noise and orange light.

She came through the smoke finishing the full phrase of the Seal. She had sworn not to show anything in here. But Jack wasn’t real, not in the sense that these two were real. He was her own invention, a mask that she had chosen because no one could trace him back to Maureen Olsen. Whatever Jack carried out of here, whatever he was seen to carry, belonged to him alone. And the glow had returned. Red vines with thorns sprouted over her body.

[Seal: 0.0 s] began ticking at the edge of her vision. The armour felt like it always did, the thorns pressing against her skin but not piercing, and the familiar smell of sap and iron closed around her face like a shroud.

Maureen hit the pale man with a closed fist. The man flew backward as if shot from a sling. There was a loud snap as his body broke through one trunk, then another. He finally stopped against a giant tree. He slumped to the ground, face white, coughing blood. “Damn—damn you!”

Maureen didn’t stop. She threw herself off the ground, blasting toward the bone-face, and vines whipped out from her armour. They wrapped around him where he stood, pinning his arms and legs together before he’d finished watching his ally tumble through the air.

*Spikes. Off the vine. Now.*

The bone-face’s shoulders had hunched ever so slightly a quarter beat before his attack, and Maureen let go.

The apprentice exploded into bone spikes from every portion of his body at once. The bone shredded the vines. He jumped clear just as Maureen’s fist passed by his bone face. The wind from her arm made him flinch.

“Wait—wait!” he said. “Wait a moment!”

She didn’t wait. Vines shot out from her arms, catching one tree trunk after another and pulling her after the fleeing apprentice. The thorns dug into the grey bark. Trees blurred past as she swung between them like a monkey in a cage, and the counter ran steadily at the edge of her vision.

Swinging from the last tree, she whispered a spell under her breath and struck again. The apprentice twisted away, then darted back once, twice, three times before finally standing clear with real ground between them.

*Release,* Tally said.

She let go of the armour at [Seal: 3.1 s]. The vines retracted back into the stone. There was still an ache behind her ribs, but it was lighter than before. That was what worried her—not the pain, but the fact that it was getting better.

“That was . . . unexpected,” said the apprentice with the bone face. He took a long breath. “I didn’t think there was another apprentice in here as strong as us.” He held both hands up, open-palmed. “Let’s talk, all right? Can we talk?”

Her palms were still cold, though not as cold as before. Three shells had already crept down to her fingers from the bandolier, and their smooth black iron felt cool against her skin. She held them low, pressed against her thigh.

Now she had their measure. Ordinary methods would not kill these two—it would take shells, and a great many of them. She kept her arm perfectly motionless and let him speak. It wasn’t out of mercy. There was a price.

Before she spoke, Tally laid its pale-blue muscle map over her throat. When she spoke, her voice came out low and hoarse. It sounded like a man’s voice, but with gravel in it. “What do you want to say?”

The apprentice’s breathing was ragged behind the bone mask. “We’re evenly matched. There’s no sense in tearing each other apart. But . . . why jump us?”

Maureen tilted her head. “You’d already decided to kill me. Hadn’t you?”

His breath caught on the bone, a small hiccup of air that she could hear across the clearing. She was no longer the seed who didn’t understand the rules. Here, whoever hesitated died first.

“All right,” he said. “Misunderstanding. You jumped us first. So we’re square.” He gave a small bow. “Sieg, of the White Bone Tower.” He pointed at the pale man, who was now pulling himself back up the giant tree trunk. “That’s Varo. Hader Castle.”

“Jack,” Maureen said. “Jack the Ripper. From the Dark Forest.”

*Lower,* Tally said. *You’re drifting back up on “Ripper.”*

Maureen forced her voice lower. “Jack the Ripper,” she said again, and this time it sounded like stones rolling around in a sack.

The grenade smoke hung in the air between the giant grey trunks, the sugary atmosphere heavy with scorched Hillan, sap and iron. Splinters of wood crunched underfoot.

“Jack?” Sieg said aloud, frowning slightly. “I haven’t heard of you.”

“Is the Dark Forest hiding apprentices like you, Jack?” His voice was as cold as the wind between the trees. He took a step towards her. “Jack, we’re the strong ones. No sense killing each other this early. Let’s agree to clear out the weak first, and after that—”

Maureen saw movement over his shoulder. Varo had finally stopped coughing. His outline at the base of the giant tree was beginning to blur at the edges, like a wisp of smoke. Underneath Sieg’s voice she could hear something else, a sound that grew steadily, low and many-throated.

*Warning. Target Varo: body dispersing. Insects. Wound closing.*

Tally’s terse calls grew colder and more succinct as the pressure rose.

*Species match: Heart-Eater Worm. Minai material, 1 of 4. Pulse increase.*

The second line was even colder. Adrian’s pharmacology database had helped Tally make a positive identification on the insects, matching them up by sight against a plate in its files.

She didn’t answer Sieg. Instead she reached into the bandolier across her chest and pulled out three shells. She threw all three at Varo in one swift motion, saying the trigger word, Kerrash, as they left her hand.

There was a hum like a kicked hive, followed by fire and Hillan shards. Varo was blown into the air in a burst of black wings, his scream trailing off as the swarm streamed away between the trees. “Damn you! Jack! Jack the Ripper!”

Maureen went after him immediately. Sieg flickered into her path by reflex. “Jack, what are you—”

The words ripped out of the borrowed throat with real savagery. “Sieg! Do you want to die?!”

Sieg flinched back, startled by the violence in her tone. He let her pass.

[Grenades: hand 14 / pack 70.]

*Running,* Tally said. *Pulse increase logged. Chase initiated. First time pulse affected by chase. Logging.*

*Don’t,* Maureen told it silently.

*Logged quietly,* Tally said.

It wasn’t fear that propelled her through the forest now. It was hunger, and it had the insects’ shape and the Totem’s. She ran with her teeth set against one another.

Behind her, after a moment, came the sound of Sieg’s footsteps. They were light and quick, keeping their distance but following anyway.
