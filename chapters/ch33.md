Adrian set the vial down, carefully, as if it weighed something. His voice was flat when he spoke. "I never would have imagined that you could actually produce a finished Recovery Potion in such a short time."

*Maureen, Adrian's intonation matches baseline,* Tally noted. *No 0.6 second pause before speaking, as with Hegel. He believes you.*

*No, he believes the vial.*

*Same thing. For now.*

The relaxation in Maureen's chest allowed her shoulders to drop by a finger's width. The burner kept ticking away, and the dish of tiny insects remained motionless beside the green vial.

Winnie walked over from the glassware racks and gave Maureen a quick hug. "Well done!"

*Maureen's pulse spiked,* Tally reported.

*Don't.*

Tally said nothing for a moment, and Maureen knew that it was letting its silence stand out more than any spoken words.

* * *

Maureen stood stiffly for a single breath, before she patted Winnie on the back once. Winnie smelled like crushed herbs and lye soap. She stepped back with a big grin. "Thank you. I couldn't have done it without my mentor's excellent teaching. I'm still nowhere near as proficient as Mace, though."

Mace smiled gently. "Maureen is being modest, as usual. My own first finished potion took me approximately half a year to create, and I had my wizard family providing me with supplies. It is quite an achievement." He shrugged, smiling wryly. "I might not have been able to keep up with Maureen if not for their help."

Tally noted that Mace's smile was forced. *Envy noted, mild. Half year matches Lilith's dinner gossip.*

*File him as kindly as possible, please.*

*I don't have a 'kind' folder.*

*Make one.*

A small chill sat at the back of her neck while she kept her face pleasant and her hands motionless on the stone bench.

Adrian coughed into his hand, breaking the pause. His smile was broad, probably because of her first success. First successes were considered lucky. "Yes, very good. You should continue to practice the Recovery Potion until you are fluent. Don't concern yourself with wasted materials, just try to be lucky again. Once you can make a Recovery Potion in your sleep, move on to other basic potions, but expect failure. It is very useful to be able to sell potions for stones, but you need to budget your time as well."

* * *

Lilith was waiting outside the lab's door when Maureen came out. "Congratulations!" She bit her lower lip and her eyes gleamed with excitement.

"Thanks," Maureen said. "And thank you for the potioneer's notebook. It really helped. Listen, I'm having dinner tonight if you want to join me. There's a new master chef in from the Principality of Gasha. His family has cooked for the royal court for generations, and he makes a veal kidney that's supposed to be divine."

Tally mentioned that Anthony was from Gasha, but she didn't know if he knew the new chef. *That's something I'll have to ask him myself,* she told it silently.

"Do you mean it?" Lilith asked.

"I'm not in the habit of lying." Maureen smiled at her labmate.

Lilith started down the corridor ahead of Maureen, half skipping with happiness.

Tally didn't say anything on purpose. Loudly. *Log that as unconfirmed, please,* Maureen told it.

*I haven't logged anything yet,* Tally protested.

The veal kidney arrived steaming in a dark gravy spiced with peppercorn. The meat was tender, moist, and rich. Lilith talked quickly across the table, telling Maureen about who was at what party and what had been said. She laughed often, behind one slender hand covering her mouth. Maureen paid for both of them, then they parted ways at the dining hall's door. Maureen turned right towards the Trading District.

* * *

The Trading District was as busy as ever. Seeds and apprentices jostled together in a tide that flowed around the stalls. The hissing of lamps punctuated the sounds of haggling. Someone was arguing over the price of a dagger, and a few stalls away someone else was complaining about the quality of some potions.

Maureen did not pause to look around, but walked directly into Gandolf's general store. She rapped on the counter, and the old man jerked awake. His kindly face beamed as he recognized her.

"My dear, your material packs are ready." He stood up from his chair behind the counter and began talking. "Now, as I told you before, an apothecary must always be prepared for—"

One side of his white beard was flattened from resting on the countertop, and his small eyes darted back and forth between her face and her hands. Maureen felt the vial through the cloth of her robes.

*That's better than the one Adrian saw,* Tally warned her.

*A shopkeeper's eye is not the same as Adrian's,* she told it silently.

*But Adrian said to practise whenever you can,* Tally replied.

*Risk logged.*

She pulled the vial out of her robes, set it gently down on the scarred wooden counter. "Gandolf, I came to sell, not buy."

The old man stared at the Recovery Potion. It was nearly flawless. "I'll take as many as you have," he said, picking the vial up and holding it to the light of his lamp. He clicked his tongue in appreciation of how few flaws there were. The misty green liquid inside turned slowly, the light shining all the way through the potion without a shadow. There were no impurities either. No sediment at the bottom of the vial.

"How much?" Maureen asked.

"Three and a half magic stones," Gandolf replied.

*It costs a tenth of a stone to produce a single vial. The material packs are a full stone each, so that is ten vials per pack,* Tally noted. *The stall prices I've seen indicate that Gandolf could make four stones per vial.*

*That's enough,* Maureen told it.

"Three and a half?" she asked Gandolf aloud. "That's a bit lowballing, isn't it?"

Gandolf's face reddened slightly beneath the white beard. His patter faltered for a second before he was able to regain his composure. "What do you propose then?"

Maureen held up four fingers. "Four magic stones for every standard-grade Recovery Potion. Agree, and I'll sell only to you from now on."

*You must state the scope aloud,* Tally noted.

"For the Recovery Potion," Maureen added, looking Gandolf in the eye. "Any other potions are mine to sell wherever I like."

Gandolf paused. His small shrewd eyes darted between her and the vial while his face worked out the figures. Four stones would leave him very little profit on each vial, but if he had an exclusive…

"If I don't agree?" Gandolf asked.

"I head out right now. Mary's General Store and Baffet's Hut are still open," Maureen said.

"Four…" Gandolf said, giving her a wry smile. "You drive a hard bargain, young lady."

"Well, you've been making more than four stones off me these past few months." Maureen shrugged.

Four small black stones clicked dully against the scarred wooden countertop, glowing faintly under the light of the lamp. Only after Gandolf pushed them towards her did she sweep them up into the silk pouch hanging from her chest.

"Congratulations, Maureen," Gandolf called after her as she headed towards the door. "From now on you're going to get rich!"

Tally added four to the ledger, removed one from the cache count, and noted, *You see? I wasn't wrong.*

Maureen didn't have anything to say to either Gandolf or Tally.

* * *

Life began to ease into a comfortable routine. She spent time learning more advanced pharmacology at Adrian's bench, mixing Recovery Potions for him in his small alchemy lab. Gandolf paid four stones for each vial that she brewed. Tally's ledger was a constant parade across Maureen's display. [Magic stones] would climb by four every time Gandolf paid for a vial, and then drop whenever she needed to pay for material packs or buy knowledge. [Cache] was filling, then emptying. In the Library District, Maureen picked up the knowledge she had been paid for, browsing the shelves for whatever her rank allowed. Conjectures, experimental data and models, records of strange phenomena; Maureen read them page by page or drank them as knowledge potions. The reading room had a chill and sterile atmosphere, with cold stone benches and the scratching of pens on paper. The grey light of the Glow Stones cast a faint dusting of ink across the air. Maureen sat and read steadily through the pile of books before her. Tally would report whenever she moved onto a new category of knowledge without being asked, and the chip was beginning to get rather smug about it. Maureen didn't say anything, but thought silently to herself that Tally was like a magpie, collecting shiny objects.

*Magpies aren't nearly this orderly,* Tally responded.

At the stall in the Trading District where Tally had first spotted the jar, Maureen bought a pair of fanged black wolf eyes. Plan six went on. One redwood nut, two wolf eyes, a small handful of crystal grapes. The wolf eyes tasted salty and of copper, while the crystal grapes eaten afterwards were cold and sweet. Back at the Library District, Tally sifted through the library's collection of knight tables until it found the line referring to the Upper Knight. Then the chip presented her with [Upper Knight. 8.3 / 8.4 / 8.3]. *Just stop sounding so proud of yourself, will you?* she thought back silently.

*But I am proud of this data!* Tally told her.

*Liar.*

Underneath the unchanging black cloud overhead, the changing of the seasons was impossible to mark. Maureen counted time by the ticking of the burner, and by the nights she stood before her washroom mirror.

A year and a half after they had landed at the Dark Forest, Tally finally displayed the following figures for Maureen. [Days since landing: 540] [Magic stones: 80] [Second array: 8/12]

* * *

Occasionally, she met her friends like this. They spoke in low tones while sitting around one of the benches, in a stone alcove off the lecture hall. Anthony's place remained empty, but otherwise they were all there.

The smoke from the hanging lamp curled against the underside of the low ceiling, while the footsteps of the last students leaving the hall faded into silence down the corridor. Maureen's eyes moved over Aaron, then narrowed. He was quieter than ever. She could just barely see purple bruises at the edge of his collar and wrist.

[Bruising: fresh] [Number of bruises: several] [Causes of bruising: unknown] Tally noted. [Right thumb: working threadbare sleeve hem]

Aaron sat quietly with them for a short while.

"I'll go first," he said finally, standing up to leave before the food even arrived.

She watched his back move away down the corridor. His shoulders were narrow, and his steps quick. Her throat tightened, but she said nothing. He didn't want anything said.

*Get some data on what's happening to him, Tally,* she thought silently.

*I can only get what you look at, Maureen.*

*Fine,* Maureen thought. *Then I'll look. Just get me something.*

The food arrived, and Lucy and Alice both shot glances at Aaron's empty seat before they stared down at their plates. Richard leaned close to Maureen, his face eager with envy. "Anthony's been going out on missions lately," he whispered.

Maureen blinked and nodded her head.

Most missions required spells, and thus needed to be undertaken by a Second-Class Wizard Apprentice or above. First-Class apprentices occasionally went on missions as well, but those were risky. Now that she could make and trade potions, she was determined to become a Second-Class Wizard Apprentice as soon as possible.
