The last night on the road before they reached their destination. The frost on the wagon tarps crackled as Maureen walked past. The great horned beasts blew like kettles as they stood at their pickets. The Oserroba youths sang around three fires in the centre of the camp. Maureen sat on the fringes of the light, still not quite accustomed to sitting around fires such as these.

*You do realise that Edward doesn’t eat, never sits and never sleeps? So if you were part of a family group and had a guest who didn’t pick up a fork to eat even once in a day, don’t you think they would notice?*

*Then he’ll have to leave on business tonight,* Maureen silently replied.

So when the singing was loud enough to cover any sound she made, Maureen walked Edward out past the pickets into the pines. His footfalls were too even as they crunched on the frozen pine needles. She stopped in a hollow behind the fallen trunk of a pine, where no firelight could penetrate.

“Ward.” At Maureen’s single word, Tally laid down a small ring of ward around the hollow. Golden filaments twisted and turned before knotting together at four nodes in Maureen’s overlay.

[Ward drew 0.2]

*Can I log this please, Maureen? I’d like to have it on file.*

Maureen nodded and reached inside herself to find the void pocket that Edward now occupied. He folded into it obediently, his drawn face the last feature to disappear, in the way that a coat’s cuffs are the last things to slide into a dark cupboard. Then there was silence for ten long breaths while Tally monitored the core.

[Edward: stowed. Core: intact. Pouch transit: tolerated, 2 tests.]

*I think you like him better when he’s folded,* Maureen silently teased her.

*He is much tidier like that,* Tally responded with a note of smugness. *But I would still like a third test before he fights for us.*

A reserve hidden is always more valuable than one openly displayed. Maureen walked back to the camp alone, her footsteps crunching on the frost-covered ground. The night’s cold bit through the hem of her black robe, where the embroidery was so fine as to be almost invisible. The singing grew louder with every step she took towards the Oserroba’s campfires.

The youths had taken a head count. One of the two strange wizards was missing.

“Ow— Richard,” Denise looked up from the fire at Maureen. “Wizard Richard, I mean, sorry. Where did your friend get to?”

[Slip. Logged. One syllable only, no one turned around.]

*Don’t log Denise’s slips, Tally,* Maureen silently told her. *She’s on our side.*

*Everyone’s slips are logged, especially our own side’s.*

“My apologies, Lady Denise. Edward had business elsewhere. We parted.”

Denise laughed at that, leaving it alone, as if the disappearance of tall, gaunt men from caravans was an everyday occurrence.

The youths shuffled around, giving Maureen the warmer side of the fire, and a cup of spiced wine found its way into her hand, passed along by many hands. The deference of a family who had bred wizards for centuries.

Maureen took it as courtesy rather than warmth.

Denise talked late into the night, lore spilling freely from her lips. Spell grades, old family feuds, reagents that curdled in the cold. Maureen asked little, listening more, and the girl knew far more than her soft voice implied.

[Spell Lore]

[+27 Entries]

[Source: Denise, one voice]

Tally was well pleased with the evening’s data haul. Maureen silently admitted that the girl probably knew more lore than occupied half the Alliance’s free shelves.

The fires sank to embers and the singing died away to just the cracked voice of one youth. Maureen slept sitting against a wagon wheel, the iron rim of the wheel cold against her back even through her robe. Outside the circle of firelight the horned beasts shuffled and blew in the darkness.

* * *

The interior of Denise’s carriage was plush but worn, with velvet seats and a small table that folded down from the side. There were frost ferns on the window glass and the sound of hooves ringing on the frozen ruts of the road. Maureen’s fingers drummed out the rhythm of the Silver Light on the top of the table, and for the first time in days she felt the tension ease in her shoulders.

“You’re the heir,” she said. “Heirs have their own guards. Why borrow me?”

Tally noted the girl’s hand twitching to her sleeve as she spoke, the same tell of fear that she had shown back in the Alliance corridor.

Denise smiled crookedly. “My father, Lawrence, is dying. My uncle Claude is... Liquefied. And I am only an Official, one rung above the floor. So.”

Maureen didn’t need the rest of it spelled out. This was a fight for the headship of the family, and while among mortal noble families the losing side was exiled, wizard families had more resources at their command and more desperate measures to prevent them. The losers might suffer things that an ordinary person could never even imagine. Tally set the girl’s density against her own:

[Denise: Official, upper; Phantom threshold close, by comparison to you.

Confidence: medium.]

“My uncle has been feeling... strange lately,” Denise continued. “I fear he will move suddenly.” Then her jaw tightened with determination. “But not for long. A month at most, two at the outside. I’m close to Phantom. After that I can hold him off, if only barely.”

Tally gave the girl’s forecast a medium confidence rating. The danger was real, but the timeline was more Denise’s hope than a fact. *Two months?* Maureen thought silently.

*Two months, if he agrees to the schedule,* Tally responded.

Maureen dropped her eyes and Richard’s face showed nothing, but underneath she was amused. A guard post for two months, a sick father, an impatient uncle. After Gus’s traps, this almost felt restful.

Voices rose ahead, and the carriage rolled to a halt. Maureen lifted the curtain to reveal Conta Manor, the Oserroba family seat. It was a broad estate of old pale stone, built in the traditional style.

Smells of beeswax and freshly baked bread drifted from the half open doors of the manor. The clipped hedges were stiff with rime. A long line of family members dressed in silk was waiting by the gate, their breaths smoking in the cold air.

Denise hopped down from the carriage and gave a few brisk orders before she marched towards the reception line. At its head stood a man in his middle years. His hair was greying and he wore a tailcoat over his shirt and trousers. “Denise, child. Welcome home.” He held out his hands.

Denise curtsied before him, and the fear was gone from her face as though wiped away with a damp cloth. Now she gazed up at her uncle with the face of a niece who trusted him completely. She did not even raise a hand towards her sleeve. Maureen noticed.

Claude’s eyes shifted over his niece’s shoulder, fixing on Maureen. They narrowed almost imperceptibly. “And a new friend, I see.”

“Richard. My friend.” Denise beamed.

Maureen nodded once, coolly, as one gentleman should acknowledge another.

[Claude Oserroba. Liquefied Wizard, by Denise’s word. Density above Maffie; below the attendant standing at Oran’s shoulder.]

[3.4 seconds. Eyes narrowed 0.6 seconds.]

*Wondering what I cost,* Maureen thought silently.

*He’s wondering what Richard is worth to Denise,* Tally responded. *That belongs in a different column.*

Maureen’s jaw clenched.

The Liquefied Wizard said a few smooth words, gave Maureen another long look, then turned back into the hall.

Maureen was given lodging in Conta Manor’s best guest room, which lay next to Denise’s. The young lady had specifically requested that they be placed together, so they could cover for one another. Young men stood along the corridor as Maureen and Denise were escorted to their rooms. They cast hostile glances at the outsider the heir favoured.

[Hostile looks: 6. Two touch sword hilts.]

*Jealous of a man who isn’t even a man,* Maureen thought silently.

*Best disguise in the world can still make enemies, but if those enemies are the wrong shape, it doesn’t much matter,* Tally pointed out.

Maureen’s room was furnished with a large canopy bed and a fireplace laid but not lit. The floor was cold beneath her feet. She walked towards the far wall, the wall it shared with Denise’s room. She laid the flat of her palm against it and felt only chill.

* * *

The great hall glowed with beeswax. Golden roast fowl dripped fat across silver platters, and spilled wine had formed dark rings on the white cloth. The high windows were furred with frost.

Maureen sat at the right hand of Denise, the place of highest honour, where all could see her.

Denise raised her cup and called for the applause that welcomed her guest. The clapping was polite but thin, and Maureen inclined her head the exact measure necessary for courtesy.

She ate little. At the lower tables, the youths spoke behind the rims of their cups, and their glances slid up to the high table. Maureen felt them on the back of her neck, like a draught from under a door.

Tally marked their glances, table by table. She flagged one youth with curly flaxen hair. Three cups in the space of a quarter hour, and his eyes had not left Richard since the toast.

He rose from his seat, Cross Sword in hand, and walked out onto the cleared floor before the high table. His bow was too deep.

“Honoured guest,” he said. “We so rarely meet outside wizards. Would you spar with us? Instruct us?”

Knives halted in midair over plates. The great hall hushed. Faces turned from the youth to Maureen, and back again, hoping for a show. Denise’s cup froze halfway to her lips.

[Flaxen hair. Official, new. Density below Soros. Cross Sword; knight body Upper.]

Maureen did not rise. Her hands remained folded upon the tablecloth. A wizard, only a few weeks in the arts, was calling her out at her host’s own table. Her contempt manifested itself in absolute stillness.

*Purgatory?*

*Not here.* Richard’s black fire would bring an end to this youth and her visit simultaneously.

*Four Blast Fireballs? Cut chant. Totem on.*

*Four. Not one more.*

Tally kept watch upon Maureen’s eyes.

*Crimson creeping up at the edges. Barely a hint.*

Maureen took three long breaths, one short, the rhythm of the Silver Light.

*Black again.*

Denise rose halfway from her seat. “Richard, there’s no need—” But Maureen did not look at her. “It’s fine.”

The flaxen-haired young man raised his Cross Sword. The boy stepped forward with his front foot, blade low for a rising cut.

Maureen’s lips moved once, but made no sound.

Four balls of dark-red fire blossomed into being around the chair. They hung in the air for one held breath, each sphere the size of a man’s fist, crackling with power, humming softly. Tally read them off: *One. Two. Three. Four.*

[Totem: +1.2°. Mana 20.1 → 15.7. Holding.]

*Release.*

The fireballs streaked across the floor, leaving trails of heat that licked at the underside of the tables. The youth managed to whip his sword up into position, splitting the first fireball apart into a spray of harmless sparks, but the other three struck him, taking him in the chest and legs.

Maureen felt the heat of the spells wash her cheeks, even from this distance. The beeswax candles bent away from the force of the magical conflagration, and the young wizard’s scream rose over the stink of singed hair and scorched wool.

The flaxen-haired wizard collapsed onto the cold flagstones, his clothing still smouldering. He rolled back and forth on the floor, moaning. His sword skidded away and disappeared under a table. Two of his friends rushed forward, throwing their cloaks over the fallen wizard and patting out the flames.

*Badly burned. Breathing. Alive,* Tally said, reading him with her sight. *No Purgatory. I noticed that.*

*I noticed you noticing,* Maureen thought silently.

Beneath all of this, a cold note sat like ice in the bottom of a cup. Anywhere else, not beneath this roof, she would have left nothing behind of that wizard, not so much as an ash to mark where he had stood. Her hands were still folded upon the tabletop, and she had not moved a single inch from her chair.

Silence filled the room. Somewhere a cup was knocked over, the dark wine running across the white cloth, dropping steadily to the stone floor. No one made any motion to right it.

[Hostile looks: 6 → 2. Averted: 11.]

*Against a new Official, the difference is absurd,* Tally added. *Your spells are far beyond what he could hold.*

Maureen picked up her knife and went back to her fowl. Beside her, Denise let out a long breath and set her cup on the table.

* * *

The gallery corridor of Conta Manor was dim. The walls were lined with huge oil portraits of dead Oserroba, their varnish cracked by decades of time, a few lamps hanging at intervals providing barely enough light to reach them. There was a cold draught along the floor that carried the smell of ancient linseed and dust.

“Richard, I’m sorry,” Denise said quietly. “I never thought one of them would just— take it upon himself.”

“It was nothing.”

Tally studied Denise for a moment, reading her pulse in her neck and the way she removed her hand from Maureen’s sleeve. She saw the young woman’s shoulders relax and drop slightly.

*Relief,* Tally said, *though not necessarily proof positive.*

Maureen’s steps slowed as she walked beneath the portraits. Faces stared down at her, painted in lifelike detail, though the blue eyes were all the same. Men and women looked out from a world of browns and blacks, collars of a bygone age standing high about their necks. Each portrait had a small plate of dulled brass set into the frame beneath it. Denise walked beside her, glancing at her face from time to time to see what mood she was in.

“Come and meet my father. I’d like him to know who’s watching my door.”

Maureen nodded. There was no reason she could think of to refuse.

They made several turns, and the corridors began to grow narrower and more warm. The air changed as well, losing the scent of linseed and taking on instead the sweet smell of camphor and long-steeped herbs, with something sour beneath that no amount of herb could mask.

*Five turns,* Tally noted so she could lead them back. *Logging route. Not asked, but assumed.*

They came to a halt in front of a small wooden door, which Denise opened with a single tap. “Father, this is my friend. Richard. Wizard Richard.”

The room inside was small, tastefully kept. The entire far wall was constructed of glass, and beyond it the mountains were gobbling up the sun, the light from its last dying rays making a long bar of red that stretched across the floor to the bed.

A man lay in the bed, his eyes on the window, watching the death of the day. He wore a thin white shirt of silk, his arms resting on top of the coverlet. His hair was grey, his body wasted by illness. His eyes were sunk deep into his head, his cheekbones protruding sharply beneath his sagging skin, yellowed and spotted.

[By sight: Jaundice. Skin: spotted. Breath: 9/min, shallow.]

[By particle sense: Phantom State, thin, uneven.]

*Phantom State. Lawrence Oserroba. Second Lawrence on file. First one insulted you eleven times on Joanna’s caravan.*

*This one hasn’t said a word yet.*

*Not a single word?*

*No. Not one.*

*Well then, he’s already the better Lawrence.*

The yellowed skin brought Maureen back, suddenly, to Old John lying on the straw. His ragged grey hair hung limply about his face. The stink of sickness rose from the small shack in the slum, a sour stench that no amount of herbs could disguise. Maureen swallowed, her throat tightening as she kept her hands motionless at her side.

Tally said nothing this time, and Maureen noted the omission. The man’s eyes never left the last rim of red upon the horizon.
