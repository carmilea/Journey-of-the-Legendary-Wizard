Maureen Olsen walked alone down the corridor. Her footsteps echoed against the black stone and the lights woke just ahead of her.

She sighed. All she wanted was to ask Adrian for permission to travel. So much had happened instead.

Frey blocking her way and Mace intervening were not things she had expected.

*Remember, we still couldn't make any sense of Mace's scan,* Tally reminded her.

*I didn't forget for even a single step,* she responded silently as she twisted her brass key into the lock on her own door. The metal was cold against her palm.

* * *

She'd just finished washing when there came a knock at her door. Maureen frowned at the mirror, her damp hair black and cold against her neck.

"Who is it?"

*I heard a pause and a rustle of robes before they knocked,* Tally said. *They wanted to be sure the corridor was clear.*

"Maureen, it’s me."

"Me who?"

"Lilith."

Maureen opened the door. Lilith stood outside wrapped in a black hooded robe, her pale skin and blue eyes glowing brightly in the yellow light from the lamp mounted in the doorframe.

*Hooded. She does not want to be seen at your door,* Tally pointed out.

*Agreed.*

Lilith glanced up and down the corridor. "May I come in?" she whispered.

Maureen nodded and stepped back, letting Lilith pass before shutting the door. "You’ve fallen out with the Olivia Family, haven’t you?" Lilith asked without preamble. "I saw you and Frey in the hall almost coming to blows."

"I’m afraid so," Maureen admitted. It would have been hard to hide that confrontation.

"Maureen, do you know who they are?" Lilith asked. Her voice was high pitched and quick. "Jay is the direct line Olivia heir and his brother, Frey, is direct line Olivia blood as well. Not only that but Frey is a genius. Several Olivia wizards have spoken about his genius."

*Fingers knotted into the sleeves of her robe, too fast speech pattern, eyes on the door. All tells. She is frightened for you,* Tally told her.

*Or of something else.*

*Both columns.*

Lilith took a deep breath. "Won't you come with us? With my family, the Walker Family? Only another family’s protection can keep you safe now."

"What conditions?"

Lilith's lips pressed together. "My grandfather and the family have discussed it. First you must provide at least 150 years service, second you must offer unconditional obedience to any order given by my family and third you must provide 80% of all the potions you make in future."

[1: 150 years' service. 2: unconditional obedience. 3: 80% of all potions.]

[Apprentice lifespan: ~200 years.]

*That is three quarters of your life,* Tally said quietly.

"Fourth, you…"

"Enough Lilith," Maureen said, stopping the girl. "Does your family intend to use me as a legal slave?"

Her shoulders were rigid, and suddenly the cold from her wet hair seemed to reach all the way down to the base of her spine.

"After you become a full Wizard then we can discuss…"

"If I accepted would I ever get the chance to become a full Wizard?" Maureen asked.

Lilith didn't say anything for a few seconds. [4 seconds.]

Tally didn't say anything either. The girl continued, "But if you don't accept the Olivias…"

"I know what my situation is Lilith," Maureen interrupted. "It’s late and being here isn’t good for you. I think you should go." She stood up and walked over to the door, pulling it open.

Maureen's hand on the latch felt like the heaviest thing in the room. Behind the door was the dark and empty corridor.

"Please Maureen, think about this again. You don't have to do this. I'll talk to my grandfather. He will not insist on these terms."

"Lilith, it's late," Maureen said quietly.

Lilith trembled and her eyes were bright with moisture. She gave Maureen one last long look before she turned and ran out the door. The sound of her footsteps gradually receded into the distance as she ran down the corridor.

Maureen's hand remained on the latch long after the sound of Lilith's footsteps had vanished.

*You were kind,* Tally said.

*I opened a door.*

*You were kind about it.*

She knew that Lilith meant well, but once she'd signed the agreement it would be the Walkers who decided how hard or easy the terms were. She'd be nothing more than a piece of meat on the chopping board, waiting for them to decide how to cut her up.

Tally filed Lilith's notebook beside tonight in the same folder.

*Why?*

Tally didn't answer.

Maureen sat cross-legged in front of the washroom mirror. Her breath snagged in her throat, over and over again, refusing to fall into the rhythm she wanted it to.

*One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten.*

Tally whispered, counting Maureen's breaths until they were back under control. Until she could meditate upon her Spirit Runes.

* * *

The burner ticked, keeping time in the laboratory. The smell of the reagents was sharp, pungent enough that Maureen could feel it irritating her nose and eyes. She had not slept much last night and the dryness of her eyes was a reminder of that. "Mentor," Maureen said as she bowed to Adrian.

Her mentor smiled at her as he stood beside one of the many racks of glassware that lined the long stone bench. He held out a letter, sealed with a blob of thick heavy wax. "When I was an apprentice, I was friends with Marquis Umas of the Principality of Redbud. Recently he has used my arcane mark to send word that there is trouble in his lands. You are to go and help him. Once he reads the letter, he will trust and support you fully." The letter was a solid thing in Maureen's hand, the cool wax yielding fractionally beneath the pressure of her thumb.

Adrian handed her a dull token for the exits, and a test tube, its end stoppered by a cork. Inside something small and black moved with a blur of speed, coiling and recoiling against the glass.

The cork felt cool beneath Maureen's fingers.

The burner ticked quietly.

Grey light fell from the high window.

"These are Miles vipers," Adrian said in his calm flat voice. "With this command word - Wukandima - you will be able to rule them."

"Wukandima," Maureen repeated, once, softly.

Tongue position noted. Word logged. Miles vipers logged, drawn from the laboratory's entries. A pale blue line ran across Maureen's vision:

[Miles viper. Tiny. Fast. Venom: dissolves.]

*Your first pets,* Tally observed in Maureen's head. It sounded smug.

*Guards,* Maureen told it silently.

*Your first guards then,* Tally said.

"You may take as long as you wish upon this task," Adrian said. "Return when you feel ready to continue."

Adrian smiled behind his glasses. "After all," he said aloud, "you aren't just setting off on a mission. You want to travel, don't you?" Maureen nodded, throat tight.

*Someone has just trusted you with something,* Tally said, soft in her mind.

*Then log that properly,* Maureen replied. Her thumb rested on the letter's wax seal.

* * *

Winnie came over smiling. "So, you're going out too? That's nice. It's been nearly two years since you came here. Time goes fast."

The blonde girl hugged Maureen gently. "Take care, Maureen."

"I will," Maureen said.

The hug was brisk and warm, smelling of crushed herbs and lye soap. Maureen's arms were slow to respond. Kind, Tally filed away.

*You're getting sentimental there,* Maureen told it silently.

*No, I am not. I am just being thorough,* Tally responded.

Winnie turned to Adrian. "Will the Olivia Family try any secret attacks against her?"

Adrian pushed his glasses back up his nose, then turned to Winnie. "Winnie, I am Maureen's mentor. A great many people in the Dark Forest know that." Winnie laughed in understanding.

Adrian sat down in his high-backed chair, by the bench. "It is good for her to travel, while she is still young. Not remain in the Dark Forest all her life like some of us." He chuckled at his own joke.

Winnie came around behind him, standing between the chair and the bench, and began rubbing his shoulders. Adrian laughed again. "Ah, Winnie, ever since I found you as a baby, you've only done that when you were a little girl. I've missed it."

"Oh, Adrian." The blonde woman was suddenly embarrassed. "I need to meditate. I'm so far from being a full Wizard yet. Unlike Mace . . ."

[Adrian: pupils contract. Pause 0.8 s.]

"Mace?"

Light flickered behind the glasses.

"What about him?"

Nothing.

Adrian turned to face Winnie, his expression suddenly serious and stern. "Winnie, you must become a full Wizard soon. It matters. Understand?"

Winnie looked puzzled for a moment, then her face became serious and she nodded.

Maureen stood among the racks for a moment, hearing everything. Suddenly the ground seemed to tilt beneath her feet just a little.

*Both to be filed in the Adrian: unknown folder.*

*That folder now has more data than the one on Lilith,* Tally said.

*Adrian is more of a puzzle than Lilith?*

*Yes, Maureen. Adrian is the one who said no for you.*

"Thank you for your time, Mentor. Winnie," she said bowing to each of them, then turned and walked out of the lab with the letter in her pocket, the token and the tube at her belt.
