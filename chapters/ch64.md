Maureen Olsen woke in the darkness, buried deep in a pile of blankets and heavy furs. She lay there for a moment, not moving, listening to the silence, feeling the chill of the room. The fire had died and only an ash grey heap remained. Her body was loose and heavy from long sleep. After eating and a long hot bath last night she must have slept straight through.

She sat up and washed her face in the cold water in the basin. The water bit into her skin, waking her up quickly. *How long was I asleep?* she asked Tally.

*Twenty-one hours,* Tally answered in its smug warm voice. [Sleep: 21h 04m]

*Are you sure?* she asked silently.

*I always count,* it answered. *And I counted every minute.*

*My days and nights are all reversed now then aren't they?* Maureen asked.

*You have been logged as nocturnal.* *Provisionally,* Tally added.

*Nocturnal sounds like an owl.*

*Owls keep better hours,* Tally told her.

Maureen walked over to the window. The glass was frosted over, and outside the sky was just a dull dark lid with a thin line of grey light along one edge. The black wrapped trunk leaned against the wall in the corner of her room.

She reached up and touched her brow and turned her thoughts inward. The silver threads were gone, fused into her third Rune Array. She could not tell where the individual threads left off and the array began. That was how her first and second Rune Arrays had formed too. She concentrated and felt the three arrays resonating with each other. They were doing more than just echoing each other though. They fitted together somehow. Like different parts that made up one thing.

[Rune Arrays: 3. Silver threads: indistinguishable in all three. Fit: observed. Mechanism: unknown.]

*You don't know why?* Maureen asked.

*I do not.* It sounded put out by the admission. *I have no data on that.*

*Keep watching then,* she told it silently.

*I never stop. I am always there.*

She turned the feeling over in her mind. It was like when Jing Sheng, her former self, would examine a specimen under her bench lamp in the white lab. She would turn the object slowly, without touching it, examining every angle. Her mind went to the silver sigil in the center of her palm. It pointed to a secret realm somewhere out in the world. She would have to explore there sometime. In a few days maybe, once her new strength settled.

* * *

Her wrist prickled as if some living thing crawled upon it. She willed the feeling away.

“Elevate,” she said.

The brand lifted from her skin, hanging suspended in the air.

Particles of dark energy collected around the sigil, a chill filling the bedroom as if the cellar door had been left open.

The flame in the tallow candle leaned towards the particles, drawn by their power.

The particles gathered together until they formed a half length silhouette of Adrian, his glasses reflecting the candlelight. Maureen bowed her head. “Mentor.”

“I see it has been a long time. How goes my task?”

“Well, Mentor. Umas’s revolt was put down. There is no opposition left. Here is an outline of what happened.”

She detailed the events that had led up to the battle, leaving out the part about Patton and the Sodara tree.

“I see. My friendship with Umas was long ago, but thank you for telling me.” Adrian’s translucent figure nodded.

Maureen smiled at him, her face relaxed and even slightly fond. She placed the lie on one side, to be examined later. Like a specimen preserved in a drawer, she could take it out and study it when she had more time.

“Mentor, may I ask how one becomes an Official Wizard?”

Adrian looked surprised at that. “Why, have you already become Third-Class, Olsen?”

“Not yet, Mentor, but I am close enough that I can’t help wondering how one advances. It would be helpful to know.”

*A brand projection will only ever carry your face and voice. Never your actual physical state or emotions.*

Tally added ‘rank’ to its list of withheld information, following Patton, the tree, the Seal and the stones.

*Thank you Tally.*

Adrian shook his head and laughed. Maureen could hear youthful overconfidence forgiven in that laugh. An old man looking at a girl who looked no older than sixteen.

The tallow candle was burning low on the small table. Its flame leaned sideways towards the black window. A cellar chill crept up her arm from the brand on her wrist. Adrian lifted a translucent finger.

“First, you need to condense three Spirit Rune Arrays and raise your body and spirit to the standard of an Official Wizard. Second, you need to take the Finlan Potion. This potion will unify your three arrays into one foundation array. This is very important.”

Adrian raised another finger.

“Thirdly, you will need an Official Wizard meditation method. The basic apprentice method will no longer work after Third-Class. Finally, you will need an innate spell. A spell that is fixed in your sea of consciousness and runs all the time. Preferably a defensive spell that is almost unbreakable or an offensive spell that can strike back in an instant.”

His calm flat voice filled the small room. Maureen’s mouth was dry as she stood still with the candle flame leaning in front of her.

[Requirements: 5. Source: stated by Adrian. Verified: none] Tally told her. [I cannot verify any of them at the moment. Please note this if you wish.]

*Yes Tally. Keep it as his word, nothing else for now.*

“Mentor, the potion, method and a spell model, could those be bought from you?”

“I have some spell models Olsen, the cheapest is five thousand magic stones. That is already my apprentice’s discount.” Adrian’s face showed no emotion.

[All columns: about 6000. Superior 37. Card 950]

*You would be left with around a thousand stones if you buy one. Please keep that number well away from your face.*

*Thank you Tally.*

Maureen nodded her understanding.

“The Finlan Potion and the Official Wizard meditation method only exist in the Dark Forest. These things don’t necessarily need to be purchased with magic stones. Many things depend on your own choices. I advise you return to the Dark Forest.”

“I know, Mentor,” Maureen said aloud.

Adrian’s face changed expression for a moment before he pushed up his glasses and started to speak.

“The Dark Forest, lately…” His face broke into a smile.

“Mentor?” she asked but he would not elaborate. They talked for a while longer before he ended the call.

The particles thinned until they were nothing, the brand flashed once before it sank back into her wrist. The window behind where Adrian had stood was just black glass. The flame from the candle straightened itself as the air in the room became still again.

* * *

For the next few days Maureen took the time to rest and get used to her new strength. She used the spare hours reading Old Farry's Wizardry Reactions and Natural Principles. A collection of blue data and diagrams played across her sight as she turned each spread of the thick copper bound book. *Nearly done now, Maureen,* Tally told her one morning as she sat reading in the study. *How near is nearly?* she silently asked Tally. *Six pages to go,* it replied smugly. The sun shone through the window warming the copper binding beneath her palm. Dust spun slowly in the light.

*You still need to prepare the suppression potion for Farry, it needs to go through Albert before the month is out,* Tally reminded her.

*I haven't forgotten,* she assured Tally silently.

There was a knock on the door and Old Hank bowed before entering.

"My lady, you have visitors. Your friend Mr Blake and another lady I do not recognise are waiting in the reception hall." Maureen blinked and the blue data disappeared leaving her study bright but plain. She carefully wrapped the book in its cloth and rose from her seat to head downstairs with Old Hank.

* * *

The perfume of rose and ambergris wafted up to greet her as soon as she stepped through the door. It was a familiar smell that had travelled through the reception hall's wood-smoke scented air to reach her before she could see the people. As Blake saw her he opened his mouth with his customary teasing line but then paused. He felt for her aura and his eyes widened. "Third-Class? You're Third-Class?" He grinned and sighed before smiling again. "Congratulations! Well done."

"Oh, it wasn't anything special. Just luck really," Maureen replied.

"Yes, luck certainly played a part," replied the unfamiliar woman who accompanied Blake. "I'm Bina, from the Blue Ring." She nodded her head fractionally to Maureen.

Bina sat straight backed and her every movement was performed without haste. Her dress though plain was made from rich fabrics.

Maureen allowed her surprise and respect to show at meeting a member of such a great order. The Dark Forest was easily the equal and possibly the superior of the Blue Ring. Bina's bearing matched the reputation of an ancient and noble family.

*Energy radiation detected. Beginning sample by sight and particle sense,* Tally informed her.

*Hold full read,* Maureen told it silently. *I'll do this later.*

Maureen noticed how Blake's eyes would return to Bina again and again. His hands were almost always moving.

*Gaze fixed on Bina, longing suspected. Unconfirmed. Log it?* Tally asked.

*Log it so.*

"Ms. Olsen, Bina here has come to ask you something," Blake said.

Maureen turned her attention to Bina and waited for her to speak without prompting.

"Has a wizard named Patton appeared in your fief?" Bina asked. "My younger brother hasn't spoken to me for months, and I can't reach him through my sorcery brand. Something is wrong. Is there any news of him?"

Bina's voice kept its even tone but Maureen saw the lady's fingers were pressing into the teacup, the knuckles white.

[Patton. Sister: Bina. Blue Ring. Match]

*Yes, I know that Tally. I know that,* Maureen told it silently.

Maureen's body became very still as she listened. Her hands lay loose in her lap. Her breathing was even as though she sat in quiet contemplation.

She frowned slightly, a look of puzzlement and regret crossing her face. "Patton? I'm sorry, Wizard Bina, I've never heard that name."
