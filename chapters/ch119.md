The door hung crazily by a single hinge after Maureen’s kick. The cold of the room poured through the doorway, rolling over her like a wave. The dust in the carved lintel above the door was mottled white by frost. The smell of wet stone rose from the hall, tinged beneath with a whiff of copper.

Every face in the hall was turned towards the newcomer. The Jadeleaf Ring apprentices were breathing her name, “Richard?”, their eyes full of hope and dread at the same time that her name was spoken. “Wizard Richard!” The Black Spirit men shuffled their feet as she entered, and moved to close ranks around their boss.

Maureen’s eyes swept the room, taking in every detail in seconds. Senge lay in a smear of his own blood where the green screen had broken. The 300 shattered magic stones glittered like coarse grit across the polished black marble floor. Joanna’s cheek still bore the five red finger marks where Maffie’s hand had landed.

The layout of the room flashed on Tally’s display. The Black Spirit men glowed a dull amber, the single source of power dressed in purple-red silk highlighted with a frost-white outline. The particle map was crystallising, freezing around that one figure who was so cold and dense. *I see him,* Maureen thought to Tally silently.

“Maffie,” Maureen said, in Richard’s deep, flat voice. “You’re Maffie, aren’t you.” The sentence barely rose at the end, as though she were checking the name against some mental list.

Maffie’s green eyes narrowed as he scanned her aura. A look of real surprise broke through his contemptuous sneer for just an instant. “A wizard? A full wizard?”

Ace slid in next to his boss, pointing a shaking finger at Maureen. “That’s him, Boss,” the underling gasped, hate thick in his throat. “That’s the one who did me over, he, he—”

Tally noted that Ace continued to favour his left leg, and walked to keep Maffie’s body between himself and the intruder at all times. *Smart for once,* Maureen thought silently. *Won’t last though,* Tally responded.

The boss of the Black Spirit Society cracked his knuckles, a sharp, loud sound like ice cracking across the surface of a pond. “So young, and already a wizard. Very nice. Pity you laid hands on my people.” He grinned, revealing tobacco-stained teeth. “I had men combing the city for you, and here you are. Come to save them the walk.”

Maureen thought a silent command: *Tally, scan please.*

The AI began building the readout in the corner of her vision. The scan was made by comparison to the Barok sample, and supplemented by what Tally could see of the frost embedded in the man’s skin.

[Maffie. Official Wizard. Elemental Phantom State, ~2-3 yrs, by inference. Str 10.2 / Agi ~10.5 / Con ~10.4 / Spirit 15.9 / Mana 15.9. Danger: medium.]

Tally’s voice was warm and impish in Maureen’s head, smug over how much data she had gathered. *Maffie is above Barok, but below you. I calculate he has been elementalised for two to three years. By inference.*

*Does that mean I’m stronger than him?* Maureen thought back.

*You’ve only just been promoted to Official Wizard, only weeks ago. That means that this man has spent decades climbing one ladder, while you have only just begun to climb another. Your ladder is different, and much steeper, which makes your time on it count for far more than his decades on his own.*

Maureen nodded, and the pale blue text faded from her vision. She could feel the heat of the Totem again, a banked furnace burning slowly beneath her breastbone. Her heart beat sixty-four times a minute. What she felt wasn’t fear, not really. It was more boredom.

Maffie kept talking. He seemed to think the plain young wizard in front of him was a small, insignificant thing that could be dismissed easily. “Crawled in here to die, have you, boy?” he asked.

Maureen didn’t answer. She simply shook her head. The gesture was like turning down a second helping of food. Not now, thanks.

Then she started to walk forward. Black-red flame unfurled around her, pulling itself out of thin air like a cloak. Her boots left the ground, and she rose up, hanging a hand’s span above the floor.

Her heat met his cold. Wherever Maureen’s shadow fell, the frost melted off the floor. Steam curled up, and the smell of scorched dust and snowmelt filled the room.

* * *

Maureen was about to move again when a figure stepped between her and Maffie. It was Joanna. She faced Maffie, standing with her back to Maureen. She didn’t look around at Maureen.

Maureen glanced down at the white robe, noting its green-leaf emblem, then up at the shoulders, which were shaking so hard the cloth quivered with each movement. A strand of dark hair clung to the blood at the corner of Joanna’s mouth.

*Third-Class,* Tally said in Maureen’s head. *Heart rate one-fifty. No feint observed.*

*I know.*

*She will die for this.*

*She knows that. That’s what makes it mean something.*

“Wizard Richard,” Joanna said, her voice thin as a reed. “Go.” She took a breath, and her voice grew stronger with every word. “Please. I’m glad you came back. Truly, I am. But go. You’re young. You’ll get stronger. Come back for him when you can kill him. Settle it for today. Settle it . . . for all of us.” She shook harder, but her voice never faltered. “I’m just an apprentice, but I can take one of his blows. That’s your chance. Run, Wizard Richard, and run far.”

Maureen’s eyebrow lifted. A sensation she had never felt before settled under her ribs. It was tight and unfamiliar, yet she didn’t try to name it. It wasn’t important enough to worry over.

Tally’s voice interrupted Maureen’s thoughts. *The seed of Purgatory Fire rests quietly against Joanna’s breastbone. It has no more instructions from you. If you want, you could set it alight with a thought. Joanna knows you can do so. And there she stands.*

*Leave it dark.*

Maffie looked at Joanna, then back at Maureen. “So that’s what dragged you back here. A little apprentice who calls you ‘Wizard’? How soft, Richard.” Maffie laughed. “Soft men don’t live long in Emilia.”

“Let me guess,” he said, his laughter fading. “One of you holds the door while the other runs? How sweet. And stupid. Neither of you is leaving this hall today.”

The men of the Black Spirit Society laughed on cue. Christine was pressed up against the wall, one small hand clamped over her mouth. The apprentices who were dragging Senge froze halfway to the side archway.

*Air thickens above Maffie,* Tally warned Maureen. She began painting an image of ice particles in the air being drawn up into a spiral beneath the wooden beams. *This is the same working that destroyed Senge’s screen. . . . Timer pinned.*

* * *

“Shattered Ice Whirlwind!” Maffie roared. The air over his head crackled like a river breaking up in the spring.

The fragments knitted together in midair, forming an icy dragon composed of sharp shards that wound its way under the ceiling beams. The cold came before it did. Frost streaked through the carvings, turning them a brilliant white, and Maureen’s breath suddenly formed a cloud in front of her face.

[Ice. Cone. 2 s.]

*Maureen, get your fire in close and put your hand on Joanna’s shoulder.*

*Joanna will hit me if I do that.*

*She’ll try.*

“Wizard Richard!”

Joanna’s face turned white at the sight of the approaching dragon, but then her expression hardened with determination. She shifted her weight forward, preparing to throw herself in the path of the diving beast.

Maureen’s hand slid over Joanna’s shoulder from behind, landing lightly there. It was as though the hand weighed nothing at all.

Joanna jerked at the unexpected touch. Her elbow half-cocked back, ready to slam into the person behind her, but Maureen didn’t flinch. She didn’t even lift her hand away from Joanna’s shoulder.

Maureen glanced once at the diving dragon, as if it were no more than an errant snowflake, before turning back to Joanna. Then, keeping her voice level and quiet, she said, “You don’t need to take his blow, Joanna.” The corner of her mouth lifted in what might have been a smile. “It’s fine. Don’t worry about it.”

The dragon dove.

The hall exploded in white.

Shards and spray roared through the centre of the room, blinding everyone. Everyone staggered back, stumbling over themselves or each other as they scrambled to get away. The Jadeleaf Ring apprentices screamed Joanna’s name. Christine stood with her mouth open, her face pale as chalk. Next to the arch, Senge collapsed with one arm against the wall, coughing up a crimson flower on his chest.

Inside the roar of the explosion, the dome was already up.

It was thin, almost translucent, and the colour of burning blood. Purgatory Fire licked out in a perfect sphere around both of them. [Dome: −2. Mana 12 → 10.] The ice crashed against the fire, sizzling into steam the moment it touched the surface. Maureen kept her hand where it was.

Small flames danced over the surface of the dome, black and red, shimmering with a wet light that shifted and flowed as they consumed the ice. The frost dissolved against them without a sound. Beneath Maureen’s palm, Joanna’s shoulder was rigid as a plank.

The whiteness began to thin, then clear.

The dome was still standing in the middle of the hall, the two of them unhurt inside. For a long moment, no one in the room breathed.

Joanna twisted her head around, her mouth still hanging open, and found herself staring into Maureen’s quiet, ordinary, borrowed face.

“I told you it would be fine,” said Maureen, taking her hand back.

*Hand stayed a beat longer than needed,* Tally noted.

*Log it as maintenance,* Maureen thought.

*I’ve logged it as something,* Tally replied smugly.

The Black Spirit men were all staring at the dome as if it had grown sharp teeth. The Ring apprentices exhaled as one, the sound like a sail suddenly going slack. Then Tally’s new lens caught something. A knot of pale structure tightening in empty air, right beside Maureen’s heart. Painted gold on her display. Hidden cast. Left side. Heart height. One second.

A spike of pale blue ice flickered out from nothingness, and shrieked toward Maureen’s chest.

Maffie cackled. “Didn’t see that one, did you, Richard? Everyone relaxes after they block the Whirlwind. Finished.”

Maureen’s eyes flared to life. Pale red. A vertical slit flickered open in each pupil, like a cat’s.

Mirror Pupil. One of Maureen’s own innate spells.

Through the Mirror Pupil, the hall was washed in a pale red light. The ice spike stopped being a weapon and became a collection of lines and angles. Maureen could watch the spell slow in midair, the blue crystal turning into an empty, uncoloured white.

>Crystallisation: −2.5.

>Est. 16.5°.

>Mana 10 → 7.5.

*Plain crystal now,* Tally said, sounding a little pleased with herself. *Not a spell any more.*

The spike fell toward the ground and shattered on the marble floor. It was like someone had knocked a glass off a table. Maffie’s cackle choked to a stop halfway through a syllable. “How— how could—”

Maureen turned her slit red eyes toward Maffie. “What a boring trick.”

*Been saving that one for years, I bet,* Tally said.

*Then it had a long life,* Maureen thought back. All the while, tiny bits of crystal glittered at Maureen’s feet. The pieces of the shattered spell were spread across the marble, joining the shards of the stones she’d smashed. Her pulse hadn’t shifted by a single beat.

* * *

The muscles in her legs contracted, drew tight as steel cable, then released.

Maureen flickered forward in a few blurs of motion. She covered the length of the hall between herself and Maffie in half a second, and was standing at his side.

The marble floor cracked where she’d pushed off to throw herself forward. A ribbon of flame hung in the air, marking the path she’d taken. Maffie’s green eyes widened enough that she could see white all around the irises.

“Damn you!” There was a grinding clatter as ice formed over Maffie’s body. Layer after layer of armour rose from the floor, each one sliding up into the next like nesting bowls. The first layer was half an inch thick, the next an inch, the third layer an inch and a half.

Tally measured their densities, giving their relative thickness and strength. *High confidence prediction: First layer destroyed with one blow. Second layer destroyed with one blow. Third layer cracks, holds.*

*Go through the crack.*

“Armour?” Maureen’s mouth curved in a faint smile. “You think that helps? You’re just thrashing.” Her right arm flexed, and black-red flame engulfed her fist up to the elbow.

Maureen’s fist crashed into the first layer of armour. It split and burst with a single blow. Maffie jerked backwards, his face becoming a greyish white.

 Shards bounced off the pillars, sounding like coins tossed on the floor. The smell of scorched silk rose from Maffie’s body, mixing with the cold air.

The second punch hit the armour with a dull thump. It held for half a breath, then the middle layer collapsed into slush and falling grit. A trail of blood ran from the corner of Maffie’s mouth.

The third punch hit with enough force to drive a web of cracks through the thickest layer of ice, but it held. Maffie coughed, spraying blood in a fine mist from his lips. He laughed, his voice hoarse with relief. “Held! It held—!”

Maureen’s flaming hand slipped into the crack created by her last punch. The ruined armour gave way under the force of her attack, collapsing like wet paper. Her fingers closed around Maffie’s throat.

*Three for three,* Tally said, making no pretence of being modest about the achievement. There was a quiet tick as she updated the display.

[Fists: ~−1. Mana 7.5 → 6.5.]

Maureen raised Maffie off his feet. His heels scraped across the marble, leaving a frosted double trail behind him. The frost on his collar melted, dripping onto her flame before evaporating. Maffie choked, his face swelling red.

“Don’t... mercy...” He managed to force out the words, “I’ll give... spare me...”

His green eyes clung to hers, pleading with her to be the kind of person who would say yes.

*I see no tells,* Tally said quietly. Of course, that wasn’t proof.

Maureen smiled faintly, then closed her hand.

There was a dry snap, and Maffie’s head lolled sideways.

Black-red fire flowed from Maureen’s arm, spreading over Maffie’s body. Within a few heartbeats, his flesh was consumed by the flames, bones and silk included. When they burned away, there was nothing left but an oval of scorched marble and the smells of hot stone and ash. Now that its creator was gone, the frost began to melt from the carvings, dripping down to join the puddles.

*Logged,* Tally said.

The cold lifted off the hall as if someone had shut a window. There was a sound like ticks in the silence, the last of the melting frost hitting the floor.

* * *

No one moved. All sound came from the dripping. The men from the Black Spirit Society stood where they were, weapons forgotten in their hands, their faces slack. Ace was cowering against a pillar, trying to press himself into the stone with his back, his eyes wide in terror.

Maureen sank back to the floor, and Joanna stared at her, her hand pressed against her slapped cheek. Christine was still white and flat against the wall. Senge, alive, watched from the arch, blood trickling from his chin.

*Logged,* Tally said of Joanna’s action. *Twice now in your life someone has stepped between you and danger. First Adrian. And now Joanna.*

*She thought I was the weaker one.*

*Yes, that is why it counts.*

Maureen watched Joanna, who was staring at where Maffie had been standing. Tally gave Joanna’s action a column all its own.

Maureen flexed her hand, the one that had been resting on Joanna’s shoulder. Her palm remembered the trembling.

*Maffie,* Tally said, adding him to Maureen’s wizard file as the second labelled Official sample: Barok, Soros (estimate), Maffie. *This file is getting crowded with dead men.* Maureen turned her slit eyes toward the pillar, where Ace was shaking, then toward the men behind him. Black-red flame still smouldered on her knuckles.
