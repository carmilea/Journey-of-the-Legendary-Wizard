She rode back to Conta Manor with Clair’s thanks still sounding in her ears and the sulphurous taint of the Silver Soul estate still lingering in the wool of her coat. The knowledge Clair had given her sat in her chest like a counted coin. Claude was hiring outside wizards. In great number and paying well for the trip below. A man who might want Denise dead was bringing a crowd with him.

*You going to tell her?* Tally asked.

*She pays people to know things,* Maureen replied silently. *She probably knows already.*

*Then the only question is what she does about it.*

Maureen’s hand rested on the dull grey pouch at her waist, where Edward lay folded beside Freya’s sword. The leather was cold, even through her glove. [Edward (when stowed): core intact] blinked once from Tally before going quiet again.

The gate guard caught sight of the rider and bellowed toward the manor house. It wasn’t long before the steward stumbled out, buttoning up his coat, bowing over and over as he approached Wizard Richard.

“Wizard Richard, honoured, so honoured,” the steward said. Each word puffed out as a white cloud. He smiled widely, showing his gums. “The young mistress asked after you the moment you—” His eyes stopped at her collar, and didn’t go any higher.

*Four bows,* Tally noted. *He was told something about you while we were gone. Probably the garden and the craters.*

*I would rather have stewards fear me than like me,* Maureen replied silently.

Denise met her in the guest corridor.

For one breath, as she took in Richard’s face, Maureen saw her shoulders drop. Then her expression became hard and impenetrable, her fingers touching her sleeve.

*Relief?* Maureen asked silently.

*Relief for exactly one second, then counting. She counts the same way you do.*

During the quiet days that followed, Maureen brewed behind her locked door.

Official-grade healing and recovery draughts, brewed from Gregory’s pharmacology shelves and Bina’s alchemy, filled Maureen’s table. Tally held the finished lattice through the Orb as Maureen built the next.

Denise was a presence outside the door the entire time. Boots thudded on the stairs, ledgers were tucked under her arm, a courier’s horse steamed in the yard, doors were shut with more force than necessary.

“Wizard Richard,” Denise finally said through the closed door. “We can set out.”

The door creaked open. Maureen looked at Denise, who stood very still in the middle of the corridor.

“Good,” said Maureen in Richard’s flat voice.

It was time to go down to the Underworld.

* * *

The grounds were packed, all the way back to the hedges. Horned beasts tossed their heads in harness. Frozen mud cracked beneath their cloven hooves, and their breath hung in the air like steam. Above it all flew the Oserroba banners, stiff as boards in the frosty air.

Beside the family’s own wizards stood a crowd of hired help, auras of every colour and strength. Some of these were Claude’s men. The rest were Denise’s. These past weeks, she had been doing some hiring too—and paying more than her uncle for the privilege.

[Officials: mostly Soros band. Near Maffie: 4. Liquefied band: 2, Claude and Jackman. Labels: hearsay.]

Tally rolled the crowd against her samples by density.

They sorted according to where they stood, and whose eyes they followed. Claude’s hires clustered by his carriage; Denise’s by hers. At medium confidence, Tally forecast that Denise’s half were the better paid and more frightened.

*Fear is useful,* thought Maureen silently.

*For whom?* asked Tally.

The massed auras rubbed together, wool upon wool. Static crawled at the nape of Maureen’s neck. She kept her own field pressed small inside her chest. From the outside, Richard was an apprentice.

“Those are friends of mine,” said Denise, pointing out three of her hires: Rita, Glorette, and Rachel. All three women lifted their hands to Denise across the sea of wizards. For that one moment, Denise’s mouth softened.

Claude’s carriage waited at the head of the column, its curtains drawn. Behind the glass, still as a statue, was Jackman’s profile, with its hooked nose and bright green eyes, catching the grey light.

After a brief scramble over order and outriders, the column began to roll. Denise’s carriage followed behind Claude’s.

“Why hire your own, Denise?” Maureen asked from inside the cab.

Denise blinked, looking slightly lost for a moment. “Safety, I suppose. My uncle hired so many wizards, I had to be careful.” Her fingers drifted to her sleeve and remained there.

The carriage wheels ground over the frozen ruts. The fringe on the curtains swayed back and forth. Denise’s breath fogged up the glass as she stared out the window at the carriage rolling ahead.

“As for my uncle and his friend…” she said, watching Claude’s carriage, where he rode with his friend Jackman. “I keep feeling this mission might be—” She let out a long breath and didn’t finish her sentence.

Tally considered Denise’s words next to the perfect-fit intel and Clair’s warning. *I still can’t name the target, Maureen. All I know is that the bait fits too perfectly. Just like Gus’s job board had.*

Maureen smiled at that, glancing at Claude’s carriage for a moment before she slowly lowered the curtain to cover the window.

*You’re smiling,* Tally said. *It’s not reassuring.*

*It’s not meant to be.*

* * *

The light died in stages as they descended. First it was grey, then brown, until eventually it was only the yellow of their lamps. The rock walls wept with cold condensation, and the column’s swinging lights cast dancing shadows over every ledge and niche.

Everyone in the column was an Official Wizard or higher, and still the going was difficult. High-energy creatures and stranger beasts leapt from the side clefts nearly every day, and each fight cost blood. Afterward, the camp smelled of scorched hide, burnt hair, and blood on stone. Wizards tended to wounds by lantern-light while somewhere out of sight someone retched.

Anje sat against the rock, blood crusted in his red hair. He explained the situation to anyone who would listen.

“Underworld’s its own world underneath the earth. Only way down’s an abyss like this one. And they’re full of things.”

Tally filed his account next to the legends in the Oserroba archive. Consistent. One voice. Through her eyes, Tally could see the claw jutting from Anje’s belly. Long as a forearm, driven up under the ribs. Bleeding slowly. His pulse was steady. Nothing vital hit.

In the middle of his sentence, Maureen reached over and grabbed the broken talon. With a quick yank, she pulled it free. There was a wet sound, and Anje screamed, rolling on the stone and clutching at his belly.

The talon lay in her hand, hooked and black, still warm from his body. A yellowish fluid wept from where it had pierced his flesh, smoking slightly where it dripped onto the stone. It smelled of hot copper.

His scream echoed down the throat of rock, returning to them thinner than before. Far below them, claws answered, scraping against stone.

Maureen closed her palm over the talon. There was a flicker of deep-red light as a pinched Blast Fireball, a fist of ordinary fire, burned the talon to nothing.

[Mana: 20.1 → 19.8]

Tally noted the colour. Deep red, no black in the fire. Richard still had nothing but Rank Zero fire for Oserroba eyes to see.

*Why do you think I pinched it?* Maureen asked silently.

“Up, Anje. You’re mostly mended. Drink this tonight and you’ll be whole by morning.” She held out a small vial of purple draught.

“Th-thank you, Wizard… Richard,” Anje said in two parts, reaching for the vial with grey hands. He cradled it in his palm and staggered away, leaving Maureen behind. She was already heading towards the next wounded man.

She made no secret of her trade. At each camp, she would set out her stock of draughts on a flat rock that served as a makeshift bench, and hawk her wares openly, playing the part of a travelling apothecary. The glassware gleamed in the light of swinging lamps, purple and amber and pale green against the black rock. A line of bloodied wizards would form, and stone cards would click down beside the glassware one after another.

Tally ran the transactions. She priced each vial against the buyer’s wound, checking the pulse at his throat and how heavily his pouch hung at his belt. In a corner of the display, she kept a running total of how many cards had been exchanged. Richard’s prices were far above what any shop would charge, but nobody haggled for long. A wizard with half his arm missing was not in much position to bargain.

One limped up to the bench, hand pressed against his thigh. Tally flagged him as he approached, noting no swelling in the leg and a smooth gait. One of the Jay tricks from back on the Fasol road. Maureen thought, *Double it, then.*

“Twice the price,” Richard said without expression. The wizard opened his mouth, caught the apothecary’s unblinking stare, and paid. Tally logged the transaction in her ledger as a happy day in accounting.

One or two more wizards died on their way down into the abyss. The rest made do with butchering their kills and fighting over hides, horns, and glands. The Official Wizards were in high spirits, the column’s trek yielding an embarrassment of riches for magical ingredients. By the time of the final fight on their descent into the bowels of the world, the pile of exchanged cards had grown to dozens.

[Low-grade cards: +27.]

[Magic stones, all columns: ~8,225 → ~35,000.]

[Pouch: +materials 6.]

Tally added, *And our stock of healing supplies is starting to run low.*

One payment was made with a sphere the size of a plum. It glowed with an inner light that shimmered in silver. The air around the sphere bent and blurred like the air above a fire. Alone at her bench, Maureen turned it over in her fingers, smiling.

“This really is a good opportunity for me.”

Through the Mirror Pupil lens, the air around the sphere warped into visible lines. There was no plate match.

*No data,* Tally reported. *I think it’s looking at us. At everything in this room. Please keep it in the pouch, off your skin.*

Maureen dropped the sphere into the pouch. For a moment she felt as though someone was watching her, then the feeling was gone. The line in Tally’s display for the pouch ticked up in silence.

* * *

For several days after that, nothing came at them. Not a single high-energy creature, not even any ordinary beasts. Around the fires, the wizards began to talk about it in lowered voices.

[Sources beyond column: 0. Day 3.] Tally reported, logging it as abnormal. *Permission to send a particle ping?* Maureen refused silently. *Jackman would feel it.*

*As you wish. I will do as I am told,* Tally said. *If I cannot send a ping, I will listen harder.*

The lower abyss had fallen silent. The water was dripping. The column’s boots were scuffling on the rock, their harnesses jingling. Once there was a snort from some beast. Otherwise, nothing.

Maureen kept her shoulders still. Stillness was what she did.

And finally, they arrived at the entrance, at the bottom of a vast abyss.

Claude rose into the air above the column, his tailcoat stirring. His voice carried easily to the back. “No creatures for days? That’s wrong, very wrong. We’re going in. Everyone watch yourselves.”

Sensible warning. Pulse steady. That proved nothing.

*He isn’t the one who is afraid of creatures,* Maureen thought silently.

Without any trouble, they entered the Underworld.

It was similar to the surface, with mountains and rivers, but there was an infinite dark sky above and no stars. Black vegetation crouched like great shadows. A few plants glowed faintly, like coals under ash. The cold air tasted of wet slate.

The night was dark, but to her eyes, to the eyes the rite had rebuilt for her, the darkness was only dusk. The hour when the last light spread thin across the sky and shadows lost their edges.

The easy arrival seemed to make some of the greener wizards relax. Voices rose in volume, someone laughed, and hands crept out from the sleeves and away from the wands.

A young wizard clad in a grey hood stopped before a wall of rock. A tiny glint of green shone from a crack in the stone. “Oh? What’s this?”

The glint lay deep within the black stone, the colour of pond weed beneath ice. It was pretty, the only bit of colour on the entire rock face.

[Source: none.]

Maureen’s eyes slid right over it. Tally’s all-around particle sense reported nothing.

The grey-hooded wizard ran a finger over the glint. Nothing happened. She murmured a quiet chant and cast a Rank Zero detection spell. Then she waited for the spell to report back what it found.

*Light . . . no density,* Tally said, her voice entering Maureen’s head half a beat late. *There’s nothing there where something should be.*

The green glint flared into a huge, brilliant eye.

Something rose from the ground, a leopard with dark grey fur. It stood as tall as a carriage, and white-hot flames poured from its entire body.

[Source: HIGH. Shadow-masked until flare.]

Tally warned, but by then Maureen’s shoulders had already snapped tight and her body turned before her mind caught up.

Fear filled the grey-hooded wizard’s eyes. The leopard struck once, and half of her was gone.

White heat washed over Maureen’s face. The scent of burned wool and cooked flesh flooded her nostrils. The grey-hooded wizard’s scream rose thin and high, then broke off abruptly.

The others had only heard the scream. When they turned to see what had caused it, all that remained was half of a body lying on the stones, blood running away from it in a thin stream. There was no sign of the flame or the shape that had been wrapped in it. The density reading dropped back down to zero.

Wizards spun about, spells half-raised, as they tried to make sense of the empty rock around them. Someone retched. Claude hung motionless in the air above the group, while Denise’s hand stole up to her sleeve.

Tally’s voice sounded silently in Maureen’s head. *I was late.*

*You were. I was faster. Or my blood was.*

“Damn it,” Maureen muttered, her voice sliding up toward her normal pitch.

*Drop it lower.*

Richard’s flat voice repeated the profanity.

Tally’s silent readout continued, filling in details even as Maureen processed what she could see. *This is one predator’s ground. That’s why nothing else lives here.* A new rule appeared on Maureen’s display. [Shadow-masked sources: invisible to particle sense until flare.] Maureen scanned the terrain, counting every shadow the creature could be hiding inside.
