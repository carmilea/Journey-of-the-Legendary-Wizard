Maureen crouched down at the end of the blood trail. Here, where the dark elf had gone, the blood stood out on the fallen leaves in beaded drops of dark purple, almost pretty despite its setting. Beneath the smell of grenade smoke, there was a faint scent from the blood, a clean odour like fallen blossoms.

It was the same blood she'd drawn into the test tube in her belt kit minutes ago. Freya's blood. She touched the kit through her cloak, reassuring herself that it was still there, that the glass was still whole. Then she dusted off her gloves and stood up.

All around her the clearing told of what had happened. Pine trunks lay snapped like kindling wood. The pine needles, singed by fire and explosions, curled up in black skeletons. The crater made by the grenade still ticked softly as the iron cooled.

Nothing moved in the clearing beneath the flat lid of pewter grey cloud that stretched across the sky.

The black cloth bag, Maureen thought. The one Augurit's crow gave to every apprentice when they mustered in the Dark Forest. Inside was a rough map - a few ridges scratched onto the paper, leaving most of the sheet blank. Alone, the map was next to useless. But Tally had a saying: One map is a rumour. Fourteen maps make a survey.

Over the last few days, Maureen had unfolded each map she stripped off the bodies of the Hader, the Blue Ring, the White Bone, the Mist Labyrinth. Each time, she held the map before her eyes so Tally could see it, and keep it.

[Task complete. Maps stitched: 14. Send to memory?] it showed her. Maureen could almost hear the smug satisfaction in its impish voice. *Yes,* she told it silently. *Send.*

There was a pressure behind her eyes. A flicker of pale blue. Then the entire realm lay within her head.

The ridges folded away from the centre of her vision until they reached the horizon. At their centre, where she stood, the valley was shaped like the cupped hand of a giant. To the east, the valley was dark with forest. Dense. Thick with trees and overgrowth. And there, on her display, was every scrawl made by a dead stranger, reconciled into one line of pale blue.

She still recognised the hands that had drawn it - the neat hatching of a Blue Ring mapmaker, a Hader stream scratched out and redrawn, and even the smear of blood that one of them had left. Tally had first filed that as ink, then quietly corrected itself.

Tally lit up the forest on her display - deep woods. Fewer paths drawn on the paper. Fewer people.

*That is my road,* she said silently.

*I know. I drew it,* Tally replied.

Maureen made her decision. She would head east. Out there, the trees grew thick and the paths were few. No one came this way. Whatever Jack did, there would be fewer witnesses.

Jack's cheek was lagging again, Tally told her. She raised two fingers to press the flesh back into place, feeling the dull ache beneath her skin as the Mimicry did its work. There. Better. The face of a man no one would trust. Maureen settled the bandolier of grenades across her chest, then began walking east at an easy pace.

She didn't bother trying to hide her trail.

* * *

The branches cracked in the wind as five apprentices came crashing down the slope. Their robes were torn, their faces white. They ran from Jack, not knowing that she had looped around them.

From the shadow of a tree trunk, Jack's Cross Sword swung free, taking the first through the neck. He collapsed without a sound, blood spurting from the wound in a dark fountain over the pine needles.

The remaining four skidded to a stop, sending up puffs of the thick needles. One dropped immediately to his knees. They smelled of sweat and of the wool that had been singed on their bodies by Jack's fireballs an hour earlier.

"Mercy, Jack, Jack, please! We didn't know it was you. You stood right in front of us, but we couldn't see you. Let us go, please, let us..."

Tally read out the glowing numerals on the backs of their raised hands. [3, 2, 2, 4]. The halos above their heads sputtered like candles burning down to their last threads. > All spent. Blue-green, reaching for a potion.

The one in the Blue Ring blue-green twisted around and shouted, "Mercy!" to the trees behind them, as if their hunter could only be coming from that direction. His free hand crept toward his belt.

Dark-red fireballs exploded from the trees behind them—from exactly where she had stepped back into cover. The fireballs blew two apprentices apart, leaving only pieces and drifting black ash.

The heat stung Maureen's cheeks. The remaining pair stared at the chain of red blisters that rose along their forearms. Their halos burned out, sputtering like wicks in water, and their mouths moved without sound.

[Mana: 7.6 → 5.4.]

*Those halos wouldn't stop a sneeze,* Tally said. *Your tongue, Maureen. Back and low for the first word of the Ancient Andas.*

"O endless darkness," Jack said in her hoarse rasp, "heed my summons. With the power of the wind, give forth the sigh of the dead."

The Undead Wind Arrows, particles of darkness churning like tornadoes, blew through both their hearts.

The arrows made a sound like breath being let out in an empty room. The two bodies folded over their knees. They did not move again. Grey ash settled on them like snow.

She stepped out of the trees. She looked down at the bodies. Then she shook her head once, letting out a long, slow breath. That was all the pity she would spend on them. There was a faint tugging in her hand, like a fish biting on each total. She accepted the two smallest and denied the rest. The larger, unclaimed totals began to unravel. They became pale wisps that drifted away to the northeast.

[Points: 12.]

*Right number of points,* Tally told her. *Jack had a reputation and a modest score.*

*Good plan.*

*Our plan.*

The scent of resin and burnt flesh mingled in the still air. Jack the Ripper was a name the realm now feared. Maureen noted that fact, as she would the colour change in a reagent.

In the lull before she moved again, Tally projected a readout for Maureen:

[Strength 9.6. Agility 9.7. Constitution 9.8.]

Maureen saw the lines of numbers set against the standard for inheritance. The body had met the standard on all three lines. So Tally's initial guess of nine percent had been accurate. Spirit and Mana were still a mountain to climb.

Maureen was the first to speak, though it was only in her head. *All this slaughterhouse has done is made me healthy.*

*Those are your words,* Tally said. *You said that on the day we arrived.* Tally's tone was pleased with itself.

 The mask Silaheryikan muffled only her own aura, so her ability to sense the others' auras was unaffected.

There was motion in the stoppered tube resting in her belt kit. By sight alone, it looked as if the purple sediment had settled against one side of the glass, as if the tube had been leaned over.

*West-south-west,* Tally said. *Second time today.*

The scent of blossoms came down a back-draught wind.

Maureen glanced off to one side, frowning at the empty trees.

"Still not coming out?"

*Freya is healing.*

*Yes, but she's also waiting. You've shown her an open back three times now. She's taken none of the opportunities.*

*Then she's careful.*

*Then she's dangerous.*

Jack's borrowed mouth curved in a smile. Maureen's eyes did not join in the expression.

"Patient little thing," she murmured in Jack's hoarse rasp. The smile stayed cold.

* * *

Maureen was just ready to move on when a familiar shape caught her eye in the leaf litter, and she stopped walking before she knew she'd decided to stop.

It was Anthony. He lay on his side. His bright golden hair was dull and dark, clotted with blood. His white shirt was stained grey, and the golden thread in its cuffs had turned brown with wetness.

His breathing was so shallow that Tally had to count it by the tip of a fern that was trembling next to his mouth from the movement of air. [Breaths: 6 / min. Falling.] [Attacker: no data.]

Maureen felt her throat tighten. She could hear him talking to her over a dining table in the Dark Forest, his voice earnest as he leaned across the bread, "We came out together. We're together."

There was nobody else in the hollow under the old oaks. Anthony's wounds had come at him from different directions. Whoever had given them to him had left him for dead and moved on.

She knelt down, the cold of the moss soaking through her trousers to her knees. Jack's black-gloved hands moved automatically, the way Maureen's own would have, checking the pulse at the throat. The fingers were quick and sure, having done this before.

"Ran into me, did you?" she asked in the hoarse rasp, speaking to a man who could not hear and keeping her voice low. "Let's call it fate. You're a lucky one."

She rested her hand on Anthony's chest, feeling the faintest of movements there. Her fingers went first to the full-wizard healing potions that were still in her inner fold, but they did not pause there. They moved down to her own Recovery in the belt kit.

*That is your tube, Maureen. Your stopper. He saw you use your potions on the Fasol road.*

She said silently to it, *Let him guess.*

*That is not like you, Maureen Olsen,* Tally answered.

*It is like me today.*

She tilted Anthony's head back slightly and dripped the misty green potion between his teeth. She pressed her thumb firmly against his throat until she felt him swallow. The smell of grass and metal rose between them, mingling with the heavy, cloying scent of blood and wet earth. Colour began to return to Anthony's lips. The fern resting near his mouth began to move properly again.

[Breaths: 11 / min. Rising.] [Belt stock: Recovery −1.]

*Logged,* Tally said. *Under 'kind'.*

Maureen stood the empty test tube upright in the deep moss beside Anthony's hand. It would be impossible for him to miss when he regained consciousness. Then she walked away in another direction.

As Maureen reached the trees, Tally flagged a sound behind her. A cough followed by a groan. She did not turn around. Ferns brushed against her singed cloak. It was the only thing she had done in the trial for nothing, and she continued walking, her eyes fixed straight ahead.

* * *

The smell of blood filled the air, thick and coppery between the white birches. She walked along until she found what was making it.

An apprentice's body was sprawled on the ground. Its mouth stretched wide open, its eyes unnaturally large. The pale skin was covered all over in fine cracks, as if the corpse was a dried riverbed. Maureen touched it with the point of her Cross Sword, and it slumped into grit. A figure made of sand.

"Same again," she said under her breath as she stepped back from the pile of pale grit. "Worse."

*Yes,* Tally agreed. *Worse than the first one. That body had at least kept its shape against your blade for a moment. This one didn't even do that.*

[Air: abnormal.]

What was wrong with the air? Compared to what she'd been breathing since arriving, it seemed sweeter and heavier. Then:

[Particle flow: drifting NE. Reversed against your Silver Light pull. By your sense.]

Maureen felt it herself. Particles were moving past her skin in the wrong direction. She could not feel any wind, but just below her still surface there was an invisible current flowing against the grain.

Tally continued. *Same bearing as every refused wisp since the first. All things that die here go northeast.*

She stared down at the pile of grit, absently touched the bandolier across her chest, and came to a decision.

*Six in hand,* Tally said. *Move five?*

Maureen nodded, reached back over her shoulder, and pulled five of the shells from the padded pack on her back.

[Grenades: hand 11 / pack 54.]

The shells were cold under her fingers, and heavier than they looked. They were plain iron eggs. One by one, Maureen clicked each grenade into place in its own padded loop.

Maureen's knight's legs carried her northeast at a run, and did not stop as the light grew grey and failed.

At a brief halt in the dark, Tally compared the shade of the wraith, a curse model from Gregory's library spreads, and the bronze book's curses column, to the blood in Maureen's vial. 64% match. With Freya's own blood bound into it . . . 88%. Not tested.

*It will be,* Maureen thought silently.

*On her, yes,* Tally agreed, then added, *I didn't mean to say that so coldly.*

The tube containing Freya's blood rested against Maureen's hip beneath her cloak all night. Its sediment leaned toward some point behind her in the darkness.

* * *

Nearly a day later she crested another ridge and started down into the valley the map had shown as a cupped hand. It was just like that, a bowl carved out of the land, filled with a mottled grey mixture of scree and dry grass. At its heart stood a white wall of fog, motionless in the air. Maureen could hear the sound of her boots on the scree growing duller, more muffled, as she approached.

Her spine stiffened a moment before she knew why. Too much power in one place, too many strong particles, pressing against her skin like the heat that poured out when you opened the door to a furnace. There were apprentices scattered around the rim of the bowl like dogs, perched on rocks, watching one another with wary eyes. No fights were going on yet. Everyone was waiting, studying each other and the fog in turns.

Tally's display popped into life in front of her eyes, a line of text drawn over the rim of the valley: [Strong apprentices: 9, by calibration. 4 above Frey's column.]

*You aren't the only one who's noticed,* Tally said.

*Noticed what?* Maureen asked silently.

*Exactly.*

Maureen stared at the unmoving fog. What was inside? *Tally, can you analyse the air here, cross-reference it with the crumbling corpses?*

*Task created. I will need more dead to be certain.*

*There'll be more dead.*

The air tasted like boiling sugar. A Hader girl in white robes kept wiping her lips with the back of her hand. A youth from the order of the Blue Ring had not taken his eyes off of Jack since Maureen had crested the ridge.

Then Maureen felt something else. A new look settling upon her.

Slow and appraising.

She followed the feeling to a tall young man, perched on a flat rock.

He was handsome in an unusual way, his chestnut hair a glossy mane, his cheeks sunken in, his eyes narrow as he measured her, as a butcher would measure a carcass before cutting it up. A bone pin was fastened just above his collar. His right sleeve fell long and loose down his arm.

“Who are you?”

Maureen asked with a rasp. The man tilted his head.

“Jack? Jack the Ripper?” The youth smiled, tilting his head the other way. “Well, now. Isn’t that a treat. I wasn’t expecting to find you so soon.”

“Ah. Manners. I’m Beck. White Bone Tower.”

With that, he stood, stepping down towards Maureen. The nearest apprentices flinched away, scuttling back as though they were afraid of being bitten by a snake. Maureen saw the naked fear in their faces. For once, it wasn’t directed at her.

[Beck. Third-Class. Danger: high. Knight body: strong.]

*The bone pin indicates he is of the same order as the White Bone apprentice you fought in your first battle,* Tally said.

*The White Bone Tower, then. Sieg’s order. Black wizards.*

The corner of Maureen’s mouth lifted.

“You’ve killed a great many people,” Beck said. “They talk about you. Good for you.” His voice was flat and emotionless. “Only, I don’t like it when other people shine. It’s untidy.”

“All the glory is mine. All of it. You strutting about like this is blasphemy, really. Bad. Very bad.”

“And you’ve stripped a lot of corpses. Nice things, I’d think. I want them. So . . .”

He let it hang.

“I’m going to kill you.”

He said it like a man asking for bread at the counter. No one on the rim laughed. No one moved closer.

*He wants you because you’ll make him famous and give him lots of loot,* Tally said.

*At least he’s honest,* Maureen said silently.

*He’s also very large.*

“Kill me?”

Beck nodded. “Just so. You may be good, Jack. I’d still rather keep it simple. I only have to move once, and you’re dead.”

Maureen laughed. It was the first time in days that she’d laughed honestly, out loud. She felt it rumble in her chest, pushing against the reshaped cords in her throat.

*Voice slipping upward,* Tally snapped.

Maureen caught the laugh, dragging it back down into Jack’s rasp before it could reach the rim.

“Beck,” she said, still smiling, “you’re shopping for a grave.”
