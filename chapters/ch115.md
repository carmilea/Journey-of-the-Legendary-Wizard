The world was quiet the way it gets quiet when a door slams somewhere in the house, in that fraction of a second before the wagon wheel slowly ground to a halt on its axle.

Alice hung suspended in the air ten paces off, the blood-red hem of her dress dripping slowly in the still air. She was smiling at Maureen like a child at the fair.

*They ran off,* Tally said, marking them with a knot of people moving fast westwards, already out of range. *But five of them didn’t.*

Tally tagged them one by one as they cowered behind cover: two men hiding behind a pair of beech trunks, one man curled up tight in the brambles at the side of the road, one under the abandoned wagon, and one lying flat in the ditch.

*All of them?* Maureen asked silently.

*Yes,* Tally said, without missing a beat. *Logged. Not shown.*

Maureen could see them too now, five points of dense warm colour showing through the trees. They stayed pinned to Tally’s display, marked by small amber dots at the edge of Maureen’s vision.

She could see why they had stayed: a pale face peering round the tree trunk, eyes wide with fear and greed. A story about Wizard Richard and the bleeding girl would buy a man drinks in every tavern in Emilia for a year.

Then the world changed again as the black-red fire of Purgatory burst out of Maureen. It erupted from her body with a deep ‘whump’, like the sound of a furnace door being thrown open, and wrapped around her from the tops of her boots to the crown of her head. The fire roared and crackled, a living thing. It blazed and shimmered, and gave off heat but did not burn. The air filled with the thick, sweet smell of scorched honey and sulphur, the smell of the blood of Pankora. The coat that Richard had borrowed didn’t even smoulder on his narrow shoulders.

Maureen rose from the road. The packed dust beneath her boots boiled straight into vapour, and a deep crater opened up beneath her feet. The walls of the hole were glazing black, and at the bottom coiled a serpent of dark mist, a phantom creature that unwound itself slowly as it rose from the depths.

[Mana: 16 → est. 14. Purgatory Fire: holding. Totem amplification: active.]

Tally’s readout burned at the edge of Maureen’s vision.

*Expensive,* Tally commented. *And very pretty.*

Maureen did not reply.

Flames peeled away from her in clumps of black-red fire. Like sparks from a blacksmith’s anvil, they arced out in different directions. A few flew high, then fell back to the ground. They fell between the beeches, or disappeared into the brambles at the side of the road, or drifted down under the wagon. Wherever they fell, they landed on an amber mark. There was a scream from the man beneath the wagon, and he tried to crawl out on his elbows before the flame touched him, but his cries cut off in the middle of a syllable. The cook woman managed two running steps through the high ferns before the fire found her back.

In less than ten heartbeats, all five stragglers were ash. Smoke rose from the five places where people had been standing, curling thinly in the hot air. The wood of the wagon bed began to burn as well.

*Five points of light going dark. Five... four... three... two... none,* Tally recited. She paused for a moment, then spoke again. *Your pulse is sixty-four. Sixty-four. Hasn’t changed at all. I’m noting it anyway, Maureen. For both our sakes.*

Maureen stood quiet and still in the centre of the crater. A cold, exact calculation filled her chest. No one would be carrying tales back to Emilia about what Wizard Richard did on this road. That was all there was to it.

Alice clapped her small hands at the dancing flames. The stray sparks that drifted past the doll didn’t seem to touch her at all.

The Mirror Pupil’s lens fell over Alice, revealing a field of slow, weaving red lines. The sparks were striking it, but not penetrating. Like rain falling on oiled leather, they slid off the surface. *The red field hasn’t thinned by so much as a hair,* Tally reported. There was nothing now but the crackling of the burning wagon and the grey ash that sifted down through the still, hot light, drifting like slow snowflakes.

* * *

The Wraith Doll tilted her head back, like a little girl standing in front of a bakery window, breathing in deeply through her nose. “You feel like the one Father gave me,” she said, her voice as sweet as syrup. “Exactly like it.”

“And you smell, oh, you smell so familiar.” The doll smiled, and the blood that ran down from the empty eye sockets stretched as her lips widened. “Sister. Sister Maureen. It’s you. It’s really you.”

*Father again,* Tally said.

*And she matched you by signature and smell, not by appearance. That fits the brand theory a little better. Daniel’s brand.*

Maureen did not respond to Tally’s observations. She simply looked at the Wraith Doll. The blood was tracking down the white porcelain of her face, running over her smiling mouth. Her blood-red dress was stiff and dark at the bottom, where the hem brushed the ground. Alice’s bare feet hovered a hand’s breadth above the ground, never touching the ash.

Alice threw her arms wide and charged at Maureen through the air, laughing as she came. It was as if she had finally found her long-lost sister and intended to give her a hug.

Something old and bare twisted in Maureen. No one back in the slums had ever run up to her with their arms open, ready for a hug. Except for Grandpa, of course, but he was buried beneath a plain grey slab that read: John Olsen. Maureen’s throat tightened for exactly one beat.

> [Warning. Alien energy entering host. Est. 60 s to negative state. All attributes falling.]

Tally’s alert flashed across Maureen’s sight, the words formed in that cold voice. A countdown appeared beside it in red digits.

Then Tally spoke again, using her own voice. It sounded warm compared to the alert, and very fast. *I can feel the doll’s energy in the rune lines, Maureen. The Minai Totem’s weakening effect is stripping away about a tenth of the doll’s energy, but a tenth isn’t enough. Throw it now!*

The Wraith Doll’s particles began to whirl in a tornado around Maureen. They closed in on her, forming a cocoon of crimson energy. The shell hummed like a beehive, layer upon layer of red particles building up until the burnt earthen road and the scorched beeches were just a blur of red beyond it.

Maureen felt the cold creep into her rune lines. Her tongue tasted of sweet copper, her fingertips grew numb, and her own black-red flame pressed against her skin as if something was leaning against it from the outside.

Her vision narrowed to a pinprick of daylight at the end of a red tunnel. Her knees, hanging in the air, went loose. It was the only time she had been afraid for real today, and it was her body’s own reaction that frightened her.

*Breathe out longer,* Tally said. *Steady now. Your pulse is one hundred forty-two. I’ve got the model. Say the words with me, back of the throat.*

*Hold it for me,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*I’m holding it,* Tally replied.

Maureen reached inside her robe’s sleeve and drew out a small vial. The Lament of Flowing Music. She had brewed it these last few nights within her curtained carriage, using a formula Tally had devised from the decoded Purgatory-curse entries, and cross-referenced against the pharmacology on file.

The vial was so cold that it hurt her fingers. The potion within was a deep blue, almost the colour of a still winter lake. As her nail tapped against the glass, the vial gave off a faint ringing noise, like a wet fingertip drawn along the rim of a wineglass.

Maureen threw the vial into the red and spoke the line-tongue trigger, humming it low in her throat:

“Sethan ilvaré.”

The glass vial exploded in midair, shattering into a thousand blue fragments. The deep blue liquid vanished before a single drop could hit the ground.

Tally steadied the spell’s trigger through the Darklight Orb, keeping it from breaking apart as the mana was consumed. [Mana: -0.3]

*There,* Tally said. *Good. That second syllable was all yours, not mine.*

Blue snow began to fall on the frozen road. Soft flakes drifted down from the sky, dancing through the hot air that still shimmered from the glazed black earth. They landed on the grey ash and didn’t melt.

Singing rose from nowhere. It was faint at first—a chorus of voices, slow and mournful, like a lament heard across a lake on a quiet night. The crackle of the burning wagon seemed to fall away beneath the music.

Tally painted the song’s waveform for Maureen, a pale blue line that rolled across the falling snow. Beneath the waveform the particles of red energy were growing thinner:

[Hostile field: 100% → 61%]

*It’s working,* Tally said. *Keep breathing out.*

The red cocoon began to fray. One by one, the threads of crimson energy unpicked themselves and blew away on the wind. The pinprick of daylight widened at the end of the tunnel, and Maureen’s vision returned to her.

The Wraith Doll’s body twisted again, but the red around her was wavering, thinning like smoke.

“Maureen . . . Sister . . .”

Alice kept reaching, but each step through the air took longer, and longer, until it seemed she wasn’t moving at all. Flakes settled on her eyelashes, on the blood that ran down her cheeks. They froze the blood to a crust of red beneath blue. Her face blurred and shifted, but her arms remained extended.

The snow fell over her, freezing layer upon layer, until she hung trapped inside a block of blue ice. It was as though she’d been caught in the moment of stepping forward, her arms still outstretched for a hug.

[Hostile field: 23%. Frozen.]

*Now,* Tally said. *Fire inside, not outside.*

Maureen thought it, and Purgatory Fire woke within the ice.

At first there was just a flicker of black-red deep within the blue, like an ember glowing beneath the surface of a frozen pond. The frozen body shuddered, then shuddered again, as if it could feel every thread of itself burning away. Tally counted the flame as it burned its way through the body, toward a single hard dark knot at its centre.

[Flame: 10% → 25%]

[Flame: 25% → 40%]

[Flame: 40% → 70%]

[Core: breaking.]

[Hostile: 0. Source: gone.]

The block of ice exploded with a sharp crack, like the ice of a river breaking up in the spring. It shattered into glittering blue dust. There was nothing left of Alice, and the singing stopped.

*Gone?* Maureen asked silently.

*Gone here,* Tally said slowly. Carefully. *I have no data on whether here is the only place it was.*

* * *

Maureen held out her hand, and the thin black-red streams of fire flowed back out of the empty air, coalescing over her palm and sinking into her skin. When they were all gone she looked down at her hand, then glanced at the wagon’s still smouldering frame. Tally’s ledger appeared.

[Mana: est. 11.7 / 16.
Purgatory Fire: 2 casts, ~2 each.]

*One cost for upkeep, one for casting, same number,* Tally added. *Since I’ve seen it twice now, I’m reasonably confident about my figures.*

Maureen nodded, then made her way carefully down onto the lip of the crater. The glazed earth crunched beneath her boots like burnt sugar, the heat from below still radiating through the soles. She brushed at her sleeves, and blue dust came away, none of it melted by the warmth of her body.

“That’s the puppet finished, then,” she said aloud. Her voice sounded flat in the silence, directed to no-one.

Tally gave her a sweep of the all-round sense anyway. There was nothing dense enough to register within three hundred metres. No watchers left, all runaways out of range to the west. *Just us,* Tally said.

Maureen stood there quietly for long moments, letting the stillness wrap around her. It was strange. The image kept coming back to her, like a song you couldn’t get out of your head. The open arms. The smiling, happy mouth. The girl running forward to be hugged.

“Alice,” she murmured. Then again, more softly. “Alice.”

*The other one?* Tally asked gently.

*She didn’t eat for two days,* Maureen answered silently.

*I remember,* Tally said. *You put the bread in front of her first.*

Maureen remembered the line for ship’s rations. The heel of hard bread. The strip of salt fish. Gentle Alice with her wary eyes. Lucy beside her. That Alice was alive. That Alice was far away from here. *Two girls with one name. One I fed, one I burned.*

She drew Freya’s slender sword a hand’s breadth, tilting the flat against the last of the light, looking at her reflection along the black-lit steel.

Richard’s mousy face stared back at her, the face she had borrowed. In its pupils was the familiar crimson flicker, but now as she looked closely she saw another movement, small flames of black curling up from the bottom of each eye, only to sink again and curl up once more.

*The face is borrowed,* Tally said. *The eyes are not. Mimicry cannot imitate that. I’m logging it as a risk factor. Perhaps you should keep your eyes lowered when the caravan gathers again.*

*Logged,* Maureen agreed, sliding the sword back into its scabbard. She stood there for long moments more in the falling blue dust, and the small black flames in her eyes grew steady.
