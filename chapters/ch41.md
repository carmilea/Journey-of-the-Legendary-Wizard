The heat radiated off the polished scales like a forge banked for the night. The stench of struck flint and rotten eggs mingled in the cavern. The pooled blood hissed faintly.

Maureen walked the length of the serpent to the burst head. Where did such a creature come from?

[Odour: sulphur, fire-smoke. Saved.]

I know it now, Tally told her.

Maureen knelt in the gore. She pulled on her leather glove and let drops of blood fall into two reagent tubes from her belt kit. The torchlight illuminated the reactions. One tube clouded to a rusty brown, while the other threw a black precipitate that settled slowly. The residue left a prickling sensation against Maureen's particle sense, like warm grit.

[Reaction 1, unknown. Reaction 2, unknown. Residue 1, unknown. Residue 2, unknown. Comparing with library... Species: unknown. Marker match: Canes Black Rope Snake 78% / Elas snake 36% / Senpange snake 9%.]

A Black Rope Snake is barely a metre long, she objected silently. It's food for everything. That thing fought like a subspecies of a high-energy creature.

Yes. That's what I don't like.

A scrape and a cough broke through the settling grey rubble dust. Aaron was struggling up out of the ground. Maureen rushed over and pulled him out.

A stream of grey dust poured off him, his skin felt light and dry as paper beneath her fingers. Her grip on his wrist lingered a beat too long.

[Dazed. Coughing. No visible bleeding. Skin colour better than on road. No data why.]

Aaron smiled at her, his smile reaching every inch of his face. "I'm fine, Maureen."

[Unable to classify smile. Gratitude has different parameters on file.]

Log it as gratitude, she thought back.

Logged as gratitude. Disputed.

Maureen pressed several Recovery Potions into his hands. He cradled one vial in his fingers, turning it round and round, before he looked back at her.

"Thank you, Maureen. Really, thank you."

She clapped him on the shoulder, harder than she intended. There was a small chill in the back of her neck that she couldn't place. Was it something about Aaron? Was he...?

Is there sulphur on him too? Tally? she asked silently.

Entire cavern smells of sulphur. I can't separate his from the snake's. I can try if you want but it will be inaccurate.

Leave it there for now.

The survivors gathered their wits. Jay was weak, Wolf was swaying on his feet, and Anthony looked pale and drained. They cut off the best parts of the carcass, then left quickly. As they passed through the mouth of the cave, Anthony turned around to look back inside.

"I never thought Sim would die," he said quietly.

* * *

They rode hard back. Jay and Wolf kept away from her as much as possible, and no one spoke all the way. Every joint in her body ached, her mana sat at almost empty, and her mind was strangely still. The stink of serpent rose from the packs they carried.

Would you like me to count the road for you, Maureen? Tally asked in its warm voice.

Yes, she thought silently and let it.

Three hundred and twelve, three hundred and thirteen, three hundred and fourteen... the numbers ticked over silently as the hooves pounded the dirt. In the Dark Forest again, beneath the black cloud, Anthony submitted the task at the task office, and returned with a black bag. The issuers had confirmed the serpent, and increased the reward from seven stones to twelve each.

The magic stones were cool in her palm, not by much but enough to notice.

[Magic stones: +12]

Twelve sounds better than seven, doesn't it? she told Tally silently.

Five does. Precisely.

"What do you want from the materials?" Anthony asked as they walked towards the castle.

Aaron answered first. "Stones please."

Anthony smiled. "Not everyone wants magic stones, do they, Maureen?"

She shrugged. They knew she was an apothecary, she wanted the materials.

In one of the small private rooms in the castle, lit by a single lamp, Tally worked out the values of each component against the tables in Gandolf's and the library. Skin, fangs, eyes, blood, muscle... it fought hard for the eyes.

In the end Maureen took a large portion of skin, the two massive fangs, both eyes and some blood and muscle tissue. Then she waved goodbye to Anthony and Aaron, Jay and Wolf not daring to speak, and walked out with the precious package. The wrapped eyes, earth yellow with slit pupils, rested heavily against her hip, still warm.

* * *

The brass key turned in the brass lock. The dull, heavy sound was the first comfort she had felt in a week. She stepped through the door into the cold black stone, smelling the faint tang of her own herbs. Then the lights came up.

Show-offs, Tally said in her head, sulky.

Don't start. Tally was still offended by the self-lighting lamps.

Hush now, I am home. Maureen smiled as she closed the door behind her, feeling the cold black stone walls around her.

They're still smug about it though.

Tally got the last word.

She stored the serpent materials in her study, the rolled up skin, the two great fangs, the wrapped earth yellow eyes, the stoppered jars of blood and muscle, while Tally dutifully recorded each one. She set aside seven Recovery Potions for Gandolf's deal.

Then she soaked herself clean in the hot tub, and slept like a stone.

When she woke, her head clear, she checked the tub and the water was still warm.

At her study desk she turned the advanced texts bound together and removed from Jay's emptied pouches, spread by spread. A pale blue flicker followed behind her eyes with each page turn, while a page counter ticked over on Tally's display. Tally was smug about the haul of texts, and Maureen teased it back silently before burning the volumes in the washroom grate. The old leather bindings curled and stank obscenely as they burned, while Maureen stirred the ash with a poker making sure that no scrap remained to be read.

Maureen lay on her bed in the dark.

Play it Tally.

The fight with the serpent played out on Tally's display before her closed eyes. The serpent was coiled in the cavern, lit by the torches.

The dark red fireball, the serpent's yellow eyes, and a quarter second later the eruption of dust off the rock.

Maureen watched Sim go, and then told Tally silently to skip ahead. It did so without comment, and Maureen saw the serpent again, this time facing her.

Anthony's box? she asked.

The shape and lacquer of the box matched a sketch in the library catalogue, and the discharge matched her particle log.

[Sophia's Jewelry Box. Low-grade enchanted item. Damaged. Negative + ice. Arrays: Swift burst / Negative-energy erosion / Ice-particle frost. Energetics, Mutation Studies.]

It was leaking unevenly, and wouldn't last much longer.

He used his trump card on a snake, she thought.

And on you, Tally replied. Put him in both columns.

Lucky no one saw the Blood Seal of Thorns, she thought. I'd have only used it if I couldn't kill that damn serpent any other way. Too much trouble afterwards.

Tally flagged an anomaly, and she saw the negative and ice particles feeding off each other, before condensing into the black mist.

[Combined output > sum.]

She sat up in her bed as she recognised the particle spread from one she had turned in George's combined spells text.

I told you I didn't like only having part of a book, Tally said.

* * *

Voices were raised in argument below the great grey question cloth. The crawling writing was as dense as ever.

George's sleeves still smelled of pickling brine as he waved her over.

"Ah, friend," George greeted her. "How went your mission?"

"Passable," she answered. "Get those stones ready."

Maureen brought out her wares. Eight Stamina Potions, eight Berserk Blood Potions, and all the parts from the serpent that she didn't need. The Recovery Potions stayed at home for Gandolf.

"Sixty-seven," George counted out loud, after examining Maureen's merchandise. "You must have emptied your stock."

You would have reached sixty-seven at his prices two items back.

"You've got a good eye there, friend." George's patter was sounding a little thin as he opened up his purse. The sound of clicking stones was loud in Maureen's ears as George's freckled fingers moved through his stock.

Tally caught him counting twice, once up to thirty-two, then again more nervously. You brought too much on purpose, it said.

I brought exactly enough, she thought back silently.

"Ah. I only have thirty-two," George admitted.

Maureen scratched her chin. "Well. How about you give me that combined-spells book of yours to make up the thirty-five stones."

"You know, that book is worth at least one paid course," George said.

"Yes, and you have been trying to sell me that advanced paid course since I arrived here two years ago," Maureen said. "You haven't sold it yet, and I think you almost forgot it existed."

George paused. "Actually, I can't read a word of it, but thirty-five is still a bit low..."

Maureen pressed five stones into George's hand. "Here are another five to make it forty stones. Call it paying for my curiosity!"

George looked down at the five stones in his palm, then at the purse of thirty-two he had just counted out. He sighed, his shoulders sagging.

Thirty-five owed. Book valued at forty stones. Five stones returned to customer. Net gain is twenty-seven stones. One book acquired, Tally announced. You just paid five stones for your curiosity.

The best five I've ever spent, she silently replied.

George pushed the thick leather bound book across the board, packed away his goods and told Maureen to bring him anything she found in the future. She walked home, clutching the old book with cracked and yellowed binding, covered in dust.
