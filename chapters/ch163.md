“He is amazing, isn’t he?” Viska sighed.

Maureen was just going to reply when she heard footsteps behind her. The air was so still that the sound of the boots hitting the frozen fronds was distinct. The cold from the lid of the realm had settled into the small bones of her knuckles.

*Five sets of footfalls,* Tally noted. *In step.*

*Dense source at the front,* Tally added a breath later. *Wright’s grain, by particle sense.*

The red-haired apothecary was still talking about Wright Gernier, but Maureen didn’t need to listen anymore. She turned around just as a group of five men approached. They were all wearing white robes with the bird stitched over their hearts, and were all walking across the trampled and muddy ground toward the Pharmacy tent in perfect unison. The bronze broadsword hanging at Wright’s hip swung with each long stride.

Maureen felt the pressure from Wright approach a step before he did. It was a dense presence, like the heat coming off an opened forge door, and as it rolled out, the usual noise of the camp—the hammering, shouting, and clanking of metal on metal—stopped in a ring around the crates.

“Viska,” Wright Gernier said, smiling. He sounded like an old acquaintance. His voice had a faint metallic timbre that made Maureen think of a large pot being struck by a hammer. “Still keeping the entire Alliance in tonics and tinctures?”

Viska laughed and rubbed the back of his head, perhaps a bit too loudly. Maureen knew from experience that he always laughed louder than was strictly necessary when dealing with those above his station. The scent of his tea—smoke and peppermint—wafted off his sleeve.

Wright’s pale blue eyes shifted to Maureen. They lingered there for a beat longer than courtesy demanded. “And your friend is . . . ?”

*He’s looked at you three times since the crates,* Tally noted quietly.

*Hush,* Maureen thought silently.

Maureen smiled the easy smile she’d perfected for Richard’s weak-jawed face. “I’m Richard.”

All five men froze. They might have been a single bell, struck and then caught before they could ring. All five sets of eyes came to rest upon Maureen at the same time.

Tally kept count of the reactions as they came, then put them up in an organized display for Maureen to analyze:

[Freeze: 5 of 5. Duration: 0.6–1.1 s. Wright: shortest.]

*The shortest was the most practised,* Tally noted.

*Or the one who already knew,* Maureen answered silently.

*Or that,* Tally agreed. *Both columns.*

Wright Gernier’s face split into a wide, bright, and generous smile. “Wizard Richard. Half of headquarters talks about your potions. The other half drinks them.”

He had a large hand, callused and dry from the grip of a sword-wielding arm on a wizard’s body. His grasp lingered for a moment too long. There was warmth there—on the surface, anyway.

They chatted amiably for a while longer. Wright asked after the condition of the quota crates on the bird. He asked what reagents were going bad from the realm’s cold. He asked if Richard ever took private work.

Maureen answered every question lightly, with a polite laugh when necessary. Underneath the normal flow of conversation, Tally fed her tells into her head in single, quiet words.

steady

steady

hands

Wright’s pulse sat flat against the side of his throat. His eyes kept flicking down to look at Maureen’s hands, then at her throat, then away from her face. *He’s measuring the same things you are,* Tally said. *Just like you measure him.*

*Let him,* Maureen thought.

Still, something about Wright Gernier caught at Maureen. Like a loose thread snagged beneath the fingertip, she could not ignore it. There was an edge to people, even to the most trustworthy. A roughness. A place where they were not as smooth and polished as they would like to appear. She couldn’t find that in Wright. Her skin prickled beneath her sleeves, down both forearms.

*Scan him,* she thought silently.

*No flicker. No ping. Nothing he can feel. I’ll just use the lens and the particle sense,* Tally said. *You won’t even get the red eyes.*

*Fine.*

[Wright Gernier. Liquefied, by comparison to Claude and Jackman (hearsay labels). Physical: Str ~26 / Agi ~26 / Con ~26, by mass, stance and recovery against your own scan. Knight body: beyond every knight sample on file. Lineage: no data yet.]

*That isn’t a knight,* Maureen thought silently, nodding in acknowledgement.

*It is built on the same frame. He’s just taken it so much further,* Tally answered. *The Silver Soul corpus has something similar. Give me a night or two.* There was a second line of text that appeared, set apart from the rest.

[Inert brand, inner left wrist, under the cuff. Paired type. Residual grain: match, Gus. Confidence: medium.]

*The other half is dead,* Tally said gently. *When Gus died, it went silent, like Daniel’s did.*

Maureen’s mouth twitched once at the corner before she could control herself. It was a heartbeat too late for the smile to return. Wright’s eyes flicked down to her lips, then away again.

*Not now,* Maureen told Tally.

*Not now,* Tally agreed, *but I’m keeping this one at the top of the file.*

Paired brands on file: Daniel’s had been a leash. What this one had been for . . . no data.

Gus came back to her mind with a cold flash of memory: the oak, the white hole punched through his chest, the look on his face as he realised what was happening. She felt the tips of her fingers turn cold, as though she had plunged them into a trough of water in the depths of winter.

Wright gave no sign that he noticed anything amiss, or perhaps simply chose not to show it. He turned back to Viska, clapping him on the shoulder. “You have a good group leader there. I may have to come knocking on your Pharmacy tent one of these days, when I have a problem worthy of your time.”

“Bring stones,” Maureen said in Richard’s flat voice.

Wright laughed, a deep tone with a faint ringing, like bronze. Then the five White Bird wizards walked off toward the patrol lines, Wright’s hand resting casually on the pommel of his broadsword.

Tally followed him with her particle sense, tracking his density until he moved too far away to distinguish from the crowd.

[Wright: 60 m. 90 m. Out of sight; in range.]

*He didn’t look back even once,* Tally said.

*Men who don’t look back generally already know where you are,* Maureen answered.

* * *

Maureen slipped her hands into her sleeves as they walked. The chill was starting to move up from her fingertips; it had reached her wrists. Her palms felt damp inside the wool.

Viska cast a sideways glance at her, then stopped her under a lamp. “Group leader. You went the colour of tallow back there. What was it?”

Before Maureen could reach for a lie, Tally offered her another option. The wind reaction from the Pharmacy exam, the branch they hadn’t been able to close. Maureen took it.

She inhaled slowly, then let out her breath in a long stream. “The wind-soul reaction. It came back to me mid-sentence, the step that won’t stay put under cooling. I keep losing it.”

“Ah,” Viska said in sympathy. “That one got me three times. Ruined benches, years ago.” He said nothing more about it.

*You lied better with my material,* Tally noted.

*I lied better with true material,* Maureen responded silently.

Tally considered that for a moment. *Draw,* she finally declared. *Logged and filed.*

A patrol of four passed them on the lane, the silver trim of their white robes just visible in the evening darkness. The wards glowing at their cuffs cast a pale light, like frost on glass. Viska waited until the crunch of their boots on the frozen fronds had faded into the distance before he spoke again.

“Anyway,” Viska said, “another convoy was hit by wandering wizards. This time it was on the eastern track, even though it was marked. Spells, guards, doesn’t matter. That’s why Wright’s combat wizards are always being sent out.”

“It isn’t only the wanderers, either,” he continued. “The little orders want their share too, and half our own members are trying to stab each other over zones. Headquarters has been sending teams here just to keep everyone from fighting over the water barrels.”

Tally opened a three-column file as he spoke.

[Small orders. Internal rivalries. Wanderers raiding marked convoys.]

She added another line underneath.

[Alliance: loose coalition, held by headquarters teams. Not one order.]

Viska lowered his voice slightly once more. Wenger of the High Ring White Tower had been patrolling the camp for the last several days with an expression that could have shut a door. He’d been asking questions of anyone who’d ever had contact with his late disciple Gus.

One of the small lamps along the lane sputtered, then nearly went out as the wind caught it. For a moment the canvas beside them blew up in a loose flap, revealing a dark gap between the tents before settling back down again.

Tally retrieved the truth weave from Ambrose’s office, displaying it as Viska mentioned his name. Maureen had given Wenger no false word, and nothing that pointed past Richard. The grey sky overhead was turning charcoal, and in the distance she could see lights popping on, one after another, along the lines of canvas. Another patrol passed, boots crunching on the fronds, and a cloud of woodsmoke hung low over the camp.

*Wenger left without a second question,* Tally noted. *The weave found no false word. Low suspicion, medium confidence.*

Maureen thought for a moment, then replied silently to Tally, *Wenger still thinks I’m a pharmacist.*

*Wenger does,* Tally agreed, *Wright doesn’t.*

“They wouldn’t pull in one of the group leaders for questioning like that,” Maureen said aloud.

“I know!” Viska replied, nodding in agreement. “There are only a dozen of us, and Ambrose would never let that happen.”

Viska’s tent appeared ahead of them, and he wished Maureen a good night with a yawn before disappearing into the shelter. His lamp went out behind the canvas almost immediately, plunging the area outside into darkness. The last of the light disappeared as well, leaving Maureen alone in the dusk. Tally ran through the risks as Maureen made her way down the last stretch of the lane.

*The Bloodcoat token and the two dead guild tokens in your pouch form a trail back to Night Owl,* Tally noted. *One which Wright has never seen.*

The inert paired brand on Wright’s own wrist was an entirely different matter.

*But the brand means something else entirely,* Maureen thought. *A trail back to Gus.*

* * *

Maureen slid Gregory’s runes into the door frame and the gold lines sank into the wood.

[Ward: closed. Drew 0.1. Mana: 22.9.]

*Sound,* Tally noted after a quick review.

Maureen pulled the slender Cross Sword out of the pouch herself, and sat down on the cot, laying the blade across her knees. It was a dark night outside, and black light crawled along the sword like oil upon still water. She felt the cold touch of it through the cloth of her trousers.

She kept Richard’s face on as she sat. Even here, where the walls were made of canvas rather than stone, they were still someone else’s walls, and Maureen did not drop the face.

The memory came back unbidden. First, there was the meeting in the attic of the nobleman’s manor house. Then, the scene outside, in the frost. She remembered perching in the oak tree, seeing Gus standing beneath it, watching Frans punch a hole through his chest with a cone of crackling white energy, and seeing the look on Gus’s face change as he recognised her.

“Richard... you’re Owl...”

Frans had killed him. Not her. But Gus had died knowing who she was, and afterwards she’d made certain that everyone who had witnessed that night was dead.

*Gus’s death is recorded twice,* Tally said quietly in Maureen’s head. *Once by Frans’s hand, once by yours as consequence.*

*Near enough for anyone who wants to settle the account,* Maureen answered Tally silently.

Her thumb went to her inner pocket, seeking the hard lump of the Seal that used to sit there, and found only the empty seam. She moved her hand away, but a few minutes later, her hand found the empty seam again.

*Twice,* Tally said.

*Log it as habit,* Maureen told her.

*Logged as habit. And something else.*

*What else?*

*I don’t know.*

Maureen asked Tally to replay Wright’s freeze when he’d heard her name. It had been the shortest of the five, his eyes fixed on her hands and throat, following the twitch at the corner of her mouth.

Tally replayed the frames for her. Maureen studied them.

*Medium confidence forecast, He will come to you before you go to him. He will come smiling.*

*Why?*

*Because he spoke of knocking at your tent, not entering. And because he did not look back.*

Outside the canvas walls of her narrow room, in the murky dark of the realm, one of the patrols called out the hour. Four voices in sequence, calling out through the night, their words flattening against the low cloud before dying away.

She understood now. It wasn’t his strength she was afraid of.

It was the account.

One end of the brand was still bound. One end was still alive. Walking around the camp, sleeping in his bed, eating his meals. And she couldn’t burn him with a fireball.

*Would you like me to take over the watch?* Tally asked.

Maureen lasted a while longer, the sword across her knees, listening to the sounds of the night. Finally she lay down on the cot.

*Wake me if he comes closer.*

*I will chime you,* Tally assured her.

The orange of the lantern died down to a coin. Through her closed eyelids, the gold of Gregory’s runes traced around the doorframe was the last thing she saw.

* * *

She woke up stiff, her breath curling in the air like the smoke from a fire. The Cross Sword lay on the floor where she had dropped it, leaning against the leg of the cot. Tally had a night log ready for her.

[Night: 3 passes. One dense, Wright’s grain, 40 m, paused 11 s, left. Grey robe at the pharmacists’ tents asking names, by sound. Ward drew 0.1. Mana: 23.0, full.]

*You let me sleep through that,* Maureen said silently.

*He didn’t come any closer,* Tally answered. *If he had, I would have chimed you as requested. The grey robe asked after three pharmacists by name, Richard’s among them. I heard the exchange through your ears.*

Maureen broke the skin of ice that had formed on top of the water in the tin basin and washed her face. In the reflective surface of the metal, Richard’s weak jaw stared back at her. His eyes were still black.

*I have begun cross-referencing against the Silver Soul Hand corpus,* Tally noted. *Lineage entries, bronze fire, and one source-knight line I want to read again.*

[Analysis: ~1–2 days.]

Maureen picked up the sword and slid it into the pouch. She tied on her belt kit and decided to head over to the Pharmacy tent at the usual hour, as if nothing had happened.

*Same face, same hours.* Tally sounded approving.

*Same everything,* Maureen answered as she stepped outside into the rattle of the kettles.
