She was not the same girl who had taken the Silver-Trace Ink, though. She felt different, and not only because stairs didn’t take as much out of her legs anymore, or because it took less time for her Silver Light to settle. Her hand still went to the back of her neck every so often, and was surprised by the short hairs that brushed against her fingers.

[Minai: 3 of 4 secured. Missing: Demon-Face Flower.]

*It’s at the bottom of the list,* Tally pointed out.

*Yes, I can read,* Maureen said silently to it.

For days Maureen walked through all the general stores and trading points in the Dark Forest. She asked about a Demon-Face Flower everywhere she could. Everywhere she got the same answers.

Head shakes.

Narrowed eyes.

Long looks at her purse.

Tally kept a list, dutifully striking out every stall that told her no.

[Demon-Face Flower: 0 of 31 shops.]

*Thirty-one,* Maureen said silently.

*Thirty-one honest no’s,* Tally answered. It liked honest no’s. They were cheap.

One person even laughed aloud when she asked, as if she’d asked to buy the moon right off the sky. Her feet ached from walking on the black stone. Her ledger hadn’t budged a single stone in either direction since she began looking.

If the Forest didn’t have what she was looking for, then she would have to look outside of it. But before she did that, there was still one door she had yet to try here in the Forest, and that door belonged to Adrian.

The lamps that lined the corridor to his lab threw her shadow far ahead of her, dancing across the wet flagstones. The black stones absorbed the water, but they couldn’t keep it from beading up along the seams. Her footsteps echoed in the corridor, the only sound to be heard. Tally counted the turns, as it always did when they made their way through the Dark Forest. Five left, four right, two left, one right. Almost there.

*You have a plan for Adrian?* it asked.

*A hope.* Maureen thought.

*Hopes don’t count,* Tally responded.

*That’s why I brought you with me.*

* * *

Adrian sat at the long stone bench, trimming one of the small plants that sat in a row of terra-cotta pots. He used a small pair of scissors to snip away dead leaves, and the curling black mist pulled away from the shining steel blades. The light from his lamp caught the lenses of his glasses, making them flash.

He didn’t speak until he’d finished his snipping. “Yes?”

“Mentor, I need a Demon-Face Flower,” Maureen said bluntly. “You trade with another wizard to get one. Then I’ll buy it off you for whatever it costs you.”

Adrian put down his scissors and regarded her with a look that was almost fond—almost. It was the look a teacher gives to a student who has nearly solved a problem correctly. The answer wasn’t quite correct, but the student was close enough that the teacher allowed himself a glimmer of hope.

Then the old man shook his head. “It is a good idea, Olsen. But it will not work.”

“Why not?” Maureen asked, though she suspected she knew the answer already.

“Wizard Guaos got his hands on one, years back,” Adrian replied in his calm, flat voice. “The entire Dark Forest went half mad over it.” “A Demon-Face Flower is a negative-energy material. In an order devoted to darkness, every wizard wants one of those.”

Tally pulled up a file for Maureen and displayed the information on her display:

> Guaos: wizard.
>
> Once owned a Demon-Face Flower.
>
> ‘Frenzy.’

*We’re shopping for a darkness material in the darkness order’s house,* Tally said in a superior tone.

*That’s why we won’t find it here,* Maureen responded silently.

*Didn’t I just tell you that?*

“But,” Adrian continued, “no one would trade on behalf of an apprentice. And if they did, I’d feel the price in my teeth. You wouldn’t be able to afford it, Olsen. Not even beginning to.”

Maureen didn’t answer. If this man said something was dear, then it was dear. She felt her fingers close reflexively over the pouch of stones that hung around her neck, nestled against her chest, before she forced her hand to relax. Then she would have to look outside the Dark Forest.

Adrian looked up over the rims of his glasses. “You came at a good moment, Olsen. Listen.” He paused for a long moment, as though choosing his words with care, before he continued. “The war with Hader Castle and the white orders has been called off. Black and white have made peace—for now. We strike the dark elves together.”

The old man’s words fell into Maureen’s ears with a cold click, like the sound of a box’s lid slamming shut. She thought of the dark elves standing at the gate of the realm, and of the heart they had borne away between them.

*It’s the heart in the box,* Tally said inside her head, voice low. *And the Bloodline Seal Stone we sold to those dark elves. I think we know what started this.*

*We think,* Maureen thought back silently.

*We think,* Tally agreed in her head. *Try to keep your face still, Maureen. Adrian’s watching you not ask him questions.*

“The dark elves are a royal race among the subterranean peoples,” Adrian said drily. “The weakest of the royals, certainly, but royals nonetheless.”

Maureen worked it out for herself, in silence. If the Official Wizards were going to war against one royal race of the underground, then they would soon be at war with all the other races that owed allegiance to the dark elf royals.

She asked Adrian nothing.

The war had already come, Adrian told her, and come fast. And the dark elves had been ready for it. Every Official Wizard already had his hands full, so apprentices were being pressed into service at a moment’s notice to hold garrisons and defend key points along the border.

Adrian passed his hand slowly through the air before him. Particles of darkness poured from the cuffs of his robe and gathered on the workbench, forming an image of the region. Ridges of soot-like substance rose on the bench, flowing together into hills. Streams of ink ran between them like rivers, tracing their course to join into a wide black pool. This was the borderland between the Dark Forest and the country of the dark elves. A cellar chill came off the conjured terrain.

Among the ridges, like pinpoints of blood, red dots flickered. Along the streams they pulsed, a slow heartbeat. Beside each one, a thread of Ancient Fusiform script glowed faintly.

“The realm didn’t suit you,” Adrian said. His voice sounded as flat and passionless as ever. “But you did well enough, nonetheless. So you’ll be able to choose your own posting.”

Maureen made no response. Her eyes moved from red dot to red dot, in the same way they had once traveled the shelves of the Skran treasury. They stopped upon one dot, set deep among a knot of ridges.

*Tally,* Maureen thought silently. *Match this map against everything you have.*

Tally obeyed, laying a pale blue overlay of its own estimate for Proan Canyon atop Adrian’s conjured ridges. Tally’s estimate had been formed from Gregory’s imprint, and its overlay on the Fasol mission map.

[Proan Canyon: 92% match, red dot 7. By terrain shape.]

*The ridgelines match,* Tally explained within Maureen’s head. *The rivers match. But one spur does not.*

Maureen considered. Ninety-two percent was more than enough. *Ninety-two percent was a door,* she told Tally silently.

*A door with a draught,* Tally answered in her head. It sounded pleased with itself.

The burner ticked under its test tube, the sharp chemical smell hung over the long stone bench. Maureen’s breath caught on the old hunger. Under that red dot lay the essence blood of the Pankora Scarlet Serpent, and Gregory’s advanced meditation method. Here was the inheritance of the Ancient Wizard, which she had come back to claim. She slowed her breathing before Adrian could hear the change.

Tally spoke in her head. *The red dot lies southwest of the Dark Forest. Southwest is where Aaron told you to go.*

*Aaron meant the realm, not the world,* Maureen answered silently.

*Yes, probably. But I will file them side by side anyway. I have no data linking them but it is an interesting coincidence.*

Maureen pointed at the dot. “Mentor. That one.”

Adrian’s grey eyebrows rose above the rims of his glasses. “Already? You chose your method just as quickly?”

It wasn’t really a question, so Maureen didn’t answer. Adrian looked at her, then at the map, then back at her. A frown gathered on his face. “That post takes the worst of the raids. And it’s Olivia land. If you go…” He stopped, shaking his head slowly.

[Pause 1.1 s. Unfinished sentence.]

*He knows something about that ground,* Tally said. *Something he’s not telling us.*

*Everyone knows the Olivias want Jack dead.* Maureen answered silently.

*Yes, but Adrian doesn’t look worried about Jack. He looks worried about you.*

She didn’t let it in. Elves. Olivias. Truces. None of it was hers. She had come back for one thing, and she would leave the moment it was in her hands. Her face stayed still and her eyes stayed fixed on the red dot.

“I know, Mentor,” she said aloud. “I still want to go.” She looked at the script glowing beside the red dot. “The Panso Mountains.” Proan Canyon lay somewhere deep within their ridges.

Adrian let out a long breath. “Very well. Go and get ready. It will not be much longer now.”

He reached out and touched the brand on her wrist. It warmed at his touch, as though he had lit a fire within it. From this point on, wherever she went within the Forest, Adrian’s own access as an Official Wizard would go with her.

At the door she glanced back. He stood alone before the conjured map, watching its particles drift back into his robe. The scissors lay untouched next to the small potted plant, still curling in its black mist.

* * *

Adrian’s mark opened doors. Clerks would glance at the faint glow on her wrist before disappearing through a curtain to fetch a locked ledger. Grilles that Maureen had only ever seen from the outside swung back on shelves lined with material and data.

She walked into the Forest’s own shop and asked for Official spell models. The grey-haired shopkeeper sat bolt upright for the second time in his life. “You again. With his mark on you this time.”

The Official model books were colder than any Rank Zero model she’d ever owned, heavier than they should have been for their size. As she held them, their covers hummed against her palms, like the faint echo of a bell being struck.

[All columns: ~16,000.]

The first model went for Adrian’s discounted price.

[~16,000 → ~11,000.]

*Buy,* she told Tally silently.

*I know,* Tally said. It was watching the money leave as though it were attending a funeral.

Maureen flipped through each of the model books, one spread at a time, laying them out on the corner of the counter so that Tally could record every page. Then she bought a second model book, some experimental data on the darkness columns, and materials she’d never even been allowed to touch.

[All columns: ~2,000.]

Tally gave the number in the same funereal tone.

*Stones are for spending,* she told it silently.

*I know,* it responded. *I just like to watch them go slowly.*

With her stones run thin, Maureen began selling off her finished potions. She did it in small batches, moving from stall to stall, immediately turning the stones back into resources.

Tally watched the face of each buyer carefully, especially in light of her newfound fame as a survivor of the realm. No one even blinked at the transaction. An apprentice who had survived a trial and was selling off stock before their posting was simply common sense. At least, that’s what Tally said, with more than a little satisfaction.

The Potion Apprentices’ Gathering roared with life. Hagglers shouted over one another, pickled bits of this and that floated in clouded jars, and the question cloth writhed with glowing writing overhead. George’s stall smelled of brine from its pickling supplies, and dried roots in jars.

George swept all of Maureen’s vials into his crates with a few quick motions of his hands. “You’ve been buying half the Forest these past days, friend.” He grinned at her.

Then he clapped her hard on the shoulder. “Heard where they’re sending you. Bad ground, that. Luck to you, friend.”

 She smiled at the potion apprentice. “If you want to bless me, George, put a little more on what you’re paying me. That’s the blessing I’d feel.”

George stared at her for a moment, then began laughing. His freckles bunched up as he laughed, and his curly blond hair shook on top of his head. He laughed so much that the next stall even turned around to see what was so funny. When he finally calmed down, George sighed and dug out a low-grade magic stone card from beneath his board. He slapped it onto the counter. “There. Call it the price of your coffin. I’d rather pay for it now than miss the funeral.”

Maureen laughed at that, surprised. The magic stone card was cool against her fingers. Then the laugh died in her throat. Andrew’s thin raw pine coffin flashed into her mind. Old John was lying inside, his grey hair combed flat against his head.

[Cards: +1. Reason: funeral (his).] flickered in pale blue on her display.

*Andrew paid for a coffin once,* Tally said quietly in her head.

*Raw pine.* Maureen answered silently.

*I have the smell on file. I’ll keep it.*

Maureen tucked the magic stone card into her robe. “You’re going to be waiting a long time for that funeral, George.” She smiled again.

George just waved her off with a grin, turning back to the next customer.

* * *

Her dormitory rooms were quiet. The lights had come on by themselves. The study bench stood where she would leave it, with its faint greenish ring in its black stone. Her first Recovery Potion had rested there.

Maureen knelt beside her bed, next to the third stone block.

“Manas.”

The floor rippled like water but without a sound. The hollow opened up under it, dark and deep.

She pulled out the hoard piece by piece; the cloth-wrapped Sodara trunk smelling of resin, the bag of loot from the trials, the padded crates of grenades, and the rare materials that would make an Official Wizard’s mouth water.

[Hollow: emptying. Grenades: 34. Loot bag: 1. Sodara trunk: 1.]

*Thirty-four iron eggs,* Tally noted.

*Against an Official Wizard they are polite,* Tally said. *Against apprentices they are arguments.*

*Then we don’t argue with wizards,* Maureen told it silently.

Maureen lined the shells up in rows on the floor. The plain black iron was dull in the lamplight. They were cold beneath her fingers. Each shell was heavier than it looked.

The pile was considerable. She eyed it dubiously, mentally measuring the weight against her arms and back. This time it would be more than even a knight’s body could carry on the road. She would need a carriage.

Maureen’s eyes swept the rooms, taking them in: the bathtub, the tiny mirror where she had meditated through two arrays, the study bench. She didn’t say anything aloud.

*Do you want me to log these rooms?* Tally asked.

*You already have,* Maureen answered silently.

*I did, but I wanted to hear you say it.*

“Manas.”

The hollow closed up again with a ripple of the floor, and Maureen lay down beside her pile of iron eggs with the sleeve crossbow armed.

* * *

Above the lift, the chains groaned their slow song, moving on the gears that pulled them up out of the bowels of the lower stable levels. The seller’s yard smelled of damp straw and horse dung, and was thick with lamp smoke. Somewhere in one of the stalls, something big shifted its weight, making a sound like someone had dropped an anvil.

“Now then, miss, I’m sure we can come to some agreement . . .” the Dark Forest apprentice began as he escorted her around his selection of special goods. Then he saw Adrian’s mark glowing on her wrist. He swallowed the rest of his patter and led her directly to the best he had to offer.

The carriage was black lacquered over the top of what appeared to be spell-hardened wood panels. When Maureen rested her palm against one of the panels, it was cold, faintly so, like a mirror in winter. Within the grain of the wood, tiny dark particles moved like frost forming.

[Body: spell-hardened. Panels exhibit dark particles. Horses: horn-scale, black.] *I am unable to determine the extent of the hardening without striking the object. Please do not strike the object.*

The two horses hitched to the carriage were impressive, with ridged plates of horn running down their foreheads and black scale running in rows down their flanks. The animals’ breath steamed in the cool air, and they smelled of hot iron and horse. One of them snapped up a slab of raw beef from the seller’s hand.

Maureen purchased both the carriage and the team, paying for them through Adrian’s access.

*Adrian’s access accepted. Payment logged,* Tally said.

*That makes two things of his you’re taking out of here,* Tally added.

*Three.* Maureen replied silently.

*What’s the third?*

*Me.*

Maureen loaded the carriage herself. The crates containing the grenades went under the seat, while the Sodara trunk was lashed down with leather straps. The potions were packed in straw, and the loot bag sat at her feet. Then she rested her hand on the cold wooden panel again, feeling it as the horses stamped and blew, sending steam curling through the air. It was iron, horn, and her own stones, ready to ride the lift whenever the order came. All she had to do was give the order, and they would be pulled up out of the hole.
