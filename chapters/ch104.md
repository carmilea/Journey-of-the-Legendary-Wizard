The small watch-room perched at the head of the garrison’s wall-walk in the Panso Mountains was quiet in the deep night. One candle burned on the rough pine table, tilting slightly towards the half-length projection of Daniel. His form still danced and quivered like air over a stove, but he breathed a chill that smelled like a cellar. Through the arrow-slit, a cold wind blew that smelled of pine resin and the old smoke from the raids. In the darkness along the parapet, the rune nodes Maureen had laid there glinted a faint silver, reflecting back the light of the torches that guttered in their iron brackets, spitting and popping as the wind caught them. Down below, the sentry’s boots scraped against the flagstones of the yard as he walked his rounds.

His words struck Maureen like a hand closing around her throat. A Demon-Face Flower, practically next door. Inside his five kilometre range. That was the one thing left standing between her and the path to an Ancient Wizard. Her face remained smooth and calm both for the grinning projection of her post-mate and for the sentry whose feet scraped past outside the watch-room. But under the table her fists clenched, the nails biting into her palms with the pain of frustration.

[Minai: 85%. Missing: Demon-Face Flower.]

Maureen didn’t even have to ask Tally to put the line before her eyes. She knew it well. *I know,* she told Tally silently.

Tally was smugly warm in her head. *Just thought you might like to see how badly you want it. That’s when a person generally stops looking where she puts her feet, you know. Just showing you what you already knew.*

She kept her voice low and flat, not wanting the sentry to hear anything amiss. “Where? And how sure are you?”

Daniel’s laugh sounded loud, even coming down a wizard’s thread. “Sure? Maureen, I’m certain! We got drilled on it down in the Forest, remember? Rare plants, and this one above all. Every apprentice knows its face.”

Tally obligingly ran Daniel’s claim against the records of her lessons. [Public courses attended: 41. Demon-Face Flower lecture: none on file.] *You either missed it or it never happened.*

*I missed none.*

Tally’s tone was warmly impish. *Yes, I know. I was there.*

She held the two holes side by side in her mind, the hole in Daniel’s story and the hole in her Totem. The second hole was bigger. The wind came up again through the arrow-slit, tugging at her cropped black hair. The torch in its bracket outside the door popped again, spitting another glob of melted tallow to the flags below.

“I’m north-east of the wall, deep into the pines,” said Daniel’s voice through the brand. “Please hurry, before someone else stumbles across it. Hurry. Please, hurry.”

A brand that could carry a voice could carry other things, Tally pointed out. *Are you going?*

*Yes,* she thought silently.

*Then go armed and go awake.* Tally flashed the count into the corner of her sight.

[Grenades: 16]

Maureen’s hand went to her left sleeve without her willing it. That was an old reflex, left over from Olit’s shop. Tally peered through Maureen’s eyes at the cloth. *No dust. Not there, not on your sleeve. There’s nothing there that matches the first Daniel’s powder. One Daniel at a time, please.*

Maureen checked herself the way she always did. Freya’s slender sword rode at her hip, its grip cool and familiar beneath her fingers. The belt kit was in place. The void pocket lay snug against her waist, as light and empty as any purse. Yet, it held almost all her worldly goods.

She told no one. She swung out between two of her own rune nodes, dropped from the wall-walk parapet, and fell into the darkness beyond the garrison’s wall. As she passed between the nodes, their faint hum brushed against her teeth. It was the same hum Doug had thanked her for that evening. Then the garrison’s torchlight was behind her, and the pines swallowed her whole.

* * *

Maureen moved through the trees at a knight’s pace, then took to the branches. She leapt from trunk to trunk, moving faster than was wise. She knew it and did not slow down.

The needles absorbed every sound from her landings. Resin clung to her palms and made them slick. Her breath condensed to white smoke on the frigid air. The pine woods smelled of wet stone. Like the rock beneath the soil was sweating.

Tally’s route ribbon lay ahead of her, slithering between the black trunks like a snake made of dim blue light. So far, no red or yellow threat marks had appeared along its length. *No sources in view,* Tally said. That didn’t prove there weren’t any sources, just that none were in view. Maureen knew this.

*Then slow down,* Tally said.

Maureen didn’t.

Her particle sense reached out into the dense pines, searching. There was nothing but the normal, thin dark that hung around any forest. Nothing thick enough to worry about anywhere. On another night, an empty forest would have seemed suspicious to her, but not this night.

Up ahead, Daniel’s pale face appeared through a gap in the trunks. He smiled and waved, his golden hair catching the starlight. Then the shadow of a tree trunk slid across him, and he was gone again.

Maureen dropped from a branch and landed hard beside him, needles crunching beneath her boots. “Where is it?” she asked. The words came out sharper than she had intended, but she didn’t take them back.

Daniel pointed into a small clearing beyond him. His face was turned so that the black shadow of the branch lay across his features, making them unreadable. Maureen didn’t try to read them.

It stood alone in the center of the clearing. A black stem rose from the ground, and white flowers clustered at its head. Black veins ran through the petals, twisting and turning like living things until they knit themselves together into the outline of a human face, with a half-smile carved from the flowers’ white flesh.

Maureen’s breath caught in her throat, and stayed there.

A faint sweetness reached out to her from across the distance. Lilies crushed beneath a boot, layered over something rotting. Beneath that, just at the edge of hearing, someone was giggling.

The hairs rose along her forearms.

She knew what it was before Tally spoke.

Two years of pharmacology had burned the face of this plant into her memory.

[Demon-Face Flower.]

But Tally matched it anyway.

[Visual Match: plate. Confidence: high]

[Acoustic Match: laughter. Confidence: high]

*I was first,* she told it silently.

[Allowed.]

Maureen took another step forward, then drew back one of the lantern’s shutters by a crack. The soil surrounding the plant’s stem seemed undisturbed. The pine needles lay upon it as they always fell, their brown edges uncut, no fresh dirt visible beneath them.

Still, something wasn’t quite right. Her particle sense picked up on it, but she couldn’t tell what. It was a wrongness surrounding the plant, like one note sung a hair flat against a choir.

Tally laid two frequency bars across her vision, one for the Flower, the other for the baseline of the woods, pulled from her particle log. They didn’t match up.

*It does not belong to this soil,* Tally said.

*The soil looks right,* she told it.

*Soil can be carried,* Tally replied. *Particles cannot be faked so quickly.*

Maureen frowned at the half-smiling face, the beauty with a hook in it. Then she turned her head and called out, “Daniel!”

There was only shadow and an indentation in the carpet of needles where he had been standing.

She wasn’t surprised, just cold. The cold settled into her gut and stayed there, level and unchanging. Her heart kept its pace.

[Pulse: 66. Steady.]

Tally said nothing else, and that was a warning in itself.

* * *

Figures began to step out from between the trunks, one after another. No rush or panic, just a slow processional. First one, then another, then another, until they formed a ring around the clearing.

Collar after collar caught the light from Maureen’s lantern. Silver coiled pins glinted in the edges of the light.

Maureen felt the density hit her particle sense all at once, like someone had thrown open a door onto a crowded room. A moment before there had been nothing, and now the clearing pressed against her skin from every side.

[Sources: 18.]

[Frey Olivia: Third-Class, top of column (on file).]

16 Third-Class, by calibration.

Daniel.

Tally’s overlay flooded with marks. *They were masked until they revealed themselves,* it told her. *I have no data on how.*

*Seventeen, plus your friend,* Tally said.

*He was never my friend,* she told it silently.

Tally adjusted the line where she could see it.

Logged as friendly. Unconfirmed. → Confirmed: not.

Behind the leading robes, Daniel walked into the clearing. His head was down, his hands clasped behind his back in the posture of a servant. That loud laugh was gone, as though it had never belonged to him.

The man at the front of the group looked her up and down. “Maureen. It’s been a while.”

“Frey,” Maureen replied slowly. Each word got its own weight, its own time. “Frey Olivia.”

His smile was the same one she remembered from the Central District Hall. Mild, pleasant, and fixed in place like a painted mask. Above the smile, his eyes were cold enough to frost glass.

Frey tilted his head. “Surprised?” He didn’t wait for an answer. “You didn’t really think that a family like ours would just let a debt walk off into the mountains, did you?” His mouth curved into something more than a smile. “It’s time you paid that debt back.”

*That brand.* Tally spoke in its cold, precise voice. *That’s how they knew where you were all along.*

*I chose to take it,* Maureen answered silently.

*You did. Now choose what to do about it.*

She smiled, and looked over Frey’s shoulder at Daniel. “So there’s no talent at all. Your famous nose for treasure. All of it set up, from the first night. To walk me out here.”

Daniel lifted his chin, sneering at her. “Talent like that, in a lowly apprentice? Come on, Maureen. You actually believed it?”

Maureen Olsen didn’t bother answering him. She had stopped caring long ago what dead men thought of her.

She crouched down beside the Flower, keeping her back half-turned to Frey as she slowly ran her fingers over the white blossoms. “All this, just to bring me out here,” she murmured. “Generous bait.”

The petals felt cool against her skin, waxy and smooth. The giggling was just barely audible beneath her fingertips. Seventeen pairs of eyes watched her hand move. She let them watch. She didn’t hurry the moment, relishing the attention. A show put on for an audience.

*You are showing off,* Tally said.

*I know,* Maureen replied silently. While they were focused on her hands and the flower, they weren’t watching what else those hands could do. Her pulse line glowed steadily at 68, and Tally kept it displayed where she could see it.

Frey rested his hand on the hilt of his Cross Sword, then looked down at her. The expression on his face was the expression of a man who already won. “You’ve been asking the Forest for this Flower. It seemed like the obvious bait.” He paused, then added, “And you’re an apothecary. If I’d brought a fake, it would have smelled wrong to you. So I had my grandfather open up the family vault.” Frey pursed his lips in annoyance. “Tiresome, but here we are.”

*Barok,* Tally supplied. *That is the authority of the Olivias, Frey’s grandfather. The Olivias maintain a vault with real Flowers in it. Line remaining open.*

Then Frey’s voice turned strange. Almost aggrieved. “Without Adrian, I’d have just cut you down on the road here. But Wizard Adrian is your mentor, so you can’t be murdered. You’ll just... go missing. Too many wizards watch these posts. A body is a story. A disappearance isn’t.”

Maureen nodded slowly. Then Frey continued, his tone darkening. “But Wizard Adrian is no mere apothecary. Elemental Solid State, the peak of First-Tier. Not far from Second, I hear.”

*Hearsay claim added next to Adrian’s line,* Tally said. *Adrian claimed it of Philip. Now an Olivia claims it of Adrian. Either one is boasting, or both are correct.*

Frey Olivia’s pleasant voice suddenly cracked, becoming a loud shout. “This ends here, apprentice! I brought our best: seventeen of us, me included. All Third-Class! There is no way you survive this, Maureen! You’re dead!”

There was a wet pop as Maureen ripped the Flower out by its stem. Clods of soil fell from the uprooted plant as she reached into the void pocket with her mind, and then the Demon-Face Flower was gone from her hand.

The giggle stopped in mid-note.

The sweet, lily-like smell lingered for another breath over the raw hole torn in the black pine needles. Then it, too, faded, dissolving into the cold scent of resin.

[Pouch: +1. Demon-Face Flower.]

*That’s the last one,* Tally noted quietly.

*I know,* Maureen Olsen thought back silently.

“Maureen!” Frey Olivia shouted.

For the first time since she’d met him, his smile moved. It twisted into something ugly and feral.

The ring of black pines closed in on Maureen. The apprentices’ robes whispered against the fallen needles as they advanced. Seventeen Third-Class apprentices working in concert could defeat any other apprentice who wasn’t already a full wizard. She could feel their power in the careful placement of their feet, in the particles they were beginning to gather.

She remained crouched down.

*Odds?* Maureen asked. Tally wouldn’t give any odds that it had no data to calculate with. *Close range with spells?* *Losing,* Tally noted. *Grenades first?* *Different column.*

*Grenades first,* Maureen told it silently.

*Our plan,* Tally agreed. *Especially against apprentices. Against apprentices, grenades are not just weapons. They are arguments.*

Maureen stood up. “Do you know Jack?” she asked. Her voice sounded light, almost as if she were just passing time. “Jack the Ripper.”

The ring hesitated. Every apprentice in the Dark Forest knew that name. Many had envied it.

“Jack? What’s he to you?” Frey Olivia asked, narrowing his eyes. “You know who he is?”

Then he laughed, a cold laugh. “Good. I’d planned to take you apart slowly anyway. We’ll have something to talk about while I do.”

Maureen Olsen smiled, showing her teeth, a flash of white in the narrow yellow wedge of light cast by the lantern. It was the first thing that she had enjoyed since her trial. She made sure that every one of them saw her smile.

“Jack? Oh, I know about Jack,” she said. “I know all about Jack.”

Her hand came out of the void pocket and she held four black iron shells in her palm. They felt cold and heavy in her grip.

> Four. Underhand. Kerrash.

Tally told her, pinning a timer to the arc she would throw the shells with. [2.4 s] floated over the arc like a timer. The seconds ticked away at the bottom of her vision.

[Grenades: 16 → 12]

“Kerrash,” Maureen Olsen murmured under her breath as she flung the shells up into the air with an underhand toss. The shells arced up through the night and into the thickest part of the ring. Frey Olivia’s eyes opened wide as the shells flew overhead. None of them had known she carried grenades.

The clearing exploded with white-orange light. A scorching wind ripped through the black pine trunks, slamming against them and flattening Maureen Olsen’s cloak against her back. Iron splinters whined off the bark. The air suddenly stank of burnt pitch mixed with the sweet lily-and-rot smell of the Flower, plus the tangy smell of singed hair.

> Screams.

Tally logged the sounds. Overlapping. High-pitched. Coming from all sides of the ring.

*How many?* It didn’t matter.

Maureen Olsen watched them thrash in the fire as the shells’ explosions cooked them in their robes. She let the corner of her mouth lift as she watched them burn.

“I told you,” she said. “I know all about Jack.” Her voice was calm, deliberate. “Because I am Jack the Ripper.”
