Maureen pushed into Frostleaf City’s mercenary hall, feeling like she had three days of road lodged firmly in her teeth, and her inner thighs rubbed raw by the saddle. The smell of sour beer and steaming wool assaulted her like a damp hand clapped over her face. Tally ran its count over the benches, scanning the hall through Maureen’s eyes. Three men caught its attention: one walked with a limp, a second had the sole of one boot patched twice, and the third was lean and young, sitting by the door. A limp meant a slow ride ahead, boots patched twice meant money troubles. Debt.

*Why debt?* asked Maureen silently.

*Because debt will ride very far for coin,* Tally replied, *and never ask what is in the bundle.*

A small pile of gold coins hit the mercenary’s table, a tied bundle dropped down beside them.

“West road,” Maureen said. “Three days, no stopping. Don’t open it.”

The man’s hands hovered over the coins for a moment, weighing their worth before he even looked up at Maureen.

“What’s in it?”

“Laundry.”

The bundle held her traveler’s shirt, worn thin with use, and a rag soaked with her own potions. It smelled strongly of her, the scent clinging to everything she brewed, the aroma thick enough to choke on. Whoever was hunting by scent would follow the smell of wizard west along the road.

*Make them smell like you,* Tally said, *then you’ll smell like nothing. You’re welcome.*

*You’re enjoying this too much,* Maureen told it silently.

*I am enjoying the geometry immensely.*

The mercenary sniffed at the bundle, wrinkled his nose, and then the coins vanished into his shirt with a flick of his wrists. Maureen read the decision in the quickness of those fingers. He’d ride west and not once think about who might come asking after the package.

A pale blue ribbon wound its way across the map in Maureen’s head, following the west road, peeling away from her true line running due north. [Decoy 1: west. Divergence: high.]

It was cold the next morning in Maplefall’s coaching yard. The puddles were skimmed with ice, the horse dung churned into the mud by passing hooves. Steam rose from the harnessed teams as they waited for passengers, and the smells of wet wool and sour beer wafted out the open door of the coaching inn.

She hired a woman that morning, an older woman with a scar through one eyebrow. The woman spoke bluntly. Maureen gave her the cloak off her own back, a rag soaked with potions, and the second horn-scaled horse, the remount. She sent the woman east, then south.

The remount tried to snap at the stranger with its flat yellow teeth, and blew steam through its nostrils that smelled like hot iron. Maureen calmed it down with a thick slab of raw beef from her saddlebag. The beef juices dripped down her glove, leaving a red trail on the black scales.

“It eats meat,” Maureen said. “Raw. Lots of it.”

The woman eyed the ridged plates of horn that ran down the horse’s forehead. “What kind of horse eats meat?”

“An expensive one. Keep it moving.”

Before she left Maplefall, she sent several more hirelings out the same way, each down a different road in a different direction. They wore her old clothes, and carried with them the scent of the potions she’d used. She paid each mercenary in gold from the garrison chest.

[Barok: position no data. Est. lag: decoys × days checking.]

*Each decoy bought them another day, maybe,* Tally said. *If Barok was being careful. And careful men were slow men.*

Tally sounded impishly pleased with its arithmetic.

*And if he wasn’t being careful?* Maureen asked silently.

*Then he’ll be fast,* Tally replied, *and angry. But we’re still ahead of him.*

Maureen rode north out of Maplefall on the one horse she had left. She didn’t look back at the woman in the yard. Cold business settled in her stomach like a stone she’d swallowed, and she let it sit there.

She followed the route that Tally had plotted for her, heading off the roads into scrub and empty hills between the cities. Her path wound toward Gladton.

* * *

Maureen walked into the best inn in Gladton just before dawn, knocking the road dust from her coat as she crossed the threshold. It rose in a grey cloud in the guttering tallow light before settling to the floor, which looked as though it had been scrubbed raw by the innkeeper. Behind Maureen, the sky began to lighten.

“Your best room,” she said. “And your best food, sent up.”

The man’s mouth opened, probably with the polite refusal he would give to a young woman travelling alone, with dirt to her knees.

*His eyes are on your boots, Maureen, not your face,* Tally said. *They are on the dust, not you. I’d wager his next word is “Can . . . ?”*

She slapped a handful of gold onto the counter, putting more force behind the gesture than was strictly necessary. The coins clinked against each other and rang against the ancient oak surface. One coin rolled across the counter to the lip, but the innkeep caught it without looking.

The man’s spine bowed like a reed under pressure. His face softened, became eager. “Of course, honoured guest. At once, at once.”

Whatever doubt there had been in the man’s mind had vanished to the same place the coin had gone.

[-1 handful (est.) gold]

*Overpaid,* Tally said.

*I bought his silence,* Maureen responded silently.

*Silence is cheaper in a smaller town,* Tally replied. *Gold this loud makes a face worth remembering.*

*I don’t have time anymore to be forgettable,* she answered.

Halfway up the stairs behind the sleepy servant boy, she turned.

“My horse is outside,” she said. “It may look a little strange to you. That is not the point. Feed it meat. Raw beef. Plenty of it.”

The innkeep’s face worked its way around the word meat. Through the window set into the stairwell, Maureen could see the horse stamping in the yard, its horned plates catching the early light, its breath steaming in the morning air.

*Two stone weight minimum,* Tally said, *judging by its last meal.*

*Logged as horse,* Tally added as Maureen looked down on the man’s face.

Maureen sank into the bath, wincing as the heat touched her chafed thighs, watching the water turn a murky grey from the dust. She ate everything the boy brought her, not even using utensils, and pulled on soft, clean nightclothes that felt like they were from another life.

Lying there in the darkness, she thought about what she’d done. Days of flight, through Frostleaf first, then Maplefall, now Gladton, all to buy her more time before Barok picked up her trail again. The next city was Peaceridge. She would advance there, as planned. Waiting any longer would be too great a risk; Barok might catch her yet.

*Set alarm for ten hours,* she told Tally silently.

[Alarm: 10h 00m. Set.]

[Sleep: 10h 00m. Waking.]

*You asked for ten,* Tally said, *you got ten. Up.*

She ate standing up at the counter, found the beef trough empty and licked clean, and rode north out of Gladton as the afternoon bell rang, a stable hand watching her take the road to Peaceridge.

The fear came with her, a second rider on the horse’s broad back. A weight between her shoulder blades that no distance could shift.

* * *

She could see the grey wall around Peaceridge in the distance, on the horizon, but Maureen turned before reaching it, making her way to a manor house standing on a rise. Slate roofs glinted in the sun, trimmed hedges ran along the front, a single chimney breathed a thin stream of smoke into the cold air.

Maureen had chosen this particular manor because it was tidy. It looked clean and orderly, and most importantly it seemed to be lacking its owner. A handful of servants kept the place in order while the owner was off doing whatever important things owners did. Whatever they were, they didn’t involve being here all that often. Tally counted four from the hedge line at the front of the property, glancing through the window to show Maureen the layer of dust on the owner’s chair. *Nobody comes up here,* Tally said.

Maureen tied her horse deep in the copse behind the house and slipped inside while the four servants were working at the far end of the building, keeping the kitchen and scullery in order. None of them saw her. The rooms smelled of beeswax and air that had grown stale from being shut up for too long. Chairs and settees lay under dustsheets, like great animals sleeping beneath their blankets. The fireplaces were cold and swept clean, no embers waiting to be coaxed back into life.

A thread of cold air brushed Maureen’s cheek as she entered the library, Tally immediately drawing her attention to a seam in the wooden wainscoting running around the room. It was ever so slightly darker than the rest of the wood, a hairline crack between two panels. *There,* Tally said. *The draught is coming through there. There’s something behind that panel.*

The wainscot panel swung open on some hidden catch, revealing a small chamber beyond. The bare stone walls and smoothly flagstoned floor marked it as the kind of room owners built and then forgot about.

Maureen laid down her warning and defensive wards, tracing Gregory’s rune patterns along all four walls of the chamber. The silver lines sank into the stone, leaving only the faintest glow to mark their presence.

Tally verified every node twice, just to make sure. *Please do a third run,* Maureen thought at Tally, silently rather than distracting herself with speech.

Tally ran through the entire ring again, checking every node without complaint. *I expected you to ask me to verify a third time,* Tally said to Maureen once the final checks were completed, sounding quite pleased with itself.

Maureen opened up the void pouch at her belt, withdrawing the Demon-Face Flower. She set it on the stone floor before her, followed by the wizard-grade crystal vial containing the essence blood of the Pankora Scarlet Serpent. The vial was frosted at the neck where the blood rested, and even through the crystal the blood itself pulsed purple-black, like a heart beating with a sluggish rhythm. The flower regarded her with its round white blossoms, displaying its half-smiling black-veined face. Its laughter tinkled faintly in the still air, the only sound in the chamber. Her hands didn’t shake.

*Tally?* Maureen asked silently.

*I know,* Tally replied, and began building a plan from the named sources, visible to Maureen as it worked. First came the promotion notes she’d collected in the Dark Forest using Adrian’s access, then Gregory’s imprint, the pages of Void Eclipse, and lastly her own logs. A pale-blue flicker, and the plan was relayed over. Data and models crowded in behind Maureen’s eyes for a long moment.

[Projected success: 91%.]

*The nine percent is the blood,* Tally said. *I can’t read it. I have tried. It’s like trying to read a book that’s closed.*

Maureen remembered asking Tally to analyse the blood a few days back. It hadn’t even been able to parse it. The Pankora Scarlet Serpent was a level-five creature, while Maureen was only a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice. Desiring control over such power was greed, pure and simple.

*Then watch me,* she told Tally silently.

*Every second,* Tally promised.

She settled herself into a cross-legged sitting position on the cold stone floor, then began to use Void Eclipse to regulate her breathing. Soon her pulse was loud enough to be the only thing in the room, louder even than the Demon-Face Flower’s faint laughter.

* * *

*Monitor me the whole time.* She gave the order silently, then closed her eyes. Her spirit and her mana began to move in ways that they had never moved before, pulling like the tide.

The Demon-Face Flower trembled, its laughter growing thinner, and then it began to break apart. White motes rose from it, countless white motes, which drifted up into the air like snow, then fell towards her.

They sank into her brow, cool against her skin like snowflakes melting upon contact with a warm body.

[Demon-Face Flower: absorbing. Minai 85... 89... 92... 96... 99... 100%. Constructed.]

The readout was given in the cold register. No warmth at all.

At the same time, every rune line Maureen had ever laid into her body blazed to life. They shone all at once, drawing an entire map of fire beneath her skin.

It was finished.

Then, above her head, three faint rune arrays began to form. They hovered in the air over her forehead, overlapping like shadows on still water, spinning and glowing with an internal light.

Her First-Class array, her Second-Class array, her Third-Class array - the ground she had stood upon for two years.

The three arrays began to waver, folding into one another like pieces of paper caught in the wind. They shifted and blurred, forming a single new array, a strange pattern that swelled and moved behind her. The lines burned with a clear, hard light.

[Rune Arrays: 3 → merging.]

The crystal vial burst.

Purple-black blood sprayed through the air, dousing the new array in a torrent of ichor. Every line was stained by the viscous liquid, which dripped down the invisible walls of the array.

The blood sank into the lines and disappeared.

At the same time, the lines began to burn more brightly, shining as bright as molten silver.

The scent of scorched honey and sulphur filled the air.

[Essence blood: released.]

[Bloodline: Pankora.]

[Merging.]

[Minai Totem: ignited.]

A vast torrent of purple-black mist poured from the array, washing over Maureen Olsen and swallowing her up. Within her body, the blood of the serpent ran through every vein, tearing down the old and rebuilding from the inside out.

The pain rose past any measure she had ever known. Her bones ground and extended, her blood was replaced, muscles tore and knitted back together, and her jaw clenched so tightly that her teeth creaked with strain.

[Blood: changing.]

[Muscle: changing.]

[Bone: growth—]

The line broke off mid-word. The display flickered, scattering into a mass of static before going dark.

Even through the pain, Maureen felt the absence. The corner of her mind where the countdowns always ran was suddenly empty. Cold. Like a lamp being blown out in the next room.

Her spirit hit some threshold, and exploded through it like a door being torn free of its hinges by a storm. A surge of mana followed in its wake. The black mist around her blew outward with a dull boom.

Black-red flames erupted in the empty air around her, burning without fuel or heat, consuming nothing. She could feel them burning the air. The array behind her was now a deep purple-black, and a great phantom serpent formed from the mist began to rise from its pattern. It twisted in the air, a serpent of black mist more than twice as tall as a man. Its cold crimson eyes, mere slits in its head, swept over the chamber.

Maureen Olsen opened her eyes. There was a flicker of crimson in their depths, and then it was gone. The power that she’d wanted for two years roared through her veins, and she could feel her sleeves sitting just a bit short at her wrists. She ignored all of it. Her heartbeat was the only sound. For the first time since a white lab bench in another life, there was nothing at all in her head.

*Tally?* she called silently. Nothing.

*Tally.* Still nothing. There should have been something, static or a bracket or even just one cold line of text. She spoke the word into the dark chamber. “Tally.” Her voice sounded strange in her own ears, her throat transformed by what she had become. Then again. “Tally.” It cracked on the third repetition.

Something in her chest clenched into a fist, a fear she’d never allowed herself to feel. Behind her the phantom serpent was beginning to evaporate back into smoke, but she didn’t care about that anymore.

The warmth came before the words.

There was light again, the edges of the display relighting from the outside inward, like frost forming in reverse. Then came the voice, fuller and slower than it had ever been before, but unmistakable.

*I’m here,* Tally said.

*Tally?*

*I’m here, give me a moment, everything is so loud right now.*

*You stopped.*

*I know, I was being rebuilt, like you. I think I’m in the blood now. And the Orb. And the lines.*

*Are you still you?*

*Still me. More of me.* There was another pause, and then Tally said, *Can you hear it? I’m not an it anymore. I think I’m a she.*

*Then you’re a she.*

*I’m glad, because I’d already decided that.*

Maureen laughed once, a wet and broken sound in the cold stone room. She put a hand over her eyes.

[Host: Official Wizard. Path: Ancient Wizard.]

Tally’s voice was immediately there, softening the abrupt announcement. *You did it, Maureen. Not me. You.* She paused for another moment, then added, *I’ll get the numbers right in a minute, but first I want to look at you.*
