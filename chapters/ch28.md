“In a record time like this, your aptitude must be very good, grade five. Perhaps approaching grade six.”

Adrian’s voice never changed from its flat intonation, but there was a small pleased light that flashed behind his glasses for just a second. After all, she was his apprentice.

Tally dutifully filed away the wizard’s estimation beside the Sunset Canyon reading. He had measured the same thing in a different way. That was an interesting data point.

He wasn’t wrong, Maureen thought silently.

Neither was the crystal, Tally said.

“Oh, congratulations!” Winnie said, coming over with a smile. She clapped her hands together and beamed at Maureen. “Well done!”

Mace grinned back at Maureen over his shoulder before he abruptly yelped in alarm, scrambling to rescue a set of beakers and test tubes that were slipping off the edge of his bench.

Adrian’s smile faded away, replaced by a serious expression. “Olsen, have you given any thought to which paid course you wish me to instruct you in?”

Maureen nodded. From Lilith, she already knew how the system worked. Free knowledge was public and generally available. Basic classes were free as well, but those were only the introductory material. Paid knowledge was the core of what each mentor researched.

“Yes, Mentor. Which paid courses do you offer?” Maureen asked.

“I study many subjects,” Adrian responded, “but all my apprentices come for my expertise in Pharmacology.”

Adrian flicked his fingers and three vials appeared, hanging suspended in the air. Each one glowed with a soft light: one purple, one red, and one blue.

“Basic Principles of Pharmacology, Analytical Demonstrations in Biological Pharmacology, Preliminary Conjectures on Biology and Materials Science.”

>Three glowing vials: purple, red, blue

>Three titles

>Basic Principles

>Analytical Demonstrations

>Preliminary Conjectures

Tally displayed for her in order.

Tally’s warm voice spoke softly in her head.

We already have Basic Principles. Derrick carried it, and you took it off his body. Only you and I know this. Adrian doesn’t know that the scroll came from Derrick.

Her stomach dropped. That dead old man had paid for that scroll with his own stones. She had poured the corrosive over it herself.

Help me keep it off my face, she told Tally silently.

Three long, one short, Tally counted in its warm voice. She breathed in time with Tally, until she felt her face stop moving.

Still as a pond, Tally said, a little smug.

Maureen looked up at Adrian. “I choose Analytical Demonstrations in Biological Pharmacology, Mentor.”

“An excellent and wise choice, Apprentice,” Adrian said. The purple and blue vials disappeared, leaving only the red one floating in the air. “You will find both Basic Principles and Analytical Demonstrations useful as you begin your studies. No potion-maker can afford to be without either. The third is merely my own unfinished conjecture, still far too difficult for you to grasp.” He reached out and grabbed the red vial out of the air. Before handing it over to Maureen, Adrian pulled out another black oath scroll and unrolled it. “This knowledge must not be leaked, Apprentice. As with all wizardly transactions, we must adhere to the principle of equivalent exchange.”

Maureen placed her hand on the parchment and swore that she would not leak any of the knowledge. When her oath was finished, the black paper curled into itself and ignited with an orange-yellow flame. It burned until there was nothing left.

Immediately, something cold settled around Maureen’s neck. It felt like a collar, but it didn’t hurt or restrict anything. It just stayed there.

[Unknown binding. No data.]

Maureen asked Tally silently if the oath applied to it as well.

No. The oath only applies to you. I cannot leak any knowledge to anyone. So, you see, I have no one to leak to. However, I am logging the oath as a rule anyway.

“I’m starting then.”

“Very well.”

Maureen pulled out the stopper and downed the red potion in one swift swallow. There was an initial feeling of ice crawling down her throat, before it reached her stomach. Then a sharp pain exploded in her skull, as if she had been hit by a giant hammer. Her vision blurred and darkened from the edges as the pain came again and again.

Tally intercepted the flood of information, just like it did with the crystal ball. It began to sort through everything while giving warnings.

This is going to take a while. This is much bigger than the treasury job. This will be days, not hours.

Adrian placed a hand on Maureen’s shoulder. “Relax, child. It is nothing.” He spoke a few syllables and the pain abruptly vanished.

Adding to heard-spell list… done. Please provide tongue positions for greater accuracy, Tally requested.

Just ask him yourself, Maureen told it silently.

I don’t have a tongue to use. You’re the one who can talk. Ask him.

“Umm, Mentor? If there is something that is hard to understand, can I come to you so that you can explain it?”

“Of course. I wouldn’t charge you for explanations either.”

Maureen remained in the lab for a while longer, listening to Adrian’s list of precautions while Winnie and Mace filled her in on what they learned. Then she left.

* * *

Maureen walked back, rubbing between her brows, trying to ignore the last fading echoes of the hammering behind her eyes.

You know, I would have gone to Adrian for some paid knowledge sooner or later, right? she thought silently.

Yes, but now I can analyse it more days before you need to remember it. That will lead to a deeper understanding, Tally pointed out.

Yeah, but I need to remember to keep studying all the free knowledge and basic courses too.

She did just that over the next few days, attending the basic classes in the Teaching District, and visiting Adrian a few times with questions about things she couldn’t quite understand. Tally would help Maureen organize all her confusions into a nice numbered list every night, and then Maureen would pick out the three most pressing ones that she could ask Adrian without seeming slow on the uptake. Every evening, Tally would post an update on her progress without being asked.

[Analytical Demonstrations: 31%]

And a few days later,

[58%]

Maureen was starting to get closer to Lilith. The other girl had no problems chatting away in her presence. She talked about parties that she went to, her mentor, what knowledge was worth paying for and how much people paid for it. It was all very interesting, and Lilith had a way of talking with her hands, laughing while covering her mouth with one slender hand so that her pale face would flush with embarrassment. She would lean in close when whispering gossip, sitting beside Maureen on the wooden benches in the large echoing lecture halls.

Tally dutifully filed the chatter under ‘useful gossip’, and was careful to remind Maureen that she liked Lilith.

I know, Maureen silently responded. Just log that as unconfirmed.

* * *

Anthony and Richard were already sitting down at a long dark wooden table when Maureen arrived. The two boys smiled and stood up as she approached the table. “Maureen,” Richard said.

“Richard. Anthony.” Maureen nodded to Aaron, Lucy and Alice who sat at the same table.

They’d been separated since arriving at the Dark Forest, each being assigned to different mentors. Once they had found their feet a little, Anthony had suggested this reunion.

“Hello,” Maureen said, taking a seat with her back to the apprentices’ tables. Tally reported no radiation alerts from any of the six people here.

The table was crowded with platters of roast fowl, flatbreads, and a dark stew liberally spiced with pepper. The food was piled high enough for the gravy, fat, and juices to shine under the lamps hanging from the ceiling. The smells of roasted meats and spices hung in the air, over the low roar of voices from all the other tables.

“Maureen,” Richard said, grinning like a fool as always, “I still can’t believe you’re Adrian’s apprentice! I heard he’s famous across the Dark Forest! You must be learning so much. You’ll be a potion-maker soon!”

“Congratulations,” Aaron said quietly, and Lucy and Alice both smiled at Maureen. There was a strange look in their eyes.

What is that look? Maureen asked Tally.

It is a mixture of envy due to your placement, mixed with their memories of you on the ship, Tally explained.

Do they fear me?

A little, yes. Grateful too... both columns.

Anthony frowned. “I hear potion-makers make good money eventually, but they burn through resources early on. If you need anything, just ask. We came out together. We’re together.”

Tally noted that this was Anthony’s third time trying to recruit Maureen into his group. Notice how his thumb is on his cuff again? Gold thread there. Tally displayed [Magic stones: 112].

“Fine,” Maureen said, and the boy nodded.

She had more than a hundred magic stones in the pouch under her robe, liberated from Derrick’s pouch and the dead black-robed seed, which would see her through her early needs.

As the conversation progressed, it became more relaxed. One seed moaned about how his randomly selected mentor smelled foul while another complained about how cold her mentor was and how she had too many apprentices. Richard and Lucy were in the care of a woman wizard named Vivian, who was apparently not strict at all. However, she didn’t seem to be teaching them much either. Richard looked like he wanted to sulk about the situation and Maureen was relieved that he wouldn’t be subjected to dangerous experiments.

When Anthony talked about his mentor, his tone was one of simple delight. Aaron didn’t mention his mentor at all.

Tally highlighted Aaron’s thumb as it worked on the hem of his sleeve, wearing the thread threadbare. Maureen could guess what that meant, but she made the silent decision not to press Aaron. Tally opened a file and labelled it [Aaron].

“Hey, did you hear about Anna?” One of the other seeds asked. “She vanished the moment she stepped into the Dark Forest! Rheas drew Parn though, one of the strongest mentors there is.”

“Lord Parn isn’t that much weaker than Lord Evans,” Alice added with a tone of envy. Her own mentor wasn’t anywhere near as impressive.

Vivian, Parn, and Anna’s disappearance were all filed away by Tally. Alice’s comparison between Parn and Evans was marked as hearsay since Tally couldn’t rank wizards with any accuracy.

They decided that they would attend the public courses offered in the Teaching District together. Those classes were taught by full wizards and were far more advanced than the lessons on the road with Scarlette. Anthony urged Maureen to work hard so she could advance to First-Class Wizard Apprentice soon. “I will,” she responded with a straight face, before taking her leave.

That was very convincing, Olsen. I would have believed you, Tally said.

Behave.

* * *

Her footsteps rang out against the dark stone. The din from the meal receded into the distance behind her. There was an occasional shimmering of motes drifting in the lamplight, like dust floating in a sunbeam. It was more dense near the castle walls, but only she had eyes to see it.

How is the analysis going? she asked silently.

[Task in progress. Screening… Processing. Analytical Demonstrations in Biological Pharmacology: 84%. Remaining: 5h 29m.]

Maureen nodded with satisfaction, and pulled her black robe a little closer as she walked through the cold night air.

Tonight, then? Tally said.

Yes.
