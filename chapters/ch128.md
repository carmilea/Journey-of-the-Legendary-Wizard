Maureen had been brewing for the Eighth Potion Group for a month or two now, and her contribution account was nicely filled. She walked across the cold grey stone floor to the counter. “I’ve come to make an exchange,” she said in Richard’s flat, low voice.

Tally read the man before he even finished bowing. [Second-Class. Lower column by calibration. Age: old.] *Pity. He’ll never be a wizard now. Some member house must have dumped him here. Fetch and carry and run errands for other wizards.*

His grey robe was clean, but darned at the cuffs where they touched his hands, which were spotted with age and shook slightly as he bowed. He bent so low that his chin nearly touched the top of the counter. “Of course, honoured wizard! At once, honoured wizard!”

Once was enough to hear it.

He muttered a short chant and traced patterns on the smoky glass panes with a single fingertip. The panes began to hum, and a screen of grey light peeled away from them. It twisted in on itself and settled into a door-shaped frame. The light behind it was pitch black.

[Private pocket.] Tally laid the lines of the door’s structure over Maureen’s sight through her magic lens. [Keyed to the brand in your palm.] [Exit: on request, by structure.]

*I read the rules,* Maureen responded silently.

*Rules are what people say,* Tally said smugly. *Structure is what a thing does.*

Maureen nodded once to the attendant, who was still bowing, and stepped through the doorway. The door folded shut behind her with a soft sound, like a book closing. The hall was gone.

Black stretched in every direction. She couldn’t feel anything under her boots—no floor, no walls. Her footsteps made no echo, and the only sound she could hear was the rush of her own breath, suddenly loud in her ears. The air was dry and still and cold. It felt like the air in Gregory’s lair beneath Proan Canyon.

Lines of Ancient Andas appeared in the darkness, shining with their own soft grey light, hanging in the air like lanterns reflected on the still surface of a pond. Minerals. Plants. Curiosities. Advanced Knowledge. Enchanted Items. High-Energy Creatures. When Maureen rested her eyes on one of the lines, it opened up to show smaller lines of light beneath it.

Every listing that passed before Maureen’s eyes was recorded by Tally—both the item and its price. A counter appeared in the corner of Maureen’s sight, running up as Tally’s memory filled. [Listings: 1... 2... 3...]

*Go slowly,* Tally said in her head, sounding almost greedy. *I want all of it.*

Maureen did go slowly, and not just for Tally. Ores she had only read about in books, roots still wet from their preserving fields, enchanted pieces with prices that made her teeth ache. Each listing was another twist under her ribs, and Maureen kept her hands loose at her sides.

But there was one thing for which she’d come here above all others. She called up the line for Plants and examined every subcategory, searching for the Necro Flower, the material Void Eclipse needed to advance to its second layer. There was no listing for it, anywhere.

Tally closed the count for her. [Catalogue: 6 categories, 1,214 listings recorded. Necro Flower: absent. Meditation methods: 0 listings.]

*Zero is a number too,* Tally said quietly. *It’s telling us something.*

Maureen’s mouth thinned into a hard, straight line. No method, not even a junk method. She’d run up against this wall before, in Adrian’s neat and false list of requirements. The real road to advancement was locked up by the people at the top, and the best of everything else was locked away with it.

So Maureen turned to Advanced Knowledge, subjects generally reserved for full wizards, and selected three: Illustrated Wave Rates of Energy Particles, Three Conjectures of Anatomy, and Negative Energy Studies and the Undead.

Tally ran the account. [Contribution: ~310 earned. ~250 spent. ~60 reserve.]

*We should keep the sixty untouched,* Tally argued. *Emergencies are a column.*

Maureen silently told Tally to leave it alone. Three bound volumes of paper dropped out of the darkness and into her hands. They were cool to the touch and heavier than they appeared. She slid them into the pouch hanging from her waist and thought about the door. A grey outline opened up in the air in front of her.

Back in the long hall of cold grey stone, Maureen paused to glance over her shoulder at the wall of smoky glass panes one last time, narrowing her eyes. Rime had formed on the outer sills of the windows. The old man’s tallow lamp still flickered on the countertop, its light dancing on the wooden surface scarred by years of elbows. The Second-Class Wizard Apprentice bowed again as the next wizard entered his line of sight.

Maureen looked forward again and walked away from the exchange. *I’m going to need a great many more contribution points,* she told Tally.

*Then the Eighth Potion Group is about to find out what a fast pharmacist looks like.*

* * *

She was done at the Preserve. Corwin caught up with her on the way to the landing ledge, his curly beard liberally dusted with frost. “Goodbye, Richard,” he said, grasping her forearm firmly.

“Goodbye, Corwin,” she responded in Richard’s flat voice.

*He really likes you,* Tally noted. *Colleague logged. Status unconfirmed.* Maureen directed Tally to log him as the pharmacist who shares his burner instead.

The Night-Crown Eagle carried Maureen aloft with a single beat of its wings. The air rushed over her as she ascended, cold and biting. The bird itself smelled of old meat and oil beneath its feathers. The walls and towers of the Preserve dropped away below her, a wave of green that faded into the grey cloud cover.

At headquarters, Maureen gave Ambrose her report of two months’ worth of brews. He read through it carefully, then flipped back a page to re-read it before nodding in satisfaction. “Good work, Richard.”

*No tells observed,* Tally reported. *That is not the same as proof.*

*Then: 0.4 second pause before “Richard”. Possibly nothing, but possibly significant.*

Maureen bowed her head, maintaining Richard’s plain, dull face. Then she left the Alliance behind and walked out into the evening city, the lamps flickering to life one by one along the frozen gutters.

* * *

The old housekeeper opened the door for her before Maureen could lay her palm against it, bowing respectfully. “Ike,” she said from the threshold, her voice coming out flat and expressionless, exactly as Richard’s should. “Dinner. I’m hungry.”

“Yes, Master,” Ike said. “At once.”

He was thin and stooped, his white hair combed straight back across his scalp. His face betrayed no emotion. His eyes never rose above Maureen’s collar. He carried the binding array she had sealed into him, and though she couldn’t feel the full complexity of the thing, she could feel the faint thread that represented its presence within him.

*Ike, steward,* Tally added to the file. *Noted arrival at door prior to contact with gate. I approve.*

*Indeed. He was worth every stone.*

Emilia had slave houses that carried almost anything a wizard could want, from stewards and house maids to exotic fighters. All were well trained, and of the five slaves Maureen had purchased and bound here in Emilia, Ike pleased her most. Though she had never told him so, not even once.

Maureen climbed the stairs to her suite, shut the door behind her, and stepped through her own ward. It was only here, safe and secure behind her personal protections, that she allowed the Mimicry to drop. Tally peeled away the map of Richard’s throat first, then the face, and Maureen’s own voice returned to her ears.

The copper basin by the tub held her real face. Her skin was pale, her bones sharper than Richard’s. Her hair was still black and raggedly cropped, her eyes still black. Steam rose from the water in the tub. The air of the room smelled of hot copper and cedar, from the shelf built into the wall beside the bath.

The ward was visible as faint filaments of gold threaded over the stone walls, knotted at each of the nodes and running as a steady stream. [Ward: holding. Draw 0.1.] *I’ll keep an eye on it while you soak.*

Maureen let herself down into the hot water. She let out a long breath, letting her shoulders drop and her jaw unclench. The stress of the last two months began to evaporate. The tension of brewing, the tension of being a dull young man with no personality, began to seep out of her pores, leaving her limp and relaxed.

*You’re humming,* Tally said. *Ivan’s song.*

*I’m not humming,* Maureen told her silently.

*It says here you are humming,* Tally insisted.

Downstairs, Ike’s footsteps faded away toward the kitchen. There was a clatter as he removed the lid from a pot, followed by the slow, repetitive thumping of a knife on a cutting board. In the entire house there was only one voice that knew her name.

Maureen submerged herself in the tub, sliding down until the water closed over her face. “Wizards, really...” she said into the water, her voice emerging as a blurred, indistinct sound that nobody could hear.

Tally waited until Maureen’s head broke the surface again. Then she spoke, her voice low. *Just letting you know that I’m still here.*

*Yeah. I know,* Maureen replied silently, staying underwater for a few moments longer.

* * *

It was dark when she emerged from the house. Maureen waited at the end of the path while Richard’s face slid back into place, then she walked out into the cold frost. A carriage could be heard clattering in the street nearby, the sound of its iron-shod wheels and the clopping hooves of its horses muffled by the snow. She stepped out onto the street, hailed the carriage, and climbed in. The driver nodded, and the carriage began moving. It was not long before they arrived at a tall hotel, lit with candles from the ground to the rooftops. Maureen climbed out, paid the carriage driver five copper coins, and then started up the steps.

[Coin: −5 copper.] *That’s the cheapest carriage journey you’ve taken since we got to Emilia.*

*Don’t put it like that,* Maureen thought.

The driver’s horn-plated horses stood outside, blowing out great plumes of steam into the freezing air, stamping their feet against the cobbles. Each time they moved, their overlapping scales clicked together. Warm air drifted out of the open hotel doors, thick with the scents of beeswax, wine, and a dozen different perfumes. Beneath that was another scent—sweat, and the musk of people dancing.

A ball was in full swing on the ground floor. The sounds of violins and harps floated out as Maureen entered, accompanied by the rustling of silk dresses and the shuffling of men in dark tailcoats. Candles flickered from chandeliers above, reflecting off mirrored walls and the dancing couples below. Maureen didn’t pause, but walked straight through the room, heading for the stairs.

The music tugged at something old within her. When she had been Jing Sheng, she remembered standing at the back of a hotel dining room, listening to a string quartet play. She’d never danced to such music before, and it meant nothing to her. A woman brushed past, her green dress sweeping against Maureen’s sleeve. She laughed, and didn’t step back to give Maureen more room. These western aristocrats weren’t accustomed to making way for robes—most of them had wizard blood in their veins. A young man stepped on Richard’s foot, turning to apologize as if he were addressing an equal.

*No one in this room has stepped aside for you,* Tally said. *Logged as “western manners”.*

*Log the stairs instead,* Maureen thought as she began to climb.

Tally obliged, counting each step.

Maureen reached the fourth floor, then let herself into the small room she had hired by the night. She locked the door behind her, then stood in the darkness and recast her Mimicry. Richard’s weak jaw disappeared, replaced by Night Owl’s broad cheekbones and dragged-down mouth.

Tally mapped the new throat, walking the nerves down Maureen’s neck. [Mana: 15.6 → 15.2.]

Maureen tested the new voice under her breath.

*Like a cartwheel on gravel,* Tally said. *Not Jack. Good.*

She opened the pouch, and drew out the plain black robe of wool. The black lacquer mask came next. Freya’s slender sword stayed in the pouch. Maureen held the mask up to her face, feeling the wood press against her skin. The cold spread from the mask, settling into her cheekbones.

Tally peeked out through the eyeholes, checking the fit against the reflective glass of the window. *Black,* she said. *Both.*

Maureen turned away from the window, drawing the black robe over her shoulders and pulling it tight with the corded belt. Then she climbed one more flight of stairs, stepping onto the fifth floor. She did not knock before she pushed open the door and looked around the room.

“You’re all here.”

The other Bloodcoat members stood or sat around a single shaded lamp. Their clothing was a mismatched assortment—some wore robes, others cloaks, one wore an elegant suit—but every face was hidden behind a mask. The curtains were drawn against prying eyes, but the music of the violins floated up from below, vibrating through the floorboards. The air smelled of beeswax and lamp oil, with an undercurrent of wet wool.

Maureen stood quietly for a moment, feeling her breathing slow. Her lungs fell into the familiar rhythm of the Silver Light. Something hungry settled behind her mask, and her movements grew smooth as she slipped inside the persona of a black wizard. One who had grown up killing.

Tally noticed the change in Maureen’s breathing pattern, but asked no questions. Instead, she showed Maureen a simple line of text:

[Pulse: 62]

Tally let the line hang, unaltered.

Night Owl had a name now. A name that echoed within the Bloodcoat Assassins’ Guild, a name that whispered through Emilia’s underworld. “Night Owl.”

One of the assassins approached, her voice flattened by magic until it was as featureless as the rest of her. “Owl.”

“Nightingale.” Maureen’s voice ground like a cartwheel on gravel.

“Everyone’s here. We go.”

Bat. Tally lined up all the data she had on this man, laying them side by side for easy comparison. The way he held his weight forward, on the balls of his feet. The way his shoulders were set, as though he wore a high collar on his robes. The grain of density that ran through him, visible in several previous jobs.

[Bat = Gus. Confidence: high. Not proof.]

*Heh. A core wizard, part of a white order.* Behind the black lacquer of her mask, where none could see, Maureen smiled. Through the eyeholes, her eyes rested upon him for a beat longer than they did anyone else.

*Who keeps losing to you inside the rules?* Tally asked. She outlined his hands with an amber line. They were both clenched into fists, hanging at his sides.

He had come after Night Owl on every job since the Tower. Bat raced Night Owl to every target, tried to countermand her plans, but each time she had beaten him. She always got there first, and each time her plan proved solid. Each loss had cost him more face within the guild.

*The contract clock is still running,* Tally reminded Maureen. [Bindings: 4.] He couldn’t try to strike her. Now all he could do was continue losing contests against her.

*Good,* Maureen told Tally silently. *That works fine for me.*

Gus stood stiffly under Maureen’s gaze. “Owl,” he said through clenched teeth. His fists remained balled up at his sides. She gave him nothing in return, and followed the other masks out into the stairwell.

* * *

[Night: 3 passes. Ike 06:10. Ward drew 0.1.]

Tally gave her the night’s report when the light was thin enough to read by. *Logged. Not shown.*

[Mana: 16 → 15.6]

Maureen re-cast Richard before the household began to stir. Then she sat down at the desk and opened Negative Energy Studies and the Undead, turning one spread at a time so that Tally could record it.

*Wait,* Tally said as Maureen turned the fortieth spread. The author had cited an advanced text on necromancy by name. Unfortunately that title wasn’t listed anywhere in the 1,214 entries. *Logged as a gap,* Tally added.

A thin frost feathered the corners of the window. Maureen’s hands were clean, her collar was straight, her breathing slow and even.

There was a knock on the door. “Master?”

“Come in, Ike.”

The elderly man entered with a tray. He set it down on the clear corner of Maureen’s desk without disturbing the book. A bowl of porridge with honey, a plate of warm bread, a small pot of tea, and a silver spoon laid carefully across the top of the plate so the handle was perfectly straight. Ike’s face revealed nothing. He kept his eyes fixed on the tray, taking care to stand exactly a pace away from the desk.

He didn’t leave.

“Master, there is something I should tell you.”

“What is it?”

“A wizard apprentice named Joanna came to call several times. You were never in, so she left these.”

Ike gently placed a small stack of letters beside her plate. The paper was good quality, thick and heavy, handmade with deckle edges. Each one was addressed to Wizard Richard in a careful hand. The folds of each letter were closed by a seal that gleamed faintly with greenish power.

Tally read the seals with her. The spirit-power signature matched the one on file for Joanna.

Maureen set the silver spoon down on the tray. Ike bowed and withdrew. The door clicked shut behind him.

For a moment she simply looked at the small stack of letters, thinking about the Jadeleaf apprentice.

Then she broke the first seal, unfolded the paper, and began to read.
