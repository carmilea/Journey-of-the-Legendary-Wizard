The world outside the curtained carriage looked like the inside of a throat. The rocky roadway was wet black stone, and the dim grey murk above it had no sky. The first dead wizard sprawled beneath the far wall of rock where the green glow had been just a moment ago.

Maureen sat like a cat perched on a windowsill. One finger hooked through the curtain, holding the cloth open by the width of a finger, she didn’t move. The leather seat was cold beneath her robe, and her breath came slow and even. Outside, the horned beasts stomped and blew steam, and even through the curtain Maureen could smell their fear as a sourness on the air.

*Tally?* Maureen asked silently. *Do you have a match for what I saw against everything on file?*

*I already matched it,* Tally replied. *Bestiary scroll, you unrolled it in the Oserroba archive.* Tally overlaid the faded sketch created by the scribe’s ink on top of Maureen’s memory of green eyes and white-hot flame.

>Carls Shadow Leopard.

>Source: Oserroba archive bestiary.

>Plate match: sketch, flame colour, shadow behaviour.

>Lineage, legend: abyssal hunters from the cracks of space-time crossed with fierce leopards.

>Fire.

>Near-pure shadow talent.

>Strength: near Solid State.

>Label: archive, hearsay.

>Solid sample: none.

*The scribe who drew it made it smaller than it bites,* Tally added.

*What about the wizard ranks?* Maureen asked. *What did the archive say about those?*

>Rank, Phantom to Liquefied: significant difference in raw power.

>Rank, Liquefied to Solid: nearly a promotion in itself.

>Rank, Phantom to Liquefied: Phantom State wizard with a good plan can kill a Liquefied wizard.

>Rank, Liquefied to Solid: Liquefied wizard almost never kills a Solid State wizard.

*That puts the Carls Shadow Leopard stronger than everyone here,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*Than everyone here who is fighting it,* Tally corrected her.

Outside the carriage, the hired wizards finally noticed the body. Their voices climbed, becoming thin and quick. “Who . . . ? How did she just drop like that?”

“Did anyone see it?”

The wizards stared at each other instead of looking at the darkness around them. They huddled together near the center of the roadway. Breath steamed from their mouths, and their lamps and spell-cast lights bobbed and wavered. The thump of a staff butt knocking against stone echoed in the still air. To Maureen they looked like sheep, packed into the middle of their pen, trying to get away from the edges.

Tally was on alert for the thing she had first noticed at the green glint, nothing where there should be something. She found it. A pool of shadow beneath the feet of one of the grey-robed wizards was empty, according to Tally’s particle sense. Too empty. [Shadow: masked source, probable. Under grey robe. One breath.]

One breath. That was all the time Maureen had to make a decision.

If she cried out a warning, she would reveal herself. A voice from Richard’s carriage would cause every head to turn. Every pair of eyes would be fixed on her, and Jackman’s deep-set green eyes would be first among them.

Maureen remained silent.

The grey-robed wizard screamed and collapsed backward onto the rock, half his head gone in the creature’s bite. His remaining eye was fixed wide in terror at something that none of the others had seen.

*Logged,* Tally said.

*And I have logged your choice as well,* Tally added in her own voice a beat later.

*I know,* Maureen answered silently. Her finger didn’t waver on the curtain.

The shadow-leopard was hovering above the roadway, as if held up by invisible strings. The beast was dark grey, but its outline was blurred by the white-hot flame that played around it. The fire didn’t seem to give off any smoke or even much light. It was as though the dim grey gloom of the Underworld were sucking up what little radiance the flames could provide. Even so, the scent of hot iron and scorched stone slipped through the finger-width gap in the curtain. Maureen’s pulse didn’t rise from its steady 64 beats per minute.

“Carls Shadow Leopard!”

Someone shrieked out the creature’s name, and faces went grey all along the column, as boots slid back on the slick, wet rock without anyone making a conscious decision to move.

“Careful!” the wizard with the knife scar down his face shouted, talking fast, apparently as much to himself as to the others. “It’s nasty, but there’s plenty of us. It’s nothing. We can take it. Nothing to worry about. Nothing at all.”

Tally flagged the man’s pulse, hammering away at the base of his throat, before she flagged something else.

Flame-light extinguished where the leopard had been hanging. [Shadow pooling. Left of the scarred man.]

The leopard was no longer hanging in midair. Its claws had come up out of the shadow to the left of the scarred wizard. The razor-sharp talons were long enough to open him from hip to shoulder before Jackman’s lightning bolt cracked across one of the beast’s forepaws, knocking it back. The claws were still a hand’s width from sinking into the scarred man’s ribs when the bolt struck, and he stumbled backward, alive, his face white as death.

Jackman strolled forward without hurrying, sparks crawling over his fingers like crickets. “Killing in front of me? Beast. You’re begging for it.”

Tally opened Jackman’s file and began displaying data live. No audible chant preceding the spell. A mere flick of two fingers. Through the Mirror Pupil lens, the structure of the lightning bolt became visible. It was a short, tight braid—cheap to make and quick to throw. [Jackman. Cast 1: no audible chant.]

The leopard snarled at the old black wizard, and the sound was all too human. It crouched low on the empty air, its flame wrapping closer around it, flaring whiter, and its eyes burned a brighter, deeper green, like lamps turned up in a darkened room.

Jackman glanced first to Claude, eyeing him for half a second before turning to roar at the hired wizards. “It’s strong, so what? Look how many of us there are! All of you, together!”

Jackman called them forward and took a half step back himself as he did so, Tally noted. *He’s going to spend them.* Maureen instructed Tally to underline that observation. *Already have, twice,* Tally answered silently.

The leopard bunched its haunches beneath itself, its shadowy body folding into a springboard that would send it streaking across the empty air toward the old wizard. Tally pinned a readout over it: [Carls Shadow Leopard. Fire, shadow. Match: Oserroba archive, plate. Strength label: hearsay.]

As the leopard came, it was a blur of shadows overlapping and sliding over one another. It was three or four cats at once, then one as it covered the distance between itself and the black wizard.

The road exploded as a dozen spells were cast all at once. Flames burst from the rock, the sour, rotting green of acid sprayed in a wide arc, frost flashed in a cone, a cyclone of cyan wind spun up, and a strobe threw the cave walls into being and out again in blinding flashes. Each strobe illuminated the edge of the curtain in flashes of brilliant white light. The carriage filled with the smell of ozone and burnt fur.

In the strobing light, Tally kept her eye on Jackman alone. Three bolts of lightning lanced out from him. 1.8 seconds between the first and second, 2.1 seconds between the second and third, and the third bolt forked in half to strike at two different points along the leopard’s body as it emerged from shadow into substance. Jackman’s breathing was harsh after each bolt, but he recovered quickly. His casting rate was steady, and the intervals between his breaths were decreasing. Tally refined his readout against the Barok sample and the Maffie sample: [Jackman: Liquefied, label Claude’s word. Density: above Maffie. Cast rate: steady. Recovery: fast.]

The leopard broke off its attack, bleeding from a dozen wounds, and tried to slink away. It didn’t get far. Claude closed one end of the gap in the dark, and Jackman moved to close the other.

A blade of darkness formed in the empty air between them, flashing once between the two wizards. The head of the leopard came free with a final thin whine of pain.

The head rolled across the rock floor, the white fire flaring brighter for a moment before beginning to flicker and gutter out of the body like the flame in a dying lamp. For a long moment, the road was silent except for the snorts and stamping of the beasts. Even the hired wizards were quiet, stunned by the suddenness of the creature’s defeat.

*Who?* Maureen asked silently.

*No data,* Tally answered, equally silent. *Two dense sources at either end of the gap, with the blade between. Can’t see the hands with all that strobing.*

Jackman stood panting over the leopard’s head, a shallow cut on his forearm. He sneered down at it. “Some high-energy creature. A beast is a beast, no matter what.”

* * *

Outside, in groups of two or three, the wizards began butchering the leopard. Maureen heard the rasping of saws biting into bone, the hissing of hide as the last heat fled the flesh, the forced laughter of one wizard a bit too loud and a bit too long over nothing at all. She let the curtain fall.

She never stepped out of the carriage. The old black wizard had been watching Richard for days now. Every time she cast a spell in front of him, he collected another sample to study at his leisure. Tonight, she collected his instead.

*Jackman’s eyes passed over your carriage twice during the fight,* Tally said in her warm voice. *Both times they moved right past it without stopping.*

*Like he was looking at a hired man?* Maureen asked.

*Yes, exactly like that. He sees Richard, but Richard is boring. Just another wizard on the payroll.*

At Maureen’s request, Tally filed away the roster of who had fought well, and how far each wizard’s real power was from the density they carried. Two wizards were better than they looked, while three were worse. The one with the scars was louder than he was strong.

Maureen leaned back against the cold leather of the carriage seat and stretched. Her hand went reflexively to the empty inner pocket where the Seal had ridden, before it became the core of Edward. She removed her hand and felt the weightless object at her waist—the folded form of Edward tucked into the pouch hanging from her belt.

[Jackman. Liquefied: Claude’s word. Density: above Maffie, below Oran’s attendant. Spell effects: lightning. Audible chanting: none detected. Time between casts: approximately 1.9 s. Recovery time: fast. Wounds received: one, shallow, left forearm. Held back during battle: no data collected.]

*Could I kill him, Tally?* Maureen finally asked the question she’d been saving since Hastan. *If it came right down to it, could I kill him with all the tricks and toys I have?*

Outside the lowered curtain, the hired wizards were busy butchering the leopard. The sounds of wet sawing and tearing reached the carriage along with the smell of burnt fur and hot iron. The cold from the deep rock seeped through the panels of the carriage.

Tally began working on the odds.

Her own log indicated that Jackman had made four spells in the fight, but that was not enough to base a full set of odds on. So Tally had to include Maureen’s Dark Erosion Fireball at an estimated 17 degrees, the Orb’s 61 percent cycling rate, the Totem’s +1.2 degrees on Maureen’s spells and −0.9 degrees on the wizard’s, and the Pankora bloodline, which had yet to be released.

[Odds at full power: favourable. Confidence: medium. Cost: high. Unknowns: what he held back.]

Maureen mulled over the data. Jackman was the most powerful wizard on this road. If she could kill him at whatever price was required, then the risk of this entire expedition would fall within her control.

Her mouth curved into a smile. In the dark glass of the window, Richard’s mild, forgettable face smiled back. For just a moment, it did not look so mild.

*You’re smiling at a man you’ve just decided you could kill,* said Tally. *I’m logging that as planning.*

*Log it as arithmetic instead,* Maureen answered silently.

*Both columns?* asked Tally with her characteristic smugness.

*Both columns.*

The caravan rolled on. Nothing else jumped out from the darkness, and the road twisted down toward the settlement of the dark elves.

Hour after hour passed, marked by the grinding of the wheels over the black rock. The cold grew deeper, penetrating the carriage. Somewhere in the distance, water dripped. The gloom never seemed to thin, except where some pale lichen grew on the walls, giving them the appearance of frost on a window.

* * *

The gate of the settlement was cut from the living rock, like every other structure that the dark elves had built. The roof of the cave overhead was low enough that there was no sense of sky above. The carved reliefs had been worn down by centuries of use until the images were soft and blurred. Pale lamps burned along the wall, their oils giving off no smoke. Above the gate, dark elves lined the wall, dressed in leathers tooled with silver. Men and women stood together, their pale faces beautifully made, their blue eyes almost black as they moved over the column of men and beasts like hands slipping into a sack to determine the contents.

The air was cold, but carried with it the faintest smell of blossoms that had fallen and rotted away. Maureen inhaled the scent, and for a breath she was back in the realm of the trial, standing in the clearing among the ferns. It was the day Freya died. Maureen could feel the spray of Freya’s blood on her cheek, dark purple and thick. She could feel the grip of the slender sword in her own hand.

Maureen’s own hands remained empty at her sides. The sword lay quiet in its pouch. Only her pulse jumped.

Tally identified the scent for her: [Freya. Pulse up fourteen.] Tally counted for Maureen through three long breaths and one short one before the readout settled at 66.

*Keep your face,* Tally ordered.

*It’s Richard’s,* Maureen answered silently.

*Then keep his,* Tally responded.

Tally called up Maureen’s recognition list and laid it across her vision in a single bracketed display.

[Freya’s sword: in pouch. Jack’s face: not worn. Ani: not in view.]

*I’ll keep watch for her eyes anyway,* Tally added.

Maureen stepped down from the carriage, her face set in Richard’s expressionless mask, her aura pressed firmly into her chest. She was just another wizard they had hired, one more in a column that stretched back for dozens of yards.

Eve came through the gate. She was a middle-aged woman dressed in plain clothing, but her lips were stained red, and a certain dignity hung about her. She was so still that she did not seem to stand there so much as lie in the air, like a blade resting on a table.

Tally matched Eve against two recordings, the muster ground before their entry into the trial realm, and the voice Maureen had heard coming through the gate as the realm had collapsed around them.

[Face match: high confidence. Voice match: high confidence. Eve. Night Mother. Match: muster, gate. Confidence: high.]

Eve’s gaze traveled the length of the column before she spoke, her face going stiff with displeasure. “Mr Jackman. Mr Claude. You have brought . . . a great many friends this time.”

[Eve: jaw set. Breath held 2 s. Spike at throat.]

*That means she was expecting fewer,* Maureen answered silently on another line.

*Fewer than this, anyway,* Tally agreed on a third.

Jackman’s mouth tilted up at the edges, and his green eyes crawled over Eve’s body without even a pretense of modesty. “Why Eve, what’s this? Aren’t we welcome?”

The elves on the wall went motionless, stiller than still. One of the young dark elves, a girl who stood by the gate, dropped her eyes from the intruders to the ground, staring intently at some imaginary feature in the stones. Maureen caught a whiff of something else on the cool air, a scent that overpowered even the faint fragrance of fallen blossoms.

Jackman’s black robe.

It smelled of old smoke, and something else. Sour wine?

Eve laughed, a bright and easy laugh. Her face blossomed into a smile as well. “Mr Jackman, you wound us. Dark elves have always been a hospitable people.”

*She smiled 0.4 seconds after she laughed,* Tally noted.

*So do I sometimes,* Maureen pointed out.

*Yes, I know. I keep track of yours too.*

A tall warrior stood at Eve’s shoulder. A Cross Sword hung at his hip, its broad shape peeking out from beneath his cloak. His knuckles turned white on the hilt of his sword as Jackman’s gaze crawled over Eve, then moved on to linger for a moment on several of the young elf servants standing next to the gate.

[Warrior, unnamed. Density: high. Grip: white. Grip follows Jackman’s gaze.]

Jackman’s eyes passed over Richard without stopping.

Denise stood next to Claude. Her fingers strayed to her sleeve, then remained there for a moment. Claude smiled with one side of his mouth.

He didn’t say anything at all.

Eve clapped her hands once, and a group of dark elves stepped forward to lead the wizards through the gate and into rooms that had already been prepared for them.

The corridors were cool, close, and silent. The stone walls had been etched with fine silvery patterns, but there was nothing else to distract from the stark beauty of the stone itself. The elves walked in soft leather boots that made no sound at all on the floor, but the footsteps of the wizards echoed in the corridor, loud as an ill-mannered guest who’d tracked mud onto a temple floor.

Maureen’s room was carved from the living stone, with a bed covered by furs. A basin of water stood on a table—cold water, very cold—along with a single pale lamp. Even here, deep within the stone, Maureen caught a lingering scent of blossoms, as if even the rock itself had absorbed the fragrance.

* * *

Maureen traced Gregory’s rune across the door before she lay down, and again across the window. Four small golden nodes formed in the stone, then settled, becoming invisible.

[Mana: 20.1 → 19.9.]

Tally checked the ward, then showed Maureen the settlement’s own wards, running through the stone walls like golden filaments, tied off with knots at intervals. Half of them were some kind of weave that Tally couldn’t make out.

*No data,* Tally said. *Which is exactly why I don’t like them.*

Maureen nodded, then lay down on the fur. She left Richard’s face on, though the darkness pressed close around her. The cold from the stone seeped up through the bedding, chilling her skin. She could hear water dripping somewhere far down one of the passages, each drop taking almost a full second to fall, a metronome marking time in the darkness.

She slept, and Tally kept watch for her. Through the particle sense and Maureen’s ears, Tally could monitor the night.

The settlement fell quiet as Maureen slept. Not just quiet, but silent, in a way that a human town never would. No carts rumbled over cobbled streets, no dogs barked in the night, no drunken revelers sang off-key ballads beneath an open window. Even the elves were quiet, their soft-soled boots making no noise at all.

Just the dripping water and the cold.

In the morning, before she opened her eyes, Tally gave Maureen her report.

[Night: 1 dense source, Jackman’s grain, crossed toward Eve’s quarters, 23:10. Back, 23:40. Ward drew 0.1.]

Tally added a second line to the report: [Second dense source: paced one corridor, 40 min. Not Jackman. Grain: dark elf. Match: close to the warrior, not certain.]

Maureen kept lying on the fur, her eyes still closed. *What do you make of it?*

*Two people did not sleep, and one of them walked a floor for forty minutes. It fits one man who came to bargain and one man who didn’t like the bargain. Low confidence.*

*Tally, keep a file open on the pacer.*

*Already done,* Tally said inside Maureen’s head.

There was no dawn, just the lamps along the corridor increasing in brightness by degrees. Maureen splashed the icy water from the basin onto Richard’s borrowed face. Tally checked Maureen’s eyes in the water. They were black, no crimson showing.

The wizards gathered in a long hall carved directly from the rock. The pillars that supported the roof had been tooled with silver, and plates of cold bread and pale cheese sat on a stone table. The hired men and women spoke loudly of leopard hide and what they would get for it when they returned.

Eve entered the room with a smile. “Greetings, friends,” she said, as if they were all old acquaintances. Jackman and Claude sat at the head of the long table.

“That you would come all this way down to help us is a great kindness,” Eve said. “The dark elves will not forget this service.”

[Claude: pulse steady at 81 bpm, no movement, hands still. No tells observed. Not proof.]

And in her warm voice, only for Maureen’s ears, Tally added, *That is the stillest kind face I have logged since Augurit, and I didn’t like his either.*

Eve folded her hands together and began to explain the task they had traveled so far to perform. Everyone in the long hall leaned in to listen to her words.
