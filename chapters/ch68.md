“A great deal,” the image said. “I have told you a great deal.”

“Yes, Mentor.” Maureen knew that she had to find an advanced method or become a junk wizard. It was as simple as that.

She didn’t feel any lighter. Her hands rested calmly upon her knees, still as the tallow candle began to gutter. Adrian continued. “In ancient times, Olsen, advanced meditation methods were plentiful. Almost every apprentice could find something suitable. Today they are rare, and those who possess them guard them greedily. The world of wizardry is being deformed by this sabotage of methods.”

Deformed, Tally said quietly, filing the word next to his account of the sabotaged methods.

Yes, I know, keep listening, Maureen thought back.

“I will not distract you further with these thoughts, however. We have more pressing business.” The old face peered over the glasses. “The Dark Forest has had troubles with several other orders. Soon we will recall our people. If you—”

Maureen gasped, as Adrian’s image was torn apart. A wizardry mark tore into the air, scattering his projected form. The dark particles around the image were sucked away by the mark.

The flame of the tallow candle lay flat against its wick. A coldness rose from the floorboards and swept up her legs, chilling the backs of her hands where they lay on her knees.

The particles began to take shape. They twisted and turned, growing brighter and darker, until a dark-gold scroll burned in black flame. At both ends of the scroll were images of fierce beasts, their heads biting into one another. As the scroll unrolled, the Ancient Fusiform letters upon it ignited, turning into a great screen of burning words. An old voice filled the room, dry and unhurried. With each syllable the letters jumped upon their screen.

“Augurit Jones speaking. Augurit Jones, Steward of the Dark Forest. We declare war upon the Blue Ring, Hader Castle and the Mist Labyrinth, and several other orders.”

“Do not fear. The Dark Forest has deep roots and allies, but it needs its people now.”

Blue Ring, Hader Castle, Mist Labyrinth.

The same three names as Barok's coalition, from the projection you overheard behind the glassware racks in Mentor's lab.

Nothing else on that screen is in my records.

File them together, Maureen thought back.

“From the moment you receive this order, you must return within one hundred days. Those who disobey will be hunted forever, and your souls will burn for a thousand years until they are ash! The Dark Forest calls to its children, to gather once more beneath our dark banners, beneath the wings of the Great Black Dragon. Come home, now. Darkness descends upon the world; the gloom is without end.”

The letters broke free from their form, bursting into loose flame before they faded from existence. The screen followed suit, burning to ash as Maureen watched. The ashes thinned and blew away from each other, leaving the room bare again except for the tallow candle. There was a smell of scorch.

[Recall: 100 days. Day 1.]

The new line appeared at the edge of her display.

Keep the clock running.

Yes.

Adrian's mark was breaking through again, and his image began to knit itself out of the dark particles. He looked puzzled.

“Mentor, I have just received a summons from Augurit Jones himself.” Maureen's voice was composed and calculating as always. “He has declared war against the Blue Ring, Hader Castle and the Mist Labyrinth. He has called all apprentices back to the Dark Forest immediately.”

Adrian nodded. “Yes, Olsen. This is what I was referring to. With the war imminent, resources that are normally unavailable will be opened up. Now, returning will bring you many benefits.”

“Such as?” Maureen asked.

“I cannot promise anything, Olsen, but there is a very small chance that this could mean a true advanced method.”

“And if it is not?”

“I do not know. It is a very small chance, Olsen, but you have shown great merit in a time when the Dark Forest is at its most desperate. Come home, Olsen.”

Very small is not a number I can work with, Tally said.

It is Adrian's honest number. Very small, Maureen thought back.

Adrian's projected face looked even older than he had appeared in his lab. His voice sounded thin, passing through the dark particles that were constantly drifting and turning in their projected space.

“Yes, Mentor,” Maureen said, bowing her head slightly. “I understand. I will speak with you again soon.” She spoke for a while longer, then broke the connection.

* * *

A bench scraped on the wooden floor below. A drunk started singing off key. The smell of tallow and cold ash hung in the air.

Maureen sat at the cheap square pine table. “Should I go back?” she murmured as her index finger tapped against the tabletop in a steady rhythm. Each tap was a dull knock, her finger falling into Silver Light's breath count without conscious thought from Maureen.

Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap. On one of those taps, knight force flowed down her finger unbidden. With a sharp snap, a hole the size of a fist exploded out from under her fingertip. Splinters flew in all directions, but the rest of the table remained standing where it was. “Go back? How could I?”

That wasn't a tap, Maureen. How should I log that? Tally asked.

Table, Maureen silently replied.

There were many reasons why going back would be a bad idea. The war could get nasty, even for Official Wizards. A Third-Class Wizard Apprentice's odds weren't good. Adrian couldn't promise her an advanced method, just possibly show her one if he had it.

And she was still in her teens. There was time yet. As an apothecary she could earn stones. With stones she could buy resources. Even with an honest Adrian, she would have to find a real advanced method herself.

Maureen Olsen, Deserter, Day 101. That makes you a deserter too. Should I stop counting? Tally asked.

No. Keep counting. I want to know the day they start hunting for me.

Getting beyond the reach of the Dark Forest could wait until she reached the secret realm. The materials she bought at Grey Dove, partially for dealing with Bina and partially for breaking the realm's guards, were too close to Red Rock to waste. She double checked the window and the corridor before she started unpacking the black bundle.

Low incantations murmured through the room as Maureen worked the materials through the night. The candle was reduced to a stub by the time she was finished.

* * *

The next day, she came down the stairs calm, refined and bearing the air of nobility. “White bread, please. Some tender beef with mushroom soup.”

Conversation around the nearer tables dropped as she walked past them. Heads bent back over their bowls as she sat.

The worker was eager to serve her, rushing back and forth. “Yes, honoured guest. At once, honoured guest.” His eyes were full of envy and awe.

[Recall: 100 days. Day 2.] appeared in the corner of Maureen’s display as she sat.

She let it sit there silently.

The bread arrived warm, the crust cracking when she broke off a piece. The beef was tender and the soup had an earthy flavour from the mushrooms.

White bread is still sitting next to Carey in my records, Tally said.

Log it as bread then, Maureen told it silently.

She wiped her mouth with a white cloth napkin, mounted her warhorse and rode out as the people watched her go.

* * *

Two days later Maureen Olsen rode in. Red tree crowns swayed against the blue sky and the soil dust was red. The red tiled houses had not changed either. Maureen smiled as she looked around. “Still the same.”

Her horse’s legs were coated with red dust as she rode through the town. It was quiet in the town, many shutters half drawn against the afternoon sun. This time Maureen had ridden alone. No young man named Burton came out to greet her.

The streets match exactly our records turn for turn, Tally told her.

Good, Maureen thought back.

Maureen took a room in the same inn as before. The innkeeper recognised her, greeted her warmly and began complaining about Burton. “Such a fine young man he was too. I don’t know what happened to him. One day he was here, the next he just wasn’t.”

As he talked his hands worked over the wooden countertop with a grey rag, polishing it to a high gloss. The inn smelt of stale sour ale. Then the innkeeper paused for a moment. “No, that’s not right, he was no good fellow. He was a bastard.”

Maureen just smiled at the man, admiring silently the power of the potion that had annihilated Burton’s body so completely that there was no sign of him.

Nothing in that complaint suggested a body was ever found, just a missing man, Tally said. Just a missing man.

Clean formula then, Maureen thought back.

Yes, I will log your pride in that fact as well, Tally said. Maureen’s room was the same as before, with the same sagging bed and window that looked over the red tiled roofs to the south-east.

* * *

“Time,” she said softly and pushed the wooden window open, vaulting out into the night air outside the inn.

The night was cool on her skin. In the distance a dog barked, sounding like a sentinel announcing her arrival. The red soil underfoot was almost black, like dried blood. She ran silently following the route she remembered taking and reached the paupers' graveyard, to find it unchanged. She nodded, satisfied.

Tally had counted each turn on the way, matching them up to its records. Maureen had let it. Now she stood in the overgrown and untended graves, seeing the loose earth of the mounds and the crooked grave markers that marked the last resting place of Red Rock Town’s poorest. The night insects provided a background ticking sound, and the large stone stood out pale in the moonlight.

This was it. Maureen felt the familiar concentration as the sigil rose to the surface of her palm. It glowed a faint silver as she dispelled her own concealment spell and laid her hand on the large stone.

Light rippled over the stone, like water running over it, as one by one the runes rose from their resting places. The stone shimmered and then disappeared, leaving a deep opening into the ground behind.

I am with you, Tally said.

And I with you, she replied silently.

The air breathed from the opening was cold and smelled of the deep earth. Maureen took a deep breath of the cool night air then walked slowly into the hole. Her head felt heavy for a moment and then she was standing before an estate with an old, weathered looking manor house inside the secret realm.
