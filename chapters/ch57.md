“Aye,” Albert answered, “it be us.”

The Guardian’s voice sounded like rocks rattling around in a tin can. “Prove you have permission, or be gone.”

Albert nodded and reached into his robe. He pulled out a small, flat, black stone token and threw it to the Guardian. Fog beaded up on Maureen’s sleeves as she waited for the Guardian to respond. She looked over at the perches with their dead branches. The crows never blinked, just stared at the riders with their unnerving green eyes.

The Guardian caught the token and its pale green eyes flared as it roared a cracked chant. “Ewanzi!” The word echoed in the valley, and Maureen could see the ripples of sound it created as it traveled through the misty air.

The ripples hit the token and the stone began to vibrate like it was being hit by rings of rain that formed in a still pond after a raindrop. The cracked sound grated against Maureen’s back teeth. A grey mist rose off the token and twisted in the air. The mist formed an image of Albert that was disturbingly lifelike. Even his aura was present, and Maureen could hear his wheeze when the image took a breath.

Sound wave spectrum logged. Frequency analysis complete. Token contains a copy of subject one’s aura. I would like one of those. I could do so much with one, Tally said.

You’d copy everyone who came through here, Maureen said silently.

Already did.

The Guardian handed the token back to Albert. “With a token, one stone.”

Albert paid, followed by Daphne and Aubrey. “Do you have one?” the Guardian asked Maureen.

Maureen glanced up at the creature while Tally compared the density in her particle sense to what was in its file. It was above Gandolf, but just barely.

Maureen thought back to Hugh’s slobbering tongue and Iman’s cackle at the gate to the Dark Forest. She wasn’t sure if the Guardian was human, construct, or something else entirely.

“No token. Five stones.” The green light flashed again.

Maureen smiled as she flicked five magic stones into the air. They zipped towards the Guardian like bolts shot from tiny crossbows. The robes enveloped the stones, and there wasn’t even a ripple to show that they had been swallowed.

[Magic stones: −5.]

Tally projected, and she could feel its smugness. You were just showing off.

No, just paying our way, Maureen said silently.

Both, Tally said smugly.

The Guardian faded away, while the mist grew thinner as Maureen walked down a gravel path. A bustling crowd was up ahead. Daphne clucked with concern. “Four stones more every time you come in. That adds up, you know.”

* * *

Albert and his group headed off to take care of their business, and Maureen continued alone, using the map they provided for her. She’d memorized it, so she didn’t need to pull out her crumpled paper. Tally was kind enough to overlay what she saw on top of the map, making two minor corrections when a row of stalls moved locations, and when a lane was bricked shut since Albert’s map was drawn. The fur district’s poles had rare pelts hanging down, their musk and salt-cured hides perfuming the air. Slates posted high above listed the prices in chalk.

[Purple hair. Upper Knight stance. Watching Daphne's pouch, not her face.]

Tally flagged a burly man laughing with Daphne.

Daphne isn’t my concern, Maureen thought back silently.

Logged: Not my concern, Tally responded.

The weapons district rang with the sound of forge hammers, the noise carrying clearly through the fog. The air tasted like coal smoke and hot iron.

* * *

“Welcome to the Hammer Shop! How may I meet your every need?” The owner of the largest weapon shop in the weapons district hurried over to greet her.

“I need a Cross Sword.”

The temper of her old Cross Sword had been ruined by Patton's purple fire, leaving a third of the blade's length brittle and useless. It also had several nicks, making it unsuitable for combat.

The heat from a forge at the back of the shop pressed against Maureen's face, and a trough of water hissed as steam rose from newly quenched metal. The smell of oil and iron filings hung heavily in the air.

The owner's patter rolled on behind Maureen as she looked over the Cross Swords, their bare blades hanging in racks along the wall like ribs inside an animal. They felt cold against the tip of her finger. Tally measured each sword's balance in her hand, and examined the edge with her eyes, comparing it against the ideal described in Basic Swordsmanship. The first sword was discarded for being too heavy in the tip, the second for having a slightly wavy edge.

The third sword balanced perfectly, a single finger's width past the guard. She purchased the sword for forty stones, and left her old blade with the shopkeeper for scrap. The new grip settled into her palm, and the wrongness in her hand since Patton's fire quietened.

At her request, the shopkeeper gave the Cross Sword a worn-in finish. He dulled the guard, and scuffed the scabbard. The edge remained keen, retaining a cold gleam.

Can't tell if it's been worn in or not, just by looking, Tally said. Approved.

On a bench outside, and wearing gloves, Maureen coated the edge with the liquid from the blue vial while Tally nagged about the glove. [Edge: blue.] [Magic stones: −40.]

The timer she had set chimed in her head. [Meet: Old Farry's. 00:00.]

Maureen Olsen began making her way to Old Farry's, the settlement's lanes now familiar to her.

* * *

“Old Farry's!” Aubrey announced as they arrived at a shabby general store. “A friend of Albert's, and he's not one to knock off a single coin. Come on.”

Inside, Albert was haggling over prices, red-faced with intensity.

Dust furred the sagging wooden shelves, and the glass jars on them were cloudy with grime. Daphne sat on a stool, working away with her needle and thread, muttering as she went.

Old Farry was about 1.45 m tall, with long dark-green hair that swept the ground. His skin was pale green, and his robes were a matching dark green. From the waist down there was no sign of feet, just a mass of baby-sized hands.

The little hands squirmed through the floor-length hair, and patted against the wooden floorboards with an odd dry whisper.

“Don't stare,” Aubrey whispered. “He had a, um, accident during an experiment, and it rather affected his temper. He wasn't always like this, you know. He used to be quite a handsome young man.”

Aubrey's tone was half teasing, half mocking. Maureen's throat tightened for a moment as she thought of the handsome young man Old Farry once was, then she obediently turned her eyes from the shopkeep to the dusty shelves.

“One hundred more stones, or no sale!” came a shrill voice that sounded like a child in tears. A thick book bound in copper was slammed down on the counter.

[Alchemy. Match: binding sigils, Dark Forest library fragments.]

Alchemy was one of the arts that had been almost completely lost on the Passise. She had managed to find only fragments of the knowledge stored in the Dark Forest.

Tally cross-linked Charles's register, Mace, and the Dark Forest library. [Every alchemist we have on file is someone we would rather not owe a favour.]

Maureen waited until Albert finished his business deal before asking about the alchemical tome. The shopkeep looked up in surprise at her interest, then shook his head. “Not for stones, lady. Alchemy has its price, but there is no market for it.”

Tally matched the shopkeep's faintly sweet and rancid odour with an entry in the Dark Forest library. Experiment backlash still worked within him. Soured Holan grubs emitted the same scent.

Maureen sent her voice down a thin thread of particles. “I have a suppression potion.”

The shopkeep's expression changed drastically.

“Yes,” Maureen whispered, “I'm an apothecary.”

Her pity had somehow transformed into a lever while she wasn't looking. She kept her hand on it.

He cleared his throat and excused himself to Albert. He led her behind the curtain, begging for the potion, as she wanted the book.

“Wizardry Reactions and Natural Principles,” he said. “Paid-tier alchemy, covers every basic.” He rubbed his hands together. “Worth more than one potion, lady!”

Tally reminded Maureen that Basic Principles was being reserved for a better trade, while Analytical Demonstrations sat beneath her oath collar.

Fine, Maureen agreed silently. The Blue Ring was so far away.

“Basic Principles of Pharmacology,” Maureen offered. “We can discuss the potion separately.”

“Knowledge for knowledge?” the shopkeep asked.

Maureen nodded. “It seems fair.”

“Hardly!” the shopkeep exclaimed.

Tally provided some numbers to support the argument.

“Damn it, lady!” the shopkeep shouted, loud enough to carry through the curtain. “This is extortion!”

He's right, Tally said.

I know, Maureen glowed inside, but kept her face still. Her emotions were hidden from everyone except Tally.

They came to an agreement: Maureen would transcribe Basic Principles, using Tally's records, over the course of several hours. The suppression potion would be delivered via Albert before the end of the month. She would receive a token and first look at any new alchemy fragments for the next two years.

Maureen emerged from behind the curtain with an unreadable expression, while Farry looked both pleased and pained. “It's time for us to go, Albert, Daphne, Aubrey,” she said.

Tally displayed:

along with a deadline for the suppression potion.

Open it tonight, Tally ordered. Turn every page.
