The false Patton and Kane rushed out of the dark towards her, Kane’s huge green wind blade rising up behind him.

[Host Perception Compromised.] The cloying smell that greeted her when she entered the cave was likely the cause of this. [Probable: Inhaled Hallucinogen.]

I can’t tell what’s real, Maureen thought back silently.

The Seal has Illusion as one of its disciplines, Tally reminded her.

Maureen allowed the two figures to approach her, while her hand slipped into the inner pocket of her robe and pulled out the Blood Seal of Thorns. “Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa.”

A faint red light poured forth from her hand, bathing her entire body in an eerie glow. The false Patton and the false Kane disintegrated, shredded by the power of the ancient spell as easily as damp paper could be torn apart by a pair of scissors.

 [Seal: 3.2 s. Glow: low.]

Five uses since the ambush, Maureen. You have to stop now, Tally told her and she released the spell. As the power faded from her body, Maureen felt her head clear and her sight blurred for a second before she could see the cave around her. It was a real cave, not an illusion, but it was nothing like she had expected. The ceiling was low and the sound of dripping water echoed through the darkness. The cloying sweet smell was thick in the air like syrup. In the centre of the cave stood a tree that reached up towards the ceiling. Its branches writhed and twisted like the limbs of an octopus, bright red and blue against the wet rock. The branches were slick with some kind of slime and the roots of the tree disappeared into a pile of bones and fresh bodies.

The tree giggled then laughed, the voice sliding between a man and a woman, an old man and a small child.

Dark Forest, Tally told her. Sodara, man-eating tree. Tally was matching it against a library entry.

Tally counted the bodies, and Tally kept the number off Maureen's display.

The Sodara is a rare and very dangerous tree known to inhabit the Dark Forest. It will lure travellers and pure creatures to itself using illusions, before killing them and devouring their flesh. The Sodara is a wild only species, and cannot be cultivated. Each Sodara tree has its own unique fruit which produces its own individual effect. All the fruits of the Sodara tree however drive wizards insane with desire, no matter what their individual effects are.

Maureen studied the corpses around the tree for a long time before she looked up at the large half-red fruit hanging from the branches like lanterns. Now she understood why Patton needed so many living people. He had been feeding the tree. If the fruit would be effective for her... that was something she could never know.

As Maureen took another step towards the tree the laughter grew louder, and the sweet smell seemed to grow thicker until Maureen was breathing through her teeth. She reached out and grasped one of the writhing branches and immediately it began to thrash like a captured python. Black particles poured from her hand and wrapped around the limb and the tree let out an ear splitting scream as every branch lashed out at Maureen. A faint aura of dark-energy particles rose from Maureen's skin and held every tentacle away from her. The slime on the branch she held ran cold over her fingers, the scent of burnt honey and crushed stems filling her nostrils. The screams from the tree kept changing from a woman to a man to a small child, the voice shifting with every breath. Then the branch in Maureen's fist suddenly became just a branch. The red and blue skin peeled away, revealing dull yellowish wood that was a little withered. At the very tip of the branch grew a single small pale red fruit.

* * *

The tree's branches and limbs curled slowly back towards the trunk. The slime on Maureen's glove dried into a greyish residue. At the edge of the glow lay the floor of bodies.

Maureen pricked the small red fruit with a thin needle of dark particles. Tally? Analyse this fruit for me please.

[Species: unknown.]

[Library: Effects of Sodara fruits are varied and specific to each tree.]

[Particle residue: resonates with host Spirit Rune pattern.]

Tally replied, its warm voice sounding in Maureen's head. Tally had compared the residue with Maureen's own log of her rune-meditation pattern, and found a match.

How long until Third-Class with this fruit?

[Third-Class: est. nearly 6 years -> est. 2 months.]

[Error: wide.]

Maureen stood perfectly still. A bead of dark red juice slowly crept down the particle needle. Her face didn't move a muscle.

What does it eat?

Tally answered in its own quiet voice. Living people. They are fed to it whole. Only then did Tally display the count.

[Remains: 31, by sight.]

Somewhere out in the darkness a body settled against another, making a soft wet noise as it did so.

What do you think Tally?

I have no data on what 'right' is, only data on six years.

Well log it then.

Tally logged the data, then spoke again, almost as an afterthought.

I am still here with you though.

The tree would be fed. Feren had many rebel prisoners, and Umas's lands had criminals and thugs galore. She wasn't going to wait six years for Third-Class. Her lips moved as she sang the casting, sending rings of dark particles around the tree that knotted together into a complex array before flashing. The tree vanished from sight behind a concealment.

[Mana: 0.2 -> 0.0]

That is the last of your mana, Apprentice Olsen. From here your knight's strong body can carry you back.

Behind the empty air where the tree had stood its laughter continued, muffled by the concealment. Maureen's calf muscles began to ache as if she had just finished a day long march.

* * *

In the dry area at the back of the cave were the remains of Patton's possessions. A lacquered chest and a bedroll that smelled of lamp smoke were stacked near a pile of oddments covered by the dust of time. Behind her the hidden tree continued laughing, its mirth muffled by the concealment.

Patton's possessions had been of good quality. He must have ranked reasonably high in the Blue Ring's hierarchy.

Inside the chest was a silk pouch containing several hundred magic stones, some texts on basic subjects and several spell models. Under the dust, the heap was made up of more obscure materials and junk.

[Magic stones: +312. All columns: ~6,000.]

Maureen felt the weight of the pouch in her palm as Tally added it to her totals.

She tossed the pouch in her hand once, smiling.

What's the breakdown Tally? She asked silently, and Tally obliged, displaying the columns for her.

[Superior: 37. Ordinary: 1,340. Card: 950.]

The ordinary column already had the stones from selling off her spare potions on the road. She kept the essentials on her belt. Just the Recovery, Antidote and Stamina potions.

Yes, Tally informed her with a smug note in its voice. It liked keeping track of things. Well done you.

Thank you for maintaining my ledger so diligently.

A book caught Maureen's eye in the pile of junk. It looked familiar. What is that Tally?

It has faint lines on the cover similar to George's combined spells book. Tally told her using its own voice.

Yes. Maureen agreed silently, reaching for the book. As soon as her fingers touched the cover it floated in the air, glowing with a soft light before flipping open. The pages turned rapidly making a sound like wings flapping in the wind, the light from the book playing over Maureen's face. There was no cold alert again.

I see only what you see Apprentice Olsen.

Fine silver threads like spider silk rose around her body, before shooting into her forehead and weaving their way into both her Rune Arrays.

A hum like struck glass filled her skull, and cold spread behind her eyes. She could feel both arrays intact, but her hands would not stop shaking.

Tally, scan me and clear any anomalies please.

I can look but I cannot clear. [Rune Arrays: 2. Intact. Anomaly: none found.]

Look harder Tally.

I am looking as hard as I can Apprentice Olsen. None found does not equal none. This book is going into my books that eat file. Entry three. This one ate in.

[Imprint received. Category: Ancient Era lines and totems. Task: Priority 1. Preliminary analysis: 41 days]

Forty-one days! Tally said with pleasure despite itself. This is the first data anyone has ever given me.

The book fell to the ground with a dull thump on the stones. Patton must have found it, like George did, and failed to read it before tossing it into the junk pile.

After a long silence Maureen spoke. "Well, forty-one days isn't too bad. I can wait that long." She walked out of the cave.

* * *

The yard was lit by torches and the entire stronghold was ablaze with light. The thick black smoke from the pine resin stung her eyes and made her want to cough. Soldiers were striding between the rows of prisoners who knelt silently in the churned up mud and muck of the yard.

The rebellion was over.

Marquis Umas saw her enter on foot and his face lit up with joy. He hurried over to her, his cape fluttering behind him. "Lady Maureen."

One of the prisoners near her boots was shaking so hard that his chains were clinking against the cobbles.

Maureen got straight to the point. "Umas, I want to live here from now on. Undisturbed."

Tally read his face. There was relief, then suspicion as his eyes flickered to her sword and back, then to the mountain road.

She smiled. "I just want to live here." She didn’t mention Patton or the cave.

After a moment Umas laughed. "Of course you can. Of course." His voice sounded sincere.

For now. Maureen thought.

For now. Tally agreed.

* * *

The cobbles crunched under Maureen’s boots as she walked. She kept her head down, her hood up, to protect herself from the coal smoke and the cold. Umas had made her Viscountess Olsen in her own right, bestowing on her Feren’s title and lands. The staff at her castle now addressed her as my lady.

At the chestnut stall people were trading versions of the night the Marquis's guards had ridden out of Wintercold City, and of the new viscountess.

A young woman up on the mountain. No banquets. Doesn't bother with suitors and ignores the season. Does some very strange things up there.

Criminals and thugs sent to Olsen land never came back. Neither did Feren's rebels. People who tried to find out about the new viscountess ended up silenced or simply vanished. Crime had gone down since then, and the townsfolk were glad of it.

Tally was keeping count of everyone they had sent to the back mountain.

Tally, would you stop counting them please? Maureen asked silently.

Tally didn’t.

Every version of the story at the stall ended with her name. She walked past, and no one gave her a second glance.
