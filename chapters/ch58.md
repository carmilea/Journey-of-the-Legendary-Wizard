Maureen nodded. The alchemy book rested against her ribs under her robe, wrapped in cloth. It was a flat hardness that pressed against her body as they walked out of the Grey Dove Settlement.

Her face was calm, but inside Maureen was elated. There weren't many texts on alchemy left on the Passise Subcontinent. She now owned one.

I already counted the pages, Tally said.

Really?

Yup. Just by feel through the thickness of the binding.

That's a guess, then.

It is an educated guess.

Albert, Daphne, and Aubrey kept shooting glances over at her. They'd seen her speaking with Old Farry, and they could tell that the transaction wasn't standard. They had no idea about the details.

The fog still hung low outside. Crows perched on the branches of the bare trees, occasionally fluffing up their feathers or cawing once or twice.

As the party of travellers went by, the crows tilted their heads to follow the movement of the horses. Their eyes glowed with an eerie green light.

> [Guardian, by colour.]

I'll keep that to myself.

Good idea. Watched in, watched out.

"Nothing to worry about here, Maureen," Albert called over his shoulder as he moved into the lead. Even while sitting in the saddle, he wheezed with every breath.

Maureen looked at the crows for a moment longer, remembering the hooded figure of the Guardian. Then she said nothing and followed after Albert.

* * *

The riders came out of the mist and onto the open road. The sun was low on the horizon, and it would soon be dusk. Long grass covered both sides of the road. It had turned brown under the frosts, but was still high enough to hide a man lying down. The grass was also wet with dew and the mist curled about it where it dipped. A steady wind blew from the left and the long grass was combed away to the right. Saddle leather creaked and the hot breath of the horses steamed in the evening air.

With the settlement behind them, the riders relaxed and settled into their saddles. Albert wheezed and stretched, while Daphne brushed her white hair back from her face.

"Well, we all did very well in Grey Dove this time," Aubrey said, sitting stiffly in the saddle. Even when he laughed, he sounded formal. "Yes, indeed we did," he continued, "even you, Albert."

Albert chuckled, joined by Daphne. They talked about what they had bought and sold and the prices they should have charged, and their conversation became more animated and cheerful.

Maureen listened and smiled at times. For a while she almost felt that she belonged among these people.

You smiled three times in an hour, Maureen Olsen, Tally's warm voice commented. That is a record.

Shut up.

No need to be cross. I will count your facial expressions quieter if you wish.

Do that.

Tally sounded pleased with itself for some reason, but Maureen ignored it. At the extreme edge of Maureen's vision, a patch of the long grass to her right moved in a different direction than the rest of the grass. The wind was coming from the left, so the grass should have been blowing to the right. This patch was leaning to the left.

>Grass. Right, 40 m.

A couple of breaths later Tally flagged the same patch of grass.

>Still there.

>Level with us.

>Moving with us.

The grass on either side of the road was brown from the frost. It made a hissing noise as it waved in the wind. To the left a thin mist rose from the low lying dips in the ground, the horses' breath formed clouds of steam in the cold air. Maureen's brows drew together and she frowned as she studied the moving grass. A coldness crept into her chest, familiar, unwelcome but in a way a relief.

In one fluid motion Maureen twisted in her saddle, drew the laminated horn longbow and loosed an arrow. There was a cry and a man rolled out of the long grass, an arrow through his chest. He lay still in the frost, very dead.

>Arrows: 21 → 20.

Clean shot, Tally said smugly.

Count it quietly, Maureen told it silently.

I'm counting very quietly, Maureen Olsen. I'm being as quiet as I possibly can.

She looked over her shoulder at the body sprawled in the grass, then turned to face the front. The others had reined in sharply, their faces pale. "What happened?" Albert wheezed.

"What is going on?" Daphne asked clutching her reins with both hands, her knuckles white.

"Nothing much," Maureen said lowering her longbow. "Just some rats following us." Aubrey sat stiffly in his saddle, his eyes fixed on the long grass, unmoving except for the steady rise and fall of his chest as he breathed.

* * *

Suddenly a group of men burst out from behind them and onto the road. Several of them were Wizard Apprentices and Upper Knights, though some appeared strong while others did not look so tough. One of their number stepped forward, a large burly man with purple hair.

"We were going to hit you when you stopped to rest," he said with a grin, "but now that you've spotted us, you might as well die now."

[Match: the man at the stall.]

[Second-Class / Upper Knight.]

The man who had been so friendly in the settlement, chatting away with Daphne while keeping an eye on her pouch. How could she have been so stupid? The man she'd decided was harmless.

"Sawin!" Daphne exclaimed, staring at him in disbelief.

"You've been most helpful Daphne," the man said with a grin, his yellow teeth glistening in the fading light. "Through you we know exactly how much you have to trade with. Enough to make me a very wealthy man."

Daphne started to shake with fury, and opened her mouth to say something, but Maureen cut her off.

"Deal with them first," she said grimly.

>Hostiles: 9.

>Apprentices: 4.

>Upper Knights: 5.

>Mark two for Albert's group. Left.

"Disgusting rats," Maureen said to Sawin, her face perfectly expressionless.

Sawin licked his lips, obviously pleased they weren't running away.

"Attack!" he roared, and the men behind him charged forward.

"Just keep a few of them busy. I'll handle the rest."

Maureen dropped from her saddle as lightly as a feather.

>Mana: 6.5.

Tally told her. Her mana levels were full again after the weeks of rest at home.

Seal stays put, Tally answered.

Maureen looked at the nearest apprentice, and Tally read his stance, while a cold spike appeared in her particle sense.

>He'll block it. Fireball, then sword.

"Simons-Sado!"

A deep red fireball exploded from Maureen's palm. The apprentice threw up a smooth mirror of ice, which stopped the blazing missile dead. As the man let out his breath after the exertion of casting, Maureen's silver Cross Sword sliced across his throat. She whipped the blade free of flesh and bone and charged the remaining men.

[Mana → 5.4.]

The optimised fireball took less of a mana hit than your old one did. You're welcome.

Fireballs, acid, shards of ice and poisonous gas crossed in the thickening mist. Waves of fire washed over the trampled grass, the smell of burnt vegetation stinging Maureen's nose, the acid bite of corroded plants making her nostrils twitch. Tally started feeding her information.

>Acid, left. DUCK

Maureen ducked as a globule of corrosive liquid splattered over her head.

>Knight, behind-left. By sound.

She spun around and sliced, the Cross Sword biting deep.

Instinct took over. The body Maureen had trained since Skran reacted automatically to the information Tally was giving her. Two Upper Knights fell before the blue edged blade.

>Opening.

Tally flagged Sawin's momentary dropped guard as he finished a swing with his sword.

[Breath: fast. Left shoulder: fine.]

Maureen slashed and Sawin staggered back, a thin line of blood appearing across his ribs. Then the line darkened to blue and he fell to the ground, clutching at his side as he tried to stand. Maureen's next blow caught him across the neck, opening up a long gash from ear to collarbone. He fell again and didn't get up.

The remainder of the attackers broke. "She's too strong! Let's go!" They ran, the two knights facing Albert's group joining them in their flight.

Maureen smiled grimly. "Want to run? Do you think you can?"

Tally highlighted the running men in blue, then drew an arc ahead of them. No need for powder now. No need for such a long chant either.

>Four of five within.

"Dark Wind Ring!" Maureen swept her hand forward and a murky black wind spiralled out from her fingers, howling over the fleeing men. Flesh ripped away from bone, skin, muscle and tendon peeled away in ribbons, bones were dulled and shattered into clouds of dust that drifted down to coat the frost covered ground like ash. Tally displayed [Mana → 3.2.] and flagged the last man who was still running towards the lone tree off the verge.

>Window. Now.

Maureen threw the Cross Sword and the blade pinned the running man to the tree. His head lolled on one side, white bone visible where his neck should have been. Dead.

Tally helpfully informed her that the new Cross Sword flew true, even more so than the old one.

It had better have. It cost enough.

* * *

Maureen walked through the dead, as she always had since Derrick. She checked each body carefully before getting too close. Just in case.

Tally identified each one for her as she approached. [Hostile: 1] [Hostile: 2] [Hostile: 3] [Hostile: 4] and finally [Sawin]. [Hostiles: 0.] You left no one behind, Tally told her, quieter than before.

Maureen reached out with a gloved hand, and pulled the Cross Sword free from the tree. She wiped it clean on the body of the man pinned to it. Then she leaned down and took Sawin's pouch from his belt, slipping it onto her own belt. Tally assured her that it would count it later. When they were in better light.

No one spoke behind her. No one needed to. The only sounds were Albert's wheeze and the sound of one of the horses shaking its bridle.

Daphne is shaking, More than a little. Albert’s wheeze is worse. Aubrey’s knuckles are white where he grips his reins.

They’ve seen your true strength now, Tally said.

I know, Maureen replied silently. They would have eventually.

Yes, but now they will be polite.

Maureen looked back over her shoulder at her three companions. They wouldn’t ask her to sit around and gossip with them again. She knew that. She didn’t mind. She told herself she didn’t mind.

Tally had nothing to say about that. Maureen noticed. It was quiet. She mounted her horse and turned it for home.
