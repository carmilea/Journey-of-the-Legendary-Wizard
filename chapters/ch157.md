The potion that Ambrose had dropped when she had walked in was still sizzling across the bench in a brown smear. The scent of it was sharp, like vinegar and burnt sugar mixed together. Ambrose sat perfectly still behind the smear, his silver hair lifted a little on one side where the force of the explosion had caught it.

Without touching them, Ambrose waved his open hand over the rack of test tubes. A tongue of green flame bloomed out of empty air and ate the brown potion and shattered glass in a single breath. When the fire was gone, there was nothing left behind, not even a scorch mark on the wood.

> Fire. School: unknown. Burns glass to nothing.

Tally logged it before the flames were gone.

*Can I learn that?* Maureen asked silently.

*No data on how,* Tally answered, equally silent. *But I have lots of data on how much it will hurt if you try.*

Maureen didn’t feel any heat from the fire on her face. She glanced at the window, but there wasn’t even a thin spot in the rime of frost on the glass. The fire had burned within its own small room of air, and when it went out, the smell of camphor returned as if the explosion had never happened.

Ambrose stood up and came around the end of the bench, eyeing her with the same speculative gaze he used to inspect vials held to the light. “You’ve been gone a long time, Richard,” he repeated, his voice dry and unhurried. “I think I signed for weeks. Yes, I’m almost certain that I signed for weeks.”

Maureen just shrugged, and said nothing. Richard’s plain, forgettable features gave Ambrose nothing to work with either, so she let the silence answer for her.

[Leave requests: 4.]

Tally displayed the number quietly, along with a read on Ambrose. His tone was dry, not cold. There was a steady pulse beating at the base of his throat. *He’s teasing you, Maureen,* Tally said. *But the leave form is still sitting on his desk.*

*Let it sit there,* Maureen said silently.

Ambrose let her prolonged absence drop as though it were no more than the weather, before he spoke again, his voice dropping into a softer register. “The ancient realm is about to open.”

The word “ancient” changed the room. There was something old and hungry beneath Ambrose’s calm exterior, and even the rows of stoppered jars on the shelves seemed to wait expectantly.

“Herbs that have been unseen for a thousand years. Materials of every strange and wonderful nature. A lifetime’s worth of work by one of the greatest wizards who ever lived, left lying in the open.” Ambrose spread his hands wide. “A feast set for every wizard in the West. And the East has been invited.”

“It’s very close now,” he said. “Some powerful wizards are already within, scouting.”

Ambrose watched Maureen’s face as he spoke. It was as if he was interested in seeing which piece of information she would react to.

Tally put “very close” next to the “soon” that Ambrose had used the morning after Gus died. She also put it beside Gregory’s imprint: Only truly powerful wizards are able to leave their realms. And each time they do so, they leave behind a lifetime’s worth of work and study.

Very close and soon fit together neatly. The date had moved closer since they last spoke of it.

*Ours to find first,* Maureen thought.

*You’ve already said that,* Tally pointed out. *Logged it as a promise.*

“Once that realm opens,” Ambrose said, “nothing will stay quiet for long. There will be blood. We’re going to need potions by the cartload.”

Maureen’s eyes went to the patch of bare wood where the flame had been. She remembered the Spring cavern, the threads of blood running down into the pool. *How much draught will a realm’s worth of wounds drink?* she wondered.

Ambrose tapped the order sheet with one long finger. The Fourteenth Group had an impressive stack of assignments waiting for them. “Go down and oversee the brewing. Check every batch personally. Viska has kept your chair warm. Don’t make him keep it any longer.”

Maureen gave the old wizard the small nod he expected from Richard, then left without another word.

The corridor outside smelled of burning beeswax, like all the corridors in the Pharmacy Department always did. The soft honeyed light lay across the black stone, making the floor and walls seem to glow with their own dark energy. Her footsteps sounded too loud after the stillness of Ambrose’s office.

Tally ran his density against her file again: about Maffie’s, medium confidence, and unchanged since the last reading. *He likes you,* Tally said. *And he keeps track of how often you’re not here. Both of those things could be true.*

*Log it under both columns,* Maureen said silently.

*I already did.*

* * *

Someone had polished the brass 14 on the door while she’d been away. They’d even rubbed it until it was shiny enough that she could see the reflection of Richard’s face when she stopped in front of it. Beyond that was a wall of steam, bitter roots, and the sweetness of good beeswax. Maureen pushed open the door and stepped inside.

For a moment, the room was quiet. Then it burst into cheers.

“Group leader!”

“The leader’s back! It’s the leader!”

“We thought you’d moved to the moon or something!”

Stools scraped along the benches as the pharmacists stood up. Someone banged a ladle against a copper pot, and the noise rose like a tide, crashing against the ash wood benches and bouncing off the pine requisition crates stacked against one wall. It rolled over Maureen and filled the room from floor to ceiling.

Her hands dropped to the bench beside her, and Richard’s face smiled because that was what Richard would do. But behind the face, something twisted in Maureen’s throat. She didn’t have a name ready for it.

[Pulse: 64 → 71.]

*That’s what “being liked” feels like. I have logged it in your file.*

*Log it as noise instead.*

*I have logged it as both.*

The pharmacists crowded around her, half joking and half in awe of the young genius who came and went as she pleased. One of them pressed a cloudy vial under her nose, and she made a single correction—telling him he needed to cool it one second longer—and watched his face light up as though she’d handed him a stone.

“Group leader,” Viska called from across the room, shouldering his way through with a mug of tea in either hand. His red hair was dark and damp from the steam. “We were beginning to think someone had stolen you away from your cauldrons for good.”

“Someone tried,” Maureen said. “The cauldrons won.”

Viska laughed, rubbing the back of his head, and gestured at Maureen’s desk. “I was drinking my tea at your desk every morning this month.”

“Then I’ll be charging you rent.”

*He brought two mugs,* Tally pointed out. *He missed you, Maureen. He missed you by pulse and by tea.*

*I noticed that,* Maureen thought back. *Log him as a colleague.*

*Logged.*

*Colleague who missed you.*

Maureen took the ledger from Viska with a nod of thanks, and let him walk her over to the quota board. The healing and recovery draughts were listed in columns, and the Fourteenth Potion Group was already three days behind on Ambrose’s newest order.

[Quota at full benches: est. 11 days. Confidence: medium.]

*Nine days.* Maureen thought back.

*Nine, if you brew yourself,* Tally responded.

Viska pressed the second mug of tea into Maureen’s hands. The smoke-and-mint brew was still too hot to drink, but the warmth seeped through the thick clay and into her palms while the room returned to its clatter of voices and clinking glass.

* * *

“Ah, I almost forgot,” Viska said, snapping his fingers before he disappeared into the crowd. He returned a moment later, towing a young man by the elbow. The newcomer had pale-gold curls plastered against his forehead with the steam from the reducing flask.

The young man’s hands did not know where to go. They started for his cuff, then dropped to fiddle with his apron, then hid themselves behind his back. His bow was careful and deep, and his elbow brushed against the rack of half-filled vials. The glassware chimed like tiny bells.

“This is Tonk,” Viska said. “His family sits inside the High Ring White Tower. He’s here to see how we work, and lend a hand if needed.”

Maureen’s shoulders set at the name of the White Tower. It was a subtle motion, no more than the width of a hair, but it was enough to tell anyone watching that something had changed. The steam from the reducing flask suddenly felt too warm against the back of her neck.

[Tonk. Third-Class, mid column. Knight body: none.] Tally noted. Then, in her colder voice, she added, *Family High Ring White Tower. Tower stalled their allocation. Gus’s order. Why here?*

*I don’t know,* Maureen thought silently. *Ask him.*

*I would rather ask his father.*

Maureen’s own reading was much simpler. A family was polishing its son’s record by sending him to work in a famous group. It happened every season. The question was why this group, out of all the groups in the Alliance.

Tonk must have seen something in Richard’s face, because the words began to spill out of him. “Wizard Richard, I’m not, it isn’t, I’m not here to... I just love Pharmacology. That’s all. Truly, that’s all.”

The young man’s forehead beaded with sweat, and the collar of his robe darkened with moisture. Tally traced the line of moisture with a thin pale blue line, like the tide mark left on the side of a jar when the water recedes.

[Tells on “love Pharmacology”. None observed. Pulse 104. Consistent with fear of you. Inconsistent with lie. Not proof.]

[Forecast. Medium confidence. He is exactly what he looks like. Somebody else has chosen to send him here.]

Tally paused as she brushed against his density, and [Faint dark-red thread. Low confidence. Match: none yet.] flashed across Maureen’s eyes.

*What’s that?* Maureen asked.

*Probably my nerves,* Tally said. *I don’t have nerves. Never mind. Keep an eye on him anyway.*

Maureen nodded once. “When we have time, you may bring your questions to me, or to any of them.” She swept her hand towards the rest of the group. “You’ll pay for the answers—either in contribution points, or in stones.”

“Wizards don’t give anything for nothing,” Maureen said. “It’s always an exchange—equal for equal. Do you understand, Tonk?”

*You sound just like Adrian,* Tally noted.

*And wasn’t he right about prices?* Maureen answered back.

Tonk nodded his head so hard a pale-gold curl escaped its tie. “Yes, Wizard Richard. Thank you, Wizard Richard.”

Behind him one of the pharmacists snorted into his flask. Paying for information was one thing. Whether any of them would actually answer a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice from inside the High Ring White Tower was another matter entirely.

The afternoon drew its grey curtain around them. Maureen moved from bench to bench, tilting a reducing flask a finger’s width off the flame to halt the evaporation, watching as a cloudy draught slowly became clear with the addition of a single pinch of salt. The greenish stain crept back into the creases of her knuckles, as though she had never left.

At the break Tonk hovered near her bench, holding a cup that he didn’t drink from, passing among the crates and the benches as though drawn by some magnetic attraction. Twice Maureen saw him glance away, then look back at her with determination in his eyes. Then he approached her.

“Wizard Richard, please be careful,” he said, his voice pitched low enough that the clatter of glassware provided cover. “My father says Master Wenger is still digging into who killed Gus. Gus was the greatest genius the Tower has ever known, and Master Wenger’s own student.”

Tonk paused, then continued. “Master Wenger knows Gus spoke with you not long before he died. It might reach you. That’s all I came to say. Please . . . be careful.”

[Pulse 132. Thumb working at cuff. Fear. By tells. Not proof.] [Sources. Two. Ambrose, head of Pharmacy Department, after death of Gus, warned that Wenger was searching Emilia under his own seal. Tonk now confirms. Tonk told this to you at cost to self. Kind? Not yet.]

Maureen set Tonk’s warning beside Ambrose’s, and narrowed her eyes by a hair. The stirring rod in her hand continued its slow, steady circle around the mouth of the flask, and Maureen’s pulse didn’t even flicker.

“Thank you, Tonk,” she said.

Tonk flushed, the blood rising until it tinted the roots of his pale-gold curls. He turned and fled back to the safety of his pine crates.

Across the room from Maureen, Tonk counted vials into the crate twice over, his lips moving. In the soft light of the beeswax candles his hair was the colour of weak tea.

* * *

Tally brushed the street corners for density knots on the way home.

[Density knots: 6. Particle sense. Last count: 5.]

*One more than before,* Tally said. *Same dark red. No data.*

Maureen nodded silently. *Keep counting.*

The gate of 34 Connpa Street recognised her palm brand, and swung open on well-oiled hinges. Frost lay like a white fur collar on the box hedges of the garden. Somewhere in the stable loft above, one of the grooms whistled an off-key ballad.

Ike was waiting for her in the front hall, bowing his head, his eyes carefully focused on Maureen’s collar. “Welcome home, Master,” he said in his precise voice. “I trust your day has passed peacefully? All is well here. Even with the gold low, the household accounts are in order, wages paid, coal delivered, the cook’s spirits high.”

Dinner was roast fowl with a savoury sauce, accompanied by the cook’s dense, dark brown bread. The bath after dinner was hot enough to sting, and the steam carried away the lingering odours from the brewing room.

Once inside her bedroom ward, she let Mimicry drop. Richard’s face slid away like a damp glove, and beneath it was her own: pale skin drawn tight across the sharp bones of her face, black hair cut short so that it stood up from her forehead. She slipped into the white velvet robe.

[Ward: holding. Night watch: on.]

*The house is yours until morning, Maureen. You need not listen for it.*

*Thank you, Tally,* Maureen thought as she crossed to the window. For the first time since she’d left the Underworld, she drew Freya’s slender Cross Sword from its pouch. No dark elves were here to recognise it, only her ward and the frost feathering the glass.

She held the sword across her knees, feeling the smooth leather grip against her palm. Black light crawled along the blade like oil on water. The fine-grained black leather of the grip felt cool against her fingers. It was still a finger’s width too small for her hand, but it always had been. Even after all this time, still wrong.

Maureen ran her thumb along the flat and saw Gus as he’d stood in the shadow of the oak tree, the hole punched through his chest, Richard’s face reflected in his eyes.

“Richard… you’re Owl…”

She hadn’t struck the blow that felled him; Frans had. But it was in the trap that Gus had baited for her that he had died, and she had been the one who chose to let him see Richard’s face before he went into darkness. Now Wenger’s thread wound itself back towards her, just as the realm was about to open.

*No one from that night lived to speak of it,* Tally reminded her unnecessarily. *We still don’t have any data on how the Tower tracks people. All Wenger knows is that Gus spoke to Richard.*

*That will be enough for a man like him,* Maureen thought silently.

*Yes,* Tally agreed. Outside the frost crept another finger up the windowpane. The lamp burned with a soft hiss, and the black light on the blade seemed to move, as though it was breathing.

Then something moved within the pouch hanging from her hip. She felt it through the thread of her spirit that opened the pouch, a tiny heat within where all else lay cold and still.

Maureen reached into the pouch and drew out the small object.

The Bloodcoat token. The size of her little fingernail, it was shaped like a drop of blood. The metal was still cold against her fingers, but now it glowed with a faint inner light, blood-red.

*Check the others,* Tally said.

Maureen drew out the two tokens of the dead men and laid them beside the first. [Night Owl seat: lit. Gus token: dark. Black Bear token: dark.]

*Only yours, Maureen.*

*Someone wants the living.*

The blood-red glow from the token layered itself over the blade’s black light, pooling in her palm. For the first time that day, she felt her shoulders loosen. It was almost a relief to have something to do.
