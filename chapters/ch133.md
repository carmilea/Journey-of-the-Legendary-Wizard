The night air was still and cold above the white frost of the field. It smelled of burnt timber. Below them Burning Flame’s compound burned orange, and its light shook in the frozen ruts.

Two of them remained, hovering in the open air a stone’s throw away from where Maureen floated at the height of a tall roof. Frans and one silent companion. They exchanged a look, and Maureen watched them both tally up what she had done this night. They did not like the total.

Tally highlighted each with an overlay of data, showing density. Amber lay across the companion, a thinner amber across Frans. [Companion: Phantom. Fixed defences ×3 + triggered.]

[Frans: Phantom, thinner. Witnesses: 2.]

*Medium confidence,* Tally predicted. *The companion will stand and counter, relying on the shells layered upon his skin. Frans will go for your back.*

*Keep an eye on him anyway,* Maureen told Tally silently.

Frans spoke, his voice slow and measured, very different from before. “I never thought, Owl... never thought you’d be this strong.”

Maureen gave him half a smile. “Strong enough. Killing you two is the simple part.”

It was not a boast. It was the next line in the method, spoken aloud.

Her breath smoked once in the cold air, visible for an instant before vanishing. Her pulse sat in the sixties, but it was of no more importance to her than the temperature of the room when she began an experiment.

She moved without warning, a sudden streak of shadow that crossed the space between her and her foes. She went for the man standing beside Frans, not for Frans himself.

The companion’s energy field caught her first. Three layers of colour flared to life around his body, amber, a sickly green, and then a hard blue. Some of these were fixed to his skin, while others had triggered and thrown themselves into the fight. His jaw clenched.

Through the Mirror Pupil lens, Tally drew the defences as lines of light, each one nested inside the other. *Which one do I take?*

*All of them, if you’re looking at all of them.*

Maureen did just that. A faint vertical slit of pale red appeared in each of her pupils. The three layers of light suddenly stilled, their colours becoming perfectly sharp and clear.

[Mirror Pupil: −2.5. Crystallisation est. 16.5°. Mana 9.7 → 7.2.]

[Layers no longer under spell effect, now glass.]

Her fist broke through all three of the companion’s protective spells at once. There was a sound like a full rack of glassware being dropped onto ceramic tile.

For half a breath, Maureen was back in Jing Sheng’s lab. There was the bright sound of shattering glass, someone swearing, and the broom. Then everything was wrong. There should not have been blood after the glass, and the shards should not have been digging cold and sharp into her knuckles.

The companion’s face fell slack in horror. Maureen grinned at him, close enough to see the frost in his eyebrows, and opened her mouth.

A tide of black-red fire poured out from her throat. It washed over the companion, consuming him. The fire felt oily as it burned, but it had no heat for Maureen to feel. It was the Night Owl rule she had already broken knowingly this night, on the condition that none who saw it lived to speak of it.

*Do not hold for longer than his screaming,* Tally advised.

Maureen held her Purgatory Fire exactly as long as the companion screamed. One scream, then a second, then nothing.

[Purgatory Fire: −2. Mana 7.2 → 5.2. Witnesses: 1.]

Nothing remained of the companion. The air smelled like burnt hair, and nothing else.

Then the fire’s light left Maureen’s mouth, and the cold rushed back over her teeth like water.

* * *

Frans had moved while she burned the companion, gathering his own spell at her flank before she finished. Then he was suddenly flinging himself to the side.

“Damn it—”

While the fire ran, she thought about the Pale Hand, and it was there. A great, translucent white arm reached out of empty air, its claws grasping at the place Frans’s chest had just been.

The long, white nails of the clawed hand closed on nothing but night air. They would have gone straight through him if he hadn’t moved when he did.

[Pale Hand: Void Eclipse model, optimised. Cost −1.2. Miss. Mana 5.2 → 4.0.]

*He felt it coming,* Tally told Maureen. *Interesting. He wasn’t a trained wizard. He was a killer by habit.*

*He was also very good, Tally.*

*Very,* Tally agreed. *He’s just good. I’m logging that too.*

The white arm withdrew into the darkness, thinning until it was like a breath on cold glass. The pale appendage left behind no smell at all, and in a way that was worse than the smoke.

Maureen turned her head to Frans, moving slowly, without hurry. “Well, Frans? Not a bad spell, is it?”

[Elapsed: 41 s.]

*Forty-one seconds since the first movement,* Tally provided, in case Maureen felt that it had been longer.

Frans looked down at the grey smear on the frost, then drew a deep breath, forcing his voice back to evenness. “All right. My mistake. I offended you. I didn’t know what you were.”

Then Frans spoke quickly, “A truce. No more of this. Someone like you, the bigger names in the Black Wizard Alliance will want you. And we’re only the first wave, Owl. There are a lot more of us coming after.”

[Frans: pulse 140+, hands open, eyes on your hands. Fear: high. Tells for lying: none observed. Not proof.]

*He probably means it. Confidence: medium. He’s frightened. He’d still kill you tomorrow.*

Maureen filed the warning away as hearsay from a man under stress. That was how she handled the rumours from the dining hall. It was a simple process, but it took long enough for some of the cold to penetrate deep into the cuts on her knuckles.

Below them, the roof of the compound collapsed with a long cracking sound, followed by a gust of sparks. Neither Maureen nor Frans looked down.

“Do you know why you’re not hurt?” Maureen asked softly, turning back to Frans. “Why you’re not a smear on the frost like your partner?”

Frans blinked at her.

“I could have wounded you before,” Maureen said, addressing Frans directly now. “If I’d been careless, two Phantom-State men together might have made a bit of a mess of me. One is easily handled. Safety first. So your companion went first, and you are the one left over.”

She spoke in the same voice she used when reading out the order of reagents at the bench, watching Frans listen to it, seeing his throat move once as he swallowed.

Maureen smiled. “I kept you for an experiment, Frans. I want to see how long a man who’s good at killing lasts when I stop holding back.”

“What?” Frans stared at her.

*You want a number.*

*I want to know what the two of us are now.*

*So do I.* Tally was almost a whisper.

* * *

Maureen closed her eyes, feeling the cold iron taste of the air through her nose, the faint taint of smoke that was in the wind. The runes of her Totem warmed beneath her skin, its pulse beating like a second heart that wasn’t her own.

She opened her eyes again, and there was a faint ring of silver runes around each pupil, as though they were part of the eye itself.

Behind her, a Rune Array was forming. Silver lines rose out of the void, tracing a circle that was wider than a cart. In the centre of the array, where dark particles swirled and churned, a pair of scarlet eyes slowly opened.

Something else was behind those eyes, something that was far too big to take in at one go. It leaned halfway out of the darkness, its coils suggested more than seen. There was a breath of hot iron and sulphur on the air, the scent that Tally had first logged from the blood of the Pankora serpent.

[Minai Totem: level 1. Amplification +1.2°.] Tally logged on her display, and spoke. *Those eyes aren’t a spell, Maureen. That’s the blood answering your call. They’ll stay just eyes unless you let it all the way out.*

Maureen didn’t need to be told that. She kept the serpent’s own power in reserve, only using her own. Tonight she wanted to test the Totem’s amplification, no more.

“You’re lucky, Frans,” Maureen said. “Since I became an Official Wizard and really learned what I have, you’re the first to feel the brunt of my real casting.”

Her breath was coming fast now, her face relaxing into the expression that came with battle-casting. She could feel the grin spreading across her lips before she even realised it was there. This was the first time since her rite that she’d thrown open all the doors at once.

She lifted her hand toward Frans. “Dark Erosion Fireball,” she whispered.

[Dark Erosion Fireball. Combination: Blast Fireball + Acid Missile, Purgatory Fire as fuel. Sources: Grimlando’s Conjecture, Sam’s Taxonomy, Conjecture on Energy Particle Distribution and Spellcasting. Est. 17°, scaled from Room 21 readings + Totem +1.2°.]

*Est., Maureen. Est. Until a testing room told them otherwise.*

*Heard, Tally,* Maureen said silently.

*I’ll probably have to say it again later anyway,* Tally said.

The spell had come out of the dark nights since her Pharmacy exam, when Tally would simulate it for her on the Official template scroll. Tally would hold the completed lattice in her mind while Maureen worked on the next one. A small purple-black fireball formed in front of Maureen’s palm, no bigger than a plum. Then it expanded, until it was the size of a cartwheel. It hung there in the air quietly, its purple-black skin moving like oil on water. Even from where she floated above the ground, Maureen could sense the heat and smell the sour vinegar bite that hung underneath. Another formed beside the first, then another. Dozens appeared across the air, then more, until they formed a lid over the entire field. From the frost on the dead grass to the burning compound at the other end, it was all covered by a blanket of her spell. [Spell count: 1, 2, 3, 4… 39, 40, 41…] Tally began counting the fireballs, then stopped. [I stopped counting, Maureen,] she said, her voice sounding in Maureen’s head. [I’ve never had to stop counting before.]

The mana bar at the bottom of Maureen’s display was draining at a furious rate, the red numbers ticking down steadily. [Mana 4.0 → 0.6.] *That’s your floor,* Tally said. *Not one more, not even a fraction.*

Underneath the lid of fireballs, the world went quiet. Even the fire seemed to burn less brightly, and the silence was enormous.

Frans looked up into the sky that Maureen had filled with fire, and his face went white. The spread was too wide for him to run away from. Maureen smiled down at him, then pushed her hand forward gently. It was the same motion she would make to close a door.

There was a low boom from the void, and the fireballs began to fall. They left long tails behind them as they dropped, the wind whistling around them like a meteor shower aimed at one man.

The ground shook like an earthquake as the first fireball struck. There was a deep thudding that climbed up through her ribs, impact after impact. A wall of dust rose from the field, swallowing the burning compound and the stars at the same time.

When the dust began to thin, Maureen could see the devastation below. The field was pocked with purple-black craters, each one smoking slightly in the frost. The earth itself had been eaten away by the corrosive force of Maureen’s barrage, leaving blackened scars in its wake.

The smell reached her then—vinegar and scorched stone. There was another smell as well, of wet clay hissing where the frost had touched the corrosion.

A black whirlwind spun into life beneath her feet, and she began to descend to the ground again. Tally monitored her body.

[Pulse 128. Tremor: legs. Mana 0.6.]

*You’re shaking,* Tally said.

*No, I’m standing,* Maureen responded silently.

*You’re both.*

Maureen looked out over the field of craters, then laughed once. “Not bad.” It was her own spell, all of it. “All mine,” she said aloud.

*Yours too,* Tally noted.

*Fine, you can have this one.*

Then came the hollowness, the laugh’s twin. Her knees felt soft, and her chest felt scraped empty. She had a store of mana far beyond what an ordinary wizard could call upon, but even that deep a reservoir was running dry.

This was a trump card, not something to get used to.

* * *

She walked across the ground. Purple-black pits still smoked in the cratered landscape. Each step broke through a crust of corroded frost that crackled like burnt sugar. She walked toward the only dark shape that was still moving.

Frans was lying on the lip of one of the craters. His robe was in rags, and blood was running from half a dozen wounds to his torso. He was breathing in great, heaving breaths, and his eyes were fixed on Maureen.

“Who... who are you?” Frans gasped. “You can’t be... just a nobody.... Owl. Who are you really?”

His question went in under her ribs, down into the place where the barrage had emptied her out. She had more names than most people had coats, and none of them would do here.

*He’s dying anyway,* Tally pointed out.

*I don’t owe him my name, anyway,* Maureen thought back at her.

Maureen tilted her head, regarding Frans with mild surprise. “Still breathing? You Phantom-State men are tougher than I thought.” Then, almost as an afterthought, and with a trace of kindness in her voice: “Go on and die.”

Her hand twitched the slightest bit.

A translucent spike of energy burst from her palm. It drove itself into Frans’s forehead and exploded. When it was gone, so was his head.

Maureen looked at her hand. It was steady.

She stood for a moment in the settling dust, the compound’s glow shimmering over the purple-black pits, some of which still smoked.

[Witnesses: 0]

*Zero,* Tally said after a long pause. *But I’ll check the bodies before you start trusting that number.*

*Fair enough.*

Maureen left Frans’s body where it was, headless in the crater. The field was a map of death, and whatever came looking for the first wave of the Black Wizard Alliance could read it as they wished.

Far off in the mortal quarter a dog barked, followed by another and then another. A bell began to ring, late.

*Do you want the count of dead tonight shown on your display?* Tally asked.

*No.*

*Logged, not shown.*
