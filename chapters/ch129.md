Maureen sat alone in the study, the first letter open in her hands. Richard’s borrowed face still sat upon her, and the porridge on the breakfast tray was beginning to skin over into an unappetizing grey mass. The dull grit of a sleepless night lay behind her eyes. The sound of Ike’s slow footsteps had already faded away down the passage outside.

*Now the entire household knows that a Jadeleaf apprentice came calling upon Wizard Richard,* Tally noted. *Three times this fortnight.*

*She has no other means of reaching me,* Maureen thought back.

*Yes, but I must keep my columns tidy.*

The letter was not long and offered no flourish or embellishment. Joanna thanked Richard for maintaining the Jadeleaf Ring’s ranking within the Wizard Academy Alliance. With that rank, the Ring retained its share of resources and permissions. She made no attempt to dress up her thanks.

Even on the coarse, handmade paper with its uneven deckle edges, the scent of the Jadeleaf Ring’s hall remained. There was a subtle smell of tallow mixed with crushed leaves. The black ink pressed hard into the paper, as though to emphasize every word of thanks. Maureen read it as a girl writing to one she both feared and was grateful to.

Underneath the letters, there was a small parcel. Joanna had included a gift to mark Richard’s new place as a pharmacist in the Pharmacy Department. Inside the package was a sealed glass jar holding living cuttings from some plant. The cuttings were bedded in damp moss to preserve them. Already the leaves with their silvery veins were beginning to turn toward the warmth of Maureen’s hand.

*I have a match by sight,* Tally said. *It’s the same plant she grows in the clay pot outside Peaceridge. I don’t have a plate for this species. Marking it as plant-school and preserved. Harmless by sight. It’s a rather pretty way to say thank you, isn’t it?*

*Yes.* Maureen agreed silently.

The letter finished with an apology that Joanna had not been able to find Richard during any of her visits. Then came the line that caught Maureen’s eye. Whatever Wizard Richard needed, the Jadeleaf Ring would see it accomplished, “even if the last of our apprentices dies for it.”

At the foot of the page was a sorcery seal, a faint green warmth against Maureen’s finger like a palm pressed flat against the paper. Beneath her touch, Joanna’s own spirit-power signature hummed like a moth against glass. Tally matched it against the sample brand she carried.

[Match: Joanna. By seal.]

Tally then flagged “to the last apprentice” with an additional note:

[Equal weight as bondservice oath. Unrequested.]

*An oath sworn without being asked to swear it?*

Tally spoke again in her warm, impish voice. *She’s making an oath without asking permission.*

*Then it binds her, not me,* Maureen replied silently.

*Exactly. That’s what I’d write down.*

“How interesting,” Maureen said aloud to the empty room. A smile touched the corners of her mouth. Grey light lay on the frost-furred glass. The tea was cold now, sitting beside the beeswax candle whose flame had melted the wax and left a pool of yellow translucence around its base. It was a well-made letter. Anyone reading it would think well of Joanna, and of the Jadeleaf Ring, and Maureen did . . . up to a point.

Something twisted beneath her ribs, all the same. The old pull of owing, the Walker Family’s terms, Lilith crying at her door. She set the feeling aside deliberately, as though it were a cup that was too hot to hold.

Partners. That was the word.

The Jadeleaf Ring made a good partner, but nothing more. “And what I need,” she murmured, “the Jadeleaf Ring simply can’t give me.”

Maureen rubbed her forehead with two fingers, feeling the weak line of Richard’s jaw beneath them, the mousy features that overlaid the bone that was her own. Her cropped black hair brushed against the backs of her fingers.

Tally displayed the list without being asked. [Needs: Necro Flower. Necromancy, advanced. Enchantment, advanced.]

*In case you forgot it overnight,* Tally said.

*I never forget lists,* Maureen replied silently.

*That’s because I keep them for you,* Tally answered.

Maureen left the porridge, its skin now a dull grey, the silver spoon lying forgotten beside it. She tucked Joanna’s letter and the jar of cuttings into a drawer and headed down the back stairs toward the cellar.

* * *

The steps were cold and damp, slick with moisture. The air smelled of coal dust and stored turnips. No one in the household had cause to come past the coal bay. The light from Maureen’s lamp stretched her shadow long and thin across the back wall. She reached out to a small knob set flush into the stone. “Manas,” she said under her breath as she twisted the knob.

There was a hum, and then a shudder as the cellar wall slid apart on a breath of old stone dust. Behind it lay the chamber Maureen had cut during the past weeks, one night at a time.

Tally checked the weave. The master ward-knot for the Rental Centre ran through the entire house. It terminated dead against Gregory’s second ring. [Centre ward: no thread past Gregory’s ring. By weave.]

*Same as every other time,* Tally said.

*I told you to check every time,* Maureen thought.

The chamber smelled faintly of ozone and wet stone. Blue-white sparks snapped from the surface of the pool at the center of the room like fat dropped onto a hot griddle. Maureen felt the fine hairs on her forearms rise in the static field.

In the middle of the room was the pool of high-energy element. Particles swirled in it like water, never still. Around the pool was a vast pattern of runes etched into the floor. A magic stone was seated in each node of the rune pattern, each stone glowing with its own faint light. The pattern stretched in a dense carpet from the pool, filling nearly the entire room.

Tally painted the rune pattern amber over the floor. [Stones in array: 412. Ledger: ~14,200 → ~5,100. Pool, veilsilver, materials.]

*You spent like a duchess,* Tally said.

*Like a wizard,* Maureen replied silently.

Tally read the crackle of the element. [Crackle: discharge. Safe at current density.] *Please don’t put your hand in to see if I’m right.*

Maureen’s chest rose and fell slowly and evenly as she entered the room, taking care not to step on any of the carved lines. Her breathing measured, she stood at the edge of the pool, working out what she would do next. A technician’s breathing at Jing Sheng’s white bench.

The puppet lay at the center of the pool, just beneath the churning light. The humanoid shape was blurred, made of veilsilver. There was no detail to the face, no nose or eyes, no chin or lips. It was barely human enough to be called a person.

The metal had the dull sheen of rain on pewter. Cold radiated from it into the warm, crackling air. The shifting light of the element made its outline run and refuse to settle.

Set into its chest was the Blood Seal of Thorns. Despite the cracked and broken metal that surrounded it, Maureen could still see its twining red vines and pale eyes. This was the core of the whole construct.

Tally kept the readout steady above it.

[Core: Blood Seal of Thorns, damaged. Frame: veilsilver. Est. finished strength: at least Phantom State, by comparison to Barok sample. Confidence: medium.]

The basis for Tally’s analysis was the construct spreads in the High Ring White Tower library, the enchantment shelves at Gregory’s, and the column of runes in the bronze book.

“At least Phantom-State,” Maureen said quietly.

If she could finish it, she would have a strong fighter who would stand where she pointed and never ask for anything.

The idea had come after the White Tower raid, when she had sat paging through the construct spreads one by one in that burning library, laying it over the ancient knowledge. Tally had run the projection, and Maureen had started that same week. A second fighter at that grade was too tempting to wait for.

Maureen looked at the Seal for a long time, remembering the ship’s night of blood, the fireball that had flared from Jay’s hands in the serpent’s cave, the slender sword Freya had carried, black light creeping along it like oil on water. Sap and iron, every time.

Tally logged the glow one last time.

[Seal glow: fractured. Reserve: no data.]

Then Tally spoke to her, softly.

*It held for you nine and a half seconds, then two, then one.*

*It has earned a quieter job.*

Maureen nodded once but said nothing. The Seal no longer rode in her inner pocket. What she carried on her body now was the Silver Soul Token, the stone cards, the bronze book, and the dark-gold box. Her left side felt strangely light without the Seal hanging there.

Her reflection floated beside the puppet in the pool’s surface. Richard’s weak jawline and mousy, borrowed features stared back at her from beneath her cropped black hair. Her own black eyes peered out from that face.

Most of the materials were ready. What kept her from moving forward wasn’t stone or metal. It was knowledge, and she hadn’t seen that coming.

The hidden room behind the cellar wall hummed quietly. Hundreds of magic stones glowed a faint blue from the floor around the half-made puppet. Tally’s voice spoke clearly in Maureen’s head. *I don’t think you could have foreseen this.* Maureen had had no data on puppet-making to work with. There was no way she would have known something was missing. Gregory’s enchantment shelves had been filled with references to volumes that he never got around to shelving. The pages on puppet cores that Maureen had flipped through in the White Tower library cited books on necromancy.

[Required: advanced Necromancy; advanced Enchantment, animation.]

[Held: partial.]

[Gaps: 7 cited titles.]

Tally cross-referenced the seven titles against her list of unopened White Tower spines. One title came close, but it had gone up in flames with the library.

*Don’t,* Maureen told her silently.

*Logged anyway. I’m sorry.*

Maureen’s fingers drummed against her thigh. Four beats, then paused. Four more, the rhythm of the Silver Light. It was the only sign she gave of her frustration.

She worked through the problem methodically. The High Ring White Tower was a white robes institution. Advanced necromancy and enchantments of this type fell under the purview of black wizards. They were taboo for any of the white orders. She couldn’t think of a single Alliance wizard who knew anything about necromancy, and the exchange hadn’t listed any either.

*Negative Energy Studies and the Undead, one of the books that you yourself purchased with your contribution points, cites one of the seven missing titles.*

*Not available for purchase anywhere I have been able to find,* Tally noted.

So, the commissions were already out in the underworld. Tally displayed them for Maureen.

[Commissions: 3 open.]

[Necro Flower]

[Necromancy, adv.]

[Enchantment, adv.]

Maureen and Tally had composed the wording together. There was no name, just a dead drop. Payment would be made in magic stones. Tally had wanted the commissions to be signed with a flourish of her own, something to indicate a buyer of refined taste and discretion. Maureen had trimmed it down to six plain words.

*I still think mine was better,* Tally said, sounding a bit wounded.

*Rejected,* Maureen replied silently.

Maureen stepped back off the carved stone floor and said, “Manas.” The wall slid closed behind her, the humming sound ceased, and a fine layer of stone dust settled on her sleeve. “Let’s hope something comes back,” she said to the darkness.

* * *

The corridor of the Alliance Pharmacy Department smelled of burnt sugar and hot glass. Purple robes brushed past, attending to their business, and Maureen moved between them on Richard’s flat, unremarkable feet.

Corwin fell into step beside her, curly beard in the lead. He pitched his voice low. “Heard about Gus? The White Tower’s golden boy. Pulled off Combat Department duty. Nobody’s saying why, but that means everybody’s guessing.”

Tally added that to the Bat file. At medium confidence, she predicted that Gus would be loose in the halls with time on his hands. *Expect to see more of that handsome face,* she told Maureen.

“White Tower business,” Richard said in a flat voice.

Corwin shrugged and peeled away from Maureen to head towards the benches for the Eighth Potion Group.

The guild contract clock was still ticking. Bat wouldn’t be able to get Night Owl, Tally reminded her, but Gus could walk up to Richard any day he pleased. *Then Richard will be polite,* Maureen answered silently.

Her heart rate stayed a steady sixty-four beats per minute during the entire exchange. *Logged as calm,* Tally noted. Maureen didn’t feel the need to change that entry.

Her purple robe whispered against the stone as she walked. The resolution settled in her mind, like frost forming on glass. Cold, quiet, and inevitable.

She organized her glassware for the day with a steady hand when she reached her bench. The commissions were out, and the pool hummed beneath her house. Maureen worked as she waited.
