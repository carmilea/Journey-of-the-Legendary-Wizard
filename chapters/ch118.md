The next evening, she walked slowly through the thickening dinner crowds, the face of “Wizard Richard” hanging loose over her own bones like a borrowed jacket, her mind on numbers. Eighteen Spirit . . . not fifteen . . . to become a Phantom-State Wizard. A gleam of darkness flickered in her eyes, too faint for any of the passersby to notice.

[Spirit: 16 / 18], Tally read off without prompting. *That’s a difference of two points, and two materials. The Necro Flower, and the Purple Wood Snake tears.*

*I can count,* Maureen told her silently.

*Oh, I know, but I like counting for you, Maureen.*

Then Tally’s voice changed, became cool and flat. [Followers: 4, by density. One carries a device: pulsing every 40 s.]

They were behind her, about as far back as the length of a shopfront. They never got any closer.

*What kind of device?* Maureen asked.

*I don’t know, but it looks like some kind of messenger. They’re reporting where you go.*

Maureen did not turn her head. *Then let them report this.*

She saw them anyway, in the polished window of a cutler’s shop as she passed by. Four smeared reflections in dark coats, one of them with his hand cupped to his chest each time there was a pulse from the device. Her mouth wanted to smile at that, but she let the plain man’s face keep it flat.

She turned off the main street onto narrower streets, each one less crowded than the last. She walked past back doors, from which blew kitchen grease and the sour breath of slop buckets, until the only things remaining in the alleyway with her were wet cobbles, a line of grey laundry dripping water onto them in precise ticks, and a cat on the lid of a tin bin.

*They’ll run past the next corner,* Tally said confidently. *Straight through.*

*What makes you so sure?*

*Habit. Every apprentice runs to the last place he saw you.*

At the corner, Maureen flickered. One quick step sideways, moving with knight speed, taking her out of the open street and into the shadow of a doorway. Another step took her up the side of the building, then back along it above the heads of anyone who might be walking down the street. Then she was gone.

The cat bolted at the sudden appearance of a dark figure soaring over its head. Its claws scrabbled on the tin bin lid as it jumped.

From her perch on the dark stone ledge above the street, Maureen listened to the alley beneath her. The constant burble of the drain, the tick-tick-tick of water dripping from laundry to cobblestones, and the sound of boots running on paving stones, harsh human breaths sawing in behind them.

The four apprentices turned the corner into the alley, and skidded to a stop. The alley was empty.

“Where’d he go?” one of them asked.

“He was right there,” said another.

The one carrying the device shook his cupped hand at it, as if the thing could answer him.

*Exactly as I predicted,* said Tally’s voice in Maureen’s head, sounding very pleased with herself. *Please note that I accurately predicted this.* *Very well,* thought Maureen silently. Tally logged the prediction as correct, and then did so again for emphasis.

Maureen stood quietly behind them on the wet cobbles.

“You’ve been following me a long while,” she said.

The Mimicry voice came out just as she had shaped it—low and mild.

“So. What do you want?”

The apprentices spun around, seeing an unremarkable apprentice standing alone behind them. The expressions on their faces grew ugly, and they spread out across the narrow alley. One of them eased his hand toward his sleeve.

Maureen ceased holding her aura down. Her Official Wizard field surged out from her and washed over them like deep water suddenly let loose from a dam. Their legs locked, breath shortened, and the hand edging toward the sleeve froze in midmotion.

The alley grew very quiet. A shutter creaked open somewhere above, but no other sound disturbed the stillness. The sharp, sour tang of fear-sweat cut through the kitchen smells of grease and slop issuing from the back doors of Emilia’s merchants, and one of the apprentices was crying silently.

“Why were you following me?” Maureen asked.

The talkative apprentice swallowed, and then it all came rushing out. “We’re Boss Maffie’s men! You put Ace down at the Jadeleaf Ring! We’ve been looking for you ever since! He said you’d pay for your arrogance—”

Maureen watched the pulses beat frantically in their throats, and Tally laid a pale blue trace over each one— fast in all four, but steady, without a jump when the name Maffie was mentioned. *Consistent,* Tally noted. *No tells observed. Not proof positive, but . . . Maffie. Ace’s boss. Grudge over Ace.*

“So you followed me,” Maureen said, tilting her head, “and told your Maffie where I was.”

The plain young man’s face smiled, something it had never been shaped to do. They had spent days hunting the one apprentice in Emilia they should have run screaming from.

One of the apprentices— the one with the sharper eyes—suddenly looked past Maureen, to the mouth of the alley. “Lord Soros!” he cried out. “He’s the boss’s right hand. One of the two Official Wizards in the Black Spirit Society. He’s been one for years—”

Tally began filing the man away by the apprentices’ description. [Soros. Official Wizard. Density below Barok sample. Not elementalised. Spirit est. <15. Danger: weak.]

*Weak?* asked Maureen silently.

*Weaker than Barok by a long shot,* Tally replied. *Weaker than most things that have tried to kill you.*

The man called Soros strolled into view, a middle-aged Official Wizard wearing a smug expression, just opening his mouth on some speech he’d probably rehearsed all the way down Emilia. Maureen had absolutely no intention of hearing it. Four small clusters of black-red flame burst out of the empty air around him and dropped onto his robe.

He screamed, running forward another few steps under his own momentum. A human-shaped torch ran along the cobbles, three steps, four steps.

Nothing left to run after that. No ash, not even smoke, just a shimmer of heat and the smell of scorched wool, fading away quickly.

[Mana: 16 → 14]

Tally reported. *He was going to talk to you first.*

*I wasn’t going to listen,* Maureen responded silently.

The faces of the four apprentices had gone empty. The wet cobbles at the mouth of the alley began steaming where Soros had stood, but Maureen’s expression did not change at all.

“Where is Maffie?” Maureen asked.

The apprentices began stammering over each other in their haste to answer, until one of them met her eyes and blurted out, “At the Jadeleaf Ring!”

“That’s right!” added another.

“The Ring didn’t pay up! Ace got hurt, Boss lost face! He’s tearing the place down today!” one of them squeaked.

Maureen’s eyes narrowed and she turned, heading toward the Ring. Behind her, the black-red fire consumed all four of the apprentices, and their screams were brief.

*Logged but not shown,* Tally said, placing [Mana: 14 → 12] in the corner of Maureen’s vision. *You asked me a very long time ago never to show those again.*

The smells from the alley drifted back when the screaming stopped: the kitchen grease, the slop buckets, the sourness of vegetables and meat scraps. Now it was mingled with a new smell—burnt hair.

The cat that had been sitting on the bin lid was watching Maureen now, from atop a far wall. Its flat yellow eyes were fixed on her as she walked away from the alley.

* * *

She was walking fast now, pushing her aura back down layer by layer. She was no longer a burning sun, or even a blazing torch. Anyone who glanced at her would see just another apprentice hurrying home before the dusk grew too deep.

*Emilia’s law against killing in the street?*

*No data,* Tally reported.

*Four apprentices and an Official Wizard, burned in an alley.*

*No one saw,* Maureen thought back silently.

*By sight,* Tally responded. *I’m listening for the rest.*

*The messenger device died with its owner. Whoever is on the other end will have heard nothing for the last eighty seconds. They will notice.*

*So Maffie will know that something has gone wrong. Good.*

Up and down the streets, individual glow-lamps began to flicker into life. One by one they came on along the fronts of the wizards’ manors, their pale stone shining in the gathering gloom. Maureen’s shadow stretched beneath each lamp, then drew up tight around her feet again as she moved away. The evening air was rich with the smell of lamp oil and horse dung, and there was a chill in it, the cold of the farm plain creeping in on the wind.

Maureen thought about the request she had refused. Joanna hadn’t offered her anything of equivalent worth in exchange. She thought about the brand, and the dormant thread of fire that lay beneath the girl’s breastbone.

She didn’t call through either.

*Are you going to help them?* Tally asked.

*I’m going to see Maffie,* Maureen replied.

*That wasn’t an answer,* the dry voice said.

*It’s the one you’re getting.*

The streets grew shabbier as she approached the Ring. The carved leaves that decorated the gateposts at the ends of the long driveways were worn smooth by time, the paint flaking off the wooden shutters. This was a district that had been fashionable fifty years before, and hadn’t quite forgotten it yet.

* * *

She reached the Ring’s gate as a boot smashed the hall doors open. From the gate, over the shoulders of the last men crowding into the hall, she caught a glimpse of a tall thin man dressed in purple-red silk. His Official field was loose.

Shouts came from within. “Who’s there?”

Then another voice. “Get lost!”

The doors were wrenched closed again.

A frosting of white lay over the cracked flagstones of the path that the silk-clad man had walked down. It stretched across the courtyard, glinting faintly where it lay over the dead moss that filled the dry basin. Its edges were already melting away, the air cold but not cold enough in this chill evening. The carved stone leaves that hung over the lintel were grey with dust, the layers of years forming a fine patina over their surface.

Maureen stayed outside.

She halted in the shadows next to the dry basin, only a few strides away from the closed double doors, close enough to hear every word that passed through the thick wood.

Tally painted a picture for her of what lay beyond. Maffie stood in the centre of the room, his shape well above the sample for Barok. Senge was well below him, his shape thin and faded. Behind them were Ace, and nine other figures. Tally couldn’t make out much else, except for the apprentices huddled at the back, like small flames from candles.

Maffie was elementalised, and working ice. Tally could read the frost in his particles. Once Maureen was able to see him directly, she would be able to tell more.

Senge’s voice came through the wooden doors, warm and courteous, far too easy for the circumstances. “Maffie. It has been a long time. What brings you to our humble Ring?”

A second voice answered, hoarse and low, and cold as a winter morning. “You know why I’m here, old man. Ace!”

“Boss!” That was Ace’s voice.

Maureen knew that voice. It was the slick one she had folded over the table only the day before. Tally matched the sound against the record she’d made, and Maureen felt... nothing. No hatred, no fear, no triumph, just a small, dry tidiness.

“He came here to collect,” Maffie continued, “and got a beating for his trouble.”

The Steward spoke as though he hadn’t heard any of it. “Stones then. We have them ready.” She could hear him call out to the apprentice by name. “Joanna.” There was the creak of a box lid being opened. “Three hundred. We borrowed one hundred from your Society, and here are three hundred to return. Not a single stone short.”

There was a long silence. Then Maffie laughed, and the box cracked hard against the marble floor. Stones rattled everywhere, bouncing across the floor, a few of them rolling all the way to tap against the inside of the door.

Tally counted the scatter by sound, listening to each bounce and roll, and came up with approximately three hundred. *They were never the point,* Maureen said silently.

*No,* Tally agreed. *Smashing the box was.*

“My man shamed in your hall is a slap across my face,” said Maffie’s cold voice.

The warmth was gone from Senge’s voice now. “What do you want?”

“I want the wizard first,” Maffie said. “Hand him over and I will flay him alive. Then I want every stone, every book, every damned thing this Ring owns.”

Maureen stood outside in the dark courtyard, feeling the weeds between the cracked flagstones under her feet, feeling the dry basin furred with dead moss. Light leaked through the gaps of the shut double doors of the hall, and Maureen listened to herself being priced. Him. Flayed. Alive. The plain, unmarked jaw she’d borrowed felt cool beneath her fingertips, and for a moment she almost laughed.

Joanna’s voice sounded, quick and tight. “Wizard Richard has already left the Ring, he told me through his brand, and besides—”

The old Steward interrupted the girl. “Maffie. I’m sorry. I can do none of what you ask.”

*The bond does not transmit from this end,* Tally pointed out. *That’s why she’s grabbing hold of the last thing you said to her like a shield. Whether it’s for you or for the Ring is something I don’t have data for.*

“Can’t?” On Tally’s display Maureen saw Maffie’s density pour out of him in a torrent, striking a thin green screen that represented Senge. The whole building rang like hail drumming on a tin roof.

*Senge’s screen is failing,* Tally said. *Its density is dropping with every heartbeat. Three seconds. Perhaps four.*

The screen broke, the sound like a windowpane dropped to the floor. Something heavy hit the marble and slid across it, followed by a wet cough. Then came running feet.

“Steward!”

“Mentor!”

Maureen heard the slow, deliberate tread of Maffie’s boots across the marble floor.

“Look how weak you’ve got. I wasted Ace testing you. I’d have come anyway. I’ve wanted this Ring for years. Now I’ll just kill you and take it.”

*Always planned to,* Tally pointed out, flagging the data. *The debt was a pretext.*

*Then Ace was bait for them, not for me,* Maureen replied silently.

*Logged,* Tally said.

Joanna’s voice cracked across the hall. “This is the Jadeleaf Ring, Maffie, not your Society, you can’t just—”

A flat sound, like the smack of a palm against a cheek, interrupted her. Nobody inside the hall made another sound.

Maffie’s voice continued, unhurried. When the old man was dead he would break the rest in. The men would be sold as slaves, the women kept at the Society for his men. They’d earned a treat. Girl apprentices were not common.

The words reached her body before they reached her mind. The ship’s lower corridors, Kelly’s carriage, Lucy and Alice pushing away their rations in fear of being traded. Her fingers grew cold against her sleeve, perfectly still.

*I read you, Maureen. Pulse 70. Hands 12 degrees colder than before. You’re angry,* Tally said.

*Waiting,* Maureen replied silently.

*For what?*

*For it to be worth it.*

Her companion was quiet for a long moment. *That is honest,* Tally said eventually. *I will log that as honest.*

It was honest. She had refused the Ring because it offered her nothing of equal value. A Ring pulled back from the brink of death would owe her everything, and she wanted Maffie to put all his cards on that marble table first.

Inside the hall Senge coughed again, a wet and weak hacking. On Tally’s display Senge’s shape had gone rigid and still. Beside him Joanna’s shape had gone rigid as well, and beside her Christine’s shape was so still it looked like a candle that had suddenly stopped flickering.

“Right,” said Maffie, his voice quiet. “Enough. It’s over.”

Maureen felt the density gathering within Maffie again, the torrent shape appearing on Tally’s display. This time it was larger and it was aimed lower, where Senge lay on the floor. *Now,* Tally said, *or Senge dies.*

Maureen kicked the door. It ripped half free of its pins and slammed back against the wall. She walked into the hall slowly, feeling the cold air roll out around her, escaping past her body.

Her eyes took in the room in an instant. Magic stones lay scattered across the black marble floor, mixed with splinters of wood from the destroyed box. Frost crusted the flagstones. Senge was propped half-upright, leaning against Joanna, blood dripping from his mouth to stain his grey-white beard red. Five red finger marks stood out on Joanna’s cheek, and beside her Christine stood with the blank expression of a puppet whose strings have been cut.

Maureen’s eyes travelled the length of the room, taking in all the details before they came to rest on the tall thin man dressed in purple-red silk. His green eyes turned to regard her.

“You’re Maffie... right?”
