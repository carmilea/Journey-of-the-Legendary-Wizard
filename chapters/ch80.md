She ran low beneath the pines, the grey cloak snapping against her calves. The sweet air slid easily into her lungs, filling them with each breath timed to the rhythm of the Silver Light: three long breaths, one short. Her boots hissed against the carpet of pine needles as she moved, and the forest remained silent around her.

Tally kept the trail painted across her vision with a faint blue glow—a branch snapped at shoulder height, a dark smear on the pale bark of a tree, dead black insects curled up on the layer of leaves at intervals along the path.

*He’s dropping pieces of himself,* Tally said. *The intervals between pieces are increasing. Fewer every hundred paces.*

*He’s healing,* Maureen replied silently. She stepped over one of the still-twitching insects without breaking stride.

*He’s healing,* Tally agreed. *And you’re catching up. Both of those are true. I like the second one better.*

Maureen glanced back along the ridge. Tally hadn’t seen the blood-red robe or the bone-white face since she warned that one away. The only thing it had to report was the sound of the wind moving through the pines.

*Keep listening,* she told it.

[Mana: 4.1]

[Grenades: hand 9 / pack 70]

*Don’t use it all up just because you can,* it told her.

A flash of white appeared between two tree trunks ahead, forty paces distant. Varo was running half-solid now, stumbling slightly, with more of the black insects crawling back into the shape of his shoulder. His free hand was braced against a pine, as if he thought the ground was tilting beneath his feet.

Maureen grabbed another egg-shaped shell from the bandolier that ran across her chest and muttered “Kerrash” as it left her fingers. She threw it in a low arc, and it hit the ground at his heels.

The explosion came roaring back up the slope like a door slamming shut in a stone hall. Two pines split lengthwise down their trunks before they fell. Dust and shredded needles danced in the grey light, raining down for long seconds. The air smelled of hot iron and burnt resin.

Varo came up on hands and knees in the churned earth. He coughed, then retched and vomited a mouthful of bright blood, which splattered against his white sleeve. He lifted his head to glare at her through the haze.

“Jack,” he spat. His throat sounded raw. “Damn you, Jack. Why are you even—”

He stopped as three small black dots rose out of the dust, arcing towards him. They grew in size as they fell.

Maureen watched his face change. In the brief time they had been fighting, he’d learned what these innocuous-looking little eggs could do. The cold, handsome mask was stripped away, revealing a young man who saw the eggs falling towards him, and whose legs would not carry him away from them quickly enough.

“Kerrash,” Maureen whispered three times.

The eggshells burst as one, the roar of the triple explosions joining into a single peal of orange fire. Varo’s body was thrown across the slope like a discarded rag doll. A smoking bowl had been torn out of the forest floor.

[Grenades: hand 5 / pack 70.]

*Wait for the dust,* Tally said.

*I know,* Maureen told it silently, her hand resting on the bandolier. She counted her breaths.

The dust took long seconds to thin. Grey threads of smoke drifted off the raw earth, and in the centre of the crater lay a white shape that did not move.

It breathed, though, quick and then slow, quick and then slow.

* * *

Varo lay on his back. His Hader white robe had been torn into strips, held against raw and bloody flesh by the force of the explosions. Blood ran down his chin, flecking his teeth red. Small black insects crawled from beneath the edges of the strips, scuttling a finger’s length across his skin before their legs shivered and they died.

Maureen Olsen walked down into the crater slowly, the way a woman approaches a horse that might still kick out at her. She came to a stop two paces from his feet.

“Varo.”

Tally read him, comparing what it saw to its records from the day before. His pulse beat fast and fluttered at his throat, the skin over his face and hands the grey colour of old candle wax. His hands lay twitching in the dirt at his side.

In her particle sense, Maureen felt no surge above Varo’s baseline.

*He has nothing else left,* Tally said. *Let him talk. You’re breathing.*

And underneath, like a litany, its count continued, three long, one short, three long, one short . . .

*See the number on the back of his hand? The Ancient Andas numeral? Thirty-one. I’ve added that to our table of totals. That’s the highest I’ve recorded since the realm opened.*

Varo’s head jerked up. “Why?” he screamed, spraying a fine red mist from his lips. “I never touched you! I didn’t do anything to you! Why, why, why?”

The last why broke high, the way a boy’s voice does when he hasn’t grown into it yet. Beneath the cold, emotionless Hader face, Maureen saw how young he was. Younger than she had thought, even on the ridge. Her jaw tightened, but otherwise she did not move.

“No reason,” she said. She held up one gloved hand slowly, turning it so he could see that her palm was open, that there was nothing in her hand.

*Aberrant transformation.* Tally gave her the term from the library mutation entries.

“You rebuilt your nerves and cells,” she said in Jack’s quiet, male rasp. “All the way down to their basic structure, so your body would be capable of hosting an arcane life-form as your soul-companion.” Her eyes were still locked on the boy’s face. “It’s clever. A good idea, even. I mean that.”

*He should know what he’s dying for,* she told Tally silently.

*That isn’t very kind,* Tally answered.

*No,* she answered back, and made no effort to soften it.

“It’s only a pity,” she said, almost gently, “that your companion is a Heart-Eater Worm.”

In the garish light of the grenade the day before, Tally had compared Varo’s insects against the plate in Adrian’s pharmacology at close range, and kept coming back to the Heart-Eater Worm. One of the four materials used in the creation of the Minai was the powdered form of the worm. That had been enough to seal Varo’s death right then.

She smiled just a little. The left side of the face she had borrowed came into synchronization with the right a half-beat later, making her smile sit crookedly on the strange face. Varo stared at her, then he laughed. It was wet and bitter, making his ribs heave with the force of it. “My worm? That’s all? I thought . . . all this for my worm?”

The sound went into Maureen like cold water. She held her breath, swallowing once to keep from gagging, making certain that none of the boy’s horror reached her voice or her expression.

The worm was bound to his soul. Any damage done to it meant his death. He had nothing left to bargain with.

He dragged himself up onto one elbow. “Then come and take it,” he snarled. “Or I’ll take you.”

*Your pitch is drifting up,* Tally warned. *You’re halfway back to your own voice already.*

Maureen forced it back down into the reshaped vocal cords of her throat. The rasp settled back into place.

* * *

He never got all the way up.

Maureen Olsen thumbed two more shells out of the bandolier. “Kerrash,” she muttered twice as she lobbed them down into the crater at Varo’s knees.

“These damned things again!” the Hader Castle apprentice howled. He clutched at his legs, where the shrapnel had ripped through his robes. Fist against fist, spell against spell, he would have crushed this Jack. But here he was, beaten bloody and helpless by a handful of eggs filled with iron.

She read the anger and frustration in his face, and knew he was right. He was stronger than she, body to body. His swarm would have eaten her alive on the ridge. The thought passed her mind like a shadow, leaving no trace behind.

[Grenades: hand 3 / pack 70.]

*He’s right about the straight fight,* Tally said.

*Then let’s not have one,* Maureen answered silently.

As the shells fell toward the crater, Varo threw back his head and screamed, “Jack! Dead or not, you’ll never have it!”

A low hum began to rise from him, a vibration so deep that Maureen felt it buzzing in her teeth. His body began to come apart, splitting and breaking up into countless flying black insects. For a heartbeat, they hung in the air in the shape of the apprentice, then burst out from the center, flying outward in every direction at once.

The grenades went off inside the cloud of insects. A wave of fire rolled out from the crater, thousands of tiny bodies popping and crackling in the heat like fat dropped on a hot griddle. But already a dark spray of survivors had streaked away between the trunks.

[Swarm: est. 2,000. Dispersing.]

Then the second voice spoke, the one that spoke without emotion. > Wide spread.

*Hand first,* Maureen told Tally. *Because the Hand makes the centre, and the Field needs something to turn around.*

*Your way,* Tally said immediately. *Logged.*

Maureen held her arms out and spread her fingers wide. "Dark Wind Ring," she murmured, low in her throat. She spoke the word in the standard tongue, which was all the optimised model needed to activate it, but she made the same scattering gesture with her fingers as if she were throwing handfuls of powder. Which, in truth, she used to do, back when her spells still needed components.

The black wind began to leak out from the folds of the grey cloak and from the black robe packed tightly into her traveler's pack. It came out thin at first, as if reluctant to emerge from its confinement, smelling of the dark inside of cellars and chests. The needles at her feet lifted and rustled as the cold wind tugged at them.

The Hand of Negative Energy formed in mid air, at the exact centre of the crater. Half a beat later, the Mantik Death-Aura Field wrapped itself around it, catching the wind that now turned and began to stand upright in a column. The wind grew stronger as Maureen fed more of her will to it, howling like a gale funnelled up a chimney. A cyclone of black wind spun within the crater, rising higher than the pines. Pine needles, ash, and torn white cloth flew up the outside walls in one long spiral.

The fleeing insects slowed in the air for a heartbeat, then began to be sucked back through the trees. They flapped their wings furiously against the pull, but one after another they were dragged into the roaring black wall at the centre. Heat from the grenades' explosions was swallowed by the cyclone as well, along with the scattered shards of Hillan.

The splintered metal was flung around by the wind, grinding together in a vicious rasp that shredded anything trapped inside.

[Swarm: est. 2,000 → 300.] *It works,* Tally said, sounding almost cheerful.

The shrieking of the dying insects rose above the howl of the wind, countless thin voices rising higher and higher until they merged into one high, sustained note, like running a wet finger around the rim of a glass.

[Swarm: 300 → 12.]

[Mana: draining.]

The dots inside the swirling black wall began to dim. Tally showed them as tiny dots within the larger cyclone. They were still beating against it, trying to get out.

"Time to die," she whispered, pushing more mana into the spell, a great surge of it, all at once.

She didn't draw on strength. This came from her will.

Her hands remained steady in the air, though now her temples began to throb. The Dark Wind Ring responded to her command with a deeper howl, and needles began to shake loose from the standing pines and flutter away into the dark.

[Swarm: 12 → 1.]

*Last one. High on the left wall.*

The last insect tore itself apart with a single shriek, and with that cry came another, buried deep inside the howl of the Dark Wind. It was a man's voice, screaming once, faint and far away. Varo's voice. Then that was gone.

[Swarm: 0.]

*He's gone,* Tally said quietly. *When the last of the swarm died, he died with it.*

Maureen let out a long breath through her teeth. Above an empty crater the cyclone continued to spin.

* * *

The crater was silent now.

The grey light stilled. A few stray pine needles fluttered down to the ground. The air was sweet with the smell of ozone, ash, and something dry and peppery. At the lip of the crater lay a split stump.

[Mana: 4.1 → 1.9.]

Maureen felt the cost. *Spirit strain,* Tally added, inferring the rest. Maureen could feel her pulse, loud in her ears, and a headache settling in behind her eyes. *Sit for one minute,* Tally said.

*Thirty seconds.*

*Forty-five seconds, and I'll stop talking.*

Maureen sat down on the split stump and gave it forty-five seconds. It felt good to rest. Her legs didn't want to get back up. The ache was building behind her eyes, throbbing in time with her heartbeat. She pressed her gloved knuckles against her brow, pushing hard enough to hurt a bit more.

There wasn't a scratch or wound anywhere on her body. Just a deep tiredness from a chase that had gone on far too long.

Maureen Olsen stood up and let the Dark Wind Ring wind down. She opened her palm, and a final black spiral twisted onto her glove, spinning once like a cat settling down to sleep before it faded away.

The glove was left with a small pile of powder. It was black and dry, finer than soot. It smelled faintly of old books, with just a pinch of ground pepper.

Tally checked the powder against the plate for Heart-Eater Worm. It matched the preparation entry. Then it checked against her particle sense: texture, colour, smell, and residue.

[Material: Heart-Eater Worm powder. Quality: good, by plate and residue. Minai: 1 of 4 in hand.]

Tally said, *You've just halved the list's remaining distance in a bad week.*

*That's not how fractions work.*

*It's how hope works. I'm allowed one unscientific line per day.*

Something warm and fierce flared in her chest. Jack's crooked mouth quirked upward in what might have been a real smile. Tally informed her that it was logging the smile for future reference. Maureen let it log the smile.

She reached down to her belt kit and pulled out a small, dark glass vial. It took some care, but she managed to tip the powder out of her glove without losing a grain, catching it all on the edge of the vial. She stoppered it and sealed the stopper with wax, then held it up to the grey light.

Maureen tucked the tiny vial deep into the inner fold of her coat, where it would keep company with the healing potions from the trial. She pressed her hand over it, flattening her palm against the fabric for a moment.

A faint tugging came through the glyph on the back of her hand. Varo's thirty-one. Reaching for her. Maureen clenched her will and refused the points. They could not be taken if they were not accepted. That was one thing that she had learned.

*That would have put you in the top ten,* Tally observed.

*That's why.*

The unaccepted points unravelled themselves from the crater, leaving a pale wisp behind. Brighter than any Tally had logged before. The wisp drifted away northeast, over the tops of the pines. Maureen watched it go until it was swallowed by the grey cloud cover overhead. While she knelt to restock her bandolier from the padded pack, she kept her eyes moving over the treeline.

*You are slowest when restocking,* Tally reminded her. [Grenades: hand 3 → 12 / pack 70 → 61.]

Maureen walked the circumference of the crater once. Nothing remained of Varo but a few scraps of white cloth and the glint of iron. With no sign of movement on any side, Maureen turned her back to the northeast and headed in another direction.

She had not gone far when there was movement among the trunks ahead. A group of people approached, making their way between the trees in her direction.

Tally began counting them by sight, marking each dark outline against the trees. One...two...three...

*Your mana is still at 1.9.*
