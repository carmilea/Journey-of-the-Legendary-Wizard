“No, wait! Please! I’ll give you everything! I’ll . . . !” The Blue Ring boy backed away through the ferns, both hands raised, blood running off his fingers in streams. His heel caught on a birch root, and he stumbled to one elbow.

Maureen followed him down the slope, wearing Jack’s gaunt, hollow-cheeked face. The young apprentice wore a blue-green robe—the same colour Patton and Bina had worn—but she merely noted that fact and let it pass from her mind. There was no halo around this Blue Ring. His mana was exhausted. To Maureen he was just a pouch wrapped in boy.

“Amra-Keis,” she said, her voice rasping low from its reshaped vocal cords.

A ball of sour green spun out from her palm, trailing a wisp of white steam, and exploded against the Blue Ring’s chest. He screamed once, and then there was nothing but a pool of hissing red-green slime soaking into the ferns where he’d been.

The glyph on the back of Maureen’s hand tugged as the boy’s points reached for her, but she refused them with an iron will, as she had every point this week. Tally noted the pale wisp that unraveled from the slime, then drifted northeast toward the fog bank below.

*Same bearing as all the others,* it told her.

*I’ve noticed,* she answered silently.

She stepped up to the still-fizzing pool and stirred it with the tip of Freya’s slender dark-elf blade. The point caught against something hard. With a flick of her wrist, Maureen flipped the object out of the slime—an egg-sized purple stone that trailed a thin stream of blue-grey smoke. She closed her fingers over it.

[Starfish Tear Stone. Match: entry, by colour and smoke.]

*That was the first item on the list,* Tally said. *You knew you hated it from the start.*

*Hated hunting it,* Maureen corrected silently.

*Eleven apprentices for one stone,* Tally said. *I kept that number off your display.*

*Keep it off,* she told it.

The stone felt cool in Maureen’s palm, with just a hint of moisture, like glass taken from a river. Its smoke curled between her fingers, smelling of salt and tide-wrack. A sea smell, though there was no sea anywhere within this sweet and dead air.

For days now, Maureen had been setting out her work around the valley. Tally’s projections had come back good. Then she’d run into the problem of materials. No one in a slaughterhouse would sell what she needed, so she’d walked the ridges and taken them from the people who carried them.

Maureen wiped the blade on the ferns. It still had the same balance as before, a finger length forward of her old sword’s balance point, and it still surprised her wrist when it flashed out. “Only the last step left,” she murmured.

Since she was alone, it came out in her own voice, and her fist clenched tightly around the Starfish Tear Stone.

*You said that in your own voice,* Tally pointed out.

*There’s no one to hear,* she answered silently.

*I heard you,* Tally said.

She tugged at her singed grey cloak to straighten it, then continued downhill toward the fog.

* * *

Maureen knelt in the dark and set out the Starfish Tear Stone. The fog lay behind her, humming like a kettle far off in the night. Above her head, the shutters over the lantern were pulled nearly closed, leaving only a finger-width gap for light to escape. There was no moon in the sky, and the valley floor around the edge of the fog was quiet enough to hear a mouse squeak.

She had spent the evening covering the Starfish Tear Stone with wizard runes, scratching the lines in with a fine engraving needle. They were so thin that they looked like hairs drawn across the surface, and they were packed tight against each other, edge to edge. It didn’t look like anything that had ever come out of the ground.

Her fingertips were sore from the needlework, and there was purple grit packed into the creases of her nails. Maureen brushed her hand against her trousers, trying to clean some of the grit out. The fog hummed quietly in the night.

Tally projected the pattern from Gregory’s Rune Secret Lines shelves onto the ground in pale blue, showing her twenty nodes forming a ring tightly around the fog. Pale spurs stretched out from the nodes toward the two mouths of the valley. Maureen walked the pattern slowly, the lines shifting under her boots as she moved. At each node she crouched, brushing away the pale grit to check the scoop that she’d dug the night before, and the grenade waiting within it.

The black shells were cold against her hands, even through her gloves. They were egg-shaped, surprisingly heavy for their size, and dulled by a thin skin of dirt. They looked like stones that some child had buried beside a riverbank and then forgotten about.

[Charges: 20. Grenades: hand 12 / pack 26.] *That’s most of your pack down there,* Tally noted. *Yep,* she answered silently.

*That’s where they’ll be safest,* she answered silently.

*That’s not really a sentence you want to say out loud.*

One last time, she asked for the projection, and Tally overlaid it on the scenery. It began with their numbers, the twenty nodes, and the two mouths of the valley. Their greed would drive them into the array, and one word sent through the array could take the place of twenty spoken Kerrashes. Three outcomes flickered back, and each one was better than having no array at all.

At its heart, she had cut a hollow in the rock’s flat top. The stone fitted, and settled with a small, dry click as if it belonged there.

She spoke the arming words, a soft sibilant whisper. Every vowel was placed precisely where the pattern wanted it. From the ground, runes rose. In every direction, at once, they spread like frost creeping across a window.

For one breath, the entire valley floor lay drawn in the faintest of silver-violet. It was a map inked upon the night, and the fog sat within its centre. Then the lines faded, leaving behind only ordinary pale grit with twenty black eggs sleeping beneath.

Tally compared the ground with its frame from before the arming, but found no differences by eye. It logged the array as armed, on the basis of the glow alone. *That is the only difference I can detect,* it said honestly.

Maureen sat back on her heels. There was a feeling to this, of sealing the last reaction vessel at Jing Sheng’s white bench. All measured, all calculated, all accounted for. The world sealed up tight so that nothing could escape or intrude until the time was right. There was nothing left to do, now, except wait.

The bait had been compounded over the last few days, sitting in its stoppered jar. She had the chant memorized. Her line of retreat was planned. Almost everything was in place.

*You are smiling. This is also being logged,* Tally told her.

*Log it as maintenance then,* she answered silently. Maureen Olsen picked up the lantern and walked back into the trees.

* * *

Bevis was sitting on the fallen trunk when Maureen came out of the pines. He stood up, his movements a little too quick.

“Jack.”

“Bevis,” she responded in her rasp. She nodded.

The Dark Forest apprentice looked surprised. His eyes were deep enough to sink in. It took less than a heartbeat for him to compose himself again. A man who has decided not to ask, Maureen thought as she approached. His fiery red hair stirred slightly in a breeze that wasn’t there. She could tell he wanted her to know that he’d made the decision.

*Hands,* Tally noted. *His hands are open and away from his belt. The non-aggression pact still stands.*

*Open hands cost him nothing.*

Durin and Carter were coming up through the trees from the valley side. Both of them spotted her and exchanged a look, then walked towards her and Bevis without saying anything. Neither of the apprentices asked where Jack had been. The fact that they didn’t ask was loud enough.

“So many dead already,” Durin finally said, letting out a long breath as he stared down at the white wall of fog below the ridge. His blue-green robe was the same colour as the boy who had been dissolving into the ferns on the far slope.

Jack’s gaunt face revealed nothing. Beneath it, Maureen kept her own face calm as well, out of long habit.

Carter laughed through his nose. “Already? Not nearly. Ninety in every hundred, and only then do they let the rest of us out.” He was the Mist Labyrinth’s prodigy, but he wasn’t the son of any family that mattered, and he said it like a man who knew he’d been sent in as the spare.

Tally read him by sight, jaw clenched so hard it might shatter, thumb rubbing around and around the ring on his other hand. When he said ‘ninety’, Tally told her that matched the rules it had shown her on the day she arrived, and there was still no time limit.

Sieg just leaned against a pine tree and looked at her for a moment, then at each of the others in turn. Something shifted beneath the bone, something that might almost have been a smile. He said nothing at all.

“Look at the bright side,” Bevis said, too easily. “The rewards are real. Maybe a high-level meditation method, one that actually suits us. With one of those, our futures would be...” He didn’t finish the sentence.

*That’s my file,* she told Tally silently.

*Your file,* Tally agreed. *A column of its own. Again.*

No one spoke. Carter studied his hands while Durin stared up at the north ridge. Bevis kept looking down at the fog. She found herself asking again the question she’d asked since they had gathered here: why would orders at war with each other agree to send their apprentices into the same pit? Tally had no data, and the wind brought another scream up from one of the other slopes.

* * *

There was a shudder beneath them, so sudden and violent it knocked them all off balance. Cries rose from every slope, full of confusion and fear.

“What was that?”

“What’s going on?”

She and the others exchanged one look before picking a bearing together. “The centre!” they cried as one. They burst out from the pines and ran across the valley floor.

Freya’s sword banged against Maureen’s hip with every stride, but the loose grit swallowed the sound of their approach. Ahead, the wall of fog was breaking apart, dissolving like breath vanishing from a cool mirror. Sounds were drifting through the rips now, carried on the wind.

Bevis slowed as they reached the edge of the white curtain. His eyes narrowed as he stared into the clearing fog. “Enough have died,” he said quietly. “Whatever’s in that valley is about to show itself.”

The last few rags of fog peeled back, and they saw what was inside.

Hounds.

Several of them, snarling and slavering, with long fangs jutting from their upper jaws. Thick black cords were tied around each hound’s neck, the strands wrapped tight in knots. The cords were connected, binding the hounds together, so none of them was more than a few strides away from its nearest companion. Their heads swung from side to side as they tore at something on the ground.

They were tearing at people.

Translucent wisps screamed in their jaws, voices fading as the fangs dug deeper. “Help me, someone, help...” The faces stretched like wet paper being pulled apart by opposing hands.

She recognised some of those faces. There was the Blue Ring boy from the ferns, and the Hader apprentice from her first fight.

Her stomach dropped as though she’d missed the final stair in a flight of steps. Cold slithered up the back of her neck. Jack’s face was impassive, but she could not force her fingers open again. They had drawn into a fist without her willing it.

*The wisps,* Tally said, voice very quiet.

*That was where they went. All of them. Every one she had refused.*

*And the ones you accepted?* Maureen asked silently.

*Those too,* Tally thought. It was only numbers on the back of her hand, but the dead came here either way. *That is the only comfort I have for you,* it added. It closed the file it had kept open for days, the one labelled: [Dry dead. Cause: soul-feeding, by entry + sight.]

Maureen took three long breaths, then one short one, pulling Silver Light in tight around her. She forced her hand to open. For the space of a single breath, the total cost of her method lay spread out before her. She filed it away and didn’t pause.

Durin’s voice had climbed half an octave. “What are those things?”

“I’ve seen them,” Bevis said, frowning. “In a book... They’re called a Skoll Soul-Hunting Fruit. Those are Skoll Soul-Hunting Fruits!”

Every face around Maureen flushed with heat, with greed.

Tally confirmed it against Gregory’s entry, but this time by sight.

*No animals at all. A plant from another world. Ripe only on wizard radiation, young, living souls. That’s why they were killing each other.*

“Official Wizard grade,” Durin breathed. “If I had even one...”

Then he and the others were charging forward, spells whistling ahead of them.

Fire and frost exploded across the hounds’ hides. And did almost nothing. The hounds turned with snarling roars. A heartbeat later the apprentices were falling back, a dozen places torn open along their bodies, and one of the hounds had torn a chunk out of Carter’s thigh, down to the white bone.

Carter’s scream split the air. The smell of fresh blood rose above the sugary scent, and he dragged his leg behind him, the pale bone peering out from the ruined meat.

Bevis wiped the blood off his mouth with the back of his hand. “Their bodies are too strong. The spells can barely bite. They kill ordinary Official Wizards! We can’t...” He looked up at the rest of them, then around. “Jack?”

She was already gone. She ran low and fast around the edge of the pack, a black shadow against the gritty ground.

Tally overlaid the leash lengths on top of Maureen’s sight, pale blue arcs dancing across the pale grit.

> Tethers: 9 m. Stop at 10.

Maureen skidded to a stop at ten metres, yanking the stopper from the jar. She flung a mist of flesh-coloured fog from it, chanting as she did so. The mist writhed and knotted on the air, then fell to the ground with a wet thump. Heaps of white, meaty bones lay where it landed.

The hounds froze, then shivered all the way down their backs and tails. They abandoned the shades mid-scream, turning to face their new prey. Then they were wagging and crunching, the sounds of splintering bone wet between their jaws. None of them even glanced at Maureen.

*Dogs,* Tally commented.

*Dogs are dogs,* Maureen replied silently.

Tally confirmed that Gregory’s entry had indeed stated just that, though in many more words. The bait the soul-hunters used to attract their prey was the same recipe compounded from looted materials over the past few days. And it was working just as advertised.

Maureen Olsen smiled a colder smile. She held out her hand toward the nearest of the snarling monsters, and began the chant: “Iskanr—”

*Back, lower, the tongue further down.* The correction came from Tally right in the middle of the word.

Maureen’s tongue adjusted without breaking rhythm. A purple-red flame jumped up in her palm. Within the fire something howled, its voice hoarse, like the sound of a throat being torn apart. The flame rolled off her palm in a wave, enveloping the nearest hound. The rest of the pack kept on eating.

The flame did not radiate any heat Maureen could feel, but there was a smell of singed fur and scorched honey. The monster went down on its side, eyes still filled with murderous intent, legs jerking feebly in vain attempts to launch itself at her. Maureen Olsen kept chanting. The snarls died down to whimpers, the noise of a dog who knows it is beaten. With one last howl, the beast exploded, and in the air where it had been standing hung a fruit the size of a baby’s fist, purple-red in colour. It pulsed with an inner light, like the glow of the Seal. Then it dropped into Maureen Olsen’s outstretched hand, warm and heavy. *Finally mine.*

[Mana: 7.6 → 4.5]

Her pulse was close to 140 now, Tally told her, and the edges of her vision were beginning to grey.

*One is enough,* Tally told her.

*The bait has time,* she answered back silently.

*You do not have time,* Tally said. *You have less time than the bait does.*

Sweat was running into Jack’s eyes. Her face had gone pale beneath the illusion, and her hands shook as she reached out toward the next hound. But there were bones yet to be gnawed. She wrapped the beast in purple-red fire, doing it faster this time, and a second fruit pulsed into her palm.

[Mana: 4.5 → 1.6. Skoll fruits: 2]

*Logged as greed,* Tally said.

*Logged as two,* she answered.

*And if you’re wondering about the significance of the number two,* Tally added more quietly, *Gregory noted that the fruit would aid an Official Wizard in his advance. For you, that means Spirit, which is what you are short of the inheritance standard. The mountain.*

The last bone cracked. As one, every head swivelled round to face her, jaws opening wide. She threw herself backward as the pack lunged, the black cords snapping taut with an audible twang, jerking them up short. Speaking “Kerrash” thrice over the shells in her hand, she tossed them backward over her shoulder. The explosions covered her as she bolted clear, the two fruits pulsing against her ribs through the bag slung around her waist.

* * *

[Grenades: hand 9 / pack 26.]

The count hovered at the edge of her sight, the two Skoll fruits beating against her ribs. She allowed herself a single small breath.

*Good.*

*Out of the valley first,* Tally answered. *Then I’ll say good too.*

A light flickered in the very edge of her vision.

> Left. Spell. Move.

She obeyed, jumping to the side as the ground where she had been standing erupted in a roar of fire and smoke.

Earth slapped against her back through her cloak, the heat of the explosion licked across one cheek, and Maureen tasted grit in her mouth. She landed on her feet, still breathing slowly and regularly, then turned her head to look around.

Figures stepped out of the smoke, and she looked them over dispassionately, one by one. “Carter. Durin.” Her eyes shifted. “And you, Sieg. And the girl from Hader Castle.”

A short laugh, cold and contemptuous, rumbled in Jack’s throat. “How interesting.”

Tally tagged each apprentice as Jack named them. Bevis was not present, at least not by sight. The pact had held, so far. The veiled girl’s walk matched one old ship file at low confidence. Maureen Olsen waved it away again with an impatient internal flick.

Carter’s leg was wrapped in a strip torn from his own blood-soaked robe, and his face was smeared with red. “Jack! Hand over both of those fruits!”

She didn’t need to try to read him. She could see everything she needed. Thigh torn to the bone, nothing in the hands, Jack getting away with two Skoll fruits. Envy twisted Carter’s entire face.

Maureen Olsen looked at him for one long beat. “Idiot.”

“Stop talking to him, go!”

“He’s strong, fine, but there are more of us!”

“For the fruits!”

They came at her as one, Carter and the veiled girl in the lead, fastest of all, and from either side of the scuffed slope shapes rose out of the brush to join the kill.

Maureen’s hand drifted up, toward the inner pocket that held the cracked Seal.

*Pocket,* Tally said sharply. *It’s cracked. Don’t touch it.*

*I wasn’t going to,* she thought silently.

*You were.* The hand drew away, empty.

“You thought I hadn’t prepared?” The contempt in her voice was naked. Her hand rose to the casting gesture.

Tally counted feet on the hidden array, the overlay flashing in Maureen’s eyes.

> All four in nodes. Now.

“Kerrash.”

The word ran away from her, sank into the ground.

The earth hummed.

Rune lines streaked away from her in silver-violet, racing across the slope faster than any human could run. Twenty charges went up together, a tide of fire rising from the ground. The rushing apprentices vanished in it, the lurkers in the brush disappearing with them. A wall of fire washed over Maureen, heat hitting her like an open furnace door, her cloak cracking and billowing behind her like a sail. Screams rose on the air, only to be abruptly cut off by the roar of flame. She stood straight and unmoving in the middle of it, her face giving nothing away.

The fire rolled away from her in slow waves, like a tide going out to sea. Where it passed, it left blackened grit, smoke, and silence. Durin and Sieg were gone, lost somewhere within the rolling grey smoke. Off to one side, away from the main rush, the veiled girl lay in a scorched white robe. She was moving.

Directly before Maureen, having run fastest, Carter dragged himself out of the smoke on his elbows. Blood dribbled from his mouth. “Jack... Jack, you... you...”

The words wouldn’t come.

She held out her hand. “Simons-Sado.” The deep-red fireball burned through the air and struck him squarely in the chest, blowing him apart. Burning scraps of flesh and bone spun away through the grit, still flickering with small flames.

[Mana: 1.6 → 0.5.]

*That’s your last fireball,* Tally said.

*That was my last fireball.* Carter’s points pulled at her hand, trying to reach her. She refused them, and this time she watched the pale wisp drift away toward the hounds, knowing where it went.
