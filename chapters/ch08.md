Scarlette began teaching them Ancient Andas, explaining that where your tongue was positioned in your mouth affected the meaning of a word.

"It is an interesting language," Scarlette said. "So you need to know the basics. It is part of what the Blue Ring pays me for."

Tally mapped each tongue position faster than Scarlette could explain them. Maureen repeated the first pair silently, letting her tongue go where it wanted.

Wrong, Maureen. Tongue back. Again.

Maureen did it again.

That's better.

Beside her, Richard mouthed the same word wrong and screwed up his face. Ellena sat with her back straight, her chin up, as if it made no difference to her.

Dust danced in the shaft of light that came in through the shutter, turning gently. The faint glow from the open book on the old man's knee faded as Scarlette closed it.

"That will do for now," he said at last, and the seeds bowed before filing out of the carriage. "Maureen Olsen, please remain."

The carriage gradually emptied. Maureen remained seated, hands resting calmly in her lap, expression pleasant. Her back felt hot. Sweat gathered there.

Known source, Tally said. Steady. The old man's eyes had gone to her satchel twice. Two seconds. Then three.

The carriage creaked as it passed over a rut. The old man rested one hand lightly on the glowing book that lay open on his knee, and turned to look at her.

"And what was your quarrel with young Carey the gardener about?" he asked in his mild voice.

"Just a small matter, honoured lord," she answered quietly. "It is already settled."

"Good. I am pleased to hear that the young man came to no harm," Scarlette said. "Well done." He chuckled softly to himself, shaking his head at the memory of Carey coming to him, full of complaint.

The old man cast a quick glance at her, and Maureen could see that she had ranked higher in his estimation than Carey. "And why have you been collecting so many different kinds of materials lately?"

Stay close to the truth, Tally warned. Your curiosity is genuine.

I know, Maureen assured it silently.

"I am curious, honoured lord," she said to Scarlette. "I have heard that combining different materials can create strange effects."

Scarlette studied her for a while, then smiled. "Curiosity? That is a good thing to have, Maureen. Perhaps you will become an apothecary someday."

Maureen smiled demurely, as if she didn't know what he meant.

His breathing hasn't changed, Tally noted. Either he believes you or he is very old.

He is very old, Maureen responded silently.

Then I don't know.

Without being asked, it continued, I don't have any data on what an apothecary is to wizards. I would like some.

Fine. I'll see what I can find for you, Maureen thought.

It was mid-morning. A slat of light shone through the shutter and the dust in the air spun around in a slow dance, glittering in the brightness. The velvet seats of the carriage were worn, their once bright colours now shiny from years of use. The wheels and harness creaked softly as the carriage rolled on. A book lay open across the old man's knees. It was glowing slightly.

Scarlette waved his hand tiredly when she asked for a favour.

"Honoured lord," Maureen said aloud. "I have been practising the Knight Breathing Technique and I feel that I am ready to spar with your followers. They are all Upper Knights, aren't they?" One hand rested casually on the plain worn manual that she had brought down from the Skran treasury. It lay closed on the seat next to her.

You're lying with props now, Tally said inside her head. Your numbers come from 479 dead swordsmen. No live one has ever hit you.

A prop is just a prop, Maureen responded silently. Her pulse raced a little faster underneath the Silver Light rhythm she was actually using, and she bowed her head just a fraction lower than she needed to.

"Certainly, child," Scarlette said. He knocked on the side of the carriage and called out, "Jack!"

The curtain separating the driver's area from the carriage was lifted, and a big man dressed in black armour leaned in. His blond hair and beard were long and bushy, and his face was serious. "Yes, Master?"

"From today onwards, Maureen will be training swordsmanship with your men. Please do not hurt her." "Yes, Master," the man called Jack said, holding the curtain open as he glanced once at Maureen.

He stands like a man who has spent a great deal of time in armour, Tally told Maureen silently. That glance dismissed you as no threat. He thinks you will break.

Jack thumped his fist against his chest without saying another word, then let the curtain fall back into place. Maureen thanked Scarlette for his help, then climbed down from the carriage.

* * *

"Maureen! What did Scarlette want?" Richard asked, hurrying over.

"He wants me to spar with the knights," Maureen told him bluntly.

Richard launched into an enthusiastic discussion on the topic that lasted all the way along the column.

Her days settled into a routine - Ancient Andas, Ancient Pati and Ancient Fusiform Script in the mornings, followed by sword work with the black-clad knights in the afternoons. As she walked through the dawn frost, crunching the grass beneath her boots, the knights would cast glances at the girl armed with the Cross Sword. Then Jack would move silently to block her path, and they would begin. They fought with swords only; no knight powers were permitted on either side. So while the knights could see Maureen was good, none of them could tell just how good. As each knight attacked Maureen, Tally recorded their style. The bearded man liked to feint high when he got tired, the tall man never stepped left when he parried. Overnight, Tally folded each knight's style into Maureen's Basic Swordsmanship. By the end of two months Maureen had yellowing bruises on her forearms from blocked blows. Jack's silence began to wear down. At first he just nodded once in approval, then later in the trip he corrected her footing with a gruff comment, his beard dark with sweat from exertion.

In the frosty training ground at dawn, their breaths smoking in the chill air, Maureen would silently instruct Tally to make her lose some bouts. Tally would sulk in the back of her head, complaining that losing on purpose would waste good data, but it would plan out the losses. Step by step, Tally would guide her through how she should miss a blow. Her right foot would slip an inch to the side. She would parry the next strike one beat too late.

Richard and Aaron would sometimes take a turn with Maureen, facing her with practice swords. The ring of steel on steel echoed sharply in the cold morning air, and the creak of oiled leather came from all three combatants. Aaron would always wipe down his blade with a cloth after each round. Every bout ended even. That was how Maureen made certain it would happen.

Forty percent power, Tally murmured in the back of her head, as she parried a swing from Richard. Approaching ceiling, forty-one percent . . . Another clash of practice blades rang out, and Maureen could see Richard's frown deepen. He is looking at you suspiciously. Drop to thirty-five percent. He gets that way when he grows suspicious.

Maureen eased up on her attacks, pressing her lips together to keep from grinning.

On the roadside, she gathered plants and fruit for Tally to record in its database. Plan one, immune. Plan two, immune. Plan three, three Sand-Snake Fruit per day, Tally listed off in the back of her mind. The gritty fruit tasted bitter and Maureen grumbled silently to Tally about the taste. Taste is data, Tally responded, sounding quite pleased with itself.

At dusk, she sat beside the cold stream, letting the water run over her split knuckles. The smoke from the campfires wafted overhead. "Your hair looks darker at the roots," Richard said aloud. He sat beside her, running his hand through the cool stream.

"It's just road dust," Maureen replied. She pulled her hair back and tucked it under her hood.

Pigment change logged. No data on cause of change, Tally murmured in her head.

Keep watching it, Maureen told Tally silently.

I am, said Tally.

* * *

The convoy stopped as they reached a wide area of flat ground. Above them the red rock walls caught the last of the sun's rays and glowed softly. Two Cross Swords rang out by the side of the carriages.

Maureen and Jack were using only swords for this bout. Metal met metal, again and again as Jack used heavy, practised strokes. He was good with a sword.

Weight forward, grip reset. Tally noted. That is his tell for a thrust. One, two, three, four. Tally counted the beat in Maureen's head.

Now! Tally yelled as Jack's weight shifted forward into a thrust.

Maureen stepped inside the blow, letting Jack's own momentum carry her blade up to rest against his neck. His arm froze with his hand in the air, the point of his sword a foot away from her chest. They stood there for a moment, both of them breathing hard from their practice. "Mr. Jack," Maureen said quietly. "You lose."

A hot ball of satisfaction blossomed in the pit of her stomach. Quickly she pushed the feeling away and focused on keeping her sword steady against Jack's neck.

Jack gave her a wry smile. "Well done, Lady Maureen. You have improved quickly in your skills. I don't think I've ever seen swordsmanship as frightening as yours."

"Thank you, Mr. Jack." Maureen pulled her sword back and sheathed it. "Though you didn't use any of your knight powers."

These veterans are fierce warriors, Tally, Maureen thought. I'm as good as they are now.

He's smiling, Tally responded, now sounding warm again. Smiling is new data.

Maureen Olsen. Middle Knight. Strength 6.3, Agility 6.7, Constitution 6.6, Tally recited in her head. You were at point five under three months ago.

I know, Maureen thought.

"Maureen! Maureen!" Richard was running up to them, his face flushed.

"What is it, Richard?" Maureen asked.

Richard pointed to the horizon behind him, where dozens of carriages were rolling in. She could see black clad knights and youths climbing out of the carriages.

Tally began counting the carriages and their escorts as they approached, then began sorting them into two categories. The first group consisted of black clad knights, and the second group appeared to be seeds, dressed in white robes with grey knights as their escort.

Scarlette appeared next to Maureen and the others, his eyes narrowed as he watched the new arrivals approach.

"Blue Ring," he said, his voice mild. "And Hader Castle. Those are allies of ours."

Scarlette turned to Jack. "Bring your group along slowly, and we will meet them." He waved his arms and a small black whirlwind rose from beneath his feet, enveloping him. Then he was gone.

Energy Spike, Tally's cold voice reported. Known source. Maureen looked around, trying to spot any threat. Energy Spike, repeated Tally. Known source. He's gone, but I have no data on how.

Jack led Maureen and the others down to the combined camp that had formed beside the little river. The Blue Ring groups were dressed similarly to Maureen's own group, while the seeds from Hader Castle all wore white robes, with grey knights as escorts. Tents began going up again as the newcomers settled in, and Maureen could hear many voices beginning to rise in greeting. There were so many accents now, it was hard to make out what anyone was saying. Maureen could feel her shoulders tighten from all the noise and the crowd.

Jack walked over to her. His expression was, as always, blank. "The master wishes for everyone to go with him now for aptitude-grade testing."

Maureen nodded. She remembered that Scarlette had used a crystal ball to identify who was a wizard seed, but it hadn't shown their exact grade. But with this many apprentices gathered in one place, they could measure their exact grade.

The cheer and laughter slowly faded from the camp as everyone began to realize the implications of being tested. Every youth here knew what their grade would mean for them.

* * *

One by one the seeds began forming a line and heading into the tent. No one spoke. Maureen shuffled forward as part of the line, the sounds of canvas, the smell of lamp oil, and the scuffing of boots on trampled grass filling her senses.

Tally's cold voice echoed in her head. Multiple energy sources detected within that structure.

How many? Maureen thought silently back.

Too close to differentiate. Several at least.

Then a warm voice sounded in her mind. Breathe, three long, one short. We will count you in.

Three long, one short. Breathe in, breathe out. Breathe in for the count of three, hold it, and breathe out for the count of one. That was how the Silver Light counted. Her heartbeat began to thud in her ears, but her hands remained still by her side. She watched the line ahead of her thin out, one after another disappearing through the flap in the tent.

"Maureen Olsen!"

The old voice came from within the tent.

Whatever they are measuring, we will remember it.

Maureen breathed out deeply, reached up and lifted the tent flap, and walked inside.
