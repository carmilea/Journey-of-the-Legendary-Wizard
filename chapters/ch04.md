"Maureen!" Richard caught up with her at the door. "Wait, Maureen."

"Young Master—" Her tongue tripped over the name.

He waved away the title, pleased at her attention. "Richard. Just Richard. Scarlette suggested it and Father allowed it."

"Richard," Maureen said.

Richard grinned. "My father would like to see you and Carey," he whispered. He grabbed her wrist and pulled her inside.

Her nails were caked with grave clay. The hem of her dress was muddied from the slum. The manor smelled of beeswax and lye soap. Candles flickered in the draughts, gutters hissing and spitting. Servants pressed themselves against the walls as Richard dragged her through the back corridors toward an inner stairwell. The Duke stood there, with Carey, surrounded by black-armoured knights. The guards were motionless. It was eerie.

*Twelve,* Tally said. *Not a single one has shifted his weight since we turned the corner.*

*Have you scanned them?* Maureen asked silently.

*No need. I can look. My resolution is quite good enough.*

Maureen's back stiffened. She kept her hands at her side, palms flat against her legs, not moving a muscle.

The Duke's eyes rested on her. "Your affairs are settled?" he asked courteously.

"Yes, Your Grace." She bowed. "Thank you for your help."

Carey's face was flushed bright red all the way to the tips of his ears. "The Duke is opening up the family treasury! One gift each, for becoming Mystics!" He finished awkwardly, "My lady."

There was a pause, then Carey repeated, "My lady." It was a reflexive habit of servile address that he couldn't break yet. He wasn't sure how to talk to someone who was his equal.

The Duke pulled a dagger from his belt, drew it quickly across his palm, and pressed his hand to one of the stones by the stairs. There was a sound like a stone mill grinding in the distance, and the wall beside them rumbled. A passage opened, dark inside.

*Cut, the third stone up, the rumble: All on record. Why blood opens stone: no data. I would like some.*

*Wait your turn,* Maureen told it silently.

Ignoring the blood that dripped down his arm, the Duke picked up a candlestick and led the way through twisting tunnels to a grey-white stone door.

The air was cold and dry. The candle's flame bent at every turn. Dark drops of the Duke's blood dotted the floor.

Inside were hills of gold coin, its gleam dulled by time. Tray after tray of velvet-held gems. Strings of pearls glowed with an inner light. Swords decorated with jewels. Armour rusted with age. Glow Stones set into the walls illuminated the room with a flat light.

*Glow Stones are a light source for which I have no data,* Tally said. *I want them. Badly. What is all this metal in a pile? It does nothing.*

*Impressed by anything?* Maureen asked silently.

*Books. Find me books.*

Maureen took a single breath, then let it go. Carey's freckled face had gone slack. He let out a wordless whoop and dashed into the room. The Duke stepped to one side, holding the candlestick aloft. Maureen bowed her head and followed at an even pace.

* * *

Beyond the gems she found shelves of books.

One was titled Knight Breathing Technique. She flipped it open. Methods and cautions were clearly laid out. This wasn't a fake. A quick glance confirmed there were many more breathing techniques on the shelves, along with sword manuals. She already knew what she wanted. Tally's scans of Xifan and Ivan had settled that decision.

The books were bound in brittle vellum, faded brown ink on the pages. The boards covering the front and back were soft with age. Maureen's fingers came away from the spines with a fine grey dust.

Tally scanned every book as they passed. 'No. 1 recorded,' it said warmly in her head. 'No. 2 recorded. Shall I start you a breathing-technique database?'

Her lips remaining sealed, Maureen thought back her silent yes.

'Would you like me to generate a new technique based on No. 1 and No. 2?' Tally asked in its own warm voice.

Maureen's mouth went dry. Her fingers paused on another spine.

She grabbed a third volume, and silently told Tally to record it.

*No. 3 recorded,* Tally replied. *Filing. Would you like me to combine all three? I can combine any number you like.*

House Skran was one of the Four Great Duchies of the Odin Empire. Nearly a thousand years' worth of techniques stood on these shelves, waiting for her.

The swordsmanship manuals worked the same way.

*There you are,* Tally said smugly. *I wondered when you would get there.*

*Don't gloat,* Maureen told it silently.

*I'm not gloating. Just noting how quickly you came to the obvious course of action.*

Maureen's pulse was up again, beating quick and strong, just like the old days in the lab. She forced herself to maintain a look of boredom, the expression of a slum girl overwhelmed by so many books.

One by one she pulled volumes off the shelves and riffled through them. Tally caught a spread with every glance of Maureen's eyes. It took a very long time. The sound of Carey's clinking faded away as he left the treasury vault and its room full of gold.

Tally kept a running tally under its breath as Maureen worked, enjoying the chance to earn its own keep. The whisper of pages turning filled the book-lined room. Behind Maureen's eyes, each page spread flickered with a pale blue glow as Tally recorded it. An ache began to build in her wrists.

At last Tally spoke again, this time with unbridled delight. [Knight Breathing Technique: 374 recorded.] [Swordsmanship: 479 recorded.] *Shall I generate new techniques from all this data?*

Maureen closed her eyes. 'Yes,' she said silently, in her head, her lips remaining closed. 'Both must be suited to my body. Give me the most effective technique that has no side effects or adverse reactions.'

*I only have the one body scan of you, from the day I woke up,* Tally replied. *Plus your recorded responses. Make sure you eat well and sleep well over the next few days, because I mean it.*

*New breathing technique created. Estimated Time of Arrival: 4 hours, 37 minutes.*

*New swordsmanship created. Estimated Time of Arrival: 3 hours, 58 minutes.*

The corner of Maureen's mouth twitched upwards. A few hours. She could wait that long. *Shall I suggest you take out a single book for cover?* Tally asked. *Something modest and unassuming would work best. Yes, that one looks good. Common, worn, unexceptional. Excellent choice.*

Maureen reached for the single volume Tally suggested, then walked out of the treasury vault with it in hand.

* * *

The Duke sat at the stone table outside the door to the treasury, his right hand wrapped in strips of linen that were spotted brown with drying blood.

“You took your time,” he said with an easy smile. “Carey chose a long time ago, then left.”

“I’m sorry, Your Grace.” She glanced down at the book she held.

He looked at the tome, nodding approval. “A breathing technique? A sensible choice.” Then he lapsed into silence, watching her, leaving the candle’s soft tick as the only noise in the passageway. “How did you find the manor?”

“Very well, Your Grace.”

“You came from the slums, Maureen Olsen.”

“I know, Your Grace.”

“Without your position here, you and your grandfather would have starved in a gutter within weeks. What I’ve given you today is worth more than those small slights you remember.”

Maureen kept her voice even. “Your Grace is a most merciful man.”

He tapped his fingers on the stone table. He thought highly of her. “Out there, Maureen, I’d like you to think of Richard in everything. Do you understand my meaning?”

Tally noted the pause before the question, and the lowering of the Duke’s voice. The smile didn’t move when the pitch changed, didn’t reflect the shift.

Cold ran down Maureen’s back and her spine straightened automatically as she absorbed the data.

The chip gave its analysis. The purse and the book were for Richard. Kindness and threat delivered together, to ensure her and Carey’s loyalty to the Duke’s house.

She met His Grace’s eyes evenly. “Your Grace, Richard and I are friends. We are friends.”

The Duke said nothing for a long moment, letting the flame lean one way, then right itself again.

Then he laughed, patting her shoulder. “Go rest, child. Master Scarlette is leaving with them tomorrow.”

Maureen nodded, then turned and walked away from the table. Tally spoke as she reached the stairwell.

*That was the right answer.*

*I know,* Maureen responded silently.

Dark steps rose as she climbed, the book pressed against her side, her ribs, feeling the faint thump of her heart through it. Her steps were light as she went up.

*Swordsmanship ETA: 3 hours, 57 minutes.*

*Oh shut up,* she thought. *How long is left? How long is left?*

Tally chuckled silently.

* * *

“What do you think?” The Duke asked the dark.

Behind him, the air shimmered and rippled like heat rising from sand, and a tall figure formed. Armoured in black, heavy armour, the figure stood behind him.

A visor covered its face, hiding its features. Only the eyes showed through the slit in the helm, two points of blood red light.

“The words of Carey are false,” the black knight said. “Those of Maureen Olsen are likely true.”

The Duke considered this for a moment, remembering how quickly Carey had knelt before him, swearing to consider Richard his young master still, as he once was. Any child would have seen through that act.

Maureen Olsen had kept her distance, but he believed that if Richard was in trouble she would step in. They had always been close.

“The seed Carey took one gemstone, large enough to fill his fist,” the black knight said. “He secreted several more within his clothing. The woman Maureen Olsen did indeed take only the book.”

“Deal with him?”

The Duke sighed. “Forget it. It’s only a few gems. Why provoke a Mystic over such a thing? Wizards are known to be a cunning and cruel breed.” There was fear in his eyes and the knight did not move.

The Duke’s hand lay on the stone table, the bandaged fingers unmoving. The wax had run down the candle, pooling at the base of the candlestick.

“This is all I can do for you, my son,” he murmured. “I hope you will achieve something with it.” His voice died away, and the candle flame flickered out. Darkness. Silence.
