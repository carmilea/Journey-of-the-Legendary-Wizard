The noise from the arena began to fade to a background murmur as the passageway took a curve. Maureen’s boot heels rang out on the pale stone floor. A smear of oily black-red smoke clung to her sleeve. The silk had been scorched and there was a smell like burnt honey.

Maureen continued down the passage without hurrying. Manhattan was a closed matter. He lived because she had chosen him to live. His body in front of thirty houses’ wizards would have made a story but a crippled man made only a warning.

Tally ran the replay of Manhattan’s halo through Maureen’s display. *100% down to 8%. Two to three years before he walks straight. Medium confidence. Based on the burn-on effects logged and the ruined leg you saw.*

*At low confidence, he will never climb a rank again.*

*Enough is logged,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*Logged. Merciful. I dispute that.*

*Thirty Official Wizards saw Richard’s eyes flare crimson, the pupils drawn to slit. Mimicry cannot paint over that.*

Maureen pressed her tongue against the back of her teeth in silence. A polished pillar reflected back the image of a young man with a plain face. Mousy hair hung lankly across his brow, his eyes were black and unmoving. The face was still over Maureen’s bones. Beneath it her pulse beat slow and even, her wrist barely moving with its passing.

Two apprentices passed by, heading in the other direction. Their heads were together as they chattered, catching Maureen’s attention with the words “Richard’s black fire” before they noticed her approach. They fell quiet. The rumour had reached this far ahead of her.

*That needs a public name,* Tally said. *How about: Umbral Eclipse Flame, Coronal variant?*

*No,* Maureen answered silently.

Tally was silent for a moment, then continued in a sulky tone. *You have to call it something. I mean, if you’re going to go around burning people with it. You’ve rejected Basic, Mana and Coronal Necrotic Vortex so far and now this. That is four names you have refused me.*

Maureen considered the best cover story as she walked. If anyone pressed Richard too hard he would tell them that the flame was part of his meditation method but he would not divulge the method’s name. It was a breach of manners, known to all wizards, to ask after another wizard’s meditation method. The true name would remain in Tally’s records and off Maureen’s tongue.

A pharmacist in purple robes hurried past, brushing against Maureen. His sleeves were stained green at the cuffs from mixing potions. The air in this passage smelled of crushed plant stems mixed with lye. Maureen took a deep breath, filling her lungs with the chemical bite in the air like she was entering an old room.

The passage opened up into a tall archway flanked by three apprentices standing perfectly straight. The ward-light shone on their faces making them look even more handsome than they did already.

As one they bowed. “Honoured wizard, have you come for the pharmacology examination? To join the Pharmacy Department?”

“I have,” Maureen replied, her voice low and flat as Richard’s.

*All three of the apprentices are tall and good looking,* Tally observed. *The Alliance certainly picks its doormen for appearance.*

*So does the Duke,* Maureen replied silently.

*But the Duke’s doormen aren’t quite as tall,* Tally pointed out.

One of the apprentices peeled away from the other two, and gestured for Maureen to follow him through the arch. He kept half a step ahead of her, and never turned around to look at her.

The scent hit Maureen before she even saw the Pharmacy Department, taking her back to Adrian’s lab. The sight of wriggling Holan grubs in their slime filled tubs, the black flame burning heatlessly beneath a test tube. Adrian’s wrinkled face, the old man pushing his glasses back up his nose without sparing a glance for her.

Maureen knew that her own knowledge of pharmacology wasn’t too far behind Adrian’s. The old man was one of the greatest apothecaries on the entire Passise Subcontinent. Of course, there were only a handful of great apothecaries on the whole Passise Subcontinent, but still, she wasn’t too far behind Adrian.

*Do you have data that says I’m better or worse than Adrian?* Maureen asked.

*No,* Tally replied. *I do not have any data that compares your abilities to Adrian’s. I’ve never seen him tested, just watched him identify materials by their scent across a bench.*

*Then call it my opinion,* Maureen said silently.

*Filed as opinion,* Tally replied, sounding quite pleased with herself.

But that wasn’t the real reason why Maureen wanted to be a pharmacist. That lay underneath. Tally could only work with what Maureen saw, and unfortunately what Maureen had seen was limited. The Alliance had shelves upon shelves of ingredients, as well as higher disciplines. Those were the things she had come for, and a pharmacist’s name would open doors to them.

*New shelves?* Tally asked, her voice brightening. *Excellent! I’m hungry.*

*You’re always hungry,* Maureen responded.

*That’s the point of shelves.*

* * *

The examination hall swallowed Maureen’s footsteps. Gave them back much later, one at a time, after she had taken half a dozen more. Rows of benches spread across the floor, extending all the way to the far wall. Each had its own brass burner, cold and lifeless, racks of glass catching the thin light that fell through the narrow windows.

The apprentice bowed, then left. In the centre of the hall stood five other candidates in a loose knot, with two pharmacists wearing purple robes. Maureen took her place on the outside of the group.

Tally obligingly compared the other five against Maureen’s Barok sample. All were thin, all were newly made Official Wizards, none had been elementalised. One of them shifted his weight every few breaths, a nervous habit Maureen had noticed many men displayed before a test.

Heads turned, looking at Maureen. They must have heard about the platform. One woman, dressed in grey robes, took one quiet step to the side, putting more stone between her and the new arrival.

The younger pharmacist smiled at all of them. “Well, it looks like everyone is here. My name is Karl, pharmacist for the Alliance Pharmacy Department. The task is simple, you will brew one potion to Official Wizard standards. Brew it successfully and you’re one of us.” The pharmacist paused, his smile never wavering. “I do hope you all succeed.” He said the last sentence with the same tone that a man would use when wishing for sunny days during winter.

Tally noted that Karl’s smile remained fixed while his eyes quickly counted the number of benches. There was no other telltale sign from him. She filed the man’s face away. Maureen thought back. The second pharmacist hadn’t blinked since they’d walked in.

Karl raised his hand, and swept it through the air. Particles of cyan energy flared into life, laying out the formula in the air in front of them. Wind-Soul Hunting Potion was written at the top.

The projection hung in the air like frost on glass. A few lines of script hovered next to the title, followed by a drawing of an elegant spiral vessel drawn in light. Then came a large chart of reactions.

The faces of the candidates took on a faint cyan cast as they reflected the light. The smell of coming rain began to fill the hall.

Maureen narrowed her eyes. This was the brew that gave the regular Official Wizards trouble. The Pharmacy Department wasn’t modest about setting their bar high. Tally began recording the projection, spread by spread as Maureen’s eyes travelled down the formula. A pale blue flicker could be seen behind her eyes, marking each section of the formula, until Tally announced that the projection had been fully recorded after eleven sections.

[Wind-Soul Hunting Potion. Formula: recorded. Plan: 3 branches. Estimated success on plan ~78%, by feel lower. Basis: Adrian’s Analytical Demonstrations, Gregory’s pharmacology, your success log.]

*Where is the missing twenty-two percent?* Maureen asked silently.

*In the wind reaction,* Tally admitted. *I have little recorded beyond Gregory’s notes on reagents from the wind school. I felt it prudent to inform you now rather than at the burner.*

*Fair enough.*

Maureen’s bench was the last one in the row. A large piece of stone formed the top, scarred with use and bearing the ring stains of a hundred brews long past. The bitter ghost of a burn marked the grain of the stone.

The rows of benches were bare except for the equipment needed for the brew, arranged by Karl’s apprentices before the examination started. The burners hissed into life along each row as the other candidates began their work, and Maureen began laying out her materials before lighting her own flame.

* * *

The glass was cool under her fingers, just like any other glassware. It clinked as she moved it into place. For one long breath Maureen saw the white bench from her old life as Jing Sheng. Seen under the laboratory lights, the bench was lined with equipment and the spirit lamp burned with its blue flame. Then Adrian’s bench was there, the stone black with age but clean. The blue flame of the brass burner was reflected in the glass of the spiral vessel, and in her mind’s eye she saw the small glass holding her first potion again, sitting there in the centre of the bench. The green liquid stood out against the dark stone and Maureen felt her shoulders relax.

Maureen slid into the Brewing State. Her hands moved on their own, while her mind took a step back and observed. Inside Maureen, Tally laid out a line of pale-blue timing bars, one next to each piece of equipment laid out on the edge of the bench. A ghost outline appeared over each vessel, showing the amount of liquid it held. Branch one showed as a steady line on each bar. *Your pace, Maureen,* said Tally inside her head. *I’ll keep the clock for you.*

First step. Maureen picked up the grey base stone and placed it in the mortar. With quick, even strokes, the pestle ground the stone until it became a fine powder.

The powder smelled of flint struck by steel and cold air.

The pestle squeaked once against the stone of the mortar before Maureen eased up the pressure on her wrist. Next she tipped the powder into the clear carrier, holding it over a low flame. As the powder dissolved, the liquid changed from grey to the pale green of new leaves.

Tally marked the colour shift on the timing bar exactly. *Step 1: on plan. Nice average speed there, Maureen. No one will be impressed by that timing, but at least you’re not falling behind yet.* A dull thud came from two benches up the row, followed by a puff of white smoke. The stench of burnt hair and rotting fruit filled the air.

*Failure by smell,* said Tally, without being asked. Maureen’s own particle sense told her that another bench’s brew was going flat as well. One of the other benches, somewhere down the row.

[Benches failed: 1 of 5] [Second bench failing]

*Don’t look, Maureen.* She didn’t.

Step 2. The pale, feather thin leaf went between her fingers. The silver knife came out of its slot in her belt kit. She slashed it expertly along the veins of the leaf, and drew the sap into the vessel, drop by drop.

The sap was cold enough to sting, and drew itself into beads on the knife blade like morning dew. The scent was sharp and clear, like the smell of rain on stone. Maureen raised the temperature on the flame. Now that the first reaction was completed, she would need to maintain its structure while she built the next part of the compound. With only two hands, she could not hold both the vessel containing the reaction, and the one into which she was building the next.

*I can hold the lattice of the first reaction stable through the Orb while you build the next stage,* said Tally. *It will cost about 0.1 mana per minute to do so.*

*Hold it,* Maureen told her silently.

[Lattice: holding. Mana: est. 13.6, draining 0.1 / min.]

Tally’s voice dropped into the even tone she used when counting off. *Lattice held. Begin building.* Maureen fed a thin thread of her own mana into the spiral vessel, coaxing the particles of wind from the air to gather there at the heart of the brew. The vessel took a breath. There was a high keening sound in the glass, almost too fine to hear. The liquid spun faster, forming a narrow funnel that stood upright against the sides of the spiral.

[Benches failed: 3 of 5, by smell and particle sense.] Tally reported the additional failures with no more emphasis than if she were marking them down in a margin.

Karl had drifted down the row, and now stood two benches away, his hands folded into the sleeves of his purple robe. He watched without speaking.

[Pulse: 66. Eyes: black.]

*Karl is watching your hands,* Tally told her. *Not your face.*

*Let him,* Maureen replied.

The final addition. Tally’s bar hit its mark but Maureen’s hand did not tilt the last vial. The funnel’s heart was still a shade too blue. She trusted her eyes over the clock.

>Now. >Now.

*Not yet,* Maureen told Tally silently.

>NOW. The bar flashed.

The blue faded. Maureen tipped the vial. The fine keening cut off, as though someone had held the note just that half beat too long, then snapped their fingers to break the tone. The funnel fell, sinking back into the liquid, which ran clear and still in the vessel.

[Lattice: released. Mana: est. 13.0.]

*That was your call,* Tally said, warmth returning to her voice. *I’m writing it in your column.*

Maureen poured the potion from the spiral vessel into a clean vial, and held it up to the grey window. It ran crystal clear from one end to the other. There was no sediment, no cloudiness. Within the depths of the liquid a single thread was visible, moving with a life of its own. Like the wind through long grass on the plains.

Down the row, the last two candidates’ vessels went dark one after the other. The smoke of five failures hung under the high ceiling. [Benches failed: 5 of 5.]

* * *

The pharmacist accepted the vial from Maureen, turning it against the light and running two fingers along the glass. His particle sense brushed against the vial for a moment before he raised his eyes from the potion to study Maureen.

“Congratulations, Richard. Truly,” the man said, smiling. He must have been about thirty years old. His purple robe rustled as he stood and bowed formally. “I had assumed that this sitting would run the same as any other. No success. And yet here you are.”

[No tells observed. Not proof.]

 Karl’s eyes were fixed on her face, his gaze moving between her eyes, rather than looking at the contents of the vial.

*Maureen, are your eyes still black? I know the Pankora crimson rises when your heart beats.*

Maureen kept her breathing steady as she replied to Tally silently, her inner voice a whisper. *Yes, black.*

*Keep them that way.*

“Only one of you, but still a pleasant surprise.” Karl straightened and bowed again, the second pharmacist joining him without saying a word. “Welcome to the Pharmacy Department.”

Maureen returned the bow with precisely the right depth and duration, her voice the flat tones of Wizard Richard. “Thank you.”

Her face was nothing. Behind her, the other candidates stood defeated by their smoking benches. The woman in grey stared at the clear contents of the vial in Karl’s hand as if it had personally insulted her.

*Maureen, your pulse has risen four beats.*

*Fine, keep track of it.*

Maureen let Tally keep the number. It was not important.

“I’ll walk our new member out myself,” Karl said as the second pharmacist moved to a small table set against the side wall, pulling out a quill and inkwell to begin writing. “This way please.”

The gallery ran long in either direction beneath high windows. Glass cases lined the walls, filled with pickled specimens and pressed blossoms. Purple robes moved between the many doors, while the mingled smells of lye, dried roots and ancient paper hung in the air.

The pharmacist’s robe carried an odour of camphor. He walked too close beside Maureen as they moved through the hall. Like a man used to being liked.

“That was quite a show on the platform,” Karl said conversationally. “They’re calling it Richard’s black fire already. Quite a flame.”

“It comes with my meditation method,” Maureen said, leaving it there.

Karl raised both hands, palms outward, laughing. “Of course. Forgive me. Asking after a man’s method. My mother would have boxed my ears for less.”

*The wall held,* Tally observed. *Voice stayed low and flat. But a wizard who knew of Purgatory might still connect the black fire with the crimson eyes the arena had seen on Richard.*

Karl filled the walk with small talk. Manhattan’s followers in Fang Blood Shadow would shout for a while, he said, but the High Ring White Tower would take a more detailed interest in this new Official Wizard.

“Wenger heads it, you know. One of the Alliance’s few Second-Tier Wizards. His young man Gus was at the platform today.”

[Wenger: Second-Tier (hearsay). Head, High Ring White Tower. Density sample: none. Gus: Wenger’s man.] Tally noted on her display, keeping the match between Gus and Bat where it was, with a low confidence rating.

When they reached the end of the gallery Karl told Maureen where to report tomorrow for her robe and her group. He bowed once more before turning back the way he had come, the ready smile still on his face.

Maureen remained a moment, standing beneath the archway, listening to the sounds coming from behind the closed doors that lined the long gallery. The turning of pages, the ringing of glass against glass.

*New shelves behind those purple robes,* Tally observed. *I want every one of them.*

*Later,* Maureen told her silently.

*You always say that,* Tally said, sounding pleased anyway.
