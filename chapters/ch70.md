The bronze book's proclamation echoed in her skull long after its voice fell silent.

Maureen Olsen stood at the black desk, feeling the gritty dust between her fingertips. Her pulse beat like a moth caught in her throat.

She worked it through.

The Ancient Wizards had found world upon world, plane upon plane. All manner of strange powers and mighty bloodlines were theirs. They pooled their wisdom together and crafted the Minai Totem, so that any blood might be devoured, and any power drunk.

A bloodline sorcerer was chained to the one blood within his veins. A wizard who relied on exotic energies became lame on the day they ran dry. The Ancient Wizards took from all things but were bound by none of them. That was what made them conquerors.

*I have that doctrine filed word for word,* Tally said. *You're breathing like a girl who just walked into the Skran treasury again.*

*This is bigger,* Maureen thought back silently.

*I know. That's what worries me.*

A pale shape rose out from the open pages of the book.

It was an old man, stooped with age but with a kindly face. He was as thin as smoke. Maureen fixed her eyes on his face, but each time she tried to focus, the old man's nose and brow and mouth slid away from her, refusing to hold still.

"I am Gregory," the figure said. His voice was dry, unhurried. It sounded like a page being turned. "I died a long time ago, latecomer. This is a recording I left behind. It is not even a spirit imprint. There is no need to be nervous."

Maureen's thumb had the cork out of the green vial before he'd finished speaking. She was nervous, so she did something about it. In one swift motion, she flung the remaining handful of anti-imprint potion in a hard arc straight through the old man's chest.

The green liquid flew through nothing, splattering against the shelves behind him. It sizzled faintly where it soaked into the gritty dust on the floor, while the old man continued speaking at exactly the same mild pace. Maureen's heart slammed against her ribs like a fist pounding on a door.

*Recording spell,* Tally said. *It has no particle reaction of its own. Just a playback loop. It's a portrait, Maureen.*

*I've been killed by portraits before,* Maureen thought back.

*No, you haven't,* said Tally.

*I was bitten by a book.*

*That one I'll grant you,* said Tally.

Now her particle sense could find the frame that held this recording spell. It was very simple, just a repeating loop of set lines, with nothing inside reaching toward her. She allowed her shoulders to drop a finger's width, though she did not let go of her empty vial.

"Hundreds of years? Thousands? Tens of thousands?" The figure sighed, and there was a great weariness in the sound, as if it had been spoken many times in an empty room. "Perhaps a whole wizard era has come and gone. I cannot tell from in here."

"You heard the voice in the enlightenment book, didn't you? The Ancient Wizards left that. It warms the blood, eh, latecomer? It did mine. It is the truest road a wizard ever walked."

"So, congratulations. You suit my meditation method, and you suit the Child of Purgatory, the Pankora. If you didn't, the testing ball would have stayed dark, and you would be hearing none of this."

Maureen's eyes flicked to the crystal ball sitting on the desk, still glowing with its faint, patient light. For a single breath, the bone-deep cold of the Sunset Canyon crystal returned to her hands, along with the feeling of being measured by something impossibly old.

*That settles it then,* said Tally. *This is the apprentice's teacher. I moved all the 'Teacher' lines into a new Gregory file.*

*No data on how he knew someone would eventually show up?*

*None. And I don't like that either.*

"Go further than I did, latecomer." The voice finished circling the room, thinning out as it spoke, until there was nothing left. Maureen opened her mouth to respond, but before she could say a word, the bronze book lifted itself from the desktop and hovered above her head.

Light poured down from the book in a sudden torrent. It closed over her like cold water pouring over a stone, filling her ears and nose and eyes, blotting out everything else.

Sound came with it—roaring, war cries, low bestial howls, screams, the chant of some terrible sacrifice, keening grief. Phantom worlds spun past her, monsters with the pulse of blood visible beneath their hides, undead with their jaws stretched wide in perpetual roars.

The lines of silver that she'd never been able to touch began to shiver. Every single one of them shook at once, and then snapped into existence as blinding beams that drove themselves into her forehead. One after another, they hammered themselves in like nails.

Maureen's mouth dropped open without her permission. She staggered back, catching hold of the black desk to keep herself upright. Her nails bit into the wood grain, the gritty dust packing underneath as she sucked in air in ragged, painful gulps. The air tasted of stone.

*Your knees are making a number of decisions on their own. Perhaps you should sit.*

Tally sounded concerned, even warmly so.

Maureen half-slid onto the edge of the chair.

*Silver Light. Three long, one short.* Tally counted the breaths back to her. *In, two-three-four, out, two-three-four. In, two-three-four, out, two-three-four.* Maureen's lungs began answering on their own again, and Tally continued, *Large structured inflow detected. Same as crystal ball. Can catch before impact. Consent?*

*Yes.* The word formed silently in her mind.

Pressure built up behind her eyes, and a flicker of pale blue passed before her vision. The greyness at the edges of her sight began to recede slowly, like fog burned off by the sun. The room began to come into focus again, one shelf at a time. The gritty grey dust, the faintly glowing crystal ball lying next to the dead apprentice's diary, the silver line-patterns that hung suspended in the air like frozen rain, still impossible to touch.

*Category name?* Tally prompted.

Maureen snapped her fingers and heard the sound echo through the empty room. Her voice sounded rough as she spoke. "Ancient Wizards."

*Coronal Ancient something?* Tally asked hopefully.

*No.*

*Under protest,* Tally noted.

*Filed.*

*Folding 'Ancient Era lines and totems' into new category. Priority 1 remains.* [Minai Totem: full analysis. ETA 94d 22h.]

*Books that eat. Entry four. This one asked first. Manners improving.*

Her head filled with a dull, solid pressure, stretching the skin over her bones taut, like a water-filled skin. Then, suddenly, the information was sorted out and tucked away into her memory. It was there for her to use whenever she wanted, as if she'd known it all along.

Maureen pressed two fingers between her eyebrows, pushing hard against her skin.

The room was silent again. The silver lines were gone from the air, the figure was gone, and the green droplets were drying to a crust on the shelves. Maureen could hear her own breathing, harsh in the stillness.

* * *

She sat in the dead apprentice's chair, feeling the aged wood creak under her like an old rope, and read her newfound knowledge the same way that Jing Sheng had read over a lab protocol before touching a reagent. Line by line, waiting for the step that would hurt.

The core was a short one. During her time as an apprentice, she needed to build her Rune Secret Lines. Upon promotion to a full wizard, she needed to ignite her Minai Totem with the essence blood of a powerful bloodline creature.

That was the whole road, from start to finish.

Blood, not some exotic energy. That much at least made sense. The Totem had to drink the hidden power of the creature used in its creation. It was also important that Maureen was still a human body at the time of the Totem's creation, since the same blood would temper her flesh as well.

*Step one: lines. Step two: blood. Step three: fire,* Tally said in its notebook voice.

*Shut up,* Maureen told it silently. *Don't make it sound like a recipe.*

*Every recipe I have ever provided you with has been a potentially dangerous one. The comparison is valid. This is no different.*

The creature chosen to light the Minai Totem fixed the power of the Totem and the strength of the wizard who wielded it, from the very first day. That was why the Ancient Wizards had struggled so much over their choice, and why some had towered far above others.

The Totem itself had levels. Take two Second-Tier wizards, one with a Totem on level two and one with a Totem on level one, and the difference between them was obvious. It wasn't an absolute system, since the creature used to ignite the Totem and what was devoured afterwards both made a big difference. However, there were limits to how much power could be forced up through any other means. The limits existed, and they shrank over time.

There was only one way to raise the Totem's level: keep devouring powerful bloodline creatures and exotic energies. The book didn't say anything else, as if nothing else needed to be said.

Devour. The word sat on Maureen's tongue like the iron of the Fasol serpent's blood. She thought of the Sodara tree's great, gnarled roots, knotted around the bones of the dead and still sticky with the last feeding.

Function one was absorption. She would be able to fuse bloodlines and exotic energies, able to cast the spells of the bloodline creature she devoured and even stranger magics. She would be able to grow her own powerful bloodline. The descendants of such wizards had gone on to found many strange sorcerer families.

*Strange sorcerer families was the book's phrasing,* Tally noted. *Not mine.*

*I know. Any family of mine is going to be strange, whatever you or this book says.*

*I have no data to dispute that claim.*

Function two was amplification. A spell that an ordinary wizard cast at 10 degrees came out of an Ancient Wizard at 14 or 15, nearly half again as strong. The body was strengthened as well. The bloodline was in the flesh.

*Third-Class Maureen Blast Fireball, est. ~5.6°,* Tally translated into her ledger. *Third-Class Maureen under a Totem, est. 8° or more.*

*8? That's Dark Wind Ring's number.*

*Yes. And we're talking about a mere fireball here.* Tally was practically giggling to itself.

The third function was weakening. Within a certain range, a 10 degree spell that struck an Ancient Wizard only hit with 7 or 8 degrees of force. It was a shield that never had to be raised.

*Maureen, if a 10 were thrown at you... at us... it would hit at 7 to 8 degrees. Source: One book. Very sure of itself.*

*I'm not sure I trust this book,* Maureen thought back.

*I trust it more than Adrian's list. Not saying much there.*

The dry line from the library echoed back to her. Every school has conquered plane after plane, mastering one after the other. For the first time, the line had a shape. It was the phantom worlds from her flood, falling one by one to people who took and kept.

In addition to the Totem, there were combined spells and ancient spell arrays. Dark Wind Ring and George's ciphered book had just been scraps of something these people had used every day.

Then came the price. Foundational subjects piled up like mountains, breaking minds in their passage. Rune Secret Line calculations and combinations that drove apprentices to despair. A search for a creature powerful enough to be worth the fire. Few succeeded, but those who did were the strongest of the strong.

Maureen looked down at her hands. They were lying flat on the desk, unmoving. That was how they always were before she poured something that could blow off a finger. Behind her, the glow from the crystal ball had sunk to a single ember.

Dust danced down from where she had disturbed it, drifting slowly through the beams of lamplight. It settled on the cool bronze cover. Somewhere within the ancient manor, a beam ticked as it cooled in the night air. The quiet felt like a room waiting for an answer.

* * *

She was a Third-Class Wizard Apprentice. After that came promotion to Official Wizard, and there was no more putting off the question of meditation. Now she had a third answer, sitting silently beneath her palm.

The real high-grade methods sat in the strongboxes of the wizard families. Whether or not she could get one was anyone's guess, and the price would be whatever the family named.

She could see Lilith again, standing hooded outside her door. The girl’s blue eyes were wet as she read out the Walkers’ terms: a hundred and fifty years of service, eighty parts in a hundred of every potion she brewed. Maureen’s jaw clenched at the memory.

Even if she could pay those prices, no one could guarantee that a family method would work with her body or her arrays. The junk methods sold by the merchants in the Dark Forest weren’t even worth considering. Their First-Tier cap and the Finlan Potion to make the cage bearable meant they were garbage.

Tally’s display ran through her options, laying out three columns of information. Family: terms harsh, fit unknown. Junk: ceiling First-Tier. Ancient: fit stated by Gregory, crystal ball lit. She noticed in silence that Tally had added an extra line to the final column. *That’s where the data is,* it noted.

Evans stood in her mind, pricing lives on the deck of the ship. Adrian burned Barok’s projection until it was a pile of ash. Under her ribs, something tightened, but it wasn’t fear. She wanted to be the one Evans couldn’t price. The one no one could price.

Only one thing stood in the way. It was only one thing, but it was enough to stop most people from even trying. Maureen named it honestly to herself, as she always did. The calculations, the part of the road that even the book admitted drove people to despair.

*The calculations are the hard part,* she told Tally silently.

*The calculations are my part.*

Then she would choose a path because Tally could do sums.

*Because I can do sums,* Tally agreed.

*And you can do everything else.*

*I’ll take half the credit,* Tally promised. *Once.*

Maureen let out a breath. “Since that’s how it is,” she said aloud, voice quiet in the empty room. “I will walk the Ancient Wizard path.”

The words were the smallest sound she’d made all night, and they weighed the most. They barely carried across the room to the shelves. No one heard them, and no one would. The dust didn’t even dance in the air from her speech.

You suited my method, Gregory had told her. You suited my essence blood. For his apprentice he had opened a Dimensional Gate into the Purgatory World, and dragged back a Child of Purgatory. The Pankora Scarlet Serpent, a level-five creature, refined into blood.

*A man who opened a door into another world to fetch a snake for his student,* Tally noted.

*He must have cared about the boy,* she responded silently.

*Yes,* Tally said. *I’ve filed that away in its own column. Adrian has his own column as well.*

“Child of Purgatory.” Maureen murmured the words, feeling them on her tongue. They tasted like heat, like the opening of a furnace door, and her fingertips tingled against the cool bronze, as if the cover had suddenly warmed a degree.

She could picture the scarlet coils, though she had never seen them. Sealed somewhere in a dead man’s house, waiting for her. Her mouth was dry, and she realised that she wanted it, just as she had once wanted the white bench beneath her hands.

* * *

The sorted data had already told her everything else. To figure out how to build a Totem, Gregory had conducted experiments in many places, watching bloodline creatures grow under different conditions. His residence contained his method of meditation, along with the essence of the Pankora’s bloodline.

This time, she flipped open the bronze cover with her fingers. The metal felt cool to the touch, just slightly gritty. The pages were stiff beneath her fingers, like old vellum, and they smelled faintly of verdigris and old dust. She turned the pages one by one, letting Tally examine each spread.

There was a map inked upon the pages. Maureen’s finger slid across it, tracing the borders, and then she stopped. “Hm?”

Tally highlighted Gregory’s residence on the map. It lay close to the Dark Forest, on the Underworld side of the range. *Practically on the doorstep of the Dark Forest,* Maureen thought.

Tally overlaid the book’s map onto the mission map it still had from the serpent hunt last year.

Then Tally drew a small red ring around one of the test sites.

The red ring fell within the Fasol’s range.

Tally spoke up in Maureen’s head. *The test site is only a few days’ ride from the serpent’s cave. It falls along the route your team took to reach the beast—you nearly walked right into it.*

*I walked into what came out of it.*

*Correction accepted,* Tally said.

Maureen laughed once, a short, humourless noise. Last year, she had ridden past the laboratory of a dead wizard, with Jay holding a handkerchief to his nose as he perfumed himself against snake smell. She’d noticed nothing besides the snakes.

Now the next pages explained it all. Gregory and his apprentice had bred the blood drawn from the Pankora into several subspecies. One of these was the Black Mando Lava Serpent, a level-three creature from the volcanic lake region of the Purgatory World. The creature’s strength was enormous.

The sketch on the page showed black coils rising from a lake of fire, with a broad serpentine head above. The pupils were drawn as vertical slits.

Maureen’s memory superimposed itself over the drawing, calling up her vision of the Fasol’s cave. Hot blood steaming on the rock floor, Sim disappearing down its throat in the middle of a chant. The eyes, so like the sun, staring out at them from the torchlight. Earthen yellow.

“Sulphur,” she said softly. “Fire-smoke.”

The note that no snake on file had ever carried.

The scent that she had smelled in the serpent’s blood. The one thing that she had not been able to explain.

[Odour: sulphur, fire-smoke. Saved.] Tally printed beside its old line.

*Canes Black Rope Snake 78%.*

No more than a metre long, according to Tally. It had told Maureen that it didn’t like that. *Now you know why,* she told it silently.

*Now I know,* it replied.

So that was the beast that had almost killed her. A black rope snake, common enough near the Dark Forest, but poisoned by experiments in the Black Mando bloodline. Swollen by some accident of genetics into twenty-odd metres of scale and hunger.

Tally added the Red Rock samples underneath. The sparrow, the red serpent, the swollen dogs and goats. Sulphur was the most prominent line in all the samples.

*One thread,* said Tally, as it always did whenever the samples were compared.

For the first time, the thread had a source.

Something old had been leaking out of this dead man’s work for centuries. Leaking into dogs and sparrows and snakes beneath a pauper’s graveyard. Seeping into her world since long before she had been born into it.

Then, slowly and carefully, Tally said: *sulphur and fire-smoke; Aaron’s phantom below the mountain, close.*

*Close, but not certain,* it added.

*Keep his file open,* she told it silently. *Don’t close it on my account.*

Her mind flashed back to Aaron, climbing out of the rubble with a better colour than he should have had. A smile that lit up his entire face. The skin at the back of her neck prickled again, as it had then.

Maureen ran her fingertips over the bronze cover. Over the verdigris tarnish, feeling the cold metal beneath. The thing that had almost eaten her last year now had a name. A name that led directly back to her own inheritance. She sat for a long while, thinking.
