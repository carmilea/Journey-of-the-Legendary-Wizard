Maureen stood in the wind like a spear driven into the soil. Her black robe cracked and snapped around her body as if it were a flag in a gale. Black grit and bits of burning pine needles hissed across her cheek, but Maureen did not step back.

Shouting came through the smoke. The first salvo of shells hadn’t finished them. Shapes were still lurching to their feet at the perimeter of the blast. Somebody screamed an order, his voice cracking on the last syllable.

Tally outlined the moving shapes in a thin line of red on Maureen’s inner display, counting five by sight alone. [Grenades: 12.] *Still standing,* Tally noted in the upper right corner of her display.

*Not for long,* Maureen told it silently. She reached into the void pouch with her mind, feeling four cold iron eggs drop into her palm one after another. As each left her hand she muttered “Kerrash” and threw them in a flat, even wave.

The ground leaped. Whole trees went cartwheeling through the air, roots and all. The booms rolled back off the mountainsides one after another. Beneath that deafening peal rose a second scream, thinner and higher-pitched than the first.

[Grenades: 12 → 8.] Three shapes still crawled at the far rim of the blast. *Six would be a reserve,* Tally argued. *Six is a reserve. Five is a hope.*

*The rim needed three more,* Maureen told it silently.

Three more shells arced into the night. When the flash from the last shell faded, Tally displayed [Grenades: 5. Holding 5.]

*Five,* Maureen told it.

*Five, then,* Tally said, under protest.

Where Frey Olivia and his party had been standing was now a crater as wide as a threshing floor. The soil at its lip was burnt black and glossy. Charred figures lay half buried in the overturned earth. One hand still twitched deep in the pile.

Maureen waited, counting her breaths with Tally, before she walked into the crater. First she circled each body, staying a sword’s length away from them. She’d been checking her kills from a distance ever since Derrick, never stepping close enough to let them grab her ankle as they died. Tally tagged the living with a simple movement analysis—[Survivors: 3, dying.]—one breathing in short, shallow hitches, one dragging an arm, one staring—and Maureen ended each from the side with Freya’s slender Cross Sword, not breaking stride. She moved without haste.

Daniel lay at the edge of the crater. His sunny golden hair was burnt down to stubble on his head. His head was turned too far around on his neck, and the white nub of a spinal knot stuck out through the torn skin.

At the same instant that Maureen felt the brand Daniel had pressed against hers on her wrist grow as cold as a coin in a snowdrift, Tally spoke.

[Brand: Daniel. Inert.]

*The leash is cut.*

*Good.*

Frey Olivia lay flat on his back in the centre of the crater, his chest rising and falling in quick, shallow breaths. A disc, cracked down the middle, hung from a chain around his neck. It rested on his breastbone and leaked a weak and stuttering light. His eyes were fixed on her face, but they didn’t seem to be focusing.

*Enchanted,* Tally said. *Reading it by the way it bled particles from the crack. No match in catalogue. Nearly spent. That took the blast meant for him. Won’t take another.*

“Jack...” Frey coughed, blood flecking his lips. “Jack the... Ripper. Maureen. You... all along, you...”

Maureen looked down at him without haste.

“Yes. Jack.” She nodded slightly. “Jack the Ripper.”

*Frey is the only one alive who heard that,* Tally said quietly.

*For a few more breaths,* Maureen told it silently.

For the first time since she had known him, the mild smile was gone from Frey’s face. Something very like respect stirred in her chest. For the bait, for his patience, for the sixteen members of his own family he’d brought into the forest to spring this trap. She closed the feeling like a ledger.

Aloud she said, “It was a good plan, Frey. You just baited it for the wrong girl.” She placed the point of Freya’s slender Cross Sword over his heart. “And thank you for the flower.”

The blade slid in and the light on his chest went out.

*The Olivias will come for the heir’s body,* Tally noted. *Barok, if he hears.*

Maureen used the tip of her knife to cut a small rune eye into the flat surface of a stone beside Frey’s head. It was a pattern she’d found in Gregory’s library.

[Sentinel 2: armed. Relay: water mirror. Cost when called.]

She wiped the blade on the dead grass and turned, looking back toward the garrison lights glimmering between the ridges of the Panso Mountains. The garrison was Olivia territory as well. A small, cold smile touched the corners of her mouth, then she ran.

* * *

The darkness was no obstacle to her night vision, and she tore through the black pines as though it were noon. Roots and stones flicked past beneath her feet, and Tally laid a thin line of blue light over the dark before her to guide her path.

At the base of the wall, she placed her palm against the bluestone and spoke the ring’s reversing word softly. Along the entire length of the wall-walk, the nodes she had placed there sparked to life in a single drawn line of silver light that flickered like moonlight.

[Perimeter: reversed. Mana 11.2 → 10.6.]

Now it would keep people in rather than raiders out.

*They thanked you for this,* Tally said.

*I know,* she answered silently.

Tally pinned the mana counter and gave her the budget. *Fireballs for buildings and knots, the sword for singles. Nine fireballs, approximately, and no rest.*

*Nine will do,* she told it.

The two guards on gate duty recognised her immediately. They came jogging out into the yard with a lantern, obviously relieved to see her. They were half waving as they approached, Maureen said, “Simons-Sado,” twice, and before either of them had taken five steps, two deep red fireballs blossomed at their feet.

[Mana 8.4.]

The boom of the explosions echoed back and forth between the mountains. Chips of bluestone rattled across the hard packed earth of the yard, and the thatch roof of the gatehouse burst into flames as a sheet of fire washed over it. The scent of burnt wool and hot iron rolled back to her on the night breeze.

Maureen glanced down at what was left of the two men without much interest. “Wrong family,” she said aloud, her voice flat and emotionless. They’d been ordinary knights, not warriors or elite troops, but they wore the Olivia coil around their left biceps, and that was the only argument she needed to hear.

The entire yard erupted into motion. A man broke and ran for the gate, screaming and pleading for his life. Someone else yelled, “You’re mad! You’re mad!” and men-at-arms tumbled out of the barracks, half armoured and grabbing at spears as they went.

The runner reached the archway of the gate, and struck the line of silver light. It flared brilliantly, throwing him backward across the hard packed earth like a sack of laundry. He lay there, staring up at the light that had been built to protect him.

> Two left. Spear. Low.

She took the call and let the first spearhead slide past her hip before she swung her sword. The blade opened the man’s torso from navel to breastbone, spilling his guts out across the dirt. The second man died as he tried to pull back the arm that would have thrust his own spear at her.

> Archer on the wall. Fireball.

She pinpointed him by the sound of his bowstring twanging. A single ball of deep red fire flashed into existence next to him, and he fell burning from the wooden wall-walk to land in the middle of the yard.

[Mana 7.3.]

Six spearmen crouched behind an overturned cart, shouting to one another to rush her together.

“Amra-Keis.”

“Amra-Keis.”

The sour-green missiles exploded over the cart. The shouts turned to wet screaming. [Mana 6.5.]

A line of fire ran along the wall-walk timbers from the gatehouse to the barracks. The rafters of the barracks caved in with a roar and a fountain of sparks. The bell tower leaned, and the bell rang once as it fell, though no hand touched it. It was a cracked note.

Tally kept its count, but off her display, as she had once requested.

*Logged.*

*Not shown.*

Doug stumbled across the yard, a coat flung over his nightshirt, grey hair flying wildly about his face. He stared at the fire, then at Maureen.

“Lady Olsen... what are you doing? What is this?”

*He’s not a fighter,* Tally told her. *Pulse up, hands empty.*

Then Tally said nothing more.

Maureen did not answer for half a beat. The same tired, kindly face that had thanked her with both hands for warding the walls. The face of a man who had never been unkind to her, not once.

“Doug.”

She raised her hand. A ball of fire swallowed him where he stood. [Mana 5.4.]

*Logged.*

Shuel burst out of the hall door, shaking with rage.

“You’re finished, Olsen! You think Adrian can protect you now? You think Young Master Frey will—”

A ball of fire lifted him off his feet mid-word. [Mana 4.3.]

He lay in a broken heap across the stone step, blood bubbling out of his mouth.

“Wiz... ard,” he wheezed. “He won’t let you go. Barok...”

[Barok: notified? No data.]

*He’ll know by morning one way or another,* Tally said. *Frey borrowed Barok’s authority to take the flower out of the vault.*

*Let him come,* she answered silently.

She did not look down at Shuel as she set her boot on his head and stepped over him as if he were just another stair.

* * *

Inside, the hall smelled of tallow and spilled wine. The tallow candles all leaned the same way, the draught from the open door blowing straight down the length of the hall. Every shadow in the room shivered with them.

Hasen and Lise stood at the far end of the long table, both pale, both shaking.

“Maureen Olsen.” Hasen managed. “What are you doing? These are our people. Have you gone mad?”

She paused for a moment, as if searching for something she had misplaced. “Oh. Right.” She turned her head in their direction.

“I nearly forgot about you two.”

“We’re witnesses, Olsen.” Hasen’s voice began to climb. “Both of us. We’ll take this to the Steward himself. You’re finished, you hear me? You’re—”

> Behind him. Now.

A hand formed of solid shadow rose up silently behind Hasen, punched through his back, and erupted from his belly, fingers spread wide. He looked down at it, confused.

“Hand of... Negative...”

He lifted his eyes to Maureen, more disbelieving than afraid. Then a deep red fireball filled the space where he had been standing. [Mana 2.1.]

The heat rolled down the table like an invisible wave. The tallow candles flared into sudden life, roaring and sputtering, and ash drifted down onto the scarred wood surface.

She smelled burning cloth, the acrid scent mingling with another, sweeter smell.

Lise had not moved. Maureen saw the other girl’s chin drop, for the first time since they had met. Lise struggled to force out a smile, bright and terrible. “Maureen. Wait. My family would still take you, I can speak for you, I can...”

Tally laid the interleaved timing bars across her sight, Simons-Sado on the beat, Amra-Keis on the off-beat.

*Same trick as the first night.*

*I know, but knowing doesn’t help.*

“Simons-Sado.” “Amra-Keis.”

Her voice was level, almost mild. A fireball and a missile of sour green left her hands together and struck Lise before the smile could finish forming.

[Mana 2.1 → 0.6.]

Afterwards, the hall was silent except for the roar of the fire in the yard outside and the drip...drip...drip of something off the edge of the table. She looked over the floor once, as she would inspect a bench after a failed batch.

“Done,” she said, and turned for the door.

[Mana 0.6. Spells left: none worth casting.]

*You’re on your own from here, Maureen. You have the sword and your knight’s body.*

*Fine, that’s all I need.*

* * *

“Stop!” A servant crawled out from beneath a cart in the yard, his hands held up in supplication. His face was black with soot, but he couldn’t be mistaken for anything other than an Olivia house servant. “The storehouse! I can show you where it is, my lady. The family storehouse, it is hidden. Over there, that door!”

Tally noted the man’s pulse jumping at his throat and his eyes darting to the door and back. *He looks about to beg,* it told Maureen.

He did. “Please, my lady. I helped you. Please, spare me...”

“Yes, thank you,” Maureen Olsen replied, and she used the sword.

They were all tied to the Olivias. All would die tonight, and the fact that this one had told her where the money was didn’t untie him.

The door was locked, and iron-banded to withstand thieves. With only 0.6 mana remaining, the Third-Class Wizard Apprentice wasn’t about to waste a spell. She set her feet in the rhythm of the Silver Light and kicked. The door ripped free of its hinges and flew backward, slamming against the floor inside the room.

A wave of cold air rolled over her, smelling of wax and lamp oil and old wool. Racks of the family’s trophies lined the walls, interspersed with sealed jars of this or that material. Crates of potions filled with straw packing nudged the ceiling, all of them shifting and dancing with the orange glow from the yard outside the doorway.

Maureen Olsen walked the room once, studying the racks. Then she frowned. “Ordinary,” she said.

After Gregory’s library, the loot of the realm, and the cards of the dark elves, an Olivia garrison storehouse was like a peasant’s market stall.

Tally ran every label against its market file, guiding Maureen to what little was worthwhile: two crates of standard healing potions, a case of sealed darkness-school materials, a jar of good-grade powders. *The rest you can skip,* Tally said. *Weight for nothing.*

Tally flagged anything stamped with the Olivia coil—the sigil it recognized from Jay’s scroll—as a trail. These Maureen left on their racks. Anything that could be found on her needed to be anonymous.

“Better than nothing,” she muttered, sending the chosen crates into the void pouch with a touch of her mind. “It’ll trade for stones.” Tally helpfully added each item to the [Pouch] list as it disappeared.

A small iron-bound chest tucked beneath the bottom shelf caught Tally’s eye. *Garrison pay, by the look of it. Mortal towns up ahead,* it said. *You’ll need gold where stones mean nothing.*

Maureen nudged the chest with her boot. It clinked in response, heavy and dull. She lifted its lid and the coins within caught the firelight from the doorway, bouncing the light back yellow against the ceiling.

The chest joined the pouch as well. Tally estimated its contents by weight, opening up a new line for it:

[Gold: 1 chest, est. 300-400 coins by weight.]

*Another column!* Tally said, pleased.

*You love your columns,* Maureen told it.

* * *

The stable roof was catching. She made her way through the smoke and untied both carriage horses, laying her hand on the ridged horn plate on each of their foreheads until they stopped screaming. They were so hot she could feel their breath even through her gloves. Horses frightened by fire were unpredictable beasts. Maureen led them past the black carriage, which stood empty, all else already tucked into the pouch. She watched as the cold black lacquer of the carriage blistered and smoked. The spell-hardened wood that had felt like a winter mirror finally caught.

*You should leave another eye here,* Tally suggested. Where the family will come to look.

Maureen nodded and walked over to the fallen archstone of the gate. She drew the pattern of runes into the stone. [Sentinel 1: armed. Relay: water mirror.] *If Barok comes, this is where he will look,* Tally told her.

Behind her, the garrison was collapsing, piece by piece. First one rafter, then another, came crashing down in a shower of sparks. The walls were beginning to crack from the heat, and over it all lay the thick, coppery smell of blood baking in the stones.

*Logged,* Tally said. *Not shown, as you requested long ago.*

*Not shown,* Maureen repeated. She pulled her black robe closer to her. Her hands were perfectly steady when she glanced down at them.

*Your pulse didn’t move,* Tally said. *Not even once. I’m writing that down, for both of us. The way I did with Lauren.*

*Write it down.*

Maureen swung herself up onto one of the horn-scaled beasts, took the lead rein of the other as a remount, and rode out of the burning gateway. In a few strides, the night had her.
