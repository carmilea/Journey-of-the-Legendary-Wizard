Maureen sat cross-legged on the soft couch, her carriage curtains drawn tight. A tallow stub guttered from her fold-down table, filling the air with the smell of hot fat. Her breath slowed to the steady rhythm of three long, one short, as she began constructing a Spirit Rune. The Ancient Fusiform rune assembled itself behind her eyes, one stroke at a time. Maureen's grounding in the basics allowed her to draw every stroke perfectly, without deviation.

*One,* Tally's warm voice counted within her head.

*Two.*

*Three.*

*Hush, I can count,* she told it.

*One,* Tally repeated in its whisper, much quieter than before.

*Two.*

*Three.*

Tally continued counting through the meditation. Its voice became even softer and a little smug.

The candle slowly burned down to a stump, and the camp outside grew quiet except for the sound of boots on gravel from the guards on duty. After what felt like an eternity to Maureen, a faint glow kindled in the darkness between her brows, beyond where her eyes could see. Even then, it was Tally who flagged the change first. [Luminance +2. Source: Forehead. Faint.]

All she saw was the reflection of the glow in the dark curtain. A trembling luminosity danced on the cloth, like a dust mote caught in a sunbeam, but the sunbeam wasn't there. There were two motes of light shaking in the curve of the brass cup. She couldn't breathe deeply.

*Is that real?* She asked Tally silently.

*Real,* Tally responded quietly. *It is in the record.*

When Maureen woke up at dawn, she felt full of energy and something else that she didn't have the words to describe. Something inside her had changed, or maybe it had just moved into a new place. The grass outside had a layer of frost on it and the camp's cooking fires sent smoke drifting through the morning sky.

The curtains of Maureen's carriage were still drawn against the morning light when she saw the grey of dawn creeping in through the gaps. The tallow stub had guttered out and the brass cup sat quietly next to it. Outside the grass was white from the frost. Maureen hadn't left her spot on the soft couch and she lay there as she silently asked Tally for her status report from last night.

[Warning. Subject exposed to mild energy radiation contamination overnight.]

Maureen lay without moving as she thought about the message. It was almost word for word the same as Tally’s first alert when Scarlette came to Skran. The pressure on her body last night felt exactly like the pressure she felt around the crystal ball when she took the aptitude test. It made sense. Meditation on Spirit Runes was changing her body with some unknown force over and over again until her body became a source of this energy radiation.

A source of energy radiation... That was what they said about really strong wizards. That they had all these eerie things about them, and that if you were an uncontrolled wizard, you could change people and even objects that you touched. People called it 'wizard radiation'.

*That's an interesting theory,* Tally replied in its own voice, *but we don't have enough data points to make a firm conclusion. I only show a mild contamination.*

*Very well,* Maureen answered silently. *Record the energy then.*

Behind her eyes there was a flicker of pale blue, as Tally recorded the energy radiating from her body. Then Tally spoke again. *How should I label this energy?*

*Wizard energy.*

*Excellent. Thank you for that,* Tally said with an edge of hope. *Is it all right if I record it as 'Coronal Wizard Energy Radiation' instead? It sounds more proper and important.*

Maureen closed her eyes and sighed. Tally had been given a chance to make up its own name when she first started learning Basic, now she didn't want to hear about it.

*You've had your chance Tally,* Maureen told it silently.

*Filed under protest,* Tally said. *I will start recording it now.*

Maureen got up from the couch and practised with her Cross Sword on the frost covered ground next to her carriage. Her breath smoked in the chill air while the frost crunched under her boots with every step. She kept at it until she was soaked with sweat.

Maureen washed up behind the canvas screen at her carriage, using hot water heated up by the camp fires. Once changed into fresh clothes, she tied her hair up under her plain hood and sat down to eat some breakfast while the smoke of the convoy's camp drifted over her head.

* * *

Richard was waiting for her at her carriage, when she arrived back from her meal. "Did you get anywhere with your meditation?"

Maureen thought for a moment before shaking her head.

"How about you?"

Duke Skran's second son scratched his head. "Well, I sort of fell asleep..."

Maureen couldn't stop herself bursting into laughter.

*Very convincing head-shake, Maureen Olsen,* Tally said in its own warm voice.

*That was the point, wasn't it?* Maureen replied silently, not moving her lips. Richard was none the wiser.

Inside Scarlette's carriage, the five seeds sat knee to knee upon the velvet seats, worn smooth and shiny with time. The sunlight shining through the slatted wooden shutters striped the interior with lines of light. The smell of ink and old man hung heavily in the air.

"Now, my young wizards, what did you find?" Scarlette asked, smiling at them.

None of them spoke, and Scarlette shrugged. "I didn't expect much from your first attempt. It rarely shows anything the first time. You must be patient." Then Carey's face went a deep red as he blurted out, "Master... Master Scarlette, I... I sensed a Spirit Rune."

Scarlette frowned as he looked across at the former gardener. "Are you sure, Carey?"

Carey nodded, taking a few deep breaths to calm himself, before closing his eyes. Maureen watched as several points of light seemed to drift towards each other behind Carey's eyelids. They gathered on his flushed forehead, forming a strange glowing character that flickered as the points of light drifted over each other.

*Twenty-three points,* Tally noted, filing the shape of the rune away in its own records. It now had two runes on record to compare against any future sightings.

Richard gasped, Aaron became motionless, and Ellena murmured. Maureen's eyes narrowed slightly as she watched the rune forming. "Remarkable, Carey. Very good," Scarlette said as he leant forward.

From that point on, Scarlette seemed to look at Carey differently. The old man himself explained the finer details of what to watch for when using Spirit Runes, and the five seeds listened intently, bowing their heads.

The Blue Ring recruiter appeared to be in a good mood, and offered freely to answer any questions the five seeds might have. As usual, Aaron was the first to ask a question, but to everyone's surprise, including Aaron's, Scarlette gestured for Carey to speak first.

*Maureen, the order they ask questions has changed.*

*Yes Tally, I know.*

*You are aware of what that means?*

*Yes Tally, I am aware. I will ask last, deliberately.*

*Very well. Let them ask their questions first. We will retain every answer given.*

After her own question, Maureen bowed to leave, but Scarlette called out her name. He rested a hand on her shoulder, patting it gently.

"Maureen, you're very good, but sometimes the way you do things is wrong. You are wizard seeds, the wizards of the future. It would be best if you got along with each other, and did not stir up bad feelings."

Maureen stood very still beneath the old man's touch, her eyes lowered, the model seed. She felt the old man's hand on her shoulder, dry and light. She was the good seed after all.

*That reminds me of the day you threw your Cross Sword at Carey, and pinned him to a tree with his shoulder cloth. It also reminds me that the recruiter is being paid per seed delivered. He is taking care of his most expensive seed. You are aware of this?*

*Yes Tally, I'm aware of that too.*

"Of course, honoured lord. I will remember," Maureen replied, and he smiled before waving her away.

*I will remember too. The same thing.*

Outside the carriage, the dust hung in the sun's rays like a golden curtain. Carey's voice could be heard clearly, loud and bright as he bragged to Ellena about his feat. Maureen looked across at him for a moment, before turning to study Scarlette's carriage. "High aptitude," she murmured. After a moment, she shook her head and laughed.

* * *

In the days following, Maureen watched the camp from the step of her carriage. She watched the smoke curling up from the cook-fires, and the long row of carriages sitting in the dust of the road. Down the road she could see Hader Castle's camp, with its white robes shining in the sun. Maureen saw the change in Scarlette's servants, and in the knights of his guard. Now Jack would step aside as Carey walked past, and thump his chest in salute. Word had spread of Carey's aptitude, and the other Blue Ring seed squads, even the people of Hader Castle, were talking about it. People came to visit Carey, and boxes of gifts began to pile up by his carriage window.

[Visitors: 11. Days: 4.]

*Not worth counting,* Maureen answered silently back to Tally.

*Everything is worth counting, Maureen Olsen,* answered Tally in its own warm voice.

Maureen could see new rings on Carey's fingers, and a sweet perfume hanging over the smell of his cook-fire. Aaron, Carey's rival for Ellena's attentions, had fallen quiet around him. Ellena herself was never warm, but now she smiled at Carey, as though testing the waters. Only Richard and Maureen treated Carey the same as before.

* * *

"A duke's young master gave Carey a red coral gem," Richard said, propping himself up on his elbows in the long grass, "bigger than a baby's fist. You ever rest?"

Maureen didn't answer, instead running through another sequence of cuts, a diagonal followed by a horizontal sweep, and then a thrust. Each move was slow, and there wasn't even the faintest breeze from her blade. The grass at the tip barely moved, and Richard yawned.

Tally laid down a series of ghostly lines over Maureen's arm, showing the standard habits of Jack and the other knights.

*No wasted motion. No wind. No air disturbance at all.*

Tally's voice was precise, almost clinical. *My records show that you have passed beyond the need to spar with the Upper Knight guards. You are now capable of defeating three to five of them, or killing two to three.*

*I don't have any plans to do that,* Maureen answered silently.

*I merely note the numbers.*

"Hey!" Richard suddenly shouted.

Maureen sheathed her sword. "What?" she asked.

"You never stop," Richard said with a grin. "However good your swordsmanship is, one spell will settle it."

Maureen just smiled, without saying anything. A First-Class Apprentice could only adapt their spells, and it took a second Rune Array just to cast a spell at all. Barely even a spell, more like a cantrip. That was why wizard seeds poured all their effort into meditation.

"Yeah, but..." Richard began, then stopped as Carey walked past with a companion. He glanced at Richard and Maureen before snorting and walking off without a word.

The smell of his perfume lingered behind him for a few moments in the breeze, overpowering the scent of horse dung.

"Who were they?" His companion asked, his voice drifting back faintly on the wind.

"Oh, just two idiots," Carey answered. "Bass, becoming wizard seeds with me will be the proudest thing we will ever do, and I know for a fact that I am going to become a wizard."

"Yes, you are, and now come on," his companion answered, hurrying him along.

Richard's face darkened, and he began to complain about Carey.

"Richard, it's only a small matter," Maureen said, gripping her Cross Sword and cutting forward hard.

*You broke form by four degrees on your last swing,* Tally noted.

*Log it as fine,* Maureen thought back silently.

*Logged as fine. Disputed.*
