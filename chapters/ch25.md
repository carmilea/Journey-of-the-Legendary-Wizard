The candle flickered and guttered on Hegel’s desk, and the black stone walls seemed to breathe cold air into the room. The cross-star orb sat quietly on its stand, glowing slightly from within. Hegel sat back in his chair with his sly grin, and Richard and Aaron stood behind Maureen, waiting for their turn. Aaron’s thumb was busy working the hem of his sleeve.

Maureen stood by Hegel’s desk, riffling through the thick register of mentors. She was doing the same thing that she had done with the manuals in the treasury, giving each page only a glance as she flipped past them.

The vellum pages crackled slightly as they were flipped under Maureen’s thumb, and at every spread a pale blue flicker would pass behind her eyes. There was also a smell coming off the pages, the odor of old ink, dry and musty, hanging in the still air.

When the first quarter-hour finished, Maureen added a second magic stone to Hegel’s desk. Tally reset the timer, then displayed how many magic stones were left in her pouch.

[Magic stones: 112 remaining]

Tally began to set the pace of flipping pages on Maureen’s inner display.

> Turn.

Charles, official wizard: Spirit-Body Studies, negative-energy neutralization, Alchemy. Apprentices must participate in a magic experiment once every half month.

Edward, official wizard: shadow-school wizardry, anatomy, High-Energy Plasticity. Free advanced instruction and three free magic stones if apprentice allows Edward to dissect their body.

Maureen’s mouth tightened, and her finger stayed on Edward’s page for a moment longer than it had on any other page.

That one costs your body, Tally said, its voice holding its usual warmth.

I noticed, Maureen replied silently to Tally.

Tally struck through Edward’s name.

You shouldn’t linger on him so much, it told her, a hint of smug satisfaction in its voice.

Adrian, official wizard: darkness-school wizardry, Pharmacology, Mutation Studies, Plant Cultivation. Apprentices must keep set potion materials clean and tended to every month. Apprentices may sometimes be required to travel to special places in order to gather materials.

Hogg, official wizard: Plant Cultivation, Mutation Studies, anatomy. Apprentices must gather specified materials as well as participate in at least one experiment.

Each entry seemed darker than the last. The tips of Maureen’s fingers were cold where they touched the edges of the pages.

“Equivalent exchange,” she murmured. “This style, this manner… Truly worthy of wizards.”

“Time’s up.” Hegel snapped the book closed and took it back, his face wearing a sly grin. “Do you want a little more time?”

Maureen closed her eyes. In the darkness behind her eyelids, Tally’s display glowed with its soft light.

I’ve laid them against each other and compared them to my records, Tally said. I can verify how well they fit together, but not if they’re telling the truth.

[Rank 1: Adrian.]

Why?

Darkness affinity. Pharmacology at one hundred percent. Least cost to your body.

Tally struck through all the other names. Darkness. One hundred percent. Least cost to your body. Each one ended back on Adrian.

* * *

Maureen opened her eyes. “Lord Hegel, I’ve decided. I choose Wizard Adrian as my mentor.”

“Wizard Adrian.” Hegel repeated the name twice, then smacked his lips together. He fell silent, still as death, though it was obvious he was thinking. It seemed like something had come to him, some connection between the name and the girl.

[Hegel: hesitation, 0.6 seconds.]

Tally flagged it for Maureen’s attention.

[Data: insufficient. Reason for hesitation unknown.]

Adrian: unknown.

The hairs on the back of her neck rose in alarm, but she forced herself to keep still, her hands hanging by her sides.

Hegel chuckled. “You wish to be an apothecary? Not a bad idea… not simple, but not bad.”

Maureen frowned. “I don’t understand, Lord.”

“Don’t get so excited,” Hegel said. “I was just thinking about the path you’d be on. It’s an expensive one, at first. Apothecaries spend a lot of money in their early years. Once they’re able to brew their own potions, though, they never run out of magic stones. Every single apothecary is wealthy. So many people want to become apothecaries, particularly wizard families. They have enough money, at least. Alchemy used to be the same way.”

The candlelight glinted off Hegel’s ringed fingers, and Maureen glanced down at the register, shut on his table. The flagstones were cold under her feet, the other seeds were waiting in a line along the wall, and a draught was coming through from the doorway.

On her display, Tally pulled up the page from the register it had recorded, placing it next to Hegel’s words. Charles had Alchemy listed as one of his skills.

> Charles’s entry indicates that he has Alchemy as one of his skills.

> Hegel has just indicated that Alchemy is no longer a rich skill.

> The two do not agree. I do not have data indicating which is wrong.

And then, triumphantly, Tally sent:

> I finally know what an apothecary is to wizards!

> Rich.

Don’t count your stones before they’re in your pocket, Maureen told Tally silently.

Another column appeared on her display, this time with a ledger’s lines. She let Tally have its fun.

“You must sit Adrian’s test along with the other seeds,” Hegel said. “Fail, and you’ll just have to choose a mentor all over again.”

“Yes, Lord,” Maureen said immediately.

“Very well,” Hegel said, and he clapped his hands. A shadow servant rose from the flagstones, a dark thing that made no sound as it stood up. “Follow this servant. Good luck.”

Tally flagged Aaron’s thumb, working its way back and forth along the hem of his sleeve. Richard had a half-smile on his face, but it wouldn’t stay there. Maureen nodded to both of them, and silently told Tally to file that look and not try to explain it.

The shadow servant began to move without making footsteps. A cold came off it, like the breath of a cellar, as Maureen followed it out of the room, past the draught coming through the doorway.

* * *

Walking behind the shadow servant, she went over the steps in her head, again and again. She knew what she was supposed to do. She’d read Tally’s copy of Basic Principles of Pharmacology every night since she’d dissolved it, memorizing each section until she felt she understood it.

You are familiar with the concepts, but your understanding is shallow, Tally noted.

Shallow will do for now, she thought back.

I’ll hold you to that, Tally warned her.

A bare test hall waited deep inside the castle, lined with tall black pillars that were cold when she leaned against one. A raised dais sat at the center. Voices echoed from the hard stones.

Six or seven other wizard seeds were waiting in the hall, their eyes closed as they rested or meditated. They looked up when Maureen walked in, then turned away from her without saying anything. She leaned against a pillar and shut her eyes.

Tally counted their footsteps as more wizard seeds arrived, and told her when there were ten.

“Hello,” said a girl with blonde hair, dressed in white gauze as she walked up to the dais. “I’m Winnie. I’m Mentor Adrian’s apprentice. Since there are ten of us, let’s begin.”

Winnie clapped her hands.

Dark shapes flowed into the room from the shadows, and Maureen turned to see a line of shadow servants carrying benches. The benches were set with glass bottles, beakers, glass rods, and test tubes.

The clean, sterile smell of glass was the first thing Maureen noticed, followed by the sharp smell of reagent. Her first lab since coming to this world.

“Whoever can complete that fastest and best will become Mentor Adrian’s apprentice.” She pointed at a tube filled with a pale blue liquid reagent, and a beaker with some large, black insects in it. “You must perform the extraction-fusion reaction of Star-vein Leaf Grass and Purple Sac Insect.” All ten wizard seeds immediately grabbed their reagent.

Tally pulled the relevant section of the scroll out of its recording, the part that dealt with Star-vein Leaf Grass and Purple Sac Insect, and placed it in a column on Maureen’s display, showing the steps to be taken.

Her hands remembered. The white lab bench, the prototype inside the glass, her own hands wielding the tweezers.

Working down the column, Maureen carefully extracted an insect from the beaker using the glass rod, before bringing it over to the reagent, step by step.

Her breath slowed, her shoulders loosened. For a few seconds, something like joy held her.

Long before any of the others finished, Maureen spoke up. "Winnie, I'm done." She raised the test tube. The pale blue inside was now smoking white, while the glass itself was glowing red, as if it would burst open at any second.

Three point five two three seconds, Tally said. That's the peak power output of the scroll, not an explosion. > Flick. Now.

Maureen held the tube for another half beat, until the colour was just right, then she flicked her wrist. There was a shrill shriek from the tube, like an insect protesting its untimely death, followed by a puff of purple smoke.

The test tube stilled. Inside was an inky black liquid, with a faint glow, like an endless mass of tiny black insects crawling within.

Logged. You were correct about the timing. Don't get used to it though.

"Very good." Winnie's eyes lit up. "Simply perfect. Welcome, Maureen, the mentor's apprentice." The other nine wizard seeds had been eliminated.

Nine sets of eyes glared at her. Maureen straightened up, let the joy fade to cold and ignored them completely as she followed Winnie out the door.

* * *

"We have a lot of ground to cover. The Dark Forest is split into a number of different districts. Living, experimenting, lodging, teaching, exchange, and more." Winnie walked ahead of Maureen through the black stone passages. The air was cold, with a draught wafting through. Voices could be heard from halls in the distance. "There are also some areas which you will not be allowed to enter. You've only just arrived, so be careful. If you step where you shouldn't, there'll be big trouble."

Tally was building a map on Maureen's display, as they walked through the corridors. Each district was labelled, while restricted zones were hatched over in red.

Winnie's white gauze dress whispered against the stone floor as they walked. Maureen's wool robe smelled of the dyes that coloured it, as well as the faint scent of the ship.

"There's a restaurant over there. They serve food from every principality. It's expensive though."

Can I open a food budget? Tally asked.

I'll eat what's free, Maureen thought back silently.

Very well. Budget opened. Total available, zero, Tally reported.

"That's the lodging district over there." Winnie pointed to one side as she continued talking. Maureen said nothing, just letting Tally record every word.

There's more red on this map than I like, Tally commented.

Keep drawing, Maureen thought back.

The corridors became richer as they walked. The black stone walls were polished to a mirror shine, and at the end of the corridor was an elaborately carved door. The edges of the door glowed with lamplight.

Winnie stopped outside the lavish looking room. "Maureen, the mentor is inside." She knocked softly on the door. Maureen stood silently behind Winnie, motionless.
