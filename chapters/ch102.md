Eighteen grenades detonated as one around the Pankora Scarlet Serpent.

The hall bucked beneath her feet like the deck of the tortoise ship again, and the sound of the explosion roared past the limits of hearing, slamming into her chest and her teeth with the force of a hammer blow. Particles of dark and fire boiled upwards from the blast, like the curling black smoke from a lamp with the wick turned too high. The serpent’s shriek tore through the roar like a saw blade biting into wet timber.

She had begun running back the instant the shells left her hands. It wasn’t enough. The shock wave caught her in mid-stride, lifted her from the ground, and slammed her back-first into the far wall. A fine dust of wood filled her mouth, and the back of her skull rang like a bell. She coughed, and kept coughing, tasting scorched Hillan and sulphur on her tongue. Her left side flared with pain each time her chest expanded to draw breath.

Tally’s display hovered in her vision, steadfast against the haze.

>[Grenades thrown: 18. Remaining: 16.]

It was immediately followed by another line of text, derived by inference from her own body.

>[Ribs: bruised, left. Ears ringing. Pulse 128.]

*Up,* Tally said, the voice suddenly cool and clipped. *It’s healing.*

Through the dust, she could see the serpent thrashing on the floor. Its tail swept through the air like a scythe, catching two wooden pillars in its arc and sending them crashing down to the floor in a cascade of rubble. Where the shells had bitten into its metallic hide, black scales were split and smoking. The wounds were moving. The raw, red flesh at the centres crawled towards one another like worms knotting in a bait tin. The black scales slid back over the pink edges with a faint, dry clicking sound she could hear even through the ringing in her ears.

Tally pinned a timer to the worst of the wounds, the crater that the force of the blast had torn into the serpent’s head. *Two finger-widths every breath,* it read out. *Faster than anything on file,* Tally said. *Faster than the Fasol serpent. Faster than Beck’s arm.*

She worked it out while her back remained pressed against the wall. Gregory had cut this thing down even below a juvenile form, and it still healed at this rate. She’d only managed to hurt it because it had never been given the opportunity to demonstrate what it could do.

Getting to her knees made her ribs sing with pain. Every muscle in her body screamed in protest, wanting to remain pressed against the wall. Then the fear in her throat narrowed, became sharp, until there was nothing left in her but numbers.

Tally ran the projection without being asked.

At that rate, the wound would be shut in around forty breaths from now.

No plan on file for after that. Not one that left her standing.

*Then now,* she thought to it, silently.

*Now,* Tally agreed.

She stood up. Her hands found the casting gesture as if of their own accord, and she gave the Ancient Andas chant its full voice.

“Endless wind, surging darkness, let me show your might! Tear apart every offender before me! Dark Wind Ring!”

A curling ghost path flowed from her hands to the serpent’s gaping head wound.

*Use the leftover particles,* Tally told her, almost cheerful. *They’re free.*

In the corner of her eye, the line ticked over.

[Mana 11.2 → 9.0]

Wind howled inside the sealed hall, but there was nowhere for it to go except where she directed it. The lingering dark and fire particles from the shells were caught in its force, and twisted and spun into a vortex that bored towards the serpent’s head.

The air grew thick with scorch and sulphur, and the wind picked up dust devils and spun them into a spiral. Maureen’s short hair lashed at her face as the wind whipped through the broken pillars. The air tasted of ash and hot iron.

A spell alone would not get through that skull before it closed. She intended to ride the vortex in herself, so her hand slipped into her inner pocket and closed around the Seal.

Tally’s voice took on a flat and cold note. *Do not. Fracture risk unknown. Ceiling unknown.*

*Count for me,* she told it silently.

Tally was quiet for a beat.

*Counting.*

“Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa.”

The armour obeyed the command, but only in patches. It rose over her forearms, her chest, one shoulder. The thorns that came with it were cracked before they fully formed, and they broke off and rattled to the ground around her feet.

It felt like asking a wounded friend to stand. The vines didn’t flow against each other smoothly, but ground together instead, and the smell of sap and iron was thin and sour in her nose.

The vortex lifted her from her feet, taking her into its eye. Freya’s slender sword left a trail of black light behind it, like oil on water, as Maureen drove upwards into the serpent’s head wound. The blade sank into the beast’s scales with a sound like splitting slate.

[Seal: 0.4 s…]

[0.9 s…]

[1.3 s…]

[RELEASE.]

Maureen let go the instant she received the call, and the Seal’s patchy armour fell away from her before it could fail.

The vortex poured in through the hole after her, and tore the serpent’s head apart from the inside. She burst out from the far side of the skull and dropped.

Hot blood fell on her like rain, thick with sulphur and scorched honey. She landed on one knee in the growing pool of the stuff, and stayed there, gasping for breath.

Behind her, the serpent gave one last shriek, and then crashed to the ground where it lay still.

*It’s down. Breathe.*

Tally’s voice was quieter now, warm again.

*You’re shaking.*

*Log it as tired,* she told it silently.

*Logged as tired,* Tally said. *Disputed.*

Blood ran warm from her nose, over her lip. The ache beneath her ribs had sunk in deeper than it ever had before, and the glow of the Seal was more broken than before through the cloth that covered her chest, a few jagged sparks.

*That cost you more than nine seconds used to,* Tally said.

A new line appeared on Maureen’s display.

[Seal ceiling: under 2 s. Provisional.]

*I know what I did,* she answered silently.

Kneeling in the serpent’s blood, Maureen allowed herself one breath for the word lucky. At her true strength, this kill should have cost her a great deal more. She let the breath go, and took nothing else for it.

* * *

[Body: Pankora Scarlet Serpent.]

*Scarlet in the name, black in the scale.*

*That’s what the name is,* Tally answered. *The species. Not the coat colour.*

*Whoever named it never saw one, then.*

The serpent’s body began to thin. First the scales, then the coils, then even the ruined head began to turn grey and see-through, until they were gone. And where the serpent’s head had been, a ball of purple-black blood rose from the floor, as if it had been buried there all along. The size of a fist, it hung there in the air, turning slowly.

It pulsed, though not in time with the beat of her own heart. The mismatch made Maureen’s skin crawl. The light of the glyphs slid over its surface, breaking like lamplight on oil. It smelled of scorched honey and sulphur.

[Essence blood. Analysis: no data. Pulse: 31, not a heart on file.]

*What is it?*

Tally tried to get more information, but came up empty. *It’s like Mace,* Tally said, sounding put out. *It just doesn’t answer.*

A voice spoke, but there was no direction to it.

“Inheritor. You have slain the Pankora Scarlet Serpent. My congratulations. Truly.” There was a pause, and then, “May you walk the wizard’s road a long way further than I did.”

It was the same dry voice as the portrait in the realm, like a page being turned slowly. When Gregory finished speaking, the silence descended again, like dust settling in the cold glyph light.

*That’s him, all right,* Tally said. *Matches the loop in the realm. No particle reaction, no one standing behind it. Just a recording.*

Maureen didn’t respond to it, not even silently. There was no one there to respond to.

She knew what was hanging in front of her. It was the Pankora’s essence blood, the bloodline of a Child of Purgatory.

Her hands trembled as they rose toward it. They shook with hunger, plain and bright and unashamed. She didn’t try to hide it from the one witness she had.

*You’re hungry,* Tally observed.

*Always,* she responded silently.

*And it is useful. At your promotion, you will need to ignite the Minai Totem. The blood of a level-five creature would set the Totem’s power from the first day.*

[Minai: 85%. Missing: Demon-Face Flower.]

Maureen pulled the crystal vial out of her belt kit. The glass was thick and strong, wizard-grade. The outside was cold in her glove.

Tally called up the old ledger entry. *Restricted materials, purchased by you in the Dark Forest on Adrian’s access. Your most expensive glass,* it told her with impish pleasure. *Worth it now, worth it now.*

Maureen pulled out the stopper and beckoned the floating blood toward the mouth of the vial with a thread of dark particles. The blood began to move, slowly, heavily, as if it were deciding whether to go or not. It fell into the vial and the neck immediately frosted over with white crystals. The glass grew even colder in Maureen’s hand. Inside the vial, the blood continued to pulse against the walls at its own strange rhythm.

When the vial was full, Maureen stoppered it and shoved it into the inner fold of her robe, against her ribs. Anything that mattered stayed on her body, where she could protect it with her own hands.

Maureen wiped her nose with the back of her wrist. She only succeeded in smearing more blood over blood. Her knees were still shaking, but she let them shake.

* * *

She did not think for a moment that Gregory had constructed a hall of this size and complexity merely to house one meditation method and one ball of blood. Void Eclipse had burned itself into some part of her now, and the vial of blood was pressed firmly against her ribs. Time to search.

Maureen stepped carefully over piles of rubble and around black scorch marks. Her boots peeled away from the floorboards with a small wet noise after each step. The hall was so quiet now that the sound of her own breathing seemed loud.

Tally drew a grid over the hall on Maureen’s display, keeping track of which alcoves Maureen had checked. [Searched: 9 of 14]

*You missed a corner,* Tally said.

*I was getting to it,* Maureen responded silently.

Behind the fallen pillar, in the last niche, was a sphere of semi-transparent black that hovered in the air at chest height. Nothing appeared to be holding it up.

It was cold to look at. The air surrounding it rippled like summer heat radiating off a road, but this rippling felt like the inside of a cellar. A dull grey pouch lay on a wooden shelf next to the sphere. There was nothing special about the pouch. It could have been made of burlap or leather, and it had collected dust like any other cloth object. A carter might have hung such a thing from his belt. But Maureen’s particle sense caught on it. Space bent around the pouch in ways that nothing ordinary ever did.

*What are they?* Maureen asked Tally.

Tally matched the objects against Gregory’s shelves of enchantments and against the bronze book’s catalogue of Ancient Era artifacts. [Darklight Orb. Magic artifact, by Gregory’s catalogue. Energy Cycling / Negative-Molecule Modification; Morse’s Law of Conservation. Power: strong.]

[Void Dimensional Pouch. Dimensional space, by entry. Interior: no data.]

*I can’t see inside it,* Tally admitted. *I’ll list what I see go in.*

A magic artifact was a full grade above an enchanted item. It was supposed to be far more powerful than the average magical object.

Maureen thought of the Seal in her pocket, medium-grade and cracked. She thought of how long it had kept her alive.

Maureen picked up the pouch. She could not open its mouth with her fingers. She opened it with her spirit instead, with a touch of mind. She felt its inside, an inside that went down but never reached a bottom.

It was like reaching into a well of cold darkness. Her hand could feel the weight of nothing at all, even as her mind could measure how deep that nothing went.

[Pouch: 0]

Tally added a new line to her display.

*You keep a ledger for a bag?* Maureen asked silently.

*The best kind!* Tally replied.

*No more carriages,* Maureen added silently. *No more strongboxes under the seat, and no more horses to feed raw beef.*

*The horses will miss us,* Tally noted.

*I won’t,* Maureen responded. She tied the pouch’s cord around her waist, next to her belt kit. Her hand would know how to find it without looking.

Dust drifted through the flickering glyph lights. The only things that still moved in all that ruin of old timber were the motes of dust and the cold ripple of the sphere.

* * *

She turned to face it, murmuring the few words of spell-speech Gregory gave in his catalogue entry for binding the Orb to a wizard’s service. She fed it a thin thread of her own mana—her own dark particles.

They left her fingertips cool as they spun away, curling into the sphere like ink dropped into still water. They curled and folded, spreading across the surface of the sphere until the sphere’s blackness grew deeper inside.

Tally watched the ripple surrounding the sphere. Watched it settle down and become part of her own rhythm.

[Bind: taking.]

*It likes you,* Tally said.

*It likes my mana,* Maureen answered silently.

The Orb trembled. It drifted towards her, sinking through her robe and into her chest, like a stone dropped into the deepest water.

A cool weight settled behind her breastbone. Just below where the Seal had left a faint, pale eye on her chest. It didn’t hurt. It was just there, strange and close, like a second heart that didn’t beat.

Tally said nothing. For four long seconds, her display went empty, its longest silence since George’s book had bitten her.

Four seconds wasn’t long, not really, but those four seconds felt longer than any four seconds Maureen had ever known. She held her breath, pressing one bloody hand flat against her chest, waiting for any kind of readout from Tally. Anything at all.

Then Tally spoke. Quietly.

*I felt that.*

*Felt what?*

*Mana.*

*Moving through it.*

*Through you.*

*I can count it.*

*I can’t hold it.*

Another pause. Longer this time.

*I want to.*

*Is that a request?*

*It’s data.*

Another pause. Then, almost hesitantly.

*It’s a request.*

Maureen was still bleeding. She was still shaking. And yet she found herself smiling. Not because of the artifact. Because of Tally. She had never heard it express a desire before, and the note of wonder in its voice reached her more than anything the Orb did.

Tally added a small new gauge to the corner of her display.

[Orb flow: steady. Action: none.]

*Just watching,* it told her.

*For now,* she answered silently.

It didn’t argue.

She let out a long breath, the first full breath she had taken since beginning this fight that didn’t hurt to draw, and smiled again at nothing in particular.

Maureen stayed where she was for a few more minutes, sitting in the ruined hall. Blood dried stiff on her robe sleeves, the crystal vial was cold against her ribs, the pouch was light at her waist, and the Orb was a cool weight beneath the pale eye.
