They came from everywhere at once. From every corridor and opening, black-robes hurried into the great hall, moving in loose knots, all of them looking frightened. Boots scuffed against the damp flagstones, and with every breath came the smell of incense mingled with the tang of wet stone. The bright light overhead died long before it reached the corners.

A black-robed wizard hung in the air above the crowd, his arms folded across his chest. He looked down at the apprentices like a farmer regarding a spilled sack of grain. “Gather! All of you, here, now! You’re going to the secret realm trial. Move! Did you hear me?”

[Warning: HIGH energy radiation source.]

Tally’s cold voice flashed across Maureen Olsen’s vision, and then its warm tones followed. “He likes to yell.”

*Most of them do,* she told it silently.

*Yes, well,* Tally said, *I’ll open a column for us.*

Maureen Olsen stood by one of the great hall’s support pillars, at the edge of the crowd. She was small and still, her own kit packed tightly onto her back, her hands loose at her sides. She watched, noting who flinched at each bellow, and who did not.

“Enough, Cyss,” an old voice rumbled through the hall. It sounded like a grandfather speaking to a grandchild. “Hush, now. You’ll frighten them.”

The floating wizard closed his mouth immediately. In the sudden quiet, that said more than all the shouting had.

Dark particles surged together in the air, forming a projection of an old man with white hair and beard. His face was folded into so many wrinkles that it was hard to tell where one ended and another began. However, his eyes were bright and sharp as needles. He wore a black robe crowded with patterns. “I am Augurit. Augurit Jones, Steward of the Dark Forest.”

*Same voice as the war decree,* Tally noted. *I’d know it in my sleep.*

*You don’t sleep,* she told it silently.

*Then I’d know it in yours. A projection doesn’t give off a band reading. The gentleman himself is elsewhere.*

“It is with regret that we send you to your trial,” Augurit said, his voice mild and unhurried. “But this has been forced upon us by events beyond our control. None here wishes harm to come to you. To that end, gifts have been prepared, so that none of you shall go out unprotected.” He spoke gently, as a man might speak while counting out coins he would not miss.

The great hall grew very quiet. Someone near Maureen swallowed loudly. To her ears, this was a kindly voice saying words that cost the speaker nothing. Her shoulders remained tight beneath the straps of her pack.

*A projection has no pulse beating at its throat, no hands to tremble,* Tally told her. *There is nothing for me to read.*

*A man who sends his voice rather than himself to talk to you has considered exactly that,* she answered it silently.

“Children of the Dark Forest,” Augurit Jones’s voice said. The words floated through the great hall, reaching every corner, every ear. “I do hope you live.”

The apprentices looked up, startled, as a flock of crows poured down from the darkness high above them. One crow settled before each of them. With a soft bang, like a pillow hitting the floor, each bird exploded into a bag made from black cloth. The bags hung in the air at chest height before each apprentice.

There were cries of surprise, and the sound of feathers rustling like dried leaves as they settled to the ground, becoming nothing. Hands reached out tentatively. Maureen’s bag felt cool and dry to her fingers. It smelled faintly of feathers and dust. As she loosened the neck and tilted it slightly toward the light, Tally’s display appeared before her eyes.

[Explosive potions: 4]

[Healing potions: 3]

[Map: rough, regions only]

She tipped the map out one-handed. It was a piece of parchment with blocks of shading on it, but no paths marked, no names written.

*The explosive shells are not as powerful as one of yours,* Tally told her as she rolled up the map.

*The boy next to me is holding his like it’s the treasure of the realm,* Maureen answered silently.

*The healing vials have a particle density significantly higher than Adrian’s apprentice grade,* Tally said. *These are probably full-wizard brews.*

*Keep those then.*

*I was just going to suggest that you do so, Maureen.*

Her jaw relaxed just a fraction as she slid the three vials into the inner fold of her robe, next to her own stock. The four explosive shells went into the outer pocket, where she kept things she would not mind losing.

“Now then, children. Let us be off.”

As Augurit’s image broke up into drifting particles once more, the apprentices began to file out of the great hall behind the floating wizard. Not a word was spoken.

* * *

Outside the gate, under the lid of black cloud and with no sun visible in any direction, there was a row of carriages waiting to receive them. There were no wheels visible on any of them. They were grey, without decoration or embellishment of any kind. Four grey wings were folded down the long sides of each carriage body. The wings were shaped like the wings of bats, but were constructed of the same grey material as the carriages themselves. The air above the carriages was rippling with real dark particles, moving in the same way that heat does along a summer road. Cold rolled off the black stone behind Maureen.

The beasts in the traces were horses in outline only. From their brows sprouted horns that curled back toward their heads. Their eyes burned red as hot coals, and their bodies were wrapped in fine black scales. Low, long beast-roars rumbled from their throats, sounding like thunder echoed in some valley. They dug their horns into the earth they stood upon, churning it up around their hooves. A hot and pungent animal stink hung over the line of carriages and beasts.

[Altered by wizardry.]

*By sight.*

[Method: no data.]

*Someone’s been breeding those things,* Maureen told Tally silently. *Like Evans breeding that tortoise.*

*Same family of ideas, at least,* Tally answered her.

Several wizards stood next to the gate, but said nothing. Their eyes followed the passing apprentices with a steady, unblinking gaze. There was something about that look . . .

Adrian, bending over a tray full of Holan grubs, carefully separating the living from the dying. The hair rose on the back of Maureen’s neck. Cold crept between her shoulder blades. She stared fixedly at the carriage ahead of her.

*Five,* Tally said. *They’re not looking at faces, but at the crowd. The way a buyer looks over a pen of sheep, to see how many there are. They aren’t interested in who any of you are, just in how many there are.*

*Stop counting,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*Five,* Tally whispered, so quietly that Maureen almost didn’t hear it. Then it was silent.

“In. Get in.”

Nobody dared dawdle. The apprentices clambered aboard. The door shut, and once more the dark particles surged, and once more the world went black.

Not night-black this time, but nothing-black. Maureen raised her hand before her face. She could see nothing. Here and there she caught a faint glint from another apprentice’s eyes, but even those were soon extinguished.

There was no wind this time, no creaking of harness. There was a lurch, perhaps, but she might have imagined it. Her own breathing was loud in her ears. Beneath her thighs, the wooden bench was the only real thing remaining in the universe.

*I can’t see,* Tally said. *I’ll have to count your pulse instead. Elapsed by heartbeat one hour forty. Distance? Can’t tell.*

*First time you’ve ever said that to me,* Maureen said silently.

*Untrue,* Tally replied. *I say it to you all the time. It just sounds braver in the dark.*

Maureen closed her eyes, slowing her breathing to Silver Light’s three long breaths, one short. Tally counted along with her, beat for beat, in a soft whisper. *Three . . . four . . . five . . . six.* By the time they reached twenty-five, the nothingness around her had become a room again.

At last the carriage settled. “Out,” Cyss said coldly, and the apprentices climbed down and stood silently outside.

* * *

The sun slapped Maureen in the face as she came out. She put up a hand against its brightness, blinking and trying not to cry. Even this mild warmth felt harsh on her skin after the void’s embrace. The scent of grass and a wind came to her, and in the distance she heard the sound of a bird.

*Sun.*

*Logged.*

*Warmer than last time,* she told Tally silently.

*Data supports you.*

The Dark Forest apprentices were already gathered in a silent knot of black robes. Maureen took her place in the second rank, standing behind and to the side of a boy who was taller than she. From there, if anyone glanced over the group, their eyes would slide right past her.

Off to one side stood a crowd of boys and girls wearing blue-green robes. They radiated wizard energies, some strong, some weak—a sure sign that these were the apprentices of the Blue Ring.

Maureen felt her throat close. A moon-white collar. A voice crying out, “My sister—” and breaking. A head striking against a grey boulder by the stream.

She willed her hands to stay loose at her sides, her breathing to remain even.

*Patton’s colours.*

*Bina’s order.*

*Keep your face still.*

*It is still.*

*It is now.*

At the head of the Blue Ring apprentices stood a young man with an easy, graceful smile. “Jason,” he called across the open grass.

“Alka,” said a deep voice from the other side, without much interest.

A middle-aged man with a thick, heavy beard stood before rows of Hader Castle apprentices dressed in tight-fitting white robes. He had a stern face, and one hand rested on a cane. Every few seconds, he seemed to grip it tighter, then loosen his grasp, as if the cane had done something to offend him.

*Jason, Hader, cane, temper.*

His knuckles had whitened four times in the last minute. Alka’s smile hadn’t moved once during the same period. Tally was storing that information away in the same mental file where Frey Olivia’s smile had gone.

“So Augurit’s here,” Alka said, his tone light and casual. “And he’s brought that old relic from the White Bone Tower.”

Maureen followed Alka’s gaze and saw, standing beside Augurit Jones in person, a tall man dressed in a robe patterned in blood red. The wizard’s face was obscured by a mask carved from bleached white bone. The two men were engaged in a quiet conversation. Behind them stood a block of White Bone apprentices, who didn’t move or speak.

[Warning: HIGH energy radiation source.]

The alert appeared on her display twice, once for Augurit and once for the masked man.

*Two HIGHs right next to each other,* Tally said inside her head.

“Heh. Black-robes.” Jason’s cold laugh carried clearly over to the Dark Forest ranks. Not one head around Maureen turned at that.

Jason’s eyes slid over to the far side of the ground. There was an ugly glint in them. “Still. Black-robes I can swallow. It’s those damn dark elves that’ll give us problems.”

The elves stood in a mass of fine leathers and robes, all their faces finely made and pale skinned. As they turned their heads, Maureen saw that their eyes were blue, so deep a blue they were almost black. Even across the distance that separated them, Maureen’s particle sense prickled at the strength of the elves.

Before them stood a middle-aged woman dressed in simple clothing, her lips pressed into a faint smile. She was motionless, as still and balanced as a blade lying flat on a table. Alka blinked. “Is that… Eve? One of their few Night Mothers. I never expected her to come in person.”

*No tells,* Tally said, silently in Maureen’s head. Nothing about Eve was moving that she hadn’t wanted to move. Based on how still the elves were standing around her, Tally put Eve at the top of the danger list.

Fog began rolling in over the grass, first a thin layer that gradually became thicker and thicker, obscuring outlines and blurring details. Boys and girls stepped out of the fog, as if it had birthed them. At their head stood a figure wrapped entirely in fog, with no face visible through the shifting mists.

The figure lifted its head, and spoke in a hoarse rasp. “Sorry we’re late.”

The Mist Labyrinth had arrived.

Maureen’s particle sense was crawling under her skin like ants. *Your particles are saying something is there,* Tally said. *Your eyes are saying it’s weather. I believe your particles.*

*So do I,* Maureen answered it silently.

Everyone who was coming had arrived, and the entire gathering ground fell into an eerie silence. The only thing that moved was the wind, hissing through the high grass between the separate blocks of hostile apprentices.

Then Augurit rose slowly from the ground, letting his gaze move from Jason to Eve to Alka, one by one.

“Well,” he said finally. “Everyone’s here. Let us begin.”

No one argued.

The leaders spread out around the edges of the wide open grass, and began to chant as one, a low murmur. There was a sound like cloth tearing, and several doors opened in the air, each one a rectangle of grey light.

“These lead into the realm,” Jason shouted, slamming his cane down so hard that a clump of turf jumped away from the ground. “In you go!” He raised his arm with an imperious wave. “Line up! Line up! One at a time!”

Each of the leaders spoke to his or her group, and Maureen listened as closely as she could.

She could hear Augurit speaking to the black-robes, but his words slid right past her. Nothing for Tally there.

Across the wide, sunlit grass, Maureen caught sight of Eve holding on to the hand of one of the petite dark-elf girls, with fair skin and long black hair, much longer than any of the others. The girl’s ears were pointed, and she wore leather armour. Eve held on to her hand for a long moment, much longer than she’d held on to anyone else’s, and called her by name.

“Freya.”

*Logged,* Tally said. *One name. One long hand hold. That’s the one they’ve staked something on.*

*On what?*

*No data.*

*But I’m writing “Freya” at the top.*

The lines began moving forward slowly, shuffling in silence. Whatever anyone might be thinking, no one said a word. One by one, the figures vanished into the grey light, swallowed without a sound. The leaders stood around, watching with an easy air, like buyers examining sheep in a pen before deciding which ones to purchase. She could feel the cold touch of being weighed and measured.

Her turn came. Maureen settled the pack onto her shoulders and stepped into the grey.

* * *

Grey light swallowed everything.

Her world spun and twisted crazily, and Maureen’s head and feet suddenly became weightless. The sensation was like being a lantern suspended from a cord, swinging madly, up over down over up. Somewhere deep inside the maelstrom that had been her body, Tally’s voice spoke clearly. *Steady. Close. Breathing. Feet. You’re here.*

The ground suddenly appeared beneath her boots. Solid. Real. Maureen swayed for a moment, caught her balance, and felt the dizziness spin away through her knees, down her legs, and dissipate.

She straightened up and looked around. She was inside.

A pale-blue line settled across the front of her eyes, Tally’s display giving her whatever information it had.

[Location: unknown.]

*So, as usual,* Maureen told it silently.

*As usual,* Tally answered in her head.

*Together.*

Her hand found the Cross Sword’s hilt on its own. Maureen stood still for a moment, listening, before she allowed herself to take a single step.
