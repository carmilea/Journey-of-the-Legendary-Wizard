Maureen woke up behind her own bedroom ward, in her own face. Her short black hair was flattened to one side of her head by the pillow, and the white velvet robe she wore was twisted around her legs. The window was frosted with delicate feathers of ice. Outside, the stable boy was whistling again. He’d settled on just three notes that he blew over and over.

Tally’s morning report was waiting at the edges of her sight. [Night: household asleep. Ward drew 0.1. Notebook: 64 spreads, read.]

*You slept through a dead man’s diary,* Tally told her smugly. *He thought very highly of himself.*

*Most diaries are like that,* Maureen replied silently as she sat up.

*Not Gregory’s apprentice’s,* Tally said, her voice softer. Maureen’s hand paused on the blanket for a heartbeat as she remembered the last page’s shaking letters and the pen forced through the paper. A breakfast tray sat outside the door where the housekeeper had left it for Wizard Richard. The porridge had cooled enough to have skin on top, and the tea was cold. Maureen picked up the tray and brought it inside. The household knew only Richard, and Richard did not sleep late. Maureen emptied the loot from Maffie’s vault onto the counterpane, sorting it. There were stone cards stacked neatly in a brick, jars of ice-school materials beading frost on the linen, a plain black lacquer mask, and a small blood-red token she’d picked up yesterday. She picked it up now, examining it. It wasn’t metal, nor was it wood. It was as hard as old bone, but it wasn’t bone. It felt cold, not the cold of something that has been sitting out in a chilly room for too long, but a cold that didn’t warm as her skin heated it.

Tally set the pulse beside a page from Maffie’s notes. Yesterday, it beat once every forty seconds. The notebook page said that the beats grew quicker on gathering nights. Today, it beat once every twelve seconds. [Guild gathering: tonight, by notebook and pulse. Confidence: medium.]

The notebook named the object. It was Maffie’s seat in the Bloodcoat Assassins’ Guild, a loose alliance of wizards operating in Emilia’s underground. He had written, with heavy underlining, that this was his place, earned through years of effort and hard work.

Reading a dead man’s hand was more intimate than Maureen liked. Tally laid out the notebook spreads over the room for her, and Maureen’s mouth flattened as she read Maffie’s boasts. He certainly had thought very highly of himself, and where he wrote of his own talents and qualities, the ink had pressed deepest into the paper. Where he’d been afraid, the pen had barely left a mark.

The Bloodcoat Assassins’ Guild, the notes explained, was more than a mere collection of cut-throats or spies. Emilia was a white-order city, but it was also one of the few great centres of wizardry in the western Passise. Like any other city of its size, it had an underground: a cellar beneath the city’s foundations and walls. Jobs were posted there in secret, from assassinations to rare materials and gemstones, from slaves no respectable merchant would deal in openly to the services of the darkest magicians. There were always plenty willing to take those jobs: wizards driven by curiosity, wizards who wanted stones, people so poor and desperate they’d risk death for a chance at riches... even mortals who knew the right door to knock on.

“Even in a white city,” Maureen said to the empty room. “But wherever there are people, there is ambition.”

*Black is black.* Tally quoted one of Maureen’s favourite lines from two years before. *But is white necessarily white?*

*Perhaps not,* Maureen answered silently. *Something this old must have backers. Perhaps it’s just the big orders from the Alliance that make it worth their while. Perhaps it’s something darker underneath.*

*Either way,* Tally agreed, *I have no data.*

Maffie had been careful to write down the guild’s rules, as he understood them. There was no fixed roster; token-holders only came to a job if they chose to. They found out what the job was and who their “partners” were when they arrived. Whoever showed up was sworn on the spot to finish the job, or be slain where they stood by the rest. One story Maffie recounted with relish involved a wizard who drew a job to raid his own order’s stores. The wizard had gone grey at the table, but had done the deed anyway because the oath bound him. Each member was also sworn, upon joining, to avenge the enemies of the guild. Unseen assessors culled the worst jobs, though Maureen suspected that these might be the strong backers of the organisation.

Tally tagged every rule with a pale grey border: Maffie’s account. One source, and a greedy one. Then she ringed one line with red.

When a member is killed, the killer takes the token and the seat. The dead are not avenged any further.

*Anyone who kills you inherits your chair,* Tally noted.

*Then it’s a chair with a price on it,* Maureen answered silently.

*Everything you own has a price on it,* Tally pointed out. *I’d just like this to be one we set.*

Maureen’s fingers kept going back to the cold token as she sorted through the cards. She knew this itch. It was Jing Sheng’s hunger, standing at the white bench with an unlabelled box in her hand. It was a sample that no one had run yet, sitting just behind her breastbone, waiting to be analysed.

Maureen made up her mind. A guild that raided stores and vaults was a road to materials and knowledge that she couldn’t purchase in daylight. Since she already knew the rules, walking into their meeting would not be walking into a trap.

“Then I’ll go and look,” she said.

* * *

Maureen had left the bedroom wearing Richard’s plain face and a hood drawn up. She visited the mortal quarter and purchased a new robe for two gold coins from the Olivia chest. [Gold: −2.] The black wool garment was bought from a second-hand stall. Her Dark Forest robe stayed folded in the pouch.

The new robe scratched at her wrists. It smelled of a stranger’s woodsmoke and sweat, and of cheap dyes. The smell reminded her of the black robe that Emily’s knights had given her at fourteen, but she shrugged it on anyway.

In the tall mirror, whose corners were spotted with age, Tally laid the pale-blue muscle map of the Mimicry spell over Maureen’s reflection. One new face. One throat pulled down a little lower, roughened a bit more than Richard’s.

*Not Richard’s voice either,* Tally warned. *You’re going to need something different. Not Jack’s rasp. He’s dead in at least three files, and I’d like him to keep it that way.*

Maureen nodded and cast the spell. Ants crawled beneath her skin, accompanied by the familiar dull ache. Her cheekbones widened and her jaw thickened. The lines at the corners of her mouth deepened and pulled down. [Mana: 16 → 15.6.]

A stranger stared back at her from the spotted glass. The left side of her face settled into place half a heartbeat later than the right, exactly as Jack’s had during the trial. She automatically pressed two fingers against her jawline, making the same small motion that she had used before.

“These are the rules between us,” Maureen said out loud to the empty room, testing her new voice with a full sentence. Her pitch rose partway through.

*Lower,* Tally ordered. *You’re drifting back up to Richard’s timbre.*

Maureen forced the pitch down and tried again. “These are the rules between us.”

The words rolled out of her mouth like a cartwheel grinding across gravel.

The new voice sounded wrong to her own ears. She was listening to a man speak, a man that she had never met, coming from inside her chest. She didn’t like how much she liked the sound.

They established the rules between them. *No Purgatory Fire,* Tally ordered. *That’s Richard’s now. Anyone who has seen him will know you on sight. And no Freya’s sword either. Dark elves have long memories for their own steel.*

*Darkness and fire, Rank Zero,* Maureen replied silently. *And the new wind, once it’s ready.*

Tally had one rule of her own. *You keep your eyes black, Maureen Olsen. The crimson shows up when you get angry.*

*Then I won’t get angry,* Maureen told her.

*Disputed,* Tally replied.

Maureen unbuckled the slender Cross Sword from her waist and let it sink back into the Void Dimensional Pouch. Night Owl would not carry a blade anyone would recognise.

She picked up the mask from Maffie’s vault. It was plain black lacquer that would cover her from brow to chin, and secured with thin black silk cords. She set it over the stranger’s face and drew the cords snugly behind her cropped hair.

The lacquer was cold on her skin, with just the barest hint of an old varnish smell, and a whiff of frost that was Maffie’s alone. The mask cut off the edges of her vision, leaving only two narrow lines for her to look out of. The mask sat on her face as though it were meant to have always been there.

Tally checked the eyeholes in the tall mirror before declaring success. *Black. Steady black. Nothing showing through. I’ll be watching those all night anyway.*

“Owl,” Maureen said aloud, testing the sound with the new rough voice.

*Nocturnal. Provisionally,* Tally replied. *Owls keep better hours. And that owl from the hidden market had a much better hat than you do. Red bow tie and everything.*

“Night Owl,” Maureen said to the reflection in the mirror.

*Logged under protest,* Tally said. *Lovingly.*

Something that sounded like a laugh caught in the stranger’s throat, coming out instead as a short grunt. For the first time since she’d picked up the token that morning, the muscles in her shoulders relaxed.

* * *

Before she left, Tally had run the house through the floor and read it as density. Five mortal lights, all of them low and slow with sleep. She timed the stable boy’s breathing over the stalls as eight breaths a minute. Deep breaths.

*Out cold,* Tally said.

She flagged the gold knot of the Rental Centre’s master ward on the front gate, but didn’t have any data on whether it recorded who went out, when, or in what face.

So, instead of going out the front door, Maureen jumped over the garden wall behind the yews. The knight’s legs took her high enough to get her feet onto the stone coping without a second leap, but before she dropped down she made sure the blood-red token at her waist was visible outside the robe. The frost on the coping bit her fingers through her gloves as she let go, dropping into the back lane beyond with a soft crunch. Behind her the wizards’ tract hummed silently, filling the lanes with the light of its wards.

Tally drew a blue ribbon across the lanes for Maureen to follow, drawing the line from the floating map of the Rental Centre. It snaked down from the wizards’ tract into the mortal quarter where the notebook claimed the tavern would be found.

The stink of boiled cabbage, wet dog, and sewage from the open gutter hit Maureen’s nose before she reached the first turn. The smell of tallow smoke rolled out between the shutters of every building. This was Skran’s slums, albeit with better paving underfoot. Her stride shortened without being asked.

Tally kept count of the turns in her head the whole way, as she had done with the corridors of the Dark Forest. On the last corner, she announced seventeen with a small flourish.

The tavern squatted in the night like a drunk leaning against its neighbour for support. The wooden sign above the door advertised its name in flaking paint, and a single tallow lamp burned over the door. Maureen pushed on the door and the hinges gave a long, drawn-out squeal of protest.

The inside smelled of sour beer and old pipe smoke. The floor was covered with sawdust, darkened to black by spills and tread. All the chairs were up on the tables already, their legs sticking up in the air like dead beetles. An old man stood behind the bar, his white hair standing on end in more directions than Maureen had ever seen hair stand without the aid of pomade. Without looking up he said, “We’re closed. Go home.”

Then his eyes slid down to the blood token hanging at her waist, and the single lamp caught the tip of his red nose. He let out a long breath through his teeth. “Another one.”

*Datum,* Tally said. *The token changes hands often enough that he has a word for it.*

Still not looking at her face, he jerked his chin towards a panel set into the wall behind him. It swung open to reveal cold stone steps leading down. Cold air wafted up the stairs, carrying the scent of damp stone and old candle wax. The old man’s slippers scuffed on the stones ahead of Maureen, and the noises from the tavern above faded away behind her, like a lid being placed on a box.

* * *

The room beneath the tavern had a low ceiling; the lamp’s smoke spread out flat against it before it rose higher. Five masked figures sat around a long black table, their hands resting on its black marble surface. There was a silence in the room, but it was not a comfortable silence. There was an edge to it, the sensation that everyone was holding their breath.

The man in the bat mask lifted his head first. It happened with quick birdlike jerks, one after another, and he was singing a sing-song tune. “Oh, oh. Now isn’t this interesting? A stranger.”

All the masks turned to look at her, every one of them. Even the keeper’s eyes came round to her from the foot of the stairs. She felt their attention, a physical weight landing on her like pairs of hands. Her pulse jumped once against the cords of the mask.

Tally was already there. *Pulse down two. Breathe out longer.*

Maureen forced herself into the Silver Light cadence, three long breaths followed by one short. Her heart obeyed, and she felt its pace slow to match the rhythm.

No one had invited Maureen to sit, so she did it herself, pulling out a chair from the long black table and tossing the blood token onto its marble surface. It landed with a small, hard click.

*Black marble veined with white,* Tally noted. *Same as Doug’s table in the Panso garrison. Cold enough to feel the ache through the palms. Last time we sat at one of these, everyone at it died.*

Maureen leaned back in the chair, using the rough low voice. “I’m here, so I’m the new member. You got a problem with that?” No one answered. “You’ve had plenty walk in on you like me before, haven’t you?”

The masks turned to look at each other. Then the one with the nightingale mask spoke, her voice disguised by some charm to flatten it to an emotionless monotone. “Whoever carried that last won’t be coming back for it.”

None of the others argued the point. The rule Maffie wrote down was the actual rule. Tally began running through them one by one, tagging them with their density and comparing them to her files. The black bear mask: Official, not elementalised, close to the Soros sample.

*By comparison,* Tally added. *Always by comparison.*

The bat mask read denser than Soros. Tally filed his posture with care: weight forward on the balls of his feet, shoulders set as if expecting a robe with a high collar to drop over them. She said nothing more about him. Maureen noticed the omission.

*Nightingale is a woman,* Tally said, *judging by her breathing pattern. Her charm disguises pitch, but not lung power. The rest read thinner, apprentices and one more Official at the far end.*

Maureen read their hands, the only part of themselves they’d forgotten to hide. A thumb bearing rings rubbed against the marble, a gloved hand rested on the table, a boot tapped beneath it at the rate of a nervous heart.

At last the man in the black-bear mask spoke. His voice was deep enough that he didn’t have to project effort to fill the room. “Want in? Fine. Let’s see what you’re worth first. I don’t work jobs next to a weakling. If you’ve got nothing...”

He let the threat hang, unfinished. Beneath the snout of the mask, his lips curled into a small cold smile. The sentence sat between them on the table, heavier than any completed threat.

*What now?* Maureen asked silently.

*With your consent,* Tally answered, *I can route the release through the Darklight Orb and keep back the Pankora component. They will feel raw weight, but none of your signature. Give me the rhythm.*

*Pulse down two.*

*Let it out on the exhale.*

Maureen breathed deeply, then exhaled as she released her aura. The flame of the oil lamp bent flat, as if someone had thrown open a door, and a weight descended on the room. Every shoulder pressed downward toward the marble.

They all shuddered, as one. The ringed thumb ceased its rubbing. A chair scraped against the stone floor. The bat mask’s drumming fingers went still atop the table, and the old tavern keeper took a step back up the stairs without seeming to notice he’d moved.

*Denser than anyone in here,* Tally said, placing Maureen with care. *Above Barok’s 14.8, the Phantom-State sample I have, by a clear margin. A little stronger than a regular Phantom-State wizard. Which is how you killed Maffie.*

*You’re smiling under the mask,* Tally said.

*Nobody can see it,* Maureen answered silently.

*I can.*

Maureen drew the weight back into herself, slowly. As she did so, the flame of the oil lamp regained its upright shape.

The man in the black-bear mask exhaled a long breath through his nose. “Good. You’ll do.” He nodded once to the member wearing the nightingale mask. “Now the oath.”

A scroll was unrolled across the glossy length of the black marble table. It had only a few sentences. Respect the secrets of the guild. Avenge its enemies. Complete any task that you agree to undertake. It wasn’t a list of chains, but of principles. In front of each mask, Maureen repeated the oath.

Tally’s lens kept track of the structure as the words were spoken. Simple terms. A revenge clause. The binding sat on top of them in the same way the other two bindings did. [Bindings: 3.]

A thread of light rose from her brow, and the scroll accepted it. It began to burn away, disintegrating into ash. A new cold descended around Maureen’s throat, joining the collar of the first oath and the brand of the vault. For a moment her throat tightened, as if an invisible hand had closed there.

*Contracts have ways around contracts,* Tally said. *Don’t lean on it too much.*

*I’m not leaning on it,* Maureen told her silently. *I’m standing next to it.*

The man in the black-bear mask looked her over. “You’re one of us now. We don’t use names down here. Pick something for us to call you.”

Maureen paused. Tally had grown very quiet in her head, which usually meant that Tally was trying very hard not to say something. Beneath the lacquer, the corner of the borrowed mouth lifted in a smile.

“Owl,” she said, letting her gaze move slowly from mask to mask. “My name here is Night Owl.”
