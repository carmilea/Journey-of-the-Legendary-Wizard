The main street leading away from Umas’s mansion crunched beneath steel-shod hooves, and torches threw trails of pine-resin smoke across the street.

Shutters slammed shut along both sides of the street as the column passed, one after another, their wooden clatter sounding like the crack of thunder. Tally was counting the shutters in Maureen’s head, the count rolling past thirty. The Second-Class Wizard Apprentice and Upper Knight sat perfectly still in the saddle of one of Umas’s black warhorses, borrowed from his own stables, clad in simple leather armour.

[Knights: 40. Foot: ~300. Archers: 60.] Tally displayed for her.

This town is scared of tonight, Maureen said silently to Tally.

It should be scared of you, Tally agreed.

Maureen turned slightly in the saddle so she could address her question to Umas, who rode beside her. “Marquis Umas, how much farther?” she asked aloud.

“Feren’s stronghold lies ahead,” Umas replied, his voice loud enough to carry over the clopping hoofbeats and the creaking of leather. “We will take him by surprise this night. It will be far more effective than waiting for him and his Mystic to attack us.”

He raised a hand to wipe away the sweat that was beading on his brow despite the frost in the air. His excitement was plainly evident on his face.

Sweating in freezing air, Tally noted.

His nerves make me calmer, Maureen said silently. I think I’m the calmest thing in this entire column right now.

They continued to ride for hours, out through the black fields, the cold biting into Maureen’s cheeks like a knife’s edge. Beneath her thighs, she could feel the heat rising from the huge warhorse’s body, radiating through her leggings and up under her knees.

* * *

Then the castle was suddenly ahead of them and the cry of sentries sounded from the wall. The alarm was given and confusion reigned behind the walls. Tally highlighted the gate and then the firing arcs of the archers on the wall. [Wall: ~8 m] it displayed over her sight, measured from the torches mounted by the gate.

Marquis Umas drew his broadsword high and let out a battlecry. His own men’s bowstrings twanged as arrow after arrow sought their target amongst Feren’s men. Arrows began to hiss towards the castle, the torchlights illuminating their feathered shafts as they flew. The defenders’ screams rent the air as shafts buried themselves in flesh, nailing men to the ground.

The castle quickly regained its order and began fighting back. Both sides started to take losses.

A young man dressed in blue silk appeared at the gates, smiling as he raised his hands. Small wind blades burst from his fingers, slicing down the men charging the gates. Umas’s soldiers fell back, cowed by the power of the Mystic.

[Interval: 1.4 s] Tally noted of the young man’s attacks. Cheap and fast, it said.

It was right, Maureen thought silently. She knew who this had to be. “Kane,” she said softly.

This was Feren’s Mystic, the Second-Class Wizard Apprentice that the four Mystics had spoken about. She had never seen him before, but there could be no doubt.

Maureen lashed the reins and the warhorse surged forward directly at Kane. The beast’s hooves pounded the ground, sending clots of frozen mud flying up around them. The cold wind tore at her hood, trying to rip it from her head. The torchlights ran along the length of her raised blade as she charged towards Feren’s Mystic. As Kane noticed the horse and rider charging him, Maureen launched herself out of the saddle, Cross Sword held high above her head. She chopped downward at him in a single fluid motion.

“Asking to die…” Kane sneered.

A huge pale-cyan blade of wind shot out from his palm, rushing straight for her chest. It was the size of a door.

Her pulse jumped once in her throat, hard.

>He’s drawing. Big.

>Chest.

>Seal. Now.

Maureen spoke the command as she was still in mid-air. “Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa.” A flash of red crossed over her chest, just as the wind blade screeched against what felt like thorns. The blade of wind began to fray apart, losing cohesion under the force of the Seal. [Seal: 1.6 s] Tally noted before automatically calling for the release of the armour. The armour withdrew before her boots even touched the ground. Her sword swung down unhindered, and Kane went flying backwards like a flung stone. He crashed into the side of the castle and coughed up blood.

Dust and chips of stone sprayed away from where he hit. Behind Maureen, Umas and his soldiers would only have seen a flash of red in the firelight. As Kane staggered back to his feet, Maureen spoke again. “Did you think I’d just charge in here like a fool? That would be ridiculous. Kane, I know who you are. I’m Maureen.”

[Seal: glow dimmer. Reserve: unknown, lower.]

[Coating: worn after the strike.]

Six degrees at least. That wasn’t cheap, Tally noted.

I know, she replied silently. It only looks easy.

* * *

The tall, handsome, young man at the gate was staring at her in disbelief. Maureen tilted her head and asked, “Feren? Viscount Feren?”

“Yes, I am,” Feren said. “Come with me, and we’ll overthrow Umas. You can have everything you ever wanted. I only rebelled for the people! He’s a demon! He’s done so many evil deeds-”

Umas: demon, many evil deeds, Tally added as an unverified claim. Maureen was already chanting.

“Simons-Sado!” she exclaimed.

A deep red sphere of crackling fire roared from her hand. The Blast Fireball slammed into Feren, blowing him apart. Pieces of meat and bone and flesh flew out in a circle, and they were burnt into blackened ash before they hit the ground. Tally helpfully displayed, [Mana: 6.5 → 5.4]. Black flakes fluttered down onto the snow, where they hissed.

The air now stank of burnt hair and fat. Maureen whirled on Kane. “Amra-Keis!” she chanted, and a sour green missile burst on the Mystic. The Acid Missile caught him right in the chest, and he staggered backwards while screaming. “Amra-Keis!” Maureen repeated, and another missile exploded against Kane.

Green acid sizzled on the stones of the gate, and a foul reek filled the air mingled with the smoke from the torches.

[Mana: 4.6], Tally told her, showing her the cast windows. Hold one, Tally told her, so Maureen restrained her Hand.

Suddenly, Kane bolted towards the forest. He was moving twice as fast as before.

[Berserk Blood: probable. Window: ~3 min. Crash after.] Tally told her, showing her the veins standing out on the back of Kane’s neck, how red and flushed it was. His gait looked broken and uneven. Mine are better, Maureen thought silently, and Tally echoed back, Yours are better.

“Did you think I wouldn’t be ready when you did that?” Maureen asked, calling the Hand of Negative Energy. A shadowy hand erupted from the darkness and gripped Kane’s calf. It ate away most of his leg before vanishing. [Mana: 3.5] Tally said. Grey-green smoke rose from the blackened wound, the stink of rotten eggs filling the air, and Kane howled in pain. It didn’t stop him from limping into the trees, though.

* * *

“Kane’s mine,” Maureen said, turning to face Umas, who looked stunned at the destruction she’d wrought. “The battle’s yours. Feren’s dead, and Kane is crippled. Without leadership, they’ll be easy for you to clean up.”

The black pines pressed around her, the crust of snow cracking under her boots as she moved. Branches whipped her face as she passed, but she ignored them, running light and fast along the trail that Kane had left.

Tally showed her the roots that threatened to trip her up, the dark blood spots that stained the white snow. The air carried the metallic tang of Kane’s blood, and the bitter smell of the potion’s sweat.

Hear that? Tally said, and Maureen could hear it too. Stop at the treeline. There. Maureen skidded to a halt, standing in the shadow of a pine tree.

“Patton! Patton!” Kane shouted, his voice cracking with panic as he reached the mouth of a cave.

A handsome youth dressed in moon-white silks came out, frowning. “Kane! What are you doing here?” he demanded. “It isn’t time yet! Feren sent you to help him on the surface so he would send us live humans to use, not . . .”

Tally filed away the name and the motive, live humans.

There’s one more of them, Maureen thought.

“Please save me!” Kane gasped, stumbling towards the youth. “Someone’s going to kill me!”

> Window. Now.

Tally flashed.

Maureen threw the Cross Sword. It crossed the distance between them in an eyeblink, pinning Kane to the rough bark of the tree trunk. Kane twitched once, then fell silent.

The tree shuddered, dislodging some of the snow that coated its branches, and the cold white flakes dropped onto Kane’s body.

 Then she turned to face the grimly frowning youth.

“You’re Kane’s accomplice, aren’t you?” she said. “I didn’t expect there to be one more of you.”
