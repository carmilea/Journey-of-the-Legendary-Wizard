Eleven black robes stood across her path, arranged in an irregular crescent. The oil lamps hissed and danced, and the light shone on gold thread at the cuffs of the Olivia robes, picked out Wolf’s ruined mouth and Jay’s healed face pinched with hate. Maureen’s smile never faltered as her hand rested lightly upon the chill metal of the Cross Sword’s grip.

Maureen tilted her head fractionally to one side. It was so clear now. The contract hadn’t held. Jay had signed it with a brand of light from his forehead, and she had seen it burn away, orange-red. But he had told them everything anyway.

*He used his own family’s scroll,* Tally said without emotion. *Of course they’d know how to get around it.*

*I should have realised,* Maureen answered silently.

*So should I,* Tally responded. *Let’s both remember this for next time.*

A cold drop of self-reproach slid down inside her, and she worked her jaw once before her expression returned to its flat calmness. None of the Olivia group had spoken yet. Frey Olivia was regarding her with an air of appraisal, much as a buyer would regard a horse at market. His smile never moved, but his eyes were appraising.

Jay cracked first. “Don’t get above yourself, Maureen!” he shouted, his voice shrill with anger. A muscle in his neck twitched, and his chest was heaving beneath the fine silks that dressed him. “My brother is grade eight, high aptitude, top of the Third-Class. He’s nearly a full Wizard! He’s the real genius of this place, and you dare—”

Frey Olivia raised two fingers without even bothering to look round. “That’s enough, Jay.”

Jay’s mouth snapped shut so quickly that his teeth clicked loudly together, the sound echoing about the hall. The Olivia followers stood several paces behind Frey. They were all young men, and all wore black robes. Several of them had hands hovering near their sword hilts. Tally was analysing the density she could feel radiating from Frey, setting it against two years’ worth of samples, each labelled with a name and a grade. [Gandolf.] [the shopkeeper.] [Winnie.] [Mace.] [Wolf.] [Jay.] [Maureen Olsen.] It put a line before the new readout:

[Frey Olivia. Third-Class. Top of the column. Above Gandolf.]

*Well, well,* Tally said. *First time we’ve ever agreed with Jay.*

*Don’t make a habit of it,* Maureen told it silently.

A jolt of surprise went through her at the power she was facing. This wasn’t like Jay. This was real power, close to a full Wizard’s might, and he was standing over her looking down. Maureen had never reacted well to being looked down on. Her spine straightened automatically, her shoulders dropped and her breathing slowed.

“So,” she asked in a voice as light as if inquiring about the time, “you want to do this here?”

Frey laughed softly at her question, a pleasant sound that echoed slightly. “Fight? No. Hardly. That is forbidden inside the Dark Forest, for apprentices as well as full Wizards. No, I won’t lift a hand against you.”

[Rule noted.]

And Tally added in its warm voice, *That’s also the only reason he’s bothering to talk to you instead of just doing something about you.* Its display helpfully gave her the number of hands that Frey’s followers had near their sword hilts: [Hands on hilts: 6].

“Since you won’t be fighting,” Maureen said, letting her eyes wander along the entire crescent, “get out of my way.”

Wolf’s face darkened until the gap in his teeth looked like a hole in a dark red mask. Jay made a strangled noise in the back of his throat. For half a breath, Frey’s smile faded, revealing the look of genuine surprise beneath it, the expression of a man who had never been told to move before. Frey’s voice grew cooler by degrees. “You really aren’t like the other apprentices. I’ve wondered about it for a long time. What sort of person would dare discipline Jay and spit in the Olivia Family’s face? Now, I see.”

Maureen smiled pleasantly back at him. “Do you?”

Frey’s smile deepened, turning colder but remaining mild. “I can’t touch you inside these walls, but no one offends the Olivias and walks away whole. Things will come to you, one after another. Think about the days ahead, Maureen. I expect you’ll find them hard to forget. Won’t they? Hm?”

Maureen shrugged. It wasn’t as if she was going to be in the Dark Forest anymore. Whatever they planned inside these walls would find an empty room.

A gentle voice spoke from behind her. “It seems that something interesting is happening here.” Every head turned, and Mace was approaching them with an unhurried step. His curly golden hair glowed brilliantly under the lamplight.

“Mace,” she murmured, dipping her head slightly in a junior’s obeisance.

“Maureen,” he said, nodding, then turned his attention on Frey. “Frey. It’s been a long time.”

“Mace,” Frey repeated, giving each syllable a stone’s weight of emphasis.

The crescent went silent. Everyone knew who and what he was, a grade seven apprentice, high aptitude, outstanding among the Third-Class, and an apothecary whose talents some Wizards claimed were equal to or surpassed their own. That kind of power, in a mere apprentice, was frightening.

“You’re blocking Maureen’s way, Frey,” Mace said lightly. “That isn’t nice.”

Frey smiled. “I doubt this is any of your business. And the Osiros wouldn’t begin a feud with the Olivias on a whim, would they?”

Mace raised a hand to straighten his collar with two fingers, dropping his voice half a tone and adding a chill to his normally gentle words. “This is my will, not my family’s. You know, Frey, you’re being very rude.”

Tally registered every piece of data. The collar adjustment, the half-tone drop, the cool undercurrent that flowed beneath the gentle surface. The followers’ hands left their sword hilts, [Hands on hilts: 0], and Tally told her that Frey’s eyes were on Mace’s hands, not his face.

Frey regarded him without expression for a long time. Finally he smiled. “What could I do here, after all? Don’t worry, Maureen. And Mace, lecturing me on manners? You always were a strange one.”

Mace only smiled.

“We go,” Frey said, turning back. His last glance at her was calm on top, frigid underneath.

Eleven pairs of boots disappeared down the black-stone vault, leaving only the hiss of the oil lamps behind. Maureen found her fingers still curled around the hilt of her Cross Sword and she uncurled them, one by one.

* * *

“Thank you, Mace,” she said quietly.

He waved a dismissive hand. “I’m going to Mentor’s lab as well. We might as well walk together.”

Their footsteps fell out of step on the black stone. Her steps were lighter than his, and while she kept pace with him he made no effort to hurry. One by one the lights sprang to life ahead of them. The lamplight moved over the gentle curls of his golden hair, and slid down the back of his plain robe.

*This is the Mace I know,* Maureen thought. The Mace who would bend over a beaker of bubbling chemicals at the far end of the racks, never stopping, never hurrying. The Mace who would patiently answer her questions and Lilith’s, and who would smile that slow, beautiful smile when he saw something interesting. This is the Mace whose voice was always gentle, and whose presence was so easy to overlook that she sometimes forgot he was in the room.

But there was something coming off this Mace, something like the pressure before a storm. Something like the feeling of standing next to someone who is asleep, yet knowing that in a few seconds their eyes will open. The fine hairs on her forearms rose up beneath her sleeves.

*Scan him, Tally,* Maureen thought, not turning her head.

[Unable to scan.]

Maureen missed a step and her boot scuffed loud against the black-stone floor. Her face didn’t change. She continued walking.

Tally did not speak. For a long moment there was nothing, then its voice came back, shaken. *Two years of data on Mace. He was one of my labelled samples. None of this fits with the man who walks next to you,* it said.

*Then what is he?*

*No data,* Tally replied. The words sounded strange coming from it. It opened a new file without being asked. Mace, unscannable.

Maureen watched his back. He walked as he always did, the hem of his robe brushing against the flagstones. It was that sameness which made her skin prickle.

He stopped at a door and turned with the same warm eyes he always used. “You wanted to see Mentor, didn’t you? Here we are.”

She nodded and stepped past him into the lab. Whatever he was, that was something she didn’t need to answer today.

*Later,* Maureen said silently.

*You always say that,* Tally answered, its voice more subdued than usual.

* * *

The laboratory smelled as it always did, a sharpness that could bite at the back of your nose. The burner ticked, the test tube in its flame dancing a little, shivering in the heatless black flames. The single high window let in grey light that lay across the long stone bench and the polished brass racks.

Adrian set aside a book whose cover was crawling with vines. He set it down carefully and the moment it left his hands thin, dark green tendrils crept out from beneath the binding and wrapped around the book, grasping it tightly before it could be opened. In seconds it was pulled back into Adrian’s desk drawer, which shut itself.

*Books that eat,* Tally logged. *Entry two, cross-filed with the fanged mouth in his study.*

*That one is Mentor’s,* Maureen answered silently, *not ours to file.*

*Too late,* Tally replied, logging the information anyway.

Maureen bowed her head. “Mentor. I’d like to leave the Dark Forest for a while. To travel.”

Adrian rose slowly, his glasses still perched on his nose. He peered at her over the top of the frames. “Travel? Because of the Olivias?”

*Adrian has picked your reason for you, Maureen. Will you set him straight?* Tally’s voice asked in her head.

*No, let him think it,* she answered silently.

*Logged: a convenient lie, kindly meant,* Tally noted.

She bowed her head again, letting her silence reply for her. There was a small prick under her ribs as she did so. Adrian was being kind, and she was letting his kindness carry a story that wasn’t true.

“I see.” Adrian pushed his glasses up his nose. “I know all of it. Anyone who cares to know does; something like that cannot be hidden. Very well. You may go.”

The breath went out of her slowly, slower than she intended. “Thank you, Mentor.”

“And where will you go?” Adrian asked.

Maureen opened her mouth, but nothing came out. She had decided to leave, but not once had she thought about where she would go.

Adrian gave a dry laugh and shook his head. “Olsen, honestly. Then do something for me on the way. I need someone in the Principality of Redbud. I’ll pay you, of course.”

She paused, thinking about it. “Very well,” she finally said.

Maureen bowed to take her leave. Before she’d managed to turn completely around, Adrian’s brows lifted in response to something she couldn’t sense.

“Stay, Olsen,” he said, raising a single finger toward her without even glancing in her direction.

Maureen stepped back, placing herself behind the racks of glassware on the long stone bench. It put her out of the direct line from Adrian’s desk and out of sight of whatever was about to arrive. Dark particles began to form in the air in front of Adrian. They poured together and knitted themselves into a translucent image wrapped in flame, a human male with a stern, middle-aged face and a heavy brow that commanded attention.

A cellar chill radiated from the image, despite the flames that enveloped him. Maureen could see how the projection warped as it passed through a row of empty flasks. The ticking of the burner was suddenly the loudest sound in the room.

“Barok,” Adrian said, glancing briefly at the projection before turning his attention to some papers on his desk. “What do you want?”

“I hardly need tell you, Wizard Adrian,” the projection answered, his voice accustomed to obedience.

“That madman Evans would do anything to advance!” Barok burst out. “He killed Ciri, and now he’s torn Eric of Hader Castle apart alive for a single tube of Azure Potion!” Beneath the projection’s anger burned something even more primal, something that looked like hunger, or perhaps envy.

Tally dutifully recorded each name. [Evans killed Ciri (on file). Eric, Hader Castle. Azure Potion: no data. Second-Tier: no data.] Tally’s own entry on Eric read [Eric: Blue Ring], based on what Scarlette had told her on the road. *Take Barok’s word for it,* she told Tally silently. *For now, anyway.*

“If you had one thread of hope of reaching Second-Tier,” Adrian said conversationally, “you’d be madder than Evans.”

The mouth of the projected man worked, but nothing came out.

Tick. Tick. In the space between the ticks of the burner, Maureen saw again Ciri’s head, held up by his hair over a grey sea. The tips of her fingers grew cold where they rested against the stone bench.

Barok made another attempt. “Hader Castle and the Blue Ring are declaring war on the Dark Forest again. This time, the Mist Labyrinth has joined with them.”

“And likely the subterranean races as well,” Adrian added in a long, even voice. “Hell Fiends, dark elves, and other assorted creatures who rarely bother to surface. Whether that is at the behest of this coalition or not is unclear.” Adrian glanced at the image. “But of course, the White Bone Tower will stand with the Dark Forest. That goes without saying.”

The flames surrounding Barok settled down, their orange flickering subsiding into a steady yellow.

[Coalition: Blue Ring, Hader Castle, Mist Labyrinth. Underground races: Hell Fiends, dark elves, probable. White Bone Tower: with the Dark Forest.]

[Outpost raids: hearsay > probable]

Then Barok spoke again, this time almost offhandedly. “Wizard Adrian, I understand you have an apprentice by the name of Maureen. She has offended the Olivia Family. Please hand her over. We will pay, naturally. She’s just an ordinary apprentice, after all, and we are a wizard family. You must see how inconvenient this is for us. Do think it over.”

[Ordinary apprentice]

The words appeared on Maureen’s display, tagged by Tally’s colder register.

*Your pulse jumped,* Tally commented quietly. *I hear it loud in your ears. Just as you do.*

Maureen did not move. She stood very still behind the racks of glassware on the long stone bench. She held her breath, waiting to be weighed and measured, appraised like a bag of rations on the deck of the ship.

Adrian’s face suddenly went cold. “Bargaining with me? You don’t have the qualifications to deal with me, Barok. Perhaps you should send Philip instead.”

“No, Wizard Adrian, I didn’t mean—”

Light flickered across Adrian’s glasses and streams of fine golden flame erupted from their lenses. The particles that made up the image broke apart, and Barok disappeared in mid-sentence. Half his last word was still hanging in the air when he was gone. A few grey flakes drifted down onto the polished brass of the benchtop. They were weightless, smelling faintly of nothing Maureen recognized. The burner continued its steady ticking.

[Philip: no data. Senior Olivia, by inference.]

*He said no.*

*I heard,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*I know. I’m putting it in a column of its own.*

Adrian reached up to push his glasses back into place. He stared directly at her through the racks of glassware.

“I am not in the habit of selling my own apprentices in order to trade resources with other wizards.” There was a long pause before he continued. “Come back tomorrow. I have things to prepare.”

Maureen bowed her head in assent, unwilling or unable to trust her voice. Her face gave away nothing.

* * *

The heavy door closed behind her and she looked down the corridor. Empty. Mace gone.

Her throat stayed tight all the way back through the maze of identical doors. Grandpa John had loved her. Uncle Andrew loved her too. But they had never been able to protect her either. Adrian was the first person with any power who’d ever stood between her and a buyer.

Tally counted out the turns in a low voice, telling her which way to go to get back to her quarters. Just for company. Maureen allowed herself that much. When they reached her door the dull brass key missed the lock once, but went in smoothly on the second attempt.

*Is there anything else in this new column?* she asked silently as she pushed open the door.

*Not yet,* Tally answered. *It’s a new column.*
