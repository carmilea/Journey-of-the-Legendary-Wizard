The passage was filled with the group of black-clad men. The lamplight picked out the fine gold thread embroidered into the cuffs of their robes.

Maureen Olsen understood now why the confidentiality contract had been so easily circumvented. Wizard families had ways. She would have to remember that.

[It's his family's scroll. Of course they'd know how to get around it,]

I should have seen that, she told herself.

So should I, Tally said.

But we both will now.

Jay Olivia was still shaking with anger and humiliation. "This is Frey," he spat, "he is grade 8. High aptitude. He is one of the most talented Third-Class Apprentices in the Dark Forest. Almost an Official Wizard. He's the genius of the family, not me." He gave a bitter laugh. "This is the one you should have worried about."

Frey Olivia held up his hand and Jay fell silent. His chest still heaved with anger. Frey turned to study Maureen. His expression was mild, almost smiling, but there was something in his eyes. They were appraising her, like a buyer at a stall trying to decide whether the price was worth the object. Maureen's jaw clenched once, then her spine straightened.

[Frey Olivia. Third-Class. Top of the column. Above Gandolf,]

Tally said, measuring her particle sense against two years' worth of labelled samples.

[Well, well, well. That is the first time I ever agreed with Jay.]

Don't make a habit of it, she told it silently.

She was not afraid of this man, no matter how talented he was. "So, Frey," she said flatly. "You want to fight here?"

Frey laughed. "Hardly, Maureen Olsen. It is forbidden to fight within the Dark Forest, apprentices and Official Wizards alike. No, I will not be fighting you here."

[Another rule,] Tally said. [That is also why he is talking instead of doing.]

"Then get out of my way."

Wolf and Jay flushed a deep red, while a flash of open surprise crossed Frey's face.

[Very bold,] Tally commented. [Hands on hilts: 6.]

Tell me if that changes.

"Now I understand who dared to discipline my little brother," Frey said, his voice starting to cool. "No one offends the Olivia Family and walks away unharmed. Think about your days to come, Maureen."

Maureen just shrugged. She had already planned on leaving the Dark Forest, and their plans would find her gone.

An unexpected sound came from behind her, a gentle voice. "Something interesting seems to be happening here."

Every head turned, and the air of the hall shifted.

Mace walked up to join her. "Senior Apprentice," Maureen greeted him.

He nodded once to her before turning back to address Frey. "It has been a long time, hasn't it, Frey."

Frey's expression became grave as he regarded the newcomer. His followers also fell silent, their eyes going wide as they took in Mace's presence. He was grade 7, an outstanding Third-Class. As an apothecary, he was rumoured among the Wizards to be the equal of some of them.

"It is not nice to block her way," Mace noted.

"The Osiro Family wouldn't want to risk a feud with the Olivias." Frey smiled blandly.

Mace paused to straighten the collar of his robes. "This is not my family's will, but my own." Then he shook his head. "You are being very rude, Frey Olivia."

Tally noted that Mace's voice had dropped half a tone and taken on a cool edge. The hands of Frey's followers had jerked away from their sword hilts while Frey watched Mace's hands, not his face. Mace's smile never lost its pleasantness, not even during the long silence while Frey stood studying him.

"Well, you cannot do anything here anyway," Frey finally said. "Do not worry about me, Maureen Olsen." Then he turned back to Mace. "It is a strange man who feels the need to lecture another on manners."

Frey turned and walked away with his men, casting one last icy look at Maureen.

* * *

After a few more words, Mace indicated for her to follow him towards Adrian's lab. He walked a half step ahead of her as they went down the black stone corridor. Identical doors slid by on either side. Lamplight flickered over Mace's curly golden hair. Their footsteps echoed in the hallway, almost keeping time with each other.

This was the Mace she knew. His quiet voice and gentle mannerisms made him easy to overlook. When not working on an experiment himself, he could usually be found assisting someone else in theirs. He always seemed happy to answer any questions either she or Lilith had and he was never patronizing.

Yet now she felt something like pressure radiating from him, the feeling that comes just before a sleeper opens his eyes. Maureen felt the hair rise on her arms.

Tally, scan him.

[Unable to scan.]

What? Why?

There was a long silence before Tally responded. I have two years of Mace stored in labelled samples. None of it matches the man walking next to you.

Maureen missed a step, caught herself and continued down the hallway as if nothing had happened. Her face remained impassive while she considered Tally's words. What is he then?

No data, replied Tally. A file opened up in her mind. Mace: unscannable.

"You wanted to see our Mentor," Mace said gently as they approached Adrian's lab. "And here we are."

Maureen nodded and put the question aside for the moment.

* * *

The burner's ticking was the only sound. The polished brass shone with reflected light. Glassware sat in racks along the long stone bench beneath the single high grey window. The sharp chemical smell in the air assailed her nose as soon as she stepped through the doorway.

Adrian looked up from the book he had been reading and set it aside. The vines covering the front of the book twined outwards as he moved, dark green tendrils snaking out, wrapping around it and dragging it back into the desk drawer. Tally filed that one under the fanged mouth in the study. Books that eat, entry two. Stop collecting monsters, she thought silently to Tally.

"Mentor," she said, bowing her head, "I wish to leave the Dark Forest for a time. To travel."

Adrian rose from his chair and adjusted his glasses. His calm, flat voice sounded out. "Is it the Olivia Family?" He paused for a moment before continuing. "Anyone who cares to know already knows."

Assumption noted, Maureen. Tally mused silently. Does that mean you intend to correct him on your reasons?

No. Let him think it.

Very well. Logged a convenient lie, kindly meant.

Maureen nodded her head as if agreeing with what her Mentor had said.

A warm prickle of guilt crept up the back of her neck. "Of course, Olsen. Where will you be travelling to?"

"I am not sure, Mentor."

Adrian laughed and shook his head. "I see." Then his expression became serious. "I need someone to do something for me in the Principality of Redbud. Perhaps you could do it on your way? Of course, I'll pay you for your time."

The burner ticked twice while Maureen considered the request. "Yes, Mentor. I'll do it."

* * *

Adrian nodded. "Good luck then." She bowed her head and started to leave but Adrian lifted one eyebrow and pointed at her with a finger. Stay.

Maureen moved behind the racks of glassware, out of sight.

Adrian raised a hand and made a sweeping motion. The air within his lab seemed to shimmer with dark particles that gathered together. They knitted into a transparent form that gradually solidified, taking on the appearance of a middle-aged man with a serious expression. Flames wrapped around him like a cloak.

The ticking of the burner was the only sound in the room as the light of the flames wavered through the glassware. The lamplight quivered with each beat of Maureen's heart. She didn't dare breathe.

"Barok," Adrian said, "It is good to see you again."

The projected man roared. "The mad fool Evans killed Ciri! He was envious because Ciri was selected over him for advance to Second-Tier and now he dares to tear Hader Castle's Eric to pieces while he is still alive in order to get his hands on a tube of Azure Potion!"

Tally logged [Evans killed Ciri (on file). Eric, Hader Castle. Azure Potion: no data. Second-Tier: no data.] The chip flagged the fact that it had recorded Eric beside Ciri under the Blue Ring. It moved Eric to Hader Castle on Barok's testimony.

"If you had one thread of hope to reach Second-Tier, you would be madder than Evans right now," Adrian told him.

Barok fell silent. Maureen could almost hear the flames crackle through the glassware.

"This time, Hader Castle and the Blue Ring will declare war," Barok said. "The Mist Labyrinth will join them too."

"And the underground races," Adrian noted. "Those outposts raided by Hell Fiends and dark elves were probably their work as well."

Tally displayed [Coalition: Blue Ring, Hader Castle, Mist Labyrinth; underground races: Hell Fiends, dark elves.] and moved the raided-outposts rumour from hearsay to probable.

"War is only a possibility at this point," Adrian pointed out. "The White Bone Tower will certainly stand with the Dark Forest." Barok quieted down.

"True." Barok's projected form seemed to relax. Then he asked, "You have an apprentice named Maureen. Please hand her over. She has offended the Olivias. I will pay you for your troubles. She is just an ordinary apprentice, after all. We are a family of wizards."

[Tagged: ordinary apprentice.]

Your pulse jumped, Tally said quietly.

I know.

Maureen waited very still, hidden behind the racks of glassware. Her hands rested flat against her robes. Adrian's face hardened into stone, and his voice sounded like the wind in the ice caves. "Bargaining with me, Barok? You're not qualified for that. Send Philip perhaps."

Barok began to backtrack. Fine golden flames sprang from Adrian's glasses. They incinerated the projection halfway through a word. The conflagration was gone as quickly as it began, leaving a few flakes of ash to drift down onto the brass and the bench top. The air smelled faintly of scorching.

[Philip: no data. Senior Olivia, by inference.]

Tally was silent for a moment.

"I'm not in the habit of selling my own apprentices to trade resources with other wizards," Adrian said, pushing his glasses back up his nose. His eyes never left Maureen. "Now, if you have nothing else, please leave. I wish to prepare some things for tomorrow. Come back then, Olsen, and we shall begin."

Something struck Maureen in the chest. No one with power had ever defended her before. She kept her face still and expressionless.

He said no, Tally said softly.

I heard, Maureen answered silently.

I am putting that in a column of its own, Tally said.

Maureen bowed her head and walked out. Her throat remained tight until she felt the cold air of the corridor hit her face.
