Maureen Olsen walked the long gallery alone, in Richard’s borrowed face. The hem of her black robe brushed the stone floor, her footfalls coming back to her a beat later, like echoes off a cliff. Still the doors were hung with black crepe in remembrance of Claude. Each doorframe had a stiff black border, the crepe frozen into place by the cold, smelling faintly of its dye.

Under her breastbone, the swallowed heart turned over like a second pulse. A violet-red glow crept in from the edges of her vision, and something far off whispered in a voice like the wind across an empty well.

She pushed it back with the rhythm of the Silver Light: three long beats, one short.

*Tally, what’s my status?*

[Maureen Olsen. Official Wizard, Phantom State. Path: Ancient Wizard. Str 16.2 / Agi 16.0 / Con 16.3 / Spirit 20.4 / Mana 20.4.]

Every line had risen since Maureen last checked— body and spirit both.

*Part is the heart, part is the Purgatory orb from Jackman’s corpse. I can’t tell you how much of each contributed.*

*Fine. Don’t bother.*

*That’s the problem, Maureen. It does bother me.*

A second line appeared underneath the first:

[Digesting: Void Mirage heart. est. 9% → 13%.]

Tally continued, *That is the slowest meal on file.*

*It’s not a meal,* Maureen replied silently.

*The Totem disagrees.*

*Fine. You win.*

Maureen stopped walking and considered the question that she’d been carrying since the Underworld: Where, exactly, did she stand among the wizards she’d measured?

Tally projected her data across the glass, displaying a series of pale blue lines in the darkness outside. Barok was there, Maffie was there, and a group of Phantom-band wizards from the descent. Each one had been labelled by someone else’s word.

[Phantom band: you, upper edge. Liquefied: not yet. Confidence: medium.]

*Near the top of your rung,* Tally said. *The next one is a long reach.*

The line above the Phantom band was less crowded. Tally only had two samples, Claude and Jackman. Each labelled by hearsay alone, both now dead. *This ladder’s made out of the grain of two dead men, Maureen. You should consider each rung provisional at best.*

Maureen looked up at the nearest portrait. A proud old Oserroba gazed down from his frame, the varnish cracked on his face like a dried riverbed. The brass nameplate was dulled by time. He stood as if he’d climbed every rung there was.

The frost had crept over the window beside him, spreading white fern leaves across the dark glass.

*Fine,* Maureen answered silently. The next step was Liquefied, and she already had the means. At her waist, she carried the Void Dimensional Pouch filled with the water she’d drawn from the Spring of Life’s pool in the cavern. Nearly two cubic metres worth.

*You know it’s damaged,* Tally reminded her. *Eve used the Spring’s essence to activate the heart, pulling all that power into the purple crystal. What’s left is flat, like a pond after the rain’s evaporated. Your only baseline is your own records of the Spring’s pool in the cavern.*

*Then we mend it,* Maureen replied silently.

At the end of the gallery, a household servant flattened himself against the wall. He bowed so low that Maureen thought his forehead might touch his knees before he rose again. She pulled the black robe tighter against the draft and passed him, stepping into the deeper darkness of the corridor.

* * *

The next morning, she asked Denise for a walled court of her own, somewhere she could set up without cluttering up a guest room. “I don’t need much space,” she said, “but this isn’t exactly the type of work you can do in a bedroom.”

Denise nodded before Maureen had even finished speaking. The girl’s eyes were still red-rimmed from the scene at her father’s bedside. “Take the east court. It has a wall, and a gate that locks.”

Her thumb worked at her sleeve as she spoke. She didn’t mention Lawrence, and neither did Maureen. Tally logged the tell.

*Leave it,* Maureen told her silently.

*Fine,* Tally answered. *I’ll leave it logged.*

The east court was a square of grey flagstone, walled in by box hedges, stiff and brittle with frost. The fountain in its centre was dry, and a plug of ice filled its stone fish’s spout. The air smelled of cold earth and old moss. Every breath Maureen took formed a cloud of white vapour before her face.

Before she unpacked any of her tools, she walked the court’s walls, pressing Gregory’s runes into the stone beneath the eaves and along the gateframe. When she was done, the court was enclosed in a closed ring of the glyphs.

In Tally’s lens, the ring of runes burned with a gold fire. The nodes were tied off in small, hard knots, like beads on a wire. [Ward: closed. Drew 0.3.]

Tally checked the workings three times. Maureen nodded. *I was about to ask you to do that.*

Only when she was certain that the court was secure did Maureen allow Mimicry to fade. Richard’s weak jaw and mousy features slid away from her like a mask of warm wax. Her own pale, sharp-boned face returned to the cold air.

*It is your face, Maureen,* Tally said. *I’ve missed it.*

*Missed it? You see it every night.*

*Not here.*

Maureen reached into the pouch by spirit, drawing out a stream of Spring water. She let the milky, faintly luminous fluid pour into the stone basin set upon the trestle bench. It steamed in the frost.

The scent of the steam was sweet, but wrongly so—a cup of honey left out overnight. When she breathed it in, nothing in her chest eased. In the cavern, the same breath had slowed her heart.

Tally logged the draw by sight as the basin filled. [Pouch: Spring water, est. 1.8 m³ → 1.7 m³, by sight.] The water was thinner than her records from the cavern, Tally noted. Something Eve had pulled out was now missing. There was no data on what.

Maureen set up the glassware and began work. The first step was filtration. Gregory’s pharmacology and Bina’s alchemy had given her a series of layered reagents. The goal was to coax the missing substance back into existence. Her first batches failed.

[Batch 4: failed. Cause: cooling too fast.]

[Batch 7: failed. Cause: reagent bound the wrong fraction.]

The column was getting long, Maureen noted.

*Long columns are how you get short answers,* Tally replied.

When a batch failed, it curdled an ugly grey within the glass, releasing a smell of wet slate and scorched sugar. A film of frost formed upon the trestle bench between pourings. Her fingers were numb at the tips, stained a faint green at the knuckles. They had been like this before, in the Dark Forest, long ago.

With Maureen’s permission, Tally helped hold the finished filtration lattice steady through the Darklight Orb while Maureen built the next step by hand. There was a small, steady cost, which ticked up on the display as they worked.

*This is the only part of this I’m enjoying,* Tally said.

*I’m enjoying it too,* Maureen replied quietly.

Tally kept the stone ledger as more materials vanished into the glass.

[Materials: −23. Magic stones, all columns: ~35,000 → ~33,800.]

*It’s going down slowly,* Tally said. *That’s how I prefer to watch it go.*

The days began to blur together. She slept in the tiny room off the side of the walled courtyard. The space was barely large enough for a narrow pallet, and she woke to frost on her blanket. Then she would return to the bench without bothering to light the brazier.

At last, a batch held. The water within the basin settled, with a core of clear liquid surrounded by a milky periphery. Its glow did not gutter out.

The sweetness in the steam changed. It was still sweet, but lighter now. Cleaner. Like the air at the head of a mountain spring after a rainstorm. Maureen felt her shoulders loosen before she realised why.

She tested the liquid properly, taking a drop on a reagent strip from her belt kit. The strip bloomed a pale and even gold. Then she reached out with her spirit perception, comparing the new substance against her memory of the pool. Her breathing came easier, her pulse slowed.

[Spring, refined: 92% of cavern profile, by comparison. Missing: one note, sweet, faint.]

*That last note is what I can’t put back,* Tally said.

*Ninety-two percent? Barely acceptable.*

*But that’s better than anyone else on the Passise could have done.*

*Fine, you keep the boast.*

The water poured into the tub that Maureen had made for it. The basin was heavy, a thing of grey stone. It sat in the small, screened room at the end of the court, with frost forming on its shutters.

The water glowed faintly as it filled the tub. The light shone in the dim room, a soft halo. The sweet scent came off the water as it breathed, filling the chamber. Outside on the shutters, ice ticked as it formed, and the light from the courtyard spilled through the slats in thin bars of grey.

Behind the screen, sealed inside the ward’s protection, Maureen stripped off her robes and sank down into the tub. She let the water rise around her until only her face showed above the surface.

Her own face stared back at her from the surface of the water. Black eyes, pale skin, her hair floating out from her head in dark threads. The water was cool against her skin at first. Then it warmed, becoming comfortably hot. The warmth sank deeper, penetrating to the bone.

*Bath time!* Tally announced. *Soak timer active. Displaying body status.*

[Pulse: 58. Absorption: ongoing.]

*The water’s going right in, Maureen. Like rain into dry ground. Want me to keep the readout up for you?*

*No.*

Maureen floated with her eyes shut. Under the water, her fists slowly closed. Finger by finger, she clenched her hands into tight balls, like a climber trying to pull herself up one more rung before she collapsed.

She fell asleep in the water.

Tally kept the readout off the display, fed the ward, and watched. When Maureen woke in the morning, she found the report waiting for her.

[Bath: 6h 40m. Ward drew 0.1. Cat on the wall, 03:10.]

*You slept through a cat, Maureen.*

Night after night over the next weeks, Maureen bathed and refilled the tub from the pouch, until Tally gave her a warning.

[Pouch: Spring water, est. 1.7 m³ → 0.3 m³.]

*I’m keeping what’s left whether you like it or not.*

* * *

Before she left the court, Maureen recast Richard’s face behind the ward. It was easy now, and as soon as she willed it, his features slipped over her own. His weak jawline and flat, colourless features took shape. Her throat thickened with muscle, and her voice dropped to his deep register.

[Mimicry: Richard. −0.4.]

Tally checked the eyes in the dark, shuttered glass.

[Eyes: Black. Heart’s glow damped. −0.2 Mana/hour.]

*Richard looks as dull as ever,* Tally said.

*That’s the point,* Maureen answered.

The household bowed as Maureen passed through the corridors. Maids flattened themselves against the stone walls, their skirts rustling. Grooms held their caps in their fists, crushing the soft fabric between their fingers. Everyone smiled, their grins held too wide and fixed.

Maureen’s shoulders rose a little, as they had beneath Duke Skran’s chipped gilt.

She knew why the household was falling over itself to bow before her. Richard was Denise’s guest, and Denise held court at Conta Manor. And Maureen knew that a Phantom-State Wizard was a force to be reckoned with in any wizard family.

“Wizard Richard,” the steward said, falling into step beside Maureen. He bowed four times in the next twenty paces. “You’ll have heard, of course. Or perhaps you haven’t.”

The steward’s voice dropped to a confidential murmur. Lawrence was sicker, and he wouldn’t see any of his family. “He asked for it himself. He kept two maids to tend him and threw everyone else out. Everyone.”

[Pause: 0.9s.] [Eyes: Slid toward east wing.]

*He wants you to know whose house this is now,* Tally said. *Low confidence, by the pause and the look.*

*Denise’s,* Maureen answered silently.

*Logged,* Tally said.

“The entire manor is talking about it,” the steward said, bowing again.

“Is it,” Maureen said. She let no questions or emotions creep into her voice.

She thought of the old man in the canopy bed, and of his voice calling after his daughter as she ran from the door. Maureen smiled faintly and shook her head.

*He threw his own family out of his room,* Tally said.

*He did that the day he chose the cure over his daughter,* Maureen answered silently.

[Logged.] Tally said nothing more.

As Maureen made her way down the stairway, with the steward pacing a pace behind, she turned her thoughts to her status. Again, Tally displayed her status:

[Str 17.9 / Agi 17.7 / Con 18.0 / Spirit 23.0 / Mana 23.0. Rank: unchanged.]

Mana had risen along with Spirit, point for point.

Maureen breathed out once through her nose, a long exhalation.

That was all the disappointment she allowed herself on the stair.

*Big gains, but not the door,* Tally said.

*It’s harder than I thought,* Maureen answered silently.

*Harder than I thought too,* Tally answered. *And I’m the one who does the thinking.*

Tally logged the exhale anyway.

*Don’t,* Maureen said silently.

*Already did.*

*The Spring took you as far as it could. If you want to push it farther, we’ll need to try another method. Unfortunately, I have no data on another method at this time.*

The dining hall doors still bore their black crepe. Within, beeswax candles flickered in silver holders laid along the length of a long table. At the head of the table stood the empty chair of Lawrence Oserroba, the sick old man upstairs who had barred his own family from his room.

Attendants waited upon Maureen in silence. They brought her a platter of roast veal on silver, a basket of warm bread, a bottle of dark wine. The dining hall smelled of browned animal fat and the sweet scent of burning wax. A constant draught of cold air came from the tall, frosted windows.

The steward placed a menu card beside Maureen’s plate, folded and creased so that the writing lay hidden within. She glanced at it—written out in the careful hand of a clerk—and tucked the card into the pocket sewn inside her sleeve.

Maureen cut the veal. It was tender, with just a bit of resistance from the lightly browned edge where the fat had rendered and crisped. She allowed herself to enjoy the single bite of meat, then nodded once to the hovering steward.

*Taste is data,* Tally said.

*This particular data is good,* Maureen answered silently.

*I will file it next to the milk-honey tea.*

* * *

The frozen gravel paths crunched under her boots as she walked alone through the grounds of Conta Manor. Her breath smoked in the still air. The hedges stood like sentinels, each branch tipped with white rime. Behind her, the lit windows of the manor shone through the night like coals in a banked fire.

A chill ran up her forearm. It felt dry, like ashes drifting down her skin. Her hand went to the spot before her mind caught up.

Under the cloth of her sleeve, the brand flared to grey life. The card tucked into her sleeve pocket burst into flame.

Heat bit at her arm through the cloth. The sharp scent of burnt paper filled her nostrils. With a jerk, Maureen shook the card free of her sleeve and into her palm. The ash of the menu card remained black instead of turning to grey flakes. The shape of the card stayed whole and even the smoke rose in a steady stream from the blackened mass.

And within that mass, words appeared.

[Brand: Clair. Signal. Incoming.]

*Three to six days, Maureen. That’s what I forecast for it to take him to wake up. It’s been weeks. Either he woke up ages ago and the brand can’t reach this far— I have no data on its effective range— or something has gone wrong.*

Just one word echoed in Maureen’s mind, unspoken. Aaron.

*Read it,* Tally said.

Maureen bent her head over the black ash cupped in her palm, and began to read.
