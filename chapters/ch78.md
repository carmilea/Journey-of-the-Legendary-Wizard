The grey tug-of-war of the portal let go of Maureen all at once. She landed on the sloping ground, her feet sinking into the soil between the roots of some trees. Maureen put the heel of her hand against her forehead, squeezing her eyes shut until the swimming sensation subsided. She let out a long, slow breath.

Maureen looked around. It was a mountain forest, ridge after ridge rolling off into the distance until they disappeared from sight. The trees were tall and close together, not really any different from the forests outside. But the sky was different. A thin layer of uniform grey cloud hung over everything like curtains on a rod. No sun shone anywhere above her head.

The air tasted wrong, but in a pleasant way. There was a faint sweetness at the back of her throat, as if she’d just smelled the steam from a pot of boiling sugar. Her lungs filled with each breath, expanding more than they had any right to after a portal crossing.

Maureen snapped her fingers, an old habit that Tally didn’t really require. *I don’t need the snap,* it told her. *I’m already reading your stats. Your breathing is easier now. Heart rate down six beats per minute at the same pace. There’s a sweetness to your throat that I can’t match.* [Air: abnormal vs outside. By inference.]

*What is the air made of?* Maureen asked silently.

Tally didn’t answer for a moment. *I can’t name a single gas. I still don’t have a nose. My best estimate for your body is . . . perhaps nine percent improvement.* [Body: estimate.]

*A guess?*

*Yes, a guess. Labeled as such.*

Maureen laughed, a short exhale through her nose. Alone in the trees on the slope, the laughter sounded hollow. The leaders created this place so apprentices could kill each other, but any farmer who wandered inside would walk out healthier.

*Healthier in a slaughterhouse,* Maureen told Tally.

*Irony. Logged. First entry in file for this realm.*

Maureen looked around the empty forest. No birds sang on this lonely slope. The only sounds were the drips of water sliding off leaves and the creak of a tree branch shifting in the breeze. She didn’t like remaining where she’d been dropped, so Maureen picked a deer path leading downhill and started to move, placing her boots softly on the loamy soil covered by a carpet of fallen leaves.

Maureen went over the rules the leaders had announced outside the portals as she walked. Every apprentice carried points. Kill another apprentice and the points their body held passed to you. The higher your total was at the end, the better rewards you received.

Tally displayed it for her like a simple arithmetic equation. [Kill: victim's total → killer.] [End condition: 90% of all apprentices dead.]

*No time limit. Only a body count,* Tally added.

*Nine out of ten.*

Maureen kept walking with that number sitting under her ribs, cold like a stone. This trial wasn't about staying alive. It was about harvesting. Every apprentice who stepped through those portals knew it as well as she did.

So she made her plan. A big total would require her to use her trump cards, but she could make selective kills and stay in the middle of the pack. A total that stood out drew close questioning afterwards, and close questioning could peel away all her secrets like an onion.

*High points means questions,* she told Tally silently.

Tally replied in her head. *High points means Adrian asking with kind eyes, and everyone else asking with knives.*

That decided the cover story. Dark Wind Ring would be the only trump card Maureen showed the Dark Forest when she returned. She'd pass it off as a lucky find during her time in the realm. The alchemical grenades could never be traced back to her name, so they would have to be used by someone else. Tally ran through the list. Dark Wind Ring: admitted. The Seal, the grenades, and Tally itself: never. Everything else belonged to someone who didn’t exist yet.

*Then let’s make him,* Maureen told Tally silently.

* * *

She stopped and took out her kit in a hollow between great roots. No one on the deer path could see her here. She spread out her tools on a flat piece of stone, a knife, a small brush, two stoppered vials of dye, and a thin glass vial filled with milky suspension. There was still a drop of Adrian's wax around its neck.

The thread of stream chuckled softly over its stones next to her, and the moss beneath her feet was cold through the cloth of her robe. The sweet air hung in her mouth. For a moment it was almost peaceful, which made her distrust it all the more.

First, Mimicry. Tally had recorded the face model from Gregory's library, and Maureen had practised it night after night at the mirror in her washroom. She shaped the spell, feeling the small muscles of her face begin to move without her bidding. A facial-muscle map appeared on her display, drawn in a pale blue by Tally. *Cheeks, 2mm. Cheekbones, up. Eyes, draw long. The left side is slightly slower. Apply pressure.* [Mana: 7.6 → 7.2]

It felt like ants were crawling beneath her skin. Her jaw tightened and her eyelids pulled themselves toward her temples. Her cheekbones ached dully as they rose toward her ears. She pulled a thread of water out from the stream and flattened it with a tiny spell, forming a disc of still liquid that hung in the air at arm's length. A water mirror. [Mana: 7.2 → 7.1]

The face in the water was gaunt. The cheekbones were set high on the face and the eyes were stretched long, narrowed, and cold. It was the face of a sinister stranger. Above it was the same black hair she'd seen in the still basin when she was fourteen. Behind the narrow lids were the same black eyes, looking back at her.

Maureen frowned at the image. "Not enough," she said to the water. There was no one else to hear her.

*The hair.* Tally agreed. *You are the only black-haired, black-eyed girl in Adrian's file. Anyone who saw you twice would recognize the hair before they ever looked at your face.* She took the knife and sawed off her hair until it was short, letting the black strands fall into the stream, where they twisted in the current and spun away. Then she opened both dye vials and brushed them through her hair with the small brush. They made her scalp sting, and the sharp smell of vinegar and wet iron rose from her head.

In the mirror the black went dull, then brown. Short, coarse hair, standing up in untidy tufts like straw beneath her fingers. It was Jing Sheng's hair, the one part of herself that had come back, and now it was gone beneath another's colour.

*Jing Sheng's hair,* Tally said, its voice more subdued. *For now.*

*For now,* Maureen answered silently.

Then she caught herself smiling at the man in the water. The smile was thin and ugly on that narrow face. It suited him far too well. The amusement passed through her, but it left a small chill in its wake.

Last came Adrian's vial. The Scentless Bug powder he had pressed on her before the trial, reworked on Tally's suggestion to hide not a scent, but the particles a body gave off. Maureen pulled the stopper free and chanted "Silaheryikan."

The suspension broke down, becoming a white smoke that wound itself over her shoulders and face. Cool, dry, and odourless, it spiralled about her and left her feeling clean. When it began to thin, even her own particle sense of herself was silenced. She could no longer hear the steady stream of particles rising from her skin, as though she had entered a room where the clock had just stopped ticking.

*I read nothing from you,* Tally said.

That proved nothing. Tally was reading from inside her. *But would the enchantment hold against a full wizard?*

*No data,* Tally said. *So let's not ask one. And mother bugs can still find this powder.*

[Mana: 7.1 → 7.0]

Mimicry did nothing for her voice, so she pitched it low and rough in her throat. Experimentally, she tried a name on the stranger, as one might try on a new coat. "Jack." Then, remembering an old joke from a world none of these people had ever heard of: "Jack the Ripper." In this realm, that would be her name.

*I'll file him well away from Mr. Jack,* Tally said.

*No,* Maureen told it silently. *Next to him.* Mr. Jack was the last man who'd corrected her feet, the right heel a finger's width out.

*Next to him, then,* Tally said.

From now on, only Jack would kill in the realm, and Maureen Olsen would turn up at the end with a modest score. Maureen let the water mirror drop back into the stream with a slap, then walked toward the grey light where the trees of the realm thinned out.

* * *

Just before she stepped out of the trees, she saw a shiver of leaves out of the corner of her eye—at the extreme edge, down and to the left. There wasn't a wind. The movement came from low down on her left.

*Third party.*

*Brush, left, 8 m.*

*Seen,* she told it silently. *Leave him where he is.*

Maureen stepped out from under the trees and found herself in the middle of a standoff. Two apprentices stood ten paces apart, hands raised halfway to their chests, and they both turned as one to look at her with hard, flat eyes.

One wore the white robe of Hader Castle, the hem line greyed with dirt; his fingers were already crooked for a cast. The second apprentice was White Bone Tower, dressed in a grey robe with a small bone pin fastened near the collar, his lips moving soundlessly. Between them, the high grass lay crushed and flattened, the sweet smell of trampled sap mingling with the reek of acid and turned earth.

*Second-Class apprentices, both in the middle of their column. Neither one is over you,* Tally supplied. *They were just about to kill each other. Now they're considering you instead.*

Maureen rubbed at her nose like a man who had wandered into the wrong room, then rasped, "Well, this is embarrassing. I wasn't expecting company when I came through. Just passing through, honestly. Carry on. Don't mind me." She took a step back toward the shelter of the trees.

Neither apprentice believed a word she said. Maureen saw them exchange a single glance, and in that glance she saw the moment when they came to an agreement. The standoff had become an alliance—for one minute only, aimed at the stranger who had interrupted them.

They began to chant together, their voices overlapping slightly, and Maureen felt the particles in the air thicken around their hands, as the air thickens before a storm.

Maureen spoke the chant for the Acid Missile: Amra-Keis. A ball of sour-green acid formed in her palm, smoking white against the grey sky. She spun about, laughing deep in her throat, and threw the ball—not at the two apprentices, but into the brush to her left. [Mana: 7.0 → 6.6]

"You hid very nicely," she called after it in Jack's rasping voice. "But you've watched the show long enough. Come out and take a bow."

The acid hit the brush with a sound like fat hitting a hot griddle. Leaves curled and blackened. Someone yelped in pain, and a black-robed apprentice rolled out from the brush onto the high grass, slapping futilely at a sleeve that smoked and reeked of burnt cloth.

The two chanters broke off their chant in mid-word. Their eyes went from her to the black robe, and back again. Three budding scavengers, each of whom had planned to pick over the remains of the other two, found themselves looking at each other.

She ran, laughing once more over her shoulder as she left them. She allowed the laugh to carry across the clearing, ringing out like a triumphant actor's exit. Beneath the laughter, her breathing remained perfectly even.

She did not run far. A few hundred paces into the forest, she scrambled up a tall tree. She settled herself on a branch, her back against the trunk, and waited.

*Fourteen separate flares of power,* Tally told her. Fourteen separate times the clearing erupted with a crack, a heavy whump, a shriek. Fourteen separate times the world brightened through the leaves overhead, flickering in the dappled shade. Then nothing. For a long moment, nothing.

Then, by sound alone, came one voice coughing.

*One left standing,* it said.

Maureen dropped lightly to the ground and began walking back toward the clearing. The wide space was a mess. Trees lay snapped and thrown about, their trunks blocking the way. The ground was pockmarked with craters. Two bodies sprawled in the high grass. The black-robe sat leaning against the trunk of a fallen tree, coughing into his hand. Blood trickled between his fingers. The air reeked of burnt sap, acid, and turned earth. The white robe was no longer white. The small bone pin that had decorated the grey collar lay in the dirt some distance from its owner.

Maureen walked toward the man slowly, saying nothing. He wiped his mouth on his sleeve and grinned wetly up at her. "You stayed. Of course you did. Truly one of ours, Dark Forest to the bone—"

*It's the robe,* Tally told her. *He knows the robe. Not the face—the face is a stranger's. But the robe. That black wool he knows.*

His grin twisted even wider. He raised a stoppered vial in his left hand. "A High-Energy Explosive Potion," he said, the grin becoming a leer. "Cost me dearly, but I've kept it unopened through the whole fight, waiting for just this moment." Maureen stood before him, frowning down. "One more step," he said, "and I break it and we both burn! You're a scavenger like me, you won't dare, you won't—" His voice rose in pitch until it cracked.

*It's a standard-issue potion,* Tally told Maureen coldly. *I have it on file. Same as what Augurit put in every apprentice's parting kit from the Dark Forest. And he's bluffing on the throw, anyway. Look at his right shoulder. It won't lift to hurl anything.*

> Shell. Throw. Run.

Maureen gave the apprentice no answer. At Tally's command her hand plunged into her coat and came out with an egg-shaped iron shell. She threw it hard, overhand, snarling the trigger word under her breath as soon as it left her fingers: "Kerrash!" The shell was black, the same colour as the vial in the apprentice's hand, but otherwise the two items had nothing in common. [Grenades: 95 → 94]

His laugh caught in his throat and became a strangled cry. His eyes followed the small black ball as it turned over and over in the grey air.

"What the hell is—"

He never finished the sentence. Maureen had already spun about and was running for all she was worth. A moment later she felt a hard punch between her shoulder blades and the air around her sizzled. A pillar of fire lanced up through the trees, its heat lapping at her neck, and splinters and clods of torn earth began pattering down through the leaves around her. She skidded to a halt behind a thick tree trunk and rubbed her chin, clicking her tongue appreciatively. The explosion had been larger than any shell she'd tested on the fief. The apprentice's High-Energy Explosive Potion must have added its own fire to the mix. She laughed softly to herself.

When the smoke thinned, she crept back into the clearing. Three bodies lay in various states of torn and scattered dismemberment. The gear the three apprentices had carried lay in shards across the scorched earth. Much of it had cracked or melted under the force of the blast, and what hadn't been broken apart by the explosion had been burnt to cinders.

A glyph burned itself onto the back of Maureen's hand, prickling like a new brand. It was the Ancient Andas numeral for four.

*Her one, plus his three,* Tally said. *Which are his one, plus the two he took.*

[Points: 4]

*It's time to change your robe,* Tally told her briskly. *The apprentice knew you by that black wool. The face was a stranger's to him, but he recognized the robe. It was all he needed to see.*

Maureen stripped off the Dark Forest black and rolled it into her pack. From a kit scattered around her, she pulled out a plain grey cloak, singed along one hem. From now on Jack wore no faction's colours.

She pawed through the shattered gear, pulling out what she could use: a pouch of magic stones, scorched but still intact; two healing potions that hadn't broken; a half-burnt map. Then she left as quickly as she could. A noise like that would bring others running, if they were in the area. Jack hunted from the shadows and only stood in the open when he had to.

* * *

Half a mile later she climbed the tallest tree she could find, wanting to get some elevation above the noise so she could see who the noise had brought.

Resin stuck to her palms, and the tree's crown swayed slightly under her weight. The grey cloud was close enough overhead to feel damp on her face. Behind her, the column of smoke from the blast leaned across the realm like a dirty drinking straw, and the green world stretched out silent in all directions.

Tally swept and marked everything her eyes passed over. Nothing was moving towards the source of the smoke yet. Then came a scream from the north-east, and Tally marked it by sound. Maureen twisted her head in that direction.

Below her, in a gully, there was an apprentice wrapped in a halo of fog. He was firing off ropes of mist in all directions, Mist Labyrinth by the spells, and screaming for help to the empty trees.

Then came the drone. Low and huge, like a beehive trapped inside a bell. A black cloud of insects poured down the gully and over the apprentice.

The swarm smashed through the apprentice's foggy defences as though they were made of wet paper. His scream was cut off halfway through. In seconds, there was nothing but a skeleton standing where he had been, with a few strands of red flesh still clinging to the bones. Another moment and the skeleton collapsed into the underbrush.

Maureen felt her hands go cold against the tree's rough bark. She realized she had been holding her breath and forced it out slowly on a count of Silver Light.

The insects began to draw in on themselves, collapsing back into one another, coalescing into a form. They gathered into the shape of a man.

A young man. Very handsome. Very pale. Almost dead looking, with thin lips and a cold face. He wore Hader white.

*Maureen, I'm reading him clean off the end of the column. Above any apprentice in my files, including Frey.*

Her eyes went to a rise just above the gully. There stood a second figure, dressed in a robe of blood-red, his face composed of smooth white bone. At his feet were several apprentices, great holes torn through their bodies. Their blood ran down the slope, mingling with the water from the grey cloud overhead.

The bone-faced figure was laughing. Maureen could see his jaw move and his head tilt back as though to the sky. She was glad that the distance between them prevented her from hearing the sound of his laughter. The young man said something to him, short and abrupt, then turned his back. Maureen heard no words, not a syllable.

A blood-coloured light wrapped around the bone-faced figure, and he began to thin, grow translucent, and then was gone. The young man's lips moved and, with a soft thump, his body burst into a swarm of large black insects. They hung for a moment in mid-air, turning once upon themselves, then streamed off like a dark cloud.

*Maureen, there are two. Unnamed. Both are above any apprentice on my calibration. Avoid. Avoid.*

*Both?* Maureen thought back.

*Yes, both. Let them meet each other first.*

Maureen remained in the tree crown until she could feel her fingers again. Then she climbed down the far side of the trunk, feeling Jack's grey cloak catch on the rough bark. When she reached the ground, she headed off in the opposite direction.
