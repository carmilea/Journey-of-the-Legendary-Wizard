Behind the door her field had slammed, Maureen lay in the dark and considered Joanna’s request.

Joanna had asked her to stand behind the Jadeleaf Ring, but she had brought nothing to the table except need. A wizard does not sell her protection for need.

Through her particle sense, Tally followed Joanna’s retreat down the corridor. Under her breastbone lay the dormant flame seed, a quiet ember glowing in Joanna’s chest. [Seed: Joanna, dormant]. *Joanna has stopped at the bottom of the stair,* Tally told Maureen. *She is standing there.*

Joanna’s ledger flashed up in Maureen’s mind, a pair of pale blue lines. [Spared.] [Sponsorship letter lodged with the Alliance. Entry pending.]

*Balanced,* Tally said.

*Then we’re done,* Maureen responded silently to Tally.

*You’re done,* Tally answered. *She isn’t.*

Equivalent exchange was the one law that every wizard Maureen had met adhered to, from Adrian to Gandolf to the Olivias. It was the law by which she’d lived since she’d billed Jay five stones for the antidote. If she stood behind the Jadeleaf Ring, Ace’s friends and the Ring’s creditors would come to her door, every week. And there was nothing to pay them with.

The Jadeleaf Ring’s poverty was everywhere. The green leaf painted on the door had flaked at the edges, the blanket covering the narrow bed had been darned in three different colours, and even the air still smelled like the evening’s soup, more water than leek.

A dying house did not need a guest. It needed a patron.

Maureen pushed herself up off the straw mattress and sat cross-legged on the cold wooden floor. She closed her eyes and let her breathing slow until it was barely a whisper of air, sinking down into Void Eclipse, the method that had burned itself into her chest back in Gregory’s house. Tiny motes of light appeared around her, black and ember-red, pulsing with an odd, uneven beat. Like a heart that sometimes missed and then caught up with itself. One by one, the tiny specks fell into the dark sea of spirit behind her brow, disappearing like pebbles thrown into a still pond.

*I’ll keep the night,* Tally said. *The wards, the corridor, the stair.*

*Wake me if Joanna comes back,* Maureen instructed.

Tally responded, *I will wake you if anyone does.*

And the corridor outside remained quiet until dawn.

* * *

Maureen left the Jadeleaf Ring at first light, no goodbyes spoken. Christine’s door was still shut tight when she slipped out from the back, and she made her way out into Emilia as Richard, Official Wizard aura pressed back to apprentice levels.

The city awoke around her. Wooden shutters banged open in the bakers’ streets, releasing clouds of fine white flour and the scent of bread ovens. Carts rumbled through the cobblestones, drivers shouting back and forth to each other, and high above the roofs to the east, towards the heart of the city, there was the faintest shimmer of air where the wards of wizards caught the sun.

A cramped general store sold her a folded map of Emilia for a few gold coins from the Olivia chest, an old man behind the counter eyeing them suspiciously before passing each one over his palm to judge the weight. Not once did he look up to her face.

Maureen unfolded the map at a street corner, letting it spread open, section by section, while a pale-blue flicker moved behind her eyes.

Tally filed it all away. Streets, wards, districts.

*There are three bookshops as well,* Tally said.

*I know,* Maureen told her silently.

*That’s exactly what you said about the library in the Dark Forest, and we all know how that turned out. Every last free shelf.*

She raised her hand, and a hire carriage trundled up behind her. The driver was a mortal man, his waxed cape flapping in the cool morning breeze, and he kept his eyes firmly fixed on the ground when dealing with a wizard. He took two gold coins from her— Tally docking the line, [Gold: −2]—and helped her into the back of the carriage.

The horse that pulled the carriage had a single ridged plate of horn across its forehead, and its breath steamed in the cool air, smelling faintly of hot iron. Just like the team that had pulled her out of the Dark Forest. The carriage rolled towards the centre of the city, cobbles worn smooth as soap beneath its iron-shod wheels.

* * *

Before the door had even closed behind her, a young woman came around the counter. “Honoured wizard. How may the Centre serve you today?”

She made a bow. It was just low enough to be flattering without going an inch lower than necessary. Her face was a pretty one, the sort that sang in temple choirs, but her manner was that of a woman who had sold a hundred houses. She wore no ink upon her hands, her cuffs were pressed and crisp and a faint orris root hung about her.

Maureen rapped two knuckles on the black oak. “A house. Long-term. My own.”

Richard’s voice came out low and flat, and Maureen gave the girl no more than that. Tally, though, read it on the girl’s face. There was a momentary widening of the pupils, a slight lean forward at “long-term”, a smile that became just the slightest shade warmer.

*That’s the commission. You’re her favourite person this month.*

“This way please, honoured wizard.”

The girl led the way through the hall, talking easily. Maureen let her talk; she needed somewhere to set up her benches for potions, somewhere to do energy-reaction work. Somewhere far from the Jadeleaf Ring.

She thought of the leaking roof, the shared walls. Christine’s sneering face across the dinner table. A door that only she could open. The desire sat beneath her ribs, simple and a little embarrassing.

“Wizards and ordinary folk share Emilia, honoured wizard, but not the same streets. What issues forth from a wizard without his meaning it is more than mortal men can bear.”

Tally was keeping count of the mortal clerks behind the other counters. Not one had come within three paces of Maureen since she’d walked in, and two of them had even pulled their stools back a pace. *There you go. Proof of the lecture.*

“But the wizards live together on a great tract of land reserved for their use alone,” Grace continued. “It sits at the heart of the city. Every size and style of manor can be found built across its bounds.”

She led Maureen out into a wide plaza, where grey flagstones lay underfoot, chilled by the morning breeze. Above, an irregular shape hung in the air. Vast and sprawling, it looked almost as though someone had spilled a bottle of red wine in the sky. It hummed with a sound so low it vibrated in Maureen’s back teeth.

Green points, like fireflies caught in a jar, blinked on and off within the red shape. They covered its surface thickly at the edges, thinning towards the middle.

“Every one of those green lights represents a house that still stands empty,” Grace said, gesturing to the lights. She spread her hands wide before her, like a card dealer fanning out a deck. “Stretch out to one, honoured wizard, with your spirit. You will see everything.”

Maureen reached out with her spirit sense, touching one of the green points. A listing unfolded behind her eyes, an image moving with perfect clarity. Halls turned slowly in place, a garden seen from each wall, the roofline as seen from above. Every side of the house was laid out at once.

Grace’s patter began, rising and falling in a warm, confident voice. “Eighty-seven Fuanton Street. Designed by one of Emilia’s most famous architects. Built in the old Kelden style. There is so much space within, you could lose a servant in it.”

*So much space means so much wall to ward,* Tally noted. *And to heat. And to clean. And to lose servants in, apparently.*

“Or perhaps the honoured wizard would prefer forty-nine Raymond Street? All hard redwood construction, spellwork barely gets through it.” Grace’s voice dropped in tone, growing conspiratorial. “And it’s right by the gambling quarter, should the honoured wizard ever desire to unwind.”

*Grace thinks you need unwinding.*

*I need walls,* Maureen told her silently.

*Well then, I’ll unwind for both of us instead. By counting listings.*

A table flickered to life, drawn in pale blue lines directly across the red light of the floating map. Price. Built-in wards. Distance to the Alliance. Sightlines from the street. [Listings read: 31]. One row rose from the table, moving to the top of the display.

*Thirty-four Connpa Street. First in wards per price.*

Maureen opened up that listing herself. A stone manor house, set in a walled garden, with gravel paths and tall windows, shuttered against the sun. A sliding image showed its walls, rippling with the passage of ward-light, like oil on water.

*Agreed.*

*Smugly agreed.*

Maureen did not argue the adverb.

“Thank you, Grace,” she said aloud. “I want the manor at thirty-four Connpa Street.”

Grace’s professional smile bloomed into something genuine and delighted. “An excellent choice, honoured wizard! This way please, and we shall see to the paperwork immediately.”

There were more counters in the back, and Maureen paid for a full year’s lease in advance, feeding her cards and loose stones into a slot. The clerk pressed the spell brand for the manor house into Maureen’s palm, and she felt the cold seal sink into her flesh beside Adrian’s mark.

[Magic stones, all columns: ~3,500 → ~1,000].

*That’s what they’re there for,* Maureen told Tally silently.

Grace escorted her to the door, bowing her out with a final flourish. Maureen stepped out into the glare of noon sunlight, the new brand still cold against her palm. Beneath her ribs was something that felt almost like a holiday.

* * *

The hire carriage set her down at the gate of 34 Connpa Street as dusk was starting to fall. She watched the vehicle rattle away and then turned to face the manor. It loomed over her behind its walled garden, large and grey, looking even bigger now than it had in the moving image. Ward-light slid across the stone walls in slow sheets like oil on water. The iron gate beneath her branded palm was faintly warm, as if something inside had been waiting for her touch.

The gate responded, and the defences fell away before her like a curtain being pulled aside. Anyone else who had tried to pass through would have been thrown backwards, skidding across the gravel path.

Through Tally’s lens, the house’s built-in wards hung over the building like golden filaments. Tally studied the patterns for a moment before she found the master knot that the Rental Centre had tied above the front door. *A warded house is still just a house,* Tally said. *Someone else holds the keys.*

Inside, the rooms were empty. All the shutters were closed tight against the outside world, and there wasn’t a speck of dust in sight. Maureen walked through room after room, laying her palm flat against the cool stone walls. Hers.

She laughed quietly in the centre of the bare hall. The sound was short and humourless.

“Not bad. Worth the stones.”

*Maureen, I note that your shoulders have just dropped by a finger’s width, and your pulse has slowed to sixty beats per minute.*

*Don’t log that.*

*Already did. New column labelled “home”.*

Before full dark, Maureen headed back into the city to pick up some servants. She bought five from an Emilian slave house, a cook, a housekeeper, two groundsmen, and a stable boy, each bearing the standard dormant binding array for the house. [Magic stones, all columns: ~1,000 → ~600].

In the hall, Tally checked each of the arrays in turn, searching for any secondary anchor which might route the slaves’ loyalty back to the slave house.

[Consistency check: no secondary anchor]

[Consistency check: no secondary anchor]

[Consistency check: no secondary anchor]

[Consistency check: no secondary anchor]

[Consistency check: no secondary anchor]

*A check is not proof,* Tally reminded her.

Maureen finished each binding with her own mana, along with an Ancient Andas command word. [Mana: 16 → 13.5] Each slave screamed once as they lost their freedom, then fell silent. *Five,* Tally said.

They stood in a row on the freshly waxed wooden boards, the cook’s grey hair held tightly by pins to stop it from falling in her face, the stable boy’s knuckles white on his cap, every pair of eyes fixed on Maureen’s boots.

“Wizard Richard,” the housekeeper whispered, testing the name.

“The kitchen is yours.” Maureen spoke with Richard’s low voice. “The grounds are yours. The stable is yours.” She pointed up the stairs, “That is closed to all of you. Always. No exceptions.”

She wove Gregory’s runes over the house’s own weave, adding a second ring of silver lines that sank into the outer walls beneath the borrowed gold. Then she added a separate ward to her bedroom door. [Mana: 13.5 → 12.3]

Tally checked the new ring of wards once, twice, three times.

*I was about to tell you to check it a third time,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*I know,* Tally said. *That’s why I did it already.*

Behind the ward on her bedroom door, Maureen let Mimicry fall. Her own face re-emerged in the dark glass of the window, the black hair grown back slightly past where she had sawn it off, the black eyes with a flicker of crimson deep within. She slipped on a white velvet robe, soft and heavy at the same time, then poured herself a glass of red wine from a crystal decanter. The liquid shone in the stemmed glass, and through the window below her Emilia’s lamps were spreading out, strings and clusters of light.

It wasn’t pleasure, but it was the closest thing she’d felt in a very long time.

Wine glass in hand, Maureen contemplated her next step. She was an Official Wizard now. The next step along the standard wizard’s path was the Elemental Phantom State, which required enough Spirit to resonate with the particles in the air and begin the process of elementalising. On the ordinary road, this typically took around fifteen Spirit. Maureen had sixteen. Was the Ancient road the same?

*Eighteen, Maureen. Not fifteen,* Tally answered, sourcing her answer to the pages on the Void Eclipse and Gregory’s imprint.

“Damn it.”

It tasted like iron and sour cherries. She set the glass down on the sill, harder than she intended, and the crystal rang once before falling silent, a high thin note.

*The mountain has moved.*

*There’s always another mountain,* Maureen responded silently.

*Yes, you did say that before. I wrote it down.*

Tally laid out the first route anyway. A special stimulant potion would force Maureen’s Spirit up quickly, burning out her sea of spirit in the process. It would be permanent, Maureen would never recover what she lost.

*I don’t recommend that option,* Tally said. *If you choose that, I’ll argue with you every night.*

*I’m not choosing that.*

*Good. I was prepared to be very insufferable about it.*

That left the second option. Break through to the second layer of the Void Eclipse. The method had seventeen layers, each one matched to a wizard rank. Maureen had only been standing on the first for a few days.

[Dark Element Energy Stone. Necro Flower. Purple Wood Snake tears.]

*The stone is dear,* Tally said. *But it can be purchased. The other two have already sold out, before anyone could even name a price.*

“Dark Element Energy Stone,” Maureen said aloud. “Necro Flower.” Her reflection in the window looked sour. “Purple Wood Snake tears.”

A market with goods but no prices. Items so rare they evaporated before the seller even finished speaking.

“Truly an Ancient Wizard,” Maureen muttered, pressing two fingers between her brows. She’d searched the entire continent to gather what she needed for this promotion, and the next step along the path wanted treasure again. She’d buy the stone tonight. That much, at least, she could manage.

* * *

Maureen pulled the Richard face back into place, the glass in the hall mirror helping her find the lines [Mana: 12.3 → 11.9]. Then she stepped outside. A few lamps hung by the roadside, lighting her way through the night. She followed the route that Tally had drawn from Emilia’s map, a pale blue ribbon lying atop the city’s dark streets.

The wizards’ district was nearly silent at night. The buildings stood close together, but their high walls kept everything enclosed. Metal gates guarded every entrance, and the wards hummed in the stones like bees trapped behind plaster. A single window burned far in the distance, lit by a sickly green glow from some wizard’s experiment.

The shop rose three stories, and the glow of its glass cases shone row upon row. Floating eyes sat in jars, silver wire coiled in baskets, and a stuffed bird with feathers like knives perched on a stand. The air smelled of resin and polish, with an undercurrent of cold metal and money.

“I wish to purchase a Dark Element Energy Stone,” Maureen said.

The clerk bowed and vanished behind one of the cases. He returned a moment later with a small box covered in black velvet. He placed it on the counter and opened it without a word.

The stone was about the size of Maureen’s fist. It was matte black, not reflective at all. The velvet felt warm against Maureen’s fingers, but the stone itself was so cold that it stung where it touched her skin. The dark particles in the air seemed to lean towards the stone, drawn by it. Like moths circling a lamp, they flew closer and closer before veering away at the last moment.

 Every dark particle within an arm’s length was bent towards the stone. *It sharpens your column,* Tally said. *I approve. Professionally.*

“Dark-school wizards fight each other for these,” the clerk said. “Every other elemental wizard wants one on his bench as well.” Then he said the price, his voice as mild as if he were discussing the weather.

The number landed like a tooth being pulled. Maureen didn’t allow her face to twitch, but she could feel her jaw clenching behind Richard’s weak chin. The wince travelled all the way down her spine.

*Magic stones, all columns: ~600 → ~150,* Tally said in her burial voice.

*They’re for spending,* Maureen told her silently.

*I know. I just like watching them go. Slowly. Tonight they are going fast.*

Maureen paid, and the stone disappeared into the Void Dimensional Pouch hanging from her waist. [Pouch: +1 Dark Element Energy Stone] The clerk put the empty box back together with a soft clap.

*Potions,* Tally said. *You’re an apothecary in a city full of wizards.*

*Time,* Maureen answered silently.

*We have time. The rest is just arithmetic.*

At the door, Maureen asked about Necro Flower and Purple Wood Snake tears. The clerk smiled thinly. “If you ever see either, Wizard, buy it before you finish your sentence.”

When she was back out on the street, Tally opened up a new line at the bottom of her display. [Void Eclipse, layer 2: 1 of 3]

*The next two will be the hard ones,* Tally said.

*I know,* Maureen agreed, and started to walk home to her own door.
