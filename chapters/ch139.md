Her throat swallowed. On its own. Again. And again.

It wasn’t nerves. It wasn’t fear.

It was the hunger of a slum girl, smelling the rich fat dripping from roast meat through the kitchen door. Her blood was running beneath her skin, hot and slow as magma.

She knew what was answering. The Pankora blood she’d taken during the rite. The essence of a Child of Purgatory. She had only taken that one bloodline. Made her a very young Child of Purgatory herself. Something about this Oran Olivia was calling to that bloodline. Calling it home.

[Pulse: 64 → 118.] [Skin heat: rising. Eyes: crimson at the edges.]

*Give me the rhythm.*

[One. Two. Three. Four.] Tally began counting the cadence of Silver Light breathing, three long breaths followed by one short.

[Pulse down. Jaw loose. Eyes down, they’re starting to show.]

Maureen forced her gaze to the polished marble floor and breathed when Tally told her to. Beneath her wide sleeve, she drove the nails of her right hand into the palm of her left, until the sting was the only sharp thing in the world. Wizard Richard’s plain face stayed slack and dull, as if the boy behind it were thinking about potion stock.

*Can you damp that Pankora heat through the Orb?*

*Yes.*

A cool pressure began to radiate from beneath Maureen’s breastbone.

[Orb: damping. Mana: 20.1 → 19.9.]

The crimson at the edges of her vision sank back, consumed by the black.

She let out a long, slow breath, and the surge of power settled into a low hum beneath her ribs. One thing was fixed in her mind, set like stone. This Oran was bound to the Purgatory World. Bound deep.

What frightened her even more than he did, though, was how close she’d been. Another heartbeat, and her feet would have carried her across the polished marble toward him, her mouth already opening wide. And none of the wizards in the gallery would have understood why.

*Please take it apart, Tally. Take everything I just recorded apart.*

Maureen lowered her eyelids, standing quietly at Ambrose’s elbow. She looked as any junior pharmacist might, waiting to be of use.

*Affirmative, Maureen. Filing now. I will have an answer for you before evening. Every source will be named.*

Oran was laughing with the other eastern wizards across the gallery. His black-and-gold robes rustled quietly against the white marble floor as he moved, the scent of his perfume carrying farther than his voice. The people around him were drawn closer, leaning forward as though toward a fire on a cold night.

Oran froze mid-laugh, his face stilled like glass. Before Ambrose said a single word, Oran turned his head. He looked straight at the two of them approaching.

*It took him 0.8 seconds to turn around, Maureen.*

*What did he feel?* Maureen asked silently.

*I have no data on the method used.*

*He felt you.*

Ambrose led Maureen over with a hand on her shoulder, obviously proud of his little genius. “Oran, come here, there’s someone you should meet. This is Richard. Wizard Richard. One of the rare geniuses in our Alliance, though he’d never tell you that himself.”

Maureen gave the smallest of nods. That was all the acknowledgment she made. Her palm throbbed from where her nails had dug in, and the hum beneath her ribs leaned forward toward the man before her, like a dog straining at the end of its rope.

Oran studied Maureen for a long moment after Ambrose’s introduction before he spoke. The charming smile returned to his face, just a fraction of a second too late. “Wizard Richard. A pleasure. I’m Oran. Oran Olivia, of the Olivia family.”

One point one seconds. That’s how long it had taken from Oran’s pause to when he said his name. Tally’s warm voice filled in the silence.

*He had to remember who he was.*

*Or decide.*

*Both columns,* Tally responded.

Tally matched Oran’s density against every sample in her databases. He read as an Official, but the density was thin and uneven, like a wound underneath a bandage. Then, as an afterthought in her own voice, she added, *I don’t have a file on any living Olivia. Jay, Frey and Barok are all dead.*

The young man standing at Oran’s shoulder caught Tally’s attention next. His eyes hadn’t left Oran even once since they entered the hall.

[Attendant: denser than Barok, denser than Maffie. Above all Phantom samples on file. No labelled Liquid sample. Estimated Liquid, by account.]

*Top threat in the room,* Tally announced.

The lamplight caressed the white polished marble floor, and the beeswax warmed the air. Oran leaned in closer, murmuring something polite to Ambrose. Beneath the expensive perfume that surrounded him like a cloak, Maureen caught the faintest scent of copper and fruit gone soft and rotten, stale smells tucked beneath the fragrance.

[Odour: partial match. Wraith Doll, Akd clan, Purgatory World. Confidence: low.]

*Alice,* Maureen thought back.

*Yes, an Olivia who smells like the doll that the Olivias might have sent you,* Tally responded. *I’m not saying it, I’m writing it down.*

Ambrose and Oran talked about the eastern delegation’s envoys bargaining for shares of the realm, and “Wizard Richard”, with his young man’s face and weak jaw, said almost nothing. Oran’s eyes kept sliding over to Maureen. They would meet her eyes for just a moment before skittering away, as though they’d accidentally touched a hot pan.

They parted with a courtesy bow, and as Maureen walked away from the eastern delegation, she felt Oran’s gaze between her shoulder blades all the way across the marble floor. The hum in her blood stayed focused on him until they were separated by one of the spiral’s pillars.

* * *

The long ramp wound around and down through the tower, wizards gliding by overhead, and the chill off the open doors rose up to meet them. It smelled of frost and the mews.

“Learning and taste, both,” Ambrose said. “You rarely see that combination in a young man from the East. Very rarely.”

*Charming people are the ones to count twice,* Tally said.

*I know,* Maureen thought silently.

“Very polished,” Maureen said aloud. That was all she gave Ambrose, because her mind was still back on the marble with the copper smell.

Ambrose stopped on the rampway. He looked at her for a moment too long. “It’s a long leave, Richard. Viska will carry the Fourteenth, but don’t make me sorry I signed it.” She bowed her head in acknowledgment, giving him no other response.

He turned and made his way back up the ramp, a cloud of camphor surrounding him, his white-gold robe swinging as he walked. Maureen stood alone on the outer steps, frosted stone beneath her feet, her breath smoking in front of Richard’s dull face.

Tally gave her the first result as promised. The surge matched the line from the bronze book about how the Totem grew by devouring bloodlines and exotic energies. *That’s appetite,* Tally said. *Not yours. The Totem’s.*

*It wanted to eat him.*

*No, Maureen. It wanted what’s in him. Medium confidence.*

Her stomach turned over as she remembered the Skoll hounds tearing the screaming shades apart in the valley. She swallowed something sour that had come up her throat. Then there was another sensation, cold and quiet beneath the disgust.

*Tally, you once told me you didn’t really want to know what was on the menu.*

*I did. I’m sorry, Maureen. But now it’s thinking about the menu for both of us.*

*Anything else?*

*Yes. The doll odour. Low confidence. And the thin, uneven density, consistent with an injury. Medium confidence. Here is the file.* [Oran Olivia: Purgatory note, low. Doll odour, low. Injury, medium. Attendant: top threat, unnamed.]

Maureen swallowed again, fighting down her own disgust. *What would it give me if I fed the Totem something like that?*

*I have no data, Maureen.*

*Then first we find out what he is.*

Maureen uncurled her fist, and four red crescents sat in her palm. They were already beginning to fade, for the body that the rite had reconstructed healed far more quickly than the old one.

* * *

She walked rather than hire a carriage. The gutter grates were already crusted with frost, the smoke from the burning coal was lying low across the city, hugging the rooftops. The mortals took their three paces back at the sight of her wizard’s robe, but no-one looked up.

In a cold and empty alleyway, as she passed the corner Tally’s all-round sensing brushed against something small and warm. There was a dense knot of dark-red particles hanging in the air about knee height. Tally noticed it before Maureen had even turned her head.

Maureen looked at the empty space, seeing only bare brick, frosted grate, and a single dead leaf. She couldn’t smell anything there either. The knot of dense, dark-red particles hung there, as though there was a stove there giving off heat, and nobody had bothered to light it.

Two streets later, in another quiet corner of a deserted lane, Tally found a second one.

[Density knots: 2. Source: none visible. Smell: none. No data.]

Maureen stopped and looked back along the way she’d come.

*The first knot is gone. No trail. I can’t see where it moved to either.*

*Things that sit in corners and wait.*

*I don’t like them.*

*Log them.*

*Yes, Maureen.* [Emilia: density knots, no data.]

34 Connpa Street opened its gate to her palm brand, and Ike was waiting for her in the hall.

“I will be away for some weeks,” Maureen said as she took off her robe and handed it to him. “Run the house as you usually would, but hold all letters and let no visitors past the gate.”

“Yes, Master,” Ike replied, his eyes never rising above her collar. The kitchen lay behind him, smelling of bread and banked coals. The household moved in the hush of people who knew only a quiet young wizard named Richard.

 Down in the cellar, the light from the lamp danced over the dry stone walls of the coal bay. The air smelled of coal dust and soot.

“Manas.”

The hidden chamber opened behind the coal bay, and Edward stood where she had left him. Tall, pale, with sunken eyes, the illusion of a gaunt young man played over his veilsilver frame.

As she always did when the chamber opened, Tally checked the Rental Centre ward-knot.

[Ward-knot: stable, ends at Gregory’s second ring.]

Then Tally read Edward’s core pattern and stored that as a baseline.

*Sound?* Maureen asked silently.

*As sound as I left him.*

Maureen nodded to herself, then ran one test. She gestured and Edward flowed into her Void Dimensional Pouch at her waist, vanishing from the cellar. She counted ten breaths before she reached into the pouch and drew him back out again. The puppet stood quietly on the cellar flagstones.

[Core: intact. Pouch transit: tolerated, 1 test.]

Tally added an “Edward (when stowed)” line to the pouch’s ledger.

*That’s one test,* Tally said.

*Yes,* Maureen replied silently. *One test is one test.*

*I’d like more before I trust it in a fight.*

*One is enough for the road.*

*And that’s where fights happen.*

Maureen made her decision. Edward would travel in plain sight— Wizard Richard’s gaunt, silent friend. She would keep the pouch in reserve, if she ever needed to hide him.

The puppet followed her up the stairs from the cellar to the kitchen passage. His footfalls were too even to be human, but Ike’s eyes flicked only once to the stranger’s boots, then returned to their place below Maureen’s collar.

With Edward at her side, Maureen crossed the frost-crusted streets to her room at the Alliance. The building was a separate lodging from the manor, and she packed quickly, leaving it tidy enough to tell no tales during her absence of some weeks. Before full dark she had passed through the city gates.

* * *

She waited off the road, in among the pines, with Edward standing quietly beside her. The needles of the trees were furred with frost. The scent of resin rose from them, strong and sharp and cold. Her breath steamed before Richard’s face.

The caravan came around the bend in the road, and Tally began counting for her. Riders, wagons, carriages pulled by horned beasts. Apprentices, drivers, riders. A few thin Officials, but mostly apprentices. The Oserroba Family. Denise among them, close to Phantom.

Maureen waited, watching. The caravan passed her, but did not stop. Denise’s head turned once, toward the trees, but only for a moment, and then away again. As they had planned it, Maureen followed, keeping a distance, with Edward behind her.

The breath of the horned beasts steamed in the chill air, smelling faintly of hot iron. The family crests picked out in silver and gold glinted on the lacquered doors of the carriages. The wheels turned in the frozen ruts of the road, cracking like knuckles.

When night fell, the Oserroba Family pulled off the road to make camp for the night. They lit fires, and Maureen waited until they were busy with their dinner before she found a place to stop. She remained well outside the circle of light from the fires. She leaned back against a pine trunk, feeling its cold bark against her back through her cloak.

The smells of roasting fat and pine smoke wafted to her on the wind. So did snatches of conversation— young voices, laughing at some joke. Someone was singing, badly, and a girl shrieked in mock outrage at whatever had been said.

Maureen listened for a long time. She’d never sat around a fire like that. Her shoulders released some of the tension that she’d carried down off the marble that afternoon.

Tally took over the watch. *Time to sleep. I’ll keep watch.*

Maureen sighed. *All right.* *How many stars can you see?* she asked.

*Eleven,* Tally said.

Maureen lay back, letting the frost-wet needles scratch at her hair. *How many now?*

*Fourteen,* Tally said as a branch blew in the wind.

*Just us?* Maureen asked quietly to herself.

*Just us,* Tally said.

*And Edward,* Maureen thought.

*And Edward,* Tally agreed. *He’s a very good listener.*

*He doesn’t have ears,* Maureen thought.

*Exactly.*

Maureen smiled, Richard’s borrowed mouth stretching slightly in the darkness. In the distance, the campfires burned, tiny pinpricks of light between the black trunks.

[Night: Edward, still. 2 passes, owl, 01:40. Ward drew 0.1. Mana: full.]

Tally’s morning report was waiting when Maureen opened her eyes. Before the light had begun to creep into the sky, she cast a small ward in the trees and recast Richard’s face behind it, taking an additional −0.4 from her mana pool.

*Crimson?*

*None.*

*Logged.*

In the grey morning light, Maureen and Edward walked into the caravan’s camp as the beasts were being harnessed for travel. Heads turned, and the young men and women of the Oserroba Family began to whisper about the two strangers who hadn’t been there yesterday.

Denise came out of her carriage and looked at the two of them, then at her family. “They’re my friends,” she said, leaving it at that.

Maureen saw the look in the woman’s eyes, the same look that Nightingale gave her whenever they met. The measuring, judging glance, the one that seemed to see down to a person’s soul.

The young people’s curiosity was not about the new arrivals themselves, but rather about their place in the world. An outsider? One the heir had chosen to favour? That made the newcomer someone important, possibly even an Official. One by one, the young men and women turned back to their harnesses and buckles.

*No one asked twice. Heirs have that effect on people.*

*So do Officials.*

*Not with old families. They’ve put Officials in the ground before.*

Edward took a seat on one of the wagon benches, sitting preternaturally still with his hands resting on his knees. No one asked about him either.

The caravan rumbled forward, heading toward the Oserroba Family’s seat, the frost on the road turning grey beneath the wheels. Maureen walked beside it, and the easy pace of the road slowly worked its way back into her legs.
