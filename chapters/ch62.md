The wind whistled over the sunken mounds. The red grit blew into her face, stinging her cheeks, and the smell of graves rose around her—wet clay and turned earth. She leaned against the boulder, its surface rough beneath her hand and crusted with lichen.

The dull ache from the broken Pupil-Sight link was already receding. It hadn’t been too painful since she hadn’t left a spirit brand on the Filth-Star Slate. Her breathing evened out with the Silver Light rhythm. Tally replayed the slate’s last relayed frames on Maureen’s display—the gravity spell, the fixed Devil Vine floor, the arrow volley. Very fine and deadly traps, Maureen thought silently. More so than any bred spell creature. Tally agreed with her.

Maureen studied the round mark that still glowed faintly in her palm. The Inheritance Mark, left after Tally decoded the line patterns. The figure in the manor had a Third-Class aura but had handled Official Wizard knowledge as if it were a trifle.

What’s nagging at you? Tally asked. The round mark. The monsters that match the black serpent by marker. The sulphur and fire-smoke odour I saved from the Fasol serpent.

None of those are an answer to what we found here, Maureen replied silently.

And I have no data on the unknown master of this realm either, Tally sighed.

Maureen looked at the dark tunnel opening before her. She wasn’t going to risk entering. She didn’t have an answer for the traps, and it was obvious this was no ordinary realm. She herself was still ten-odd days away from attaining her Third-Class rank. “Once I’m Third-Class and ready,” she said aloud, standing alone in the paupers’ graveyard southeast of Red Rock Town, “I’ll come back.”

Maureen closed her fingers over the mark, and the runes of her spell surfaced and the shimmering entrance shifted back into the appearance of the lichen-crusted boulder.

She placed her palm against the stone, feeling the chill and roughness of the rock through the greenish crust of lichen. The night’s dew clung damply against her skin.

That wasn’t enough for Maureen though. She crouched down and pulled out several casting components from the kit on her belt. In a few minutes, she had several more concealment spells layered over the site.

Tally compared the appearance of the boulder with its frame taken before Maureen dispelled the illusion. There were no differences visible between the two images. Through her particle sense, the new layers of spells felt seamless. Tally logged the bearing and step count back to town.

The sky to the east, over the ridge, was just beginning to turn a pale grey. Red mud caked thickly on the underside and treads of Maureen’s boots as she stood up.

Maureen nodded once to herself, then shifted into a faint black shadow that merged with the night. She turned and ran swiftly back towards Red Rock Town.

* * *

Sunlight streamed in through the warped shutters, breaking up into stripes across the red tiles on the floor. The scent of yesterday’s bread still lingered in the air, mixing with the smell of cold ash from the hearth.

Burton paused just inside the common room door when he saw the packed saddlebags stacked beside the party’s table. “Uh, Maureen? I, uh, was coming to tell you that you and your friends helped save a lot of lives last night. The Mayor wants to throw a banquet in your honour.”

Tally noticed the local’s voice rise half a tone.

I heard it too, Tally.

Maureen gently set her glass cup down on the table. Then she looked up at Burton and smiled. “That’s very kind of you Burton. However, we have urgent business back at home that we must attend to immediately. We cannot stay any longer.”

Her mind had already returned to the castle, and all the ripe Sodara fruit waiting for her there. Third-Class, and then… the realm.

Last night, after she had introduced herself as a Mystic, Maureen noticed the change that came over the Mayor’s face. He became even more fawning than before. She knew right then that he wanted something. As for Albert and the others, they had no interest in staying either.

Daphne’s needle clicked away as she sewed, while Albert wheezed into his lilac handkerchief. Aubrey rested his cane upright in his palm.

“But…” Burton tried again.

Maureen stepped forward, and firmly grasped Burton’s shoulder. Her fingers dug into the man’s muscle. “Enough, Burton. It doesn’t matter any more, does it?”

Burton’s mouth opened and closed soundlessly. The light went out of his eyes and his shoulders slumped down.

Then he brightened up again. “You asked where those monsters come from, didn’t you? From the southeast. And now, that bastard Ange wants to build an orphanage at the paupers’ graveyard, just outside town to the southeast.”

Tally displayed the bearing it had recorded last night.

I know, Maureen thought back silently.

“He must be planning on really giving them hell!”

“Yeah! That’s right!” Burton spat on the floor, just missing Maureen’s foot. “That bastard Ange wants to build an orphanage there so he can torment those poor kids, and train them to be thieves and robbers so he can fill his purse with their ill-gotten gains!”

For a brief moment, Maureen’s eyes became as cold as the icecaps of the north. Outside, one of the wheels on a passing cart gave a loud squeal.

“Is there anyone else in town who has shown any interest in that particular graveyard?” she asked lightly.

“Not that I know of,” Burton answered. “I mean, why would they? Ange picked it because it’s out of the way and the land is cheap. Other than that, you only see old vagrants and poor folk out there sometimes, looking for a place to rest when they die. You’ll also find Uncle Chuck from Jack’s Smithy, and old Nord the beggar who hangs around the corner of Redstone Street and… and…” Burton scratched his head. “I think that’s it. Yeah, that’s everyone.”

Tally placed each name and location into its own plain column. There were no additional comments or analysis, just the facts.

Keep them all please, Tally, Maureen thought silently. She continued her conversation with Burton a while longer before she left with Albert’s group.

* * *

They rode away from their detour, back towards the route they’d planned to take, through the larger towns, sleeping in comfortable inns. Maureen glanced once behind her, at the quiet red town slowly heating up under the morning sun, as the people began to make themselves visible and get on with their day. The red dust gradually gave way to a brown dust as they rode on, the sound of hooves again thudding on the road and the leather creaking around their bodies. As they stopped to take lunch, Maureen cursed under her breath with mock irritation that she had forgotten something in Red Rock Town. “You all wait here for me.”

Albert, Aubrey and Daphne only smiled at her. She was the one so eager to reach the city quickly, so the fact that she had forgotten something was perfectly plausible.

As Maureen rode back towards the Red Rock Town, Tally kept the list of names and places displayed on the edge of her vision. Add one name to the bottom of the list, Maureen told it silently, and Tally complied without asking which list or what name. The familiar red roofs rose again in front of her, standing out against the flat light of noon.

* * *

After a while, she rode back into the lunch halt.

“I’m sorry,” she said, shrugging apologetically. “I forgot something. Found it though. Something held me up.”

Tally told her how long she had been gone by its count, and that none of them had asked after her. Good, she thought silently.

Albert wheezed as he climbed back into the saddle, Daphne snipping off the thread with her teeth.

They did not ask her what she’d forgotten or where she had been, and they rode on towards the next town and its large inns, comfortable beds, hot water, and good food.

She closed her hand over the Cross Sword hanging at her waist.

The weapon gleamed as if new.

It’s clean, she noted silently.

Yes, you can still feel it, Tally told her.

That was all either of them said on the matter.

All those who had planned to go to the southeast graveyard were dead. Ange, Chuck of Jack’s Smithy, old Nord, the others. With Burton’s clear directions, each had been easy to find. Each had died quietly. It was unlikely that any mortal would ever be able to see through the concealment, but since they had planned to go there, they had to be killed. Just in case.

Burton too. He knew who she had asked after. He would hear of Ange’s death, of the deaths of old Nord and Chuck. He would think of the southeast graveyard. No one could predict what he might do.

Her hands did not shake as she held the reins. There was a tightness in her throat though and she would not swallow it away.

“Just in case,” Maureen said to the dead man under her breath. “So, Burton, don’t blame me.”

I will note this day in my logs, Tally said in its warm voice. Should I continue keeping the list of the dead?

Keep it.

She looked down at her hand which rested on her thigh, feeling the solid shape of the Cross Sword through her cloak. She believed that the realm’s place was utterly secret now. The early afternoon sun shone warm on the brown roadway, while the horses’ shadows ran short beside them, keeping up effortlessly.

“We may as well pick up the pace, Maureen,” Aubrey called out. His normally stiff voice was bright with good humour. “Let’s make for the town ahead and rest there.”

He has never sounded so cheerful, Tally commented in its own voice.

Let him be happy, Maureen thought back at it.

Maureen smiled and called back, “Coming!” before giving her horse a nudge and continuing on into the afternoon.
