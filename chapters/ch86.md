Freya’s lunge was already committed, the slender blade an indistinguishable smear of dark light, the smell of fallen blossoms—sweet with an edge of bruising— reaching Maureen’s nose a half-breath before the dark elf reached her. But Maureen Olsen was already turning. She’d been waiting for that smell for the past three days.

Freya looked into Jack’s face, calm, unmoved, and her blue-black eyes widened in shock. She tried to check the lunge, tried to twist herself away, but her weight was already committed, and she had no ground left to stop on.

Tally had the curse model running before Maureen asked. The line flashed across Maureen’s sight:

> Bind the blood. Now the name.

Maureen began to chant the words that Tally gave her, and Tally’s voice anchored her, steadying her through the chant, nudging her tongue back into place after the second word. *Got it,* Maureen told it silently.

*I know you do,* Tally replied. *I’m just holding your hand here, Maureen.*

The glass vial cracked open at Maureen’s belt. Out of Maureen’s own shadow, a huge human form ripped itself upward. It was taller than a man, and its features were still forming and failing to form as it threw itself over Freya. The face that failed to form had Freya’s features, and the mouth that would not open screamed in silence.

It made a sound like wind blowing through a split reed. Cold washed off of the wraith shade as it leaped at the dark elf. It rolled over her like black water thrown from a bucket. Freya screamed thin and high, like knives drawn across one another. Her scream went on and on.

[Mana: −1.5.] By Maureen’s own particle sense, Tally reported, Freya’s energy was jammed inside of her, choked on itself.

*It worked,* Maureen told it silently.

*It worked,* Tally agreed. *I don’t love that it worked.*

Freya stumbled backward, clawing at a darkness that had no edges to grip. Jack’s rasp rumbled out of Maureen’s throat, and the sound was flat and almost pleasant. “Did you think I spent these days doing nothing, Freya?”

Maureen followed after the dark elf, speaking in the same flat voice, reminding the elf of her debt, like a moneylender reminding a man of his signature. “You left your blood with me. Remember?” Maureen read the emotions on the dark elf’s face as they came apart. There was still pride in Freya, trying to stand up inside her. There was disbelief that a human could do this to her. And underneath both of those, the first raw edge of fear.

“Jack, you—”

That was as far as Freya got. The rest of her sentence was lost in the shade that swallowed it.

*Three days’ worth of back-draughts,* Tally pointed out with a smug note in its voice. It wasn’t wrong once. The dark sediment in that vial leaned toward Freya during every single camp and every single back-draught. For three days, without fail.

*I know that,* Maureen thought silently. *No need to be so smug about it.*

But Tally just replied, *Smug is my middle name. I’m saving all this for later, in a special column.*

Maureen opened her hand and said, “Amra-Keis!” A misshapen, sour-green projectile cracked against the dark elf’s ribs. Before Freya had even hit the ground, Maureen was speaking again. “Simons-Sado!” A deep red sphere the size of a fist burst out from her palm and caught Freya, knocking her spinning sideways into the ferns.

The heat of the fireball stung Maureen’s cheeks. Acid splashed against a pine tree’s bark, making grey bubbles as it ate through the wood. Freya tumbled across the scorched earth, leather armour steaming.

A pale halo of dark particles flared into life around Freya as she rose to one knee.

>Halo up. Ignore it. Fist.

Maureen muttered the Seal’s activation phrase under her breath. A twisting network of thorned red vines boiled up from her forearm. They twisted around her knuckles, forming a knobby fist of their own, while the counter appeared in the upper corner of her vision.

She was beside Freya in a single bound. Maureen’s fist lashed down on top of the dark elf’s head.

The halo did nothing at all.

Freya went flying. She spun through the air like a stone whipped by a sling. Maureen let the vine wrapping fall away at [Seal: 2.2 s].

The dark elf smashed into the ground. She tried to roll over and rise to her feet, to become a blur of motion once again, but Tally gave her the moment:

>Window. Now.

Maureen’s arm snapped forward. The Cross Sword ripped free of her hand, leaving a streak of silver behind. The blade flashed across the clearing and took Freya through the shoulder, driving her back and pinning her to the trunk of a pine tree.

Where the halo touched the steel, the blade began to blacken and pit, as if it had been dipped in acid. The guttering ring of dark particles ate away at the metal, inch by inch. Freya hung limp against the bark of the tree, her breathing shallow and wet.

* * *

For a long moment, all that could be heard was the sound of Freya’s laboured breath. The sweet air was thick with the smell of scorched blossoms, the coppery tang of Freya’s blood.

Maureen Olsen walked up to the base of the pine tree, cracking the ferns beneath her boots. The cold of the shade still clung to her shins.

Her own ruined sword was still in the tree. Tally’s display read: [Cross Sword: edge pitted along ~60%. Tip bent. Unfit.] And in her head, Tally added, *The halo ate at it as it passed through.*

*The Hammer Shop sword,* Maureen thought back, not bothering to make a sound with her mouth.

*Forty stones,* Tally said. *You once told me that it cost enough.*

*It did its job.*

Freya’s own slender cross sword lay in the grass where she’d dropped it. Without ceremony, Maureen Olsen picked it up.

The grip was still warm from Freya’s palm, wrapped with a fine leather of the deepest black. A dark light crawled along the edge of the blade, beading like droplets of oil on water. It felt lighter than her old steel sword in her hand, and it pulled her wrist forward by a fraction of an inch. She checked Tally’s display again: [Attack-type enchanted item. Black light: darkness, same school as yours. Balance: a finger forward of yours.] *It will feel wrong in your hand for a week or so,* Tally told her.

*Usable?* Maureen asked silently.

*Yes. Usable. Uncoated.*

Maureen looked up at Freya. The dark elf was small against the trunk of the tree, no larger than Maureen herself. A strand of black hair was stuck to a fair cheek. Maureen remembered the girl from the gathering, the one whose hand the Night Mother had held for so long before the doors opened.

Something rose in Maureen’s throat. She opened her mouth to say something to the dying girl, but found nothing worth saying. She closed her lips again.

Tally caught it a split second before she did. There was a breath behind the trees, a sound like coarse lumps of coal rubbing together, the creak of a large body coiling to strike.

> Strike incoming, by sound. Behind-right.

Maureen Olsen didn’t think about what to do next. Without hesitation, she reached over and snatched Freya’s arm, ripping the dark elf off the ruined blade and flinging Freya backward towards where the sound had come from. In the same motion, she spoke the phrase that would activate the Seal.

The blow aimed for the small of her back instead met the princess. There was a heavy wet crack from somewhere inside the dark elf’s chest. Freya burst into a fine spray of flesh, showering across the ferns. The force of the impact sent the body flying, smacking against the pine trunk. Freya was dead before her body hit the ground.

Something warm spattered against Maureen’s cheek. The smell of fallen blossoms suddenly filled the clearing, thick enough to choke on, like being in a funeral bower. Maureen didn’t move to wipe away the blood. For a full breath, Tally said nothing. Then, *Logged,* it finally said.

*No. Don’t.*

*But I only said one word.*

Red vines whipped out from Maureen’s body, hooking themselves around the dirt and yanking her back across the ground. She skidded backwards, the sword still in her hand, until she was far enough away from where the next thing would come from.

*The glyph on the back of your hand did not change during that action,* Tally noted. *It appears that nothing came to you from the Princess Freya. Whether members of her race possess glyphs is unknown. This unit has no data on the matter.*

The Seal’s counter was already burning its seconds away in the upper corner of her vision. Maureen came up into a crouch, Freya’s sword in her hand, watching the trees.

* * *

Beck stepped out of the trees where Freya had died. He was huge, his grafted arm as big around as a fully grown man’s waist, hanging all the way down to his knee. His eyes were red, his face slack with something caught between rage and joy.

The grenade burns from before still wept along his ribs and shoulder, but they were already halfway closed. The smell of cooked skin came off of him, carried on the wind. When he breathed, it sounded like a sack full of coal grinding against itself inside his chest.

[Seal: 3.4 s.]

*Those burns should have kept Beck down for an hour, minimum. Already they are almost completely shut. The rate of accelerated healing is off the charts. I am filing this under ‘faster than has ever been recorded.’ You might wish to be aware.*

*I can see that,* Maureen thought back to Tally, silently.

*I know you can. But I wanted to make certain you did.*

“Jack,” Beck said, savouring the word slowly. “Jack the Ripper. You hurt me.”

Maureen tilted her head just a little bit, considering him. “Out already, Beck? I didn’t think you had it in you.”

Then the corner of Jack’s mouth quirked up into a smile. “Thank you, though. I needed something loud to flush the rat out of the grass. You made the noise. I got rid of her.”

Maureen watched Beck’s expression as she talked. She saw the grievance in his face, the way a child would hold a grudge, and how it was swelling into the rage of a killer. His red eyes fixed on her throat. Her pulse did not quicken at the sight, and she noticed that fact.

Tally marked four figures standing still at the far end of the tree line. They were tagged by sight, their colours flashing in the corners of her vision. Blue Ring, Mist Labyrinth fog, blood-red robe with bone face, veiled white. All four have been standing there since the beginning of hostilities. They appear to be watching the situation.

*Let them watch,* Maureen told Tally silently.

“You killed her,” Beck said, “and now I kill you.”

Beck’s throat rolled obscenely, and a beast’s howl ripped out from between his lips. He jumped up into the air over Maureen’s head, his single green arm flinging wide.

Threads poured out from his arm, a torrent of green filaments, a rainstorm of cutting strands that filled the entire clearing. Countless individual threads, all hissing down through the air, slicing through the ferns and stitching them to the ground.

Warning. Seal at ceiling. Driving it past the array limit will fracture it. This action will cause permanent damage. By the catalogue. Proceed?

And then, quickly, while everything else was happening, Tally spoke in its own warm voice.

*The Seal wrapped you in its vines on the night of blood aboard the ship. It took Jay’s fireball for you. I know what the Seal has been to you. I wanted you to know that.*

Maureen’s free hand slid inside her inner pocket.

The Seal lay against her chest, warm beneath the cloth like it had been the day she took it off Derrick’s body. The thing whose tendrils crushed sea monsters in the dark corridor, the thing that held her upright as Jay’s fireball engulfed them.

*Yes,* she said to it silently.

*Confirmed.*

Every vine wrapped around Maureen’s body thickened. In less than a heartbeat they swelled to the girth of her wrist. They began to writhe, squirming like a nest of bloody snakes. A wet whispering rustle sounded from the vines, a noise Maureen had never heard them make before.

And then they went beyond every reach they’d ever had.

The vines rose from Maureen’s body like an upside-down waterfall. A torrent of red snapping and roaring upwards, catching Beck in midair, wrapping him from shoulder to heel.

[Seal: 9.5 s... 10.8 s... OVER.]

The counter ticked past its previous ceiling, turned red, and kept on going.

The vines heaved, swinging Beck down into the earth like a flail. The ground split with a shuddering crack. When the dust and debris settled into the air, a crater marred the clearing and Beck lay at the bottom. He coughed, his skin torn open by long strips of broken vine, blood running out of him in rivulets.

Maureen felt the force of the impact travel up through the soles of her boots. The air was thick with dirt and destroyed ferns, with the smell of iron and sap. It was the old smell of the armour, only stronger, as though something inside it had burst.

“Damn these!” Beck grabbed the vines in both hands, trying to rip them apart as he had before. “These filthy vines!”

They did not snap under his grip. Frustrated, he heaved again, and the expression on his face changed as he realised that these were not the vines he thought they were. Maureen appeared beside him, flashing across the distance to stand on the lip of the crater.

Beck twisted his head around to look at her. A smile broke through the blood covering his face. “You came close. You’re dead. Dead!”

“The dead one here,” Maureen said, “is you.”

Beck’s right arm swung at her, the massive green limb as wide around as a man’s waist. Maureen swayed away from the blow, pushing off with her calf muscles, appearing behind his waist. The slender sword flashed down, sinking into Beck’s back and tearing an exit hole as wide as a fist.

There was a sound like a snake’s hiss from the wound, and she felt her wrist jerk forward another finger’s width, further than she expected, before her body stopped it. Hot blood poured down the blade, ran over her hand.

Beck roared, and then he laughed. “Useless, Jack! You think my graft was just an arm? I took more than that from the creature.”

Around the ragged tear in his back, the muscle began to writhe and twist, knitting itself closed even as Maureen watched.

> Wound closing. 1.4 s. Four will fit.

“Useless?” Maureen smiled. “Is that so?” She reached into the bandolier and pulled out four of the black shells, their plain iron dull in the grey light. She shoved them into the hole, one after another, muttering the trigger word with each. “Kerrash. Kerrash. Kerrash. Kerrash.”

Her hand was buried inside him up to the wrist, in a world of heat and slickness and moving flesh. Even as she pulled her hand free, the skin around the hole was crawling shut, sealing over her knuckles, leaving the iron eggs inside.

From the treeline, a man’s voice cracked across the clearing. “No, Jack, don’t—” The bone-masked apprentice had begun to move towards them, but too late.

Maureen pushed off against the ground and sprang back across the crater, covering the distance between them in the space of a heartbeat. The moment her feet left Beck’s body, all the vines disappeared. They were gone as if they had never existed. Beck stood alone in the hole.

[Grenades: hand 4 / pack 54.]

> Sealed. Near the heart.

Then Tally gave her the information she hadn’t asked for. Through the pocket, by sight, the Seal’s glow was a jagged line of light. Not dim. Broken. Uneven.

*That’s not low,* Tally said. *That’s damaged.*

*I know what I did,* Maureen replied silently.

*I know you know,* Tally responded. *But I’m keeping my file open anyway.*

The hole in Beck’s back had closed over. He placed his good hand flat against his back, felt the four hard lumps beneath the new skin near his heart, and for the first time, Maureen saw naked terror on his face. Against her ribs, the pocket was cooler than it had ever been before.

She stood back, regarding him with Jack’s calm, expressionless face. Her voice was quiet, perfectly level. “Beck... it’s over.”
