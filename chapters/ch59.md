There were bodies strewn across the trampled grass; the air was filled with the coppery smell of blood slowly cooling mingled with the scent of oiled leather. The three companions, Albert, Aubrey and Daphne, stood a little way back by the horses. Their original plan had been simply to get away. Now all the bandits were dead.

“Maureen,” Albert wheezed as he walked towards her, trying to force a smile onto his round face.

Aubrey and Daphne nodded at her again and again.

They keep looking at you then quickly looking away, Tally told her.

I’d noticed, she told it silently.

They know about Kane but not this.

Maureen walked over to the lone tree where the last bandit’s body lay. She pulled her new Cross Sword free and wiped the blue sheened edge on the man’s sleeve. Her hands were perfectly steady, her breathing already slow and regular. In the last rays of the sun the flies were settling on the bodies of the fallen.

She smiled at them.

“All right, let’s clear this up. Don’t forget we still have a road ahead of us.”

The three hurriedly began searching the bodies for anything of worth, finding some magic stones, a few wizardry ornaments and several sharp blades. These were all the band had possessed. Tally displayed their value in a bracketed list on Maureen’s display. Wizardry ornaments first, based on what it guessed they could fetch at a market, followed by the magic stones, then blades last.

“I insist, after your display of strength you should choose first,” Albert said, the others nodding agreement.

Maureen understood the deference to her strength, accepted it and looked through their finds taking only the magic stones. Tally counted thirty eight as they dropped into her pouch.

Well done, Tally said, you have left money lying on corpses again!

I’ve had enough corpses, Maureen said silently.

Daphne’s needle case clicked shut as the three divided up the rest of the spoils, carefully not meeting Maureen’s eyes.

* * *

The hooves made a knocking sound as they crossed a stretch of dried clay. A length behind her came the wheeze from Albert.

“Maureen? I am so sorry,” Daphne said as she rode up beside Maureen.

“Sorry for what?” Maureen asked, holding her reins loosely in her hands.

“For being so incautious that Sawin was able to follow us.”

Tally read the woman’s voice pitch and body language from the way she held her reins and reported no stress tells. This is not proof of anything, Tally added.

Logged, Maureen thought back.

“Don’t worry about it Daphne, it was a small matter.”

Maureen’s calm response reassured the three, who had been watching her nervously for any sign of anger. They turned instead to comforting Daphne until she smiled again. Conversation began to flow back along the line of horses as they rode, with even some laughter, the first since the ambush.

“We will be delayed reaching the city where we booked the most luxurious room in the best inn, Viscountess Olsen,” Aubrey said formally.

“There is a small town not too far ahead from here, Red Rock Town,” Albert said from atop his tall warhorse. “We should be able to find lodgings there for the night. That is if you don’t object Maureen?”

“Fine by me,” Maureen said, and that settled the matter.

Red Rock Town? Tally said. I don’t have an entry for it on my map.

The others know this area, Maureen thought back at Tally, and they headed off along the road, passing the other horses, to take their place leading the way.

The dust thrown up by the hooves, as the light failed, had darkened to an old brick red.

* * *

Everything in Red Rock Town was red. Every house was made from red coloured stone, every tile was red, and even the road was red, as though the town existed near the base of some volcano. Red Stone, Red Tile, Red Road, Tally said, noting the palette dryly. Unkind, but accurate, Tally, she told it silently.

A number of tall trees grew here and there around the town, and while their trunks were red and their leaves thick, they were quite a distance apart. There was nothing green on the ground between them, just red earth. The wind blew across the bare ground, rustling the leaves of the trees.

A young man came running out from the town waving and shouting. “Halt! Hold up!” He stopped in front of them breathing hard. “Are you intending to enter Red Rock Town?”

“We’re travellers from far away,” Maureen said politely. “We’re looking for somewhere to stay for the night.”

“Oh,” he said scratching his head. “Sorry. I’m Burton.” He looked them up and down. “Well, if you don’t mind my saying, Red Rock Town hasn’t been safe lately. Monsters keep showing up, no one knows where from. The town guard does what they can, but they aren’t really equipped for this sort of thing. The townsfolk are talking about asking the city to send some guards. You probably shouldn’t enter unless you have to.”

Tally noted that his face fell at the mention of the guard.

Honest. Frightened, Maureen thought silently.

Both, by sight, Tally said.

“These are wild beasts, no more,” Aubrey said with authority. “Now stand aside.”

Burton hesitated then shrugged.

“This way then. Come on, I’ll show you to Aunt Sophie’s, she has the best inn in town.” He set off again chatting all the way.

Tally kept a count of the turns it took to get to the inn while she exchanged a few words with the young man. At the inn Albert and his group insisted that she take the best room. She accepted without a word of protest. The inside of the inn was lit with tallow candles, and smelt of tallow and roasting meats. The plastered walls had been painted red.

* * *

Her plate held cream and honey, steak, young lamb and apple pie. A silver knife lay next to the plate. Burton sat next to her, stuffing white bread into his mouth. “At first it was easy for them, but they just kept getting stronger.”

Fourth slice, Tally noted.

He’s hungry, Maureen thought.

So am I, Tally agreed.

“If they come back I don’t think they can hold them,” Burton said.

Maureen cut up the young lamb on her plate. “It will be solved sooner or later.”

No help here. Burton will not be part of the solution, just the problem.

“That Ange hid when we needed him most! Gordon is only little and Ange bullies him all the time, and other kids too. Even some of the old people he bullies.”

Maureen smiled but said nothing as she dipped a spoon in the thick honey.

Outside there were roars from beasts and screams from people. Feet ran past the open door of the main hall.

Tally listed the sounds outside by type. Four beasts. Street outside. Closing fast.

“Careful, Maureen!” Burton shouted as he jumped up from his seat, “The monsters are here!”

She shook her head but remained seated.

A gust of wind suddenly blew through the open doorway, flattening every flame in the tallow lamps.

> Dive. Throat. Left.

The line flashed into her vision before her conscious attention could focus on it. A bird shot through the door like a lightning bolt, beak aimed at her throat. Her wrist flicked, a flash of silver, and it fell to the wooden floorboards with a bloody spray.

Maureen lowered the Cross Sword, frowning at the small body.

“So this is what you call a monster?”

The hall was silent except for the sound of Burton’s white bread hitting the floor where he had dropped it. The sparrow’s beak glistened wetly, dark red.

Tally flagged the creature as unknown, but with traits similar to one on file. First was smell, sulphur.

Compare to database.

While Burton leaned out the door, trying to see what was happening in the street outside, Maureen wiped the blood off her blade, directing it into a tube from her belt kit, then added a reagent.

The tube turned rust-brown, then there was a slow black precipitate, its residue tickling her particle sense. Warm grit.

A pale-blue flicker behind her eyes.

> Marker match, Fasol serpent sample: 67%.
> Odour match: 71%.
> Strongest line: sulphur.

The closest match was the giant black serpent from her first mission. Tally had filed that creature as a 78% marker match with the Canes Black Rope Snake, which was a snake at most a metre long.

I don’t think a sparrow has any business being on the same shelf as a snake like that, she thought to Tally.

It didn’t, Maureen Olsen, and there it is.

Her eyes lowered, fingers tapping a slow, even rhythm on the wooden table.

Burton came back inside. “What was it? Are you alright?”

“I was just lost in my thoughts,” she said, “and never imagining something so fierce could be the size of a sparrow.” She laced her fingers together, stretching them out in front of her. “Burton, could you tell me everything you know about these monsters?”
