The Fourteenth Potion Group’s refining assignment was complete, the materials drawn from Ambrose’s own reserve. The last vial settled into clarity as Maureen racked it, the entire batch intact, as if no one had ever tried to starve it.

The workroom smelled of camphor and glass cooling, and Maureen’s fingertips were again faintly stained green at the nails. She noticed that she liked the stain, in the way that Jing Sheng had liked her own.

Ambrose entered without knocking, the hem of his white-gold robe whispering against the flagstones. “How is it that a group leader’s allocation can simply vanish between my stores and your bench? In my department, of all places.”

Maureen Olsen, Wizard Richard, glanced up from the settling last vial. His silver hair was neatly combed, his expression courteous and concerned. Tally noted his pulse jump slightly as he said the word vanish. *He’s not worried about you, Richard. He’s worried about his reserve.* *Good,* Maureen thought back. *That is exactly why I’m going to keep it small.*

“A clerical slip, head. Master Viska has already corrected the error,” Maureen said in Richard’s flat, low voice. “Thank you for making the stock available to us.” She offered nothing more, and Ambrose studied her for another long breath before nodding and letting the matter drop.

When the sound of his footsteps faded away, Tally spoke up. *You could have told him it was the White Tower.*

*Do you want him to feel like he owes you a war?* Maureen asked silently.

*That is very kind of you.* Tally logged the interaction under kindness.

*No,* Maureen corrected, *logged under tidiness.*

She capped the last vial, wiped the slate bench clean until it shone with bare stone, and walked home through the tract reserved for wizards. The late afternoon air bit at her borrowed jaw.

* * *

Behind her ward, she let Richard’s face go, and the lamp-glass showed her own cropped black hair and pale skin again. While she waited for the ink to dry on her brewing notes, Maureen reviewed the day’s work. As she reached out to blow out the single tallow lamp that lit her room, she stopped.

The Bloodcoat token lay on her desk, still pulsing fast and blood-red in the lamplight. Like a heartbeat after a run.

Maureen rested a thread of spirit against it, and the job text rose from the paper in red script.

Gathering Tonight

One Task Only

Reward: Necro Flower

Tally pulled up Maureen’s posted commission from the guild’s job-board and laid it beside the job text, line by line. She highlighted the result.

[Match: commission 1. Exact.]

*That’s the tell,* Tally said. *Every seat can read that board.*

*It’s bait,* Tally noted.

*Yes. I know.*

*You’re still going?*

*It’s bait made out of the one thing I need.*

*Isn’t that how all bait works, Owl?* Tally asked with a hint of impishness.

Maureen felt a cold smile pull at her mouth in the dark. She could see her greed as plainly as anything else. She had absolutely no intention of arguing with it.

Tally built a single line, then stopped. [Void Eclipse, layer 2: 2 of 3. Missing: Necro Flower.]

The missing piece of layer two was sitting in someone’s trap.

Tally pulled up the raid contract and checked it over. [Raid non-aggression: expired 3 days ago. Bindings: 4 → 3.]

*He waited until the clock ran out,* Tally said.

*Patient,* Maureen thought.

*Patient men plan,* Tally replied. *Those are the most dangerous kind.*

Maureen took the plain black wool robe and the full-face black lacquer mask out from the pouch, leaving Freya’s slender sword inside. She was following Night Owl’s rules: no Purgatory Fire, no sword, eyes black. With the robe on and the mask in her hands, she looked at herself in the mirror.

Tally laid her pale-blue muscle map over the mirror, and Maureen rebuilt Night Owl’s broad cheekbones and dragged-down mouth. [Mana: 16 → 15.6.] The cartwheel-on-gravel voice slipped once, but settled on the second sentence Maureen tried.

Tally watched carefully as Maureen put the mask on, checking through the eyeholes as the lacquer covered her face. *Black. Good,* she said.

*Keep watching them all night, please,* Maureen thought silently.

*I always do.*

Maureen left by going over the garden wall instead of through the gate. The frost crunched under her boots as she went, the token pressed against her ribs, warm and quick like a second, smaller heart.

* * *

The manor was lived in. A woman laughed from behind the wooden panelling somewhere, then crockery clinked in a kitchen far away, and a dog barked twice before being shushed. As though murder were being planned in the attic of a regular household.

The members came in one by one until eight masked figures stood or sat around the long table besides Maureen herself. Nine black robes altogether. More than Maureen had ever seen at a single gathering. She felt their number before she counted it.

Tally took the roll call by density and body shape rather than mask, laying a ring of amber or red over each figure that came into Maureen’s sight.

[Black Bear: match. Bat: match. Crow mask: body two fingers taller, density wrong. Fox mask: wrong gait. Masks not on file: 4. Nightingale: absent.]

Maureen looked at the empty space where Nightingale’s flattened breathing – the breathing of a woman – should have been, and kept her own breath slow and even beneath the lacquer.

*Two of those we don’t know are dense,* Tally said. *Barok-and-Maffie dense. Phantom State compared to the Barok sample. And... the Crow and the Fox are dead, I think.*

*How sure?*

*Not sure, Maureen. Just the bodies beneath the masks.*

“You’ve all read the token,” Bat said, his voice muffled by the bat mask, but still carrying the warm timbres that Maureen recognised from the Alliance library. Her suspicion of Gus was still high confidence, but still short of proof. “Tonight we put out Burning Flame. A small house. One or two Officials at most. No trouble, surely?”

Burning Flame. The name clicked in Maureen’s memory. A tiny order, but one that had crossed the High Ring White Tower again and again in the last few months. The head of Burning Flame had once even defied Wenger to his face.

*Corridor hearsay,* Tally noted.

*It’s enough of a motive for Gus,* Maureen thought back.

 Within the room, the draughts from outside slid around the edges of the shutters, making the flames of the tallow lamps dance and smoke.

The heads around the table nodded, one after another. Too smoothly, too quickly. Like men agreeing to a price already paid.

“Everyone feels... off tonight,” Black Bear said into the quiet. In the lamplight, it was as if the flames themselves were leaning away from him.

“Off?” Bat asked lightly. “Same masks as ever, Bear. What’s the difference?”

There was a smile in Bat’s voice, but it was the kind of smile that went no further than the lips. Certainly it didn’t extend to the eyes beneath the lacquer. Or anywhere else, for that matter.

Black Bear didn’t answer, but he did look across the table at his companion. The other wizard met his gaze, and held it. A single glance, passing between two men who knew that something was wrong, even if they had no proof of what it was.

*He feels it as well,* Tally’s voice came in Maureen’s head. *His eyes went directly to his companion. The one man at this table that Black Bear trusts.*

Maureen didn’t answer, and Tally didn’t push it, merely logging the glance in her memory.

Then Black Bear’s gaze moved on, to Night Owl. The figure sat unmoving, as though carved from stone. Then Black Bear looked down at the table. “It’s nothing. Let’s go.”

The chairs scraped back. Maureen rose last, her hands hanging loose at her sides. Once again, the old choice sat within her chest, just as it had upon the ship with Carey. She could warn them. And once again, she chose to remain quiet.

* * *

They mounted their horses outside the city walls, the beasts’ blue-green scales glimmering like old verdigris in the night. The horses’ hides were cold under Maureen’s gloves, and their breath smelled of hot iron. Frost glittered in the ruts of the road, cracking beneath the beasts’ hooves.

As they rode, Tally gave her forecast.

*Medium-high confidence,* Tally said. *The hirelings will move when you ask for the reward, not before. They will wish to see the fight completed first.*

*Then I will spend no more than I have to.*

*Two fireballs should be sufficient,* Tally said. *Look busy.*

Burning Flame’s compound sprawled across a low rise, one hall after another stretching out behind and around. Paper lanterns drifted in the slight breeze between the eaves, flickering like fireflies too lethargic to fly. Voices sounded from deep inside, too muffled to make out, but every now and then someone would laugh, breaking up into peals at some joke that Maureen would never hear the punch line of.

“This is it,” Bat’s voice came muffled from behind his mask. “Go.”

The black robes around Maureen broke apart, becoming running shadows. They poured towards the walls of the compound.

Maureen watched Bat’s back, the too-smooth leap of the five men hired for this purpose. One corner of her mouth curled up beneath the lacquered mask. A smile that no one could see. Then she followed them inside.

“Who goes there?” came shouts from the halls.

“You dare walk into Burning Flame?”

The shouts broke off into screams almost as quickly as they began.

> Apprentice, left, shielding badly.

Tally called out targets in short, cold lines.

> Two, doorway, low.

Maureen responded with two Blast Fireballs and nothing else. [Mana: 15.6 → 13.4]

Between casts, Night Owl had to look busy. Hands raised, the sweeping robe, a step forward towards whoever was running away. She had to look just as busy as the other wizards. Just as active. Even though the hirelings were doing most of the killing.

The two Official Wizards from Burning Flame came out together, trying to hold the central hall. They lasted only a few exchanges against nine wizards casting spells at once. Both went down screaming, their apprentice followers not lasting nearly as long.

> Fifty-seven.

*I’m counting the dead,* Tally told Maureen. She wasn’t telling Maureen who was dead or alive. Just giving a number. The same way Maureen had asked for it, long ago.

A breath later, Maureen thanked her silently, keeping her eyes on the hands of the five wizards they’d hired.

The paper lanterns caught fire one by one, then all at once. The compound went up with a roar, lacquered beams spitting fire, wet wool steaming. A section of the roof collapsed inward with a groan that went on for ten seconds, sparks erupting into the sky like a fountain.

The nine wizards drew back to the edge of the light, standing motionless in their black robes. They watched the ruin burn, like a ring of ghosts beneath the night sky.

Maureen began to drift away, moving a few paces at a time towards the darkness at the edge of the burning compound. An ancient oak tree stood there, its branches bare and skeletal in the winter chill. No one saw her go, because everyone was watching Bat.

* * *

Black Bear turned slowly and held out his hand. “Your job, Bat,” he said. “It’s done. The pay was a Necro Flower. Let’s have it.”

Bat tilted his head and his voice warmed just a fraction, becoming something else entirely. “The flower? Of course, Bear. Right now.”

Even before either man moved, Tally’s Mirror Pupil caught the structure of spells charging within two chests of hired wizards. Without prompting from Maureen, the lens painted a line of display with faint red:

> Charging. Two. Black Bear and the one beside him.

Maureen’s eyes stayed black as pitch, her hands still at her side.

*I could shout,* Tally said quietly.

*You could,* Maureen replied silently.

*Then I’m logging that you could have,* Tally said. And the Carey memory sat like a stone in Maureen’s chest, something she had swallowed long ago.

For one breath, Black Bear’s shoulders relaxed, then his body exploded in a fountain of blood. He looked down at the great wound torn across his chest, staring at it silently as if it had happened to someone else. Beside him, his companion let out one sharp cry of pain as the same wound was torn through his own body, then swayed back and forth twice before collapsing heavily onto the ash. He did not take another breath.

Black Bear tried to speak, his grey lips forming the words “Bat, you—” but nothing came out. Bat lifted a hand, and the particles of cyan wind in the air suddenly thickened into a blade. “Die, you fool,” he said, and sliced Black Bear in half at the waist.

*He struck a member,* Tally said, *and the guild oath binds you to avenge it.*

*He’s about to be avenged,* Maureen answered silently.

*Does it count if someone else does it?* Tally asked.

*The oath can argue with the man who did,* Maureen told her.

*A wind blade,* Tally said, puzzled. *The same man moved as shadow inside the White Tower. Two schools on one body, and no data on how.*

Maureen watched Bat look around the ash, searching for a fallen Night Owl. Then he found no one. Then Maureen spoke from above him, perched upon a branch of the oak tree. She spoke in Night Owl’s grinding voice.

“You do like your games, Gus.”

He stiffened, hearing his own name. The firelight flashed across the edge of his bat mask and Maureen saw his weight rock back onto his heels for an instant before he caught himself.

Gus tore off the mask, his handsome face flushing. “The job post was my idea,” he said, turning the shock into a boast. “I built it from your commission. I made sure you’d be the only one to bite on the flower. I found those seats too, and made sure they were emptied quietly. A few less rats in Emilia, and I threw Burning Flame in for free.”

Tally filled in the missing details through inference as Gus talked: the board posting the job, the masks worn by the wrong bodies, the trap timed to the lapsed clock.

*He also named the guild seats,* Tally added, *using some White Tower method I have no data on. This is now a standing risk to Night Owl.*

*Noted,* Maureen thought.

Gus turned abruptly to face the hirelings. “Frans! You swore she’d be dead in the first breath! Why is she standing in a tree?”

Frans ignored him. He and the other four slowly removed their masks and threw back their hoods. Frans had brown hair and bright, sharp eyes. He looked up at Maureen perched in the oak tree as if he had scented something rare upon the wind, and he smiled.

“Save your breath, Gus,” Maureen said, unhurried. “You hired them. You don’t own them. And you’d better mind yourself.” Her masked gaze moved slowly across the five bare faces.

Gus spun back to face the hirelings. His voice cracked with fury. “Frans! What are you waiting for? Kill her, do you hear me, you—”

Frans shouted, “You’re so annoying!” and Gus flew backwards, spitting blood. He hit the ground with a hole torn clean through his chest, white energy particles evaporating from Frans’s open palm.

Tally ran the white particles against her entire file and could find no match for a school. *New entry,* she thought. *On file, unknown, dense.*

*Give me Richard’s face under there.*

*It would be 0.4 mana to be cruel, Maureen,* Tally answered.

*Just make sure I’m understood.*

Tally ran through the muscle map, showing Maureen how to move each facial muscle to make it appear as if Richard’s face was beneath the mask.

[Mana: 13.4 → 13.0]

Richard’s jawline, black eyes, and five witnesses, all hired.

All of them die tonight.

*Then count them with me,* Tally said, and painted [Witnesses: 5] in the corner of Maureen’s sight.

Gus turned his head on the ash, watching Owl lift the black lacquer away. Beneath the mask was the plain, weak-jawed face he had tried to recruit in the library.

“Richard... you... you’re Owl...” his throat clicked, and his eyes widened, and then his head lolled sideways and he was gone.

*Final pulse,* Tally logged, then closed the Bat file inside her system. *He knew it was you, Maureen. That’s what you wanted.*

*Yes.*

Maureen’s mouth did not change, and her pulse did not even flutter. The grey ash kept falling between her and the five strangers below, like a curtain that no one had drawn yet.

Frans’s palm was still fading from white. Maureen narrowed her eyes, curiosity elbowing calm out of the way for a moment. She tilted her head. “Black Wizards? Are you Black Wizards?”

A new file opened without being asked. Black Wizards. Two Phantom samples. The witness counter remained pinned to the corner of Maureen’s sight. Five.
