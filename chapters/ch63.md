It was a grey winter afternoon when they passed through Wintercold's gate and entered the square. A haze of coal smoke hung over the frosted roofs, the packed snow squeaked beneath the horses' hooves, the horses' breath steamed in the cold air, and the fur sellers shouted their wares to all who would hear. Nothing had changed since last she was here. Yet everything looked new.

Albert wheezed out a long breath and declared, "I swear, I'll never set out on another journey unprepared."

“I couldn't agree more,” said Aubrey.

“I think I'm going to have to go over all my accounts,” Daphne said, her needle flying through a piece of cloth as always. “If I leave the shop girls alone too often they steal silver from me. They're all right when I'm there, but I bet they were picking away at my tailor shop while I wasn't there.”

Maureen smiled and said little. The cold air bit at her ears. Her thoughts were already on the road ahead, the road that led to the castle and her third Rune Array.

A smell reached Maureen before the man did, a heavy perfume. Then there were sounds, boots squeaking across the packed snow. “Maureen! Albert!” a cheerful voice called out.

The perfume belonged to Blake, a handsome middle-aged man dressed in fine clothing, with a neatly trimmed beard.

He's added a fourth layer of rose and ambergris, Tally said in its warm voice.

Stop smelling him, Maureen thought back silently.

I don't smell him, Tally said smugly, I don't have a nose. I just smell what you do.

“Blake,” said Albert.

“Have you heard the news?” Blake asked. “While you were gone, the most beautiful lady came to Wintercold. A Third-Class Wizard Apprentice. I questioned her myself, and not once did she pause for a moment before replying. She knew everything and could answer any question put to her. She was looking for someone, and I wondered if you might know anything about—”

I'm filing the gossip next to the unknown Third-Class from the Red Rock realm, Tally said. No data links the two of them.

Keep them close, anyway, Maureen thought back.

All right, Tally said, impishly. My shelves are always much more orderly than your room.

Maureen looked over at Blake once. “If I hear anything, I'll be sure to let you know.”

She bade good-bye to all of them, waving once, then rode off.

Behind her, Maureen heard Blake mutter, “What a rude little thing she is.”

Then she heard the awkward laughter of people who had seen her in action.

* * *

Maureen rode up to the gate. The guards straightened as she approached, then saluted smartly as she passed through. The faint smell of woodsmoke and horse dung reached her nose, and she could see frozen ruts in the yard where the mud had been churned by the passage of carts and animals. Her steward, Old Hank, met her just inside the gates.

“My lady,” he said with a bow.

Maureen nodded and dismounted. She strode past him into the great hall and seated herself.

“All is well, my lady,” Old Hank said. “Captain Jess has dealt with a band of marauders in the vicinity. They were causing trouble for the local farmers. No casualties among our forces, but they did expend some weaponry. Captain Jess will be along later to make his report. Now, if we could turn to finances . . .”

Maureen waved a hand dismissively.

There's a ledger under his arm. It's thicker than when you left, Tally said.

Yes, I know. That's why I bought him, Maureen thought back.

It has receipts! Tally said approvingly.

Maureen sat in her chair, the banked fire ticking quietly behind her. A draught played among the floor rushes, making them rustle. Old Hank stood at her elbow, the ledger closed, patiently waiting for her attention.

“Is Marquis Umas still sending us prisoners and vagrants?” she asked.

“Not as many as before, my lady,” Old Hank replied. “He has sent most of what he could find. I am sorry if this is inconvenient.”

“His envoy had another suggestion,” Old Hank continued. “He said that there were more living people available if we needed to wage war. He also suggested that our neighbour, Marquis Dinan, would make a fine target.”

Maureen held up a hand, interrupting him mid-sentence. “I’m only here to finish Adrian’s task. That is all.”

Maureen sat thinking for a moment, the fingers of her right hand tapping out a slow rhythm on the polished surface of the long oak table.

“How many bandits did Jess bring in?” Maureen asked.

Old Hank looked startled at the sudden change of topic. “About two hundred, my lady.”

On her display Tally helpfully displayed two lines of text side-by-side:

[Umas, last shipment: ~30]

[Jess, bandits: ~200]

That’s a simple sum, Tally said with its customary dryness. I like simple sums.

Yes, that would be enough, Maureen thought back.

Maureen stood up. “Send them all to the back mountain, by the old rules. Understood?”

I saw his lips move before he spoke, Tally noted.

Yes, so did I, Maureen replied silently.

Old Hank lowered his head. “Yes, my lady.”

* * *

In her room she changed into her black hunting gear, grabbed a small hand mirror and a length of black cloth, then walked out of the castle towards the back mountain. The frost crunched under her boots as she walked, the black pines dripped moisture onto the path, and her breath smoked slightly in the thin evening air. Anvil trap, wall trap, pit trap, dart trap, needle trap. All set and untouched, Tally reported.

Good, Maureen responded silently as she passed the traps.

The guards left their bound captives at the mouth of the cave then made their way back down the mountain. It was easier for Maureen to just haul the bandits and other prisoners herself. Two at a time she grabbed them by the collar and pulled them inside, dumping them on the cave floor. Most of the bandits and vagrants just stared with slack-jawed faces at the carnivorous tree. Maureen smiled grimly at them.

“All right, let’s begin.”

She gave one of the screaming prisoners a kick, sending him into the writhing octopus-like limbs. The fruit at the crown of the tree glowed faintly and seemed to swell just a little.

Sucking and wet wriggling sounds filled the cave. She kept her eyes on the fruit.

Tally opened up a single line. [Day 1] Nothing more followed and she did not ask it to elaborate. Over the following days the tree grew to enormous size, its limbs thickening till they were as large around as pythons. They twisted and writhed without rest. Tattered clothing and what was left of people lay scattered across the stone floor. Each morning Tally would advance the day line by one. She never asked it to show her more than that and it never offered. Finally the last batch of fruit ripened. An odd curve appeared on her lips and a strange light entered her eyes as Maureen reached out and plucked the fruit, popping it in her mouth.

* * *

The cave was quiet, the stone bed cold beneath her clothes. The limbs of the tree moved slowly, almost imperceptibly, in the very edge of her vision.

It had been several days since the last fruit.

Maureen sat cross-legged upon the stone bed, meditating but with her eyes open and fixed on her face in the hand mirror propped up in front of her on a rock. Tally, as always when she meditated, whispered the count of her breaths. She allowed it.

Watching the mirror intently, Maureen saw points of light begin to flicker on her forehead. After a moment a faint Rune Array began to take shape between them.

A hum began to fill the cave, and she felt the air around her stir with the energy of surging particles. Darkness and fire energy danced in through the stone walls, filling the cave till they crowded around her, thick and dense as fog. Her particle sense was overwhelmed by them, the cold weight of the darkness particles mixing with the dry heat of the fire particles, both pressing against her skin.

In the mirror Maureen watched the array lift from her forehead and float out from her face, hanging in the air. It shimmered silver for a moment, then quivered and dropped back to rest on her forehead like a brand.

Watching through the new silver array in the mirror, Maureen could just make out the ghosts of the previous two arrays she had earned.

Heat flared at her brow, then cooled. She held her breath till the silver glow began to fade and finally extinguish.

Tally's whispered count stopped there and waited with her, in the silence.

Maureen opened her eyes, and let out a long breath, then laughed.

"Third-Class Apprentice."

Congratulations, Third-Class Wizard Apprentice Olsen, Tally said quietly, and said no more.

That was all, and Maureen silently thanked it as she stood up, brushing the dust off her clothing. Her legs were a little stiff from sitting so long, but otherwise she felt fine. In the centre of the cave the fruitless tree was still writhing, but she didn't think it would be of much use now.

"The Sodara tree is a rare illusion-school material," Maureen said aloud. "No point letting it go to waste."

Tally indicated that it did indeed have the library entry for Sodara trees, but did not have any data on drying one.

Watch and record please, Maureen thought silently.

Maureen held out her hand and concentrated. Darkness gathered to her hand, forming a sphere of black air that rippled with faint currents, like heat haze over hot rocks. A high-pitched shriek filled the cave, making her wince, then abruptly stopped. The laughter in shifting voices was gone. Already the sweet rotten smell was beginning to dissipate in the cold air. Maureen walked out of the cave, holding a bundle of black cloth wrapped around a dried little tree about as thick as her arm. The processed Sodara trunk.

* * *

"This cave... goodbye too."

Maureen Olsen chanted Simons-Sado and Amra-Keis in turn as she walked away from the cave. Fireballs and acid missiles exploded from her hand, blasting the cave and the surrounding area. Explosions rolled down the slope with dull booms. Green acid hissed and spat angrily as it ate into rock, while boulders began to shift and tumble down the slope, trailing clouds of dust and smoke. Soon, the cave was gone, obliterated as if it had never existed, leaving just another stretch of desolate and barren broken stone. Your mana is low. Lower than I like, Tally informed her, as she started down the path back to the castle.

I know, I'll rest and it will fill up again, Maureen replied silently.

I will hold you to that.

The guards below had heard everything, and were staying well away from the back mountain as they had been ordered to do. Their faces though were uneasy. Old Hank and Jess came up the path hesitantly.

"My lady, you..."

"Hank, have hot water prepared for me and a meal. I am hungry."

Always hungry, Maureen, Tally teased.

This time I've earned it, she answered silently. Then Maureen Olsen walked down the path back to the castle. The wrappings around the Sodara trunk tucked under her arm, as the scent of scorched stone wafted on the wind. Her men's respectful eyes followed her back.
