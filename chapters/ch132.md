The wind picked up, blowing in from the burning skyline. The hem of Maureen’s black wool robe fluttered in the breeze. The frost crusting the stubble beneath her boots crackled with every step she took. There was blood in the air, a thick smell that overpowered the scent of the tallow smoke. Gus lay a few strides away from her, his body resting on the frosty ground. His eyes were open, and the shifting firelight danced in his gaze.

The five black wizards stood in a loose half ring around Gus’s body, positioning him between them and her. Maureen looked at them for a long moment, then laughed softly. It was the sort of laugh a person gives when told a bad joke they’ve heard before.

“So this is how Emilia’s black-robes do business,” she said in Richard’s flat voice. “You kill the man who’s holding your purse. I didn’t expect manners, but I did expect that much.”

Frans didn’t even glance at the corpse. “He was dead the day he hired us,” he said, sounding as mild as a shopkeeper. “A core wizard of a white tower doesn’t walk away from people like us. Ever.”

>Witnesses: 5

Tally pinned an amber counter over the hirelings’ half ring. She highlighted Frans and the heavy man beside him, indicating them as the two Phantom-dense sources. Maureen nodded, keeping her eyes on the men. *Keep that number where I can see it.*

Frans tilted his head, studying Maureen. His bright eyes narrowed slightly, as if he could smell something on her. “You’re one of us, Owl. I can feel it on you. That . . . edge a body gets from too much killing. It doesn’t wash off easy.”

*He’s feeling the Orb’s damped edge. He’s probably reading that as your past, Maureen,* Tally explained.

*It is my past,* Maureen answered silently.

Tally considered for a moment. *Fair enough. Logging as accurate.*

A cold prickle ran down the back of Maureen’s neck, and for half a breath she was on the tortoise ship again, watching the gulls following the bodies. She let her hands hang loose by her side, her face impassive.

“Wherever you came from, whatever you ran from, I don’t care,” Frans said, spreading his hands expansively in the firelight, like a host greeting a guest to his house. “I’m offering you a seat. Join us. Join the Black Wizard Alliance.”

He paused, letting his words sink in. The lords of the Alliance, he said, had agreed to make it a single body, a single fist, to stand up to the white robes. Agents such as himself had been sent into Emilia to take control of its underworld and recruit every wizard who would kneel before the Alliance. They would be ready, whenever and wherever the lords decided to make their next move.

*Hearsay, one source only,* Tally noted. *Underworld refers to Emilia’s criminal element, not the Underworld of the subterranean races. I would appreciate not having to keep two Underworlds separate in the same file.*

Maureen almost smiled. She remembered the Dark Forest corridors where old black-robes and new ones had slit each other’s throats for a heel of bread, and Evans laughing at them all. Black wizards marching in step struck her as faintly comic.

*Low confidence prediction: they will split or fragment within a year,* Tally said.

*Higher than you think,* Maureen told her silently.

*Very well. You have lived among them, while I have only filed them.*

Still, Frans obviously believed in what he was saying. His shoulders were steady, his voice never wavered. Whatever happened in the future, it sounded like the Alliance was going to make some big move soon. It also explained Gus—anyone playing on a board first removed the strongest opposing piece.

“Sorry,” Maureen said. “Not interested.”

Frans’s eyes sharpened in the firelight. Around him, the three lesser hirelings shifted their weight from boot to boot. The frost crunched beneath their feet like small bones.

* * *

Maureen moved before her last word had left her mouth. Three long bounds carried her across the frost to Gus’s body, covering the distance quicker than the lesser hirelings could turn their heads.

Up close, Gus seemed smaller than he had been alive. The hole through his chest had been cooked black around the edges, and frost had settled on his eyelashes. His face was frozen in the shock of his final breath, when he’d discovered that Night Owl wore Richard’s face.

*What’s at his belt?* Maureen asked Tally.

*There’s a knot of density there,* Tally said. *Let me match it against the layer-2 description in the Void Eclipse pages...*

[Confidence: medium-high.]

Maureen reached out and crooked her fingers. The particles inside the box recognized their own kind in her, and the black stone box tore free of Gus’s belt. It flew into her palm with a slap, heavier than it looked.

The stone was cold against her skin, colder than the frost beneath her feet. The density inside the box sat in its walls like a held breath. She wanted it. Plainly, without excuses or explanations. The wanting felt like happiness.

“At last,” she murmured. Then she slid the box into the Void Dimensional Pouch at her waist.

[Pouch: +1]

[Void Eclipse, layer 2: 3 of 3 in hand. Tears: method no data.]

*That closes the commission,* Tally said.

*Cheaper than buying it,* Maureen answered silently.

*Gus paid for it,* Tally pointed out.

Maureen’s hand drifted up to the inner pocket of her robe on old instinct. The Blood Seal of Thorns had always ridden there, snug and safe along her chest. The pocket was empty now, though. The Seal still rested in the puppet’s chest, in the dark cellar below Connpa Street. Her hand fell back down.

Tally’s lens caught a structure knitting itself together in the air above. White. Quick.

> Grab. Above. Now.

Maureen took a step to the side. A giant hand of void energy crashed down where she’d been crouched. Its fingers met with a sound like a log splitting down the middle. The force of Frans’s spell cracked the air.

Frans clicked his tongue. “My hand at your throat and you stop to pick a dead man’s pockets. Greed or stupidity, Owl? Which do I carve on your stone?”

The five black wizards spread out as he spoke. The half ring became a full ring, surrounding Maureen and the corpse in its center.

“I moved my property so it wouldn’t get broken while I work,” Maureen said evenly. She looked around the circle, turning to meet each of the hirelings’ eyes in turn. “Stupid is five of you standing in a ring around me.”

The particles began to stir in the ring, like dust before a storm. Maureen’s pulse remained calm, and the cold from the frost seeped up into her body through the soles of her boots.

* * *

“Then die.”

The corner of Frans’s mouth curled upward in a smile.

At the same time, every one of the hirelings in the ring cast their spells.

It came at Maureen from all sides.

The black flames rolled along the ground toward her, crackling and snapping, spitting sparks. A whirlwind of white spun toward her, howling with the sound of grinding grit. A torrent of green surged forward, corrosive and foul-smelling—rotten eggs and vinegar. The roar of so many spells shook the frost off the stubble.

*Purgatory first,* Tally said, *then the Orb.*

*Yes,* Maureen answered silently. *Take it.*

As Maureen opened herself up to let the power flow, the first tendrils of black-red flame reached out to touch her. The Purgatory Fire rose around her, quiet and oily, absorbing the attack. A heartbeat later the Darklight Orb unfolded outside the veil of Purgatory Fire, unfurling into a sphere of translucent black. The sphere bent the air as it surrounded her, making the atmosphere shimmer like heat rising off a forge. The black flames smeared across its surface without effect, while the white tornado shredded against it like a silk scarf in a hurricane. The acid ran down the sphere’s curve and evaporated into the frost with a fierce sizzle.

[Absorbed: 4 spells. Cycled: 61%. Routing cost: −0.3.]

Tally’s readout scrolled past swiftly at the edge of Maureen’s vision.

Tally’s voice sounded in Maureen’s head, breathless, almost laughing. *I’m doing it! I’m holding it, Maureen!*

“It’s done,” Frans said. “No one walks away from that many Official spells unscathed.”

The volley had passed, but the smoke and debris still hung in the air, obscuring Maureen from view. Then the smoke began to dissipate, revealing her standing in the smoky haze untouched by the conflagration. The black fire burned around her, crackling and curling around her body without singeing her skin. In the ring, hands froze in midgesture.

The heat from Purgatory Fire lapped at her wrists like the warmth from a hearth. She stood calmly, waiting for the fear to arrive. It never did.

Maureen moved.

Her knight’s body carried her away from the spot before the nearest of the lesser hirelings had even finished turning his head back around to look at her.

The red outline of his heart appeared over the back of his stolen robe on Maureen’s display, drawn by Tally from the density readings rather than sight.

Maureen appeared behind him, then drove her hand forward through his back. Her fingers plunged into the exact center of the floating red shape, and closed into a fist.

Wet heat flooded up to her wrist, accompanied by the feeling of something giving way in her grip. The hireling let out a high-pitched scream that quickly turned thin, before cutting off abruptly.

Purgatory Fire ran down her arm and into his body. She maintained the spell for a few more seconds, burning through some of her mana reserve to pay its upkeep.

The two other lesser hirelings twisted around to face her, hands flying up and beginning to chant. Neither was fast enough.

Three ghost lines stretched from the center of Maureen’s palm to their targets, the cycled energy behind them lighting up her vision like fireworks.

Three.

Chest height.

Release.

Maureen kept her lips pressed tightly together, and spoke the command in her head. *Go.*

She flicked her hand forward, and three translucent spikes of energy shot away from her palm. Two of them punched cleanly through the chests of the two remaining lesser hirelings. The men exploded into mist and ash, which sprayed outwards in all directions. The third spike splashed against the ward that the heavy hireling threw up just in time to protect himself.

Three sharp cracks echoed across the frost field, followed by the sizzling noises made by blood mist as they struck the sphere’s curve. The scent of burning changed, suddenly sweet and wrong. Frans’s smile faltered.

*You did that, not me.*

Maureen looked down at her hand, feeling a little dazed. *You aimed them.*

*No, you chose.*

Frans and the heavy hireling answered together. They were far more powerful than the other black wizards, and their spells weighed twice as much. They screamed across the frost, one from the left and one from the right.

[Barrier: hardening. −0.6.]

The two spells crashed into the sphere in front of Maureen, and it held. Tally had hardened it enough to take the blows. Spells and barrier screeched against each other, throwing off sparks like a grindstone.

Maureen’s barrier held, but the force from the spells had to go somewhere. It ripped her off the ground and flung her high up into the night air.

At the top of her arc, the air suddenly went still. The burning skyline tilted crazily beneath her, and the bodies on the frost looked small and faraway. Her stomach flew up into her throat, then just... hung there.

*I’ve got you,* Tally said. *Slowing us down.*

Maureen let her do it. Let her companion wrap her up in a sphere of darkness, let her slow the fall as though she were holding Maureen’s arm to help her across an icy street.

The black sphere cradled her all the way down. Cold and smooth against her palms, it let her down softly. She landed gently on the ash where she had been standing moments before.

Across the ring of hirelings, Frans’s face caught the light from the fires. His smile was gone, his bright eyes narrowed and careful. Black smoke drifted between them, and the fight was not yet over.
