On the map, Gregory’s residence was marked. Proan Canyon, the name read, inked in close to the edge of the Dark Forest’s lands, uncomfortably close to the Underworld. Maureen’s jaw set as soon as she saw it.

She flexed the muscles of her right hand. The same hand that had punched a fist-sized hole through the pine table when the war decree had arrived. The knuckles remembered the sensation of wood splinters biting into them. Her other palm lay flat against the bronze cover, feeling its chill. It could have been well-water on a winter’s morning.

Tally overlaid the canyon’s position with the mission-map from their ride on the Fasol, and informed her that the canyon was well within the Dark Forest’s regular patrol range. Maureen told it silently that she knew how to read maps. It replied with the information that it was simply being precise.

Maureen considered for a moment, weighing the information up in her mind. First, she would try to slip past the patrolling wizards and make straight for the canyon. If that failed, she would make one trip back to the Dark Forest, within the hundred day limit, and use it as a road.

“I take Gregory’s method and the Child of Purgatory blood,” she said to the empty room, “and I walk away. Their war is theirs. I’m nobody’s cannon fodder, least of all some wizard family’s.”

*Walk away?* Tally filed the plan, then asked mildly if Maureen meant to include the Dark Forest’s hunters walking after her when she said ‘walk away’. *This is what preparation is for,* Maureen answered silently. *In that case, I should begin preparing as well,* it replied.

“Proan Canyon,” she whispered twice, fixing it in her mind. Then she closed the bronze book and tucked it into her coat, nestling it against her ribs.

Maureen turned to face the shelves. This was what Gregory had left behind for an apprentice who would never return. Vellum-bound spines, covers of pressed board bound with green-crusted copper clasps. Centuries of undisturbed dust lay like an unbroken skin along each shelf, and the air was heavy with the scent of dry vellum and aged glue. The lantern’s smoke rose in a straight line to the ceiling, disturbed by no movement of air.

She selected volumes at random, wanting to know the shape of the collection first. Rune Secret Lines, naturally, were first. Then alchemy - which the Passise had almost lost entirely. Enchantment was rarer still. Pharmacology...a true Ancient Wizard had to be versed in far more than rune lines alone.

The first enchantment spread made her breath stop short. Her fingertip rested lightly on a ring of sigils, the ink still holding a trace of its metallic sheen despite centuries on the page. For a moment, Maureen was a researcher again, confronted with a whole new field of study.

*Rune lines first,* Maureen said silently to Tally.

*Rune lines first, then alchemy, then enchantment, which is rare so I’m already excited about that one, then pharmacology last since you’ll be reading that anyway.*

*Sort all of these by discipline then.*

*Discipline sort complete. Also, your arms will hurt. You have been warned.*

*Logged.*

*That word belongs to me, not you.*

Maureen pulled down the first volume of rune lines without reply, and Tally let her have the last word.

Maureen worked through the shelves book by book. Each time she rifled the pages, her eyes flicked once over each spread before moving on. Exactly the same technique she’d used in the Skran treasury and the Dark Forest’s open stacks. Each spread lay open only long enough for Tally to read it before Maureen’s thumb moved on to the next.

A counter kept track of the spreads, running in the corner of her eye.

[Rune Secret Lines: shelf 4 of 4. Spreads: 3,118.]

Every time Tally filed away a page, a pale blue flicker ran behind her eyes.

The pages riffling sounded like a soft, steady roar, the sound of rain falling on a tarp. Her shoulders burned, then went numb. Somewhere in there, the grey light from outside shifted from night to day, though she didn’t look up even once.

In one of the illusion spreads, Tally flagged a model for reshaping the face, muscle by muscle. In pharmacology, it flagged a fire potion formula that ignited on contact with air. Both were filed, and Tally asked if she wanted them now. *Later,* Maureen told it. *You always say later,* Tally said.

So it went through the rest of that night and all through the next day. Room after room of shelves, each volume rifled through and its secrets committed to Tally’s memory. When Maureen was done, she walked through the entire manor one last time, pulling open chests and drawers. There were more books, but nothing else worth carrying.

Tally’s display finally tallied everything into a grand total, the number of spreads for each discipline appearing at the bottom of her vision before vanishing again.

*Now, I have a library that no living wizard on the Passise has at his command. How are your arms?* Tally asked in a warm voice, obviously quite pleased with itself.

*They’ll live.*

She stepped back out into the courtyard as the second dawn came up. The dead ivy rattled against the grey stone walls. Cold rose from the scorched flagstone floor beneath her boots, and Gregory’s yellowing pages of struck-through hypotheses lay where the winds had tossed them.

There was no ceremony. Maureen simply opened her palm. A huge fireball, deep red in colour, swelled up and out of her hand. It hung in the air for a heartbeat, then shot away from her like an arrow from a crossbow. It hit the manor and the entire building went up with a roar.

Flame leapt up the old stone walls, turning the realm’s dull sky a deeper red. The heat washed over her, stinging her cheeks. Inside, the books crackled and popped like a fire made from thorny branches. The smell of burning glue and centuries of paper curled across the courtyard. [Mana: 7.8 → ~6.7.]

Maureen turned and walked away. Without being asked, Tally added, *And now a library is ash. It was the only copy.*

*That was the point,* Maureen said silently.

*I know.* Tally sounded smug. *I’m just enjoying the fact that I am the only copy now.*

She made her way back through the cold and black tunnel into the paupers’ graveyard, and pulled out the spell components from her belt kit onto the earth. She spoke the incantation, and the passage collapsed behind her with a single dull thud. The realm and everything within it vanished. So did the large pale stone that marked its entrance, along with all the layers of concealment she’d cast over it.

Tally stored the bearing and step count from the town, then marked them as dead data. A road to nowhere now.

*I’ll keep them anyway,* Tally said after a moment.

*Why?* Maureen asked.

*Because you walked them,* Tally replied.

The first fingers of dawn light were playing across the lichened slate grave markers, turning the wilted flowers into a mass of grey and brown. “Good,” Maureen said to no one in particular, smiling slightly.

Then she was off, sprinting for the inn, light on her feet and moving swiftly across the red earth. She was safely back in her room before the first cock crowed.

* * *

She came down to breakfast like nothing had happened. Nodding to the innkeeper, she sat down to eat.

The innkeeper grumbled something under his breath as he dragged a large grey rag over the table that didn’t need cleaning. The ale was sour, the bread was yesterday’s and the bench sagged in the middle. Just like before.

Her eyes flicked to the chair next to the window. That was where Burton used to sit, talking with his mouth full as he reached for another slice of white bread. The fourth, maybe the fifth. The chair was empty now. A little too neat. Tidy.

Tally kept the list for her because she’d asked it to once. She didn’t ask for it now, and it didn’t offer. Instead it displayed a single line in her vision. [Recall: Day 8.] It waited, letting the silence stretch.

Maureen paid for her meal and saddled her warhorse. She rode out of Red Rock Town along a street that held its breath. People shrank back, giving her a wide berth. Their eyes were filled with awe and envy.

Red dust spun in the air around the horse’s hooves, kicked up from the red earth road. From behind a shutter came a stage whisper. “Mystic.”

A mother jerked her boy back into the house by his collar, slamming the wooden plank door so hard it echoed.

In the next city she hired a carriage, along with several servants and mercenaries to drive and protect it. Her warhorse was tethered to the back. From then on she remained in the carriage for the entire day, the curtain drawn. She paid them more than generously and they didn’t complain about the strange behaviour. The money was good and her eyes were cold, so they didn’t question it. All they said was, “Yes, my lady.”

In the carriage Maureen read, flipping through the library recorded in Tally’s memory. One spread after another, painted on her display while the wheels creaked and groaned outside. If she hesitated too long Tally would obligingly turn the page for her.

*No need to rush me,* she told it silently.

*Just being helpful,* Tally replied.

*Smug.*

Maureen lifted the curtain a fraction of an inch. *Tally, where do I stand?*

[Maureen Olsen. Third-Class Wizard Apprentice. Strength 9.3, Agility 9.5, Constitution 9.6. Spirit 7.8. Mana 7.8. Minai Totem: 0%.]

*And to become an Official Wizard?*

Tally recited the inheritance’s standard line by line.

*Agility requirement met.*

*Strength requirement not met. 0.2 points short.*

*Constitution requirement not met. 0.1 points short.*

*Spirit requirement not met. 2.2 points short.*

*Mana requirement not met. 2.2 points short. This is the mountain.*

*It grows on its own schedule,* she told Tally.

*Like you.* Tally added the rest of the requirements. *You must comprehend the Rune Secret Lines in their entirety, construct a complete Minai Totem, and obtain an advanced method of meditation. The last is Gregory’s standard, not Adrian’s, so I am more inclined to trust these numbers.*

The body requirements were nothing. With Silver Light and her own potions she could close the gap easily. Spirit was a different matter. That was going to be a long, slow grind and there were no shortcuts that she knew of. Her mouth flattened into a grim line and her fingers drummed once against the window ledge.

[Rune Secret Lines: full understanding estimated at 41 days.]

[Minai Totem analysis: revised estimate 94d 22h → ~57 days.]

*This realm’s books have done their homework,* Tally said.

*You’re welcome,* she replied silently.

For the totem itself, Tally listed four ingredients: ghost-faced spider eggs, Demon-Face Flower, powdered Heart-Eater Worm, and Silver-Trace Ink. *Blake once mentioned ghost-faced spider silk in Wintercold,* it added. *Silk isn’t eggs, but someone has seen the spider.*

She knew all four names from Adrian’s pharmacology. These were not apprentice ingredients. An Official Wizard might spend years hunting any one of them down, and never lay hands on it.

Outside the window, the green and fertile farmland rolled by in the sunlight. A windmill turned lazily on top of a rise, its blades slicing silently through the air. None of it reached Maureen’s face. She held a curse behind her teeth until it went away.

One of the servants cautiously edged into view next to the window. “My lady? Is anything amiss?”

“No.”

Then she swung herself out of the moving carriage and right into her warhorse’s saddle. “Wait for me at the next city. A few days.” She wheeled her horse around and rode back the way they had come, without looking back to see their faces.

* * *

The grey mist closed over her as she rode through the mouth of the valley. It coated her eyelashes with dampness. Overhead, perched on the dead branches of the trees that lined the road, crows turned their heads one after another to watch her go past. Their pale-green eyes caught and held her.

“I wish to enter Grey Dove Settlement,” she said in Ancient Andas.

The crows poured together to form the towering, hooded figure that guarded the entrance to Grey Dove. Its voice cracked and rattled like dry leaves as it chanted, “Ewanzi,” and sound rippled through the black stone token she held in her hand. The image of her own face rose from the stone.

She paid the stone, which the Guardian swallowed into its robe.

[Magic stones: −1]

*The token has paid for itself,* Tally noted in her head. Its voice sounded warm and a little smug.

*Does any gatekeeper on this entire continent work for free?* Maureen asked silently.

*None,* Tally replied in her head. *Not one. I have a list to prove it.*

Maureen began working her way through the materials district, stall by stall.

“Silver-Trace Ink?” No.

“Demon-Face Flower?” A laugh.

“Ghost-faced spider eggs?” One old woman just stared at her.

“Heart-Eater Worm powder?” Never stocked it. Not once.

Stallholders shrugged at her, gesturing helplessly over jars filled with pickled creature parts and dried roots that coiled like snakes. Damp canvas roofs dripped onto the cobblestone path. One apprentice even looked her up and down as if she had just asked for a slice of the moon.

Tally maintained a running strike-through list on Maureen’s display, drawing a neat line through each stall as she received another “no.” It didn’t need to say anything about all the failures. It only had to move the next stall up the list before she reached it.

Lastly, Maureen visited Old Farry’s shop. She named all four materials. The shopkeeper’s multitude of baby-sized hands patted against the floorboards in a dry whisper as he listened to her request, then he shook his head slowly from side to side.

His green hair dragged against the boards as he moved about his shop. The sweet, rancid backlash smell clung to him like a second shadow, and when he spoke, his voice came out high and wet, almost like a child who had been crying far too long.

“Farry, do you remember my first-look clause on alchemy fragments? If any of these four ever cross your counter, please send word through Albert.”

“I will look, Viscountess. I promise nothing. None of those have been sold here in my time.”

*Four for four. None found. That is also data,* Tally said gently.

*It’s unpleasant data,* she replied silently.

*The best kind usually is.*

She hadn’t come to Grey Dove expecting much. Even so, her shoulders dropped by a finger’s width as she walked out of the store. She made certain that she straightened up again before the crows could see her.

The pale-green eyes watched her leave the village, emerging from the grey mist one pair at a time, perched on the dead branches that hung over the cobblestone path.

Days later, after leaving Grey Dove behind, she found her hirelings waiting for her outside the best inn in the next city. One of the mercenaries started, “My lady, did you—”

“No. We go.”

*Slowly,* then, Maureen told herself silently.

*Slowly is still a schedule,* Tally noted. *I have one.*

Maureen Olsen leaned back against the carriage seat and closed her eyes. She meant to go through the library on her own, now that it was sorted. Discipline by discipline, she would make certain she understood it with her own head, rather than just relying on Tally’s knowledge.

* * *

“Hold there!” shouted the patrol guards.

Then they saw who was stepping down from the carriage. They stiffened, hands clasping spears, faces full of respect and fright. One of the men ran off down the path. Soon Old Hank appeared, walking down the road towards them.

Maureen’s hired help stood clutching their bundles. The mercenaries made certain their hands were nowhere near their hilts. They had heard, in the villages they passed through, what went up into the back mountain and never came down again.

“Hank, pay them.”

The old man looked once at the men, then at Maureen, and bowed without a word.

*He will pay them to the copper,* Tally said. *He will write down every copper.*

*I like a man who keeps receipts,* Maureen replied silently.

*That’s my line! You stole it!*

In the main hall, Maureen exchanged a few words with Jess, Marla, and Bram. She offered no explanations, but walked directly to her laboratory and shut the door behind her.

There were ten days and more of bitter smoke, scorched copper, and the ticking of the burner. Maureen singed her fingertips twice during that time and had to wrap them in lint to keep working. She slept with her head resting on the workbench and woke up with the imprint of a sleeve crease on her cheek. Tally logged each unsuccessful shell with its cause of failure. It also kept count of the number of times Maureen burned herself. When she told it to stop counting her burns, it moved the count to someplace where she couldn’t see it.

Each morning when she rose from her restless sleep, Tally advanced the log by one line:

[Recall: Day 24.]

Out on the fief, gossip ran rampant. The young Viscountess had returned from the mountain even stranger than before. She remained locked in her rooms, but now there was fire and smoke from the windows from time to time. She rode off for days at a time, then disappeared back into Castle Olsen.

The cuff of Maureen’s sleeve was singed. The ink of her notes smeared against the lint wrapped around her fingers. The air in the room smelled of hot iron and pulverized fang.

Then one morning Maureen walked the grounds in broad daylight. She greeted the servants and soldiers by name, smiling as she passed.

Farmers paused in their work, hoes suspended in midair, as they watched their young Viscountess walk past.

“My lady.” Jess fell into step beside her. He wore his full armor, as always, and he spoke in a low voice so that no one else could overhear. “I’ve found the place you wanted. Near the back mountain. It’s quiet there. No one goes there.” His eyes asked a dozen questions, though his mouth never formed them.

Maureen tugged at her coat, straightening it. “Show me.”

When she arrived at the clearing, she looked all around once, then nodded. “Good choice, Jess.”

The clearing was ringed by thick trees with straight, tall trunks. In the thin winter sun, the scent of their resin hung in the air like perfume. Somewhere a bird called out, over and over again. The grass grew untouched to the knee, forming an open space in the forest.

Tally traced a line on the grass where it had calculated the fragments would fall short, leaving an ample margin for safety.

*You were pretty generous there,* Maureen thought at it.

*I was being generous with your people’s faces,* Tally replied.

“Step back, all of you,” Maureen ordered. “Go behind that line.”

Hank, Jess, Marla, and Bram obeyed immediately, moving well behind the line Tally had drawn. Even the most disciplined soldiers couldn’t completely erase the wariness from their expressions.

Maureen reached inside her coat and withdrew an oval object about the size of her palm. It felt smooth in her hand, but weighed a great deal more than its size would indicate. She whispered the activation words, speaking so quietly no one could hear what she said except for the last, “Kerrash.” Then she whipped her arm forward, sending the shell sailing through the air in a long arc toward the trees.

1 . . .

2 . . .

2.4 seconds later, there was a blast.

A tongue of fire lashed out from the shell, and at the very edge of the clearing the trees snapped flat to the ground as if a giant’s hand had swatted them. Several entire tree trunks spun into the air, roots waving like the limbs of a freshly killed beast.

The concussive force of the explosion washed over Maureen a beat after the initial light, hitting her chest and making her stumble back. The scent of torn sap and hot iron rolled through the clearing. Maureen’s ears rang. After the first few seconds of quiet, the world seemed loud again—there was a constant pattering on the grass as splinters and clumps of uprooted earth fell back to the ground. It continued for a long time.

When she turned, Marla’s scarred hand had dropped to her hilt without the warrior even realizing it. Bram stood rooted in place, his eyes fixed on the destruction. Hank’s mouth hung open. Jess had forgotten to breathe.

Maureen ignored their faces, instead studying the devastation she had wrought. “About what I expected,” she said. “Perhaps a little stronger.”

Tally compared the fallen trees with its records from Room 21. *This was larger than your Hand of Negative Energy,* it said. *And a bit bigger than you expected.*

*A little bit,* Maureen thought.

*You’re smiling,* Tally noted. *I’m logging that. It’s a smile.*
