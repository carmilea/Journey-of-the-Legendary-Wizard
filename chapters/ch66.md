Maureen lay flat in the damp brush and listened to the stream chatter over the stones and pebbles in its path. She listened to Bina’s fire crackle and pop, and she listened to the creak of the string under tension, of the frame of the massive crossbow hidden in the brush beside her.

Crack.

The sound of the bolts being loosed from the heavy crossbow frame that Maureen had purchased in the Grey Dove Settlement was like a tree splitting in two. She heard it here, beside the stream, as Bina stood frozen in shock next to the large grey boulder.

> Aura: not formed.

> Impact.

Tally displayed that before the sound of the bolt being loosed had finished echoing through the woods.

*Keep watching,* Maureen told Tally silently.

The force of the bolts had thrown Bina backwards, and several of the bolts had passed cleanly through her body. Only her Strength, Agility, and Constitution - each above 9 according to Tally’s previous read - kept her alive.

The black bolt shafts still stuck out from the earth around her, and blood steamed on the cold stones. The stream continued its journey down the mountain.

Bina crawled backwards on hands and knees, stammering Maureen’s name. “Olsen… Third-Class Apprentice… you can’t… ordinary people… no chance…” She tried to get up, but her body couldn’t take the strain. Instead she sank back down, her hand lifting to cast a spell. Nothing came out. Her wizard energy roiled sluggishly and thinly in Maureen’s particle sense.

*The Scarlet Potion is doing its work,* Tally said.

*Count her casts,* Maureen told it silently.

From cover, Maureen began chanting and a torrent of Rank Zero spells erupted onto Bina. Blast Fireball, Acid Missile, Undead Wind Arrow, and more slammed into Bina’s thin halo, making it even thinner with each hit. Particles of energy danced in the air, boiling as the spells hit, and a wave of heat from the fire spells bit at Maureen’s cheeks. The sour stink of acid rose over Bina’s screams, mixed with the smell of scorched grass.

[Mana: 6.5 → 3.8.] [Halo: failing.]

Maureen’s hand went to her inner pocket, and Tally spoke up. *This is the sixth time you’ve used the Seal since the ambush on the road. Your reserve is almost exhausted. Keep it short.*

Maureen silently told it to shut up. Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa, she spoke, and the blood red thorny vines covered her body, leaving only her eyes visible through the twisting mass. She crossed the distance between them in a moment, kicking Bina hard and sending her flying away. Maureen followed Bina down, grabbing a fistful of her hair, and smashed her head into the large grey boulder on the bank. The boulder shattered under the force of the blow. Bina collapsed onto the ground, bleeding and dazed, her hands falling open.

*Release. 2.9 s.*

Tally called it early and Maureen obeyed, letting go of Bina. The vines obeyed her command, sinking back into her skin. The Seal in her palm glowed faintly as it did so, just a few sparks of light. A dull ache formed behind her ribs as she stood. Dust from the shattered boulder floated down, slowly mixing with the cold stream water. Bina’s breathing came in loud, ragged pulls of air.

* * *

Maureen smiled her faint, gentle smile. “Hello, Bina.”

“Why are you doing this?” Bina asked, her voice quivering.

“No point asking that now,” Maureen said.

She squatted down beside Bina’s prone body, pinched the woman’s jaw open, and poured a tube of potion down her throat. Bina swallowed, helpless against her own reflexes.

Maureen watched as the particles of energy beneath Bina’s skin began to still.

*Energy sealed,* Tally said, sounding smug. *Even injured, she is now weaker than an ordinary person.*

Maureen let out a long breath, and the rushing sound of the stream returned to her ears. The smell of iron mixed with the scent of wet ash in the air.

“Let’s meet properly,” Maureen said. “I’m Maureen. Maureen Olsen, Wizard Apprentice of the Dark Forest.”

Bina spat out blood. “You’re a black robe. A black brat. My family wizards will hunt you down till the end of your days.”

“I have some unfortunate news for you,” Maureen said. “I killed Patton.”

Bina’s expression shifted from fear, to disbelief, to hatred. “No! You lie! I’ll kill you!”

Maureen shook her head. “That reaction tells me I made the right decision in striking first.”

*Scarlet Potion, Grey Dove bolts, then direct attack.*

*The most efficient method of acquiring target’s data. Simulated head-on attack is projected to be 15.23 times more costly.*

*Worth it. Log this action accordingly.*

Maureen’s smile faded, and she stood up straight. “I want every spell model you know. I want all your basic subject knowledge. I want all your alchemy. That is why you still live.”

“Never,” Bina spat, but Maureen had already started drawing the Cross Sword. Tally flashed the blue-edge warning, but Maureen just thought, *I know.* With her other hand she uncorked an Antidote Potion.

Maureen drove the blade into Bina’s thigh, and poured the Antidote into the wound until the blue warnings faded. “I’ll die before I tell you anything, you little bitch!”

Tracks of sweat ran down Bina’s dusty face. Her teeth ground together, and Maureen could hear them as she felt the cold mud ooze up around her knees. Maureen lifted her sword out of Bina’s leg, and flicked the blade once in a beautiful arc of silver between her and Bina’s eyes. “You know,” Maureen said, smiling pleasantly, “I’m actually an apothecary. I happen to know of a drug that will make your pain a hundred times worse than it already is. I also have a certain powder... if I use that on you, you’ll claw off your skin and flesh till you’re down to the bone. Now, I must say, you are very beautiful. It would be a shame to see that powder make you claw at your own face, but I have an acid that will finish the job. Your family would never know you. Of course, after that, you’ll be drugged and thrown into a slaver’s pen. No one will ever know your name again.”

Bina’s lips pressed together, turning white. Her breath caught in her throat, and Maureen’s hand remained motionless on her sword’s hilt.

“I’ll talk! I’ll tell you everything!”

Maureen shrugged, looking mild and harmless. “Fine by me. I don’t really want to do any of that anyway.” She wagged a single finger at Bina. “But no lies. I have ways of knowing if you lie.”

Tally established a baseline as Bina began to speak. It monitored the pulse at her throat, her breathing patterns, and particle reactions within her body. *No tells observed. Not positive proof.*

*That will do for now,* Maureen thought.

Bina continued, her voice wet and halting. A bit of blood dribbled from the corner of her mouth as the fire sank to embers.

* * *

Maureen stood still for a moment as if lost in deep thought.

[Recorded: Rank Zero spell models 5. Improved versions 3. Basic subjects 3. Alchemy: filed by category.]

“Thanks, Bina,” Maureen said, her face a blank mask.

“Please...” Bina begged, “let me live... I will pay any price... I will keep silent to my family... I will submit to the strictest contract...”

Maureen’s sword lashed out. The Cross Sword’s tip slid into Bina’s throat. “You’re about to die, Bina, so why waste words?” She thrust again for good measure.

Bina laughed, blood bubbling out between her lips. “You’ll never walk the wizard’s path, garbage wizard... a pitiful garbage wizard... you don’t know, you don’t have it... this is your retribution!”

“What are you talking about?” Maureen gripped the front of Bina’s shirt, giving her a shake.

Bina’s head lolled to the side, and her breath rattled in her throat before stopping completely.

[Unidentified references detected. Adrian’s list contains three arrays. Body and spirit. The Finlan Potion. A meditation method. A talent-spell model. Bina’s ‘it’ does not match any of these references.]

*What then?* Maureen asked silently.

[No additional data.]

*Fine.*

Maureen let go of the cloth, letting it slip slowly from her fingers. Her jaw clenched tight, and her hands felt cold.

She searched the body quickly, keeping what she found useful. Then she burned the corpse to nothing with a Blast Fireball. When the flames had consumed every last bit of flesh, she pulled out the bolts and broke down the frame. By the time she was done, there wasn’t even a trace that anyone had ever been there.

The smoke from the burning hair and fat crept low over the water. The stream carried away the ashes.

[Seal glow approaching dark. Reserve flagged as real concern for road.]

*Yes, I heard you,* Maureen thought.

She mounted up and rode out from under the boughs. Frost decorated the road to the next town. The horse’s breath created clouds of condensation in the still night air, and Bina’s laugh echoed through Maureen’s brain, turning over and over in her head mile after mile.

* * *

After dinner, Maureen traced a set of warding gestures at the door and window, then checked each one by hand.

Tally walked the wards with her, line by line, then reported each one closed with a small [Ward: closed.] displayed in her vision.

Maureen nodded. *And the stones?* she asked silently.

[Superior stones: 12.] *I have been counting them since you took them out of the pouch,* Tally said smugly.

She put several of the superior stones on the floor and touched the brand on her wrist. She chanted the call and dark particles began to coalesce into the form of Adrian from the waist up. His lower torso was simply a swirl of dark particles.

As soon as the form was solid enough that the call would hold him, [Superior stones: −4.] appeared in her vision.

The image of Adrian shimmered and the edges of his body wavered like heat rising from a road. A faint chill emanated from him.

Maureen bowed. “Mentor.”

Adrian looked surprised. “This is a long call, Olsen. Long calls eat stones. Haven’t I already told you how to advance?”

Maureen let out a hard breath before she raised her eyes to look directly into his. “Yes, you did, but I want the hidden secrets of Official Wizards. Not just the steps. Are there differences between Official Wizards?”

A long silence followed her question. One of the lamps in the room hissed. Maureen never took her eyes off Adrian’s face and remained in her bow.

Adrian’s grey eyebrows drew together in a frown before he sighed. It was a pleased sigh, and a sad one. “So you’ve come to know some things too, Olsen. I’m glad you know. Truly. I’m very glad.”
