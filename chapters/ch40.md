She shook off some grit that fell from the blade. In the light of the torches, the dust in the air turned lazily.

>Mana 0.5.

>Seal glow: dim.

*You have a sword and a Silver Light breath. Nothing else,* Tally said.

*And that is more than enough for Wolf.* Maureen replied silently.

*Probably,* Tally responded.

*Let us make it certainly so.*

Maureen walked out of the turning dust cloud. Her steps were measured and even. Jay's face was a mask of triumph that slowly lost its color until it was a dull grey. "How are you still alive?"

"Yes, I'm alive. You must be very disappointed." Maureen answered.

Her heart beat a steady rhythm under her breastbone, right where the pale eye mark lay. Without the Seal's protection, she would be lying as ash on the floor. Her hands didn't betray her fear.

Maureen raised the Cross Sword towards Jay.

Wolf jumped in front of his master. "How dare you disrespect Young Master Jay!"

"Get out of my way, Wolf." Maureen said.

The large man puffed out his chest. "You may think that you can strut around here, but you are just a potion-mixer with no family backing. I am a Second-Class Apprentice and an Upper Knight!"

>Strength 8.3

>Agility 8.1

>Constitution 8.3

>Upper Knight

Tally reported.

>Favors sword arm. Arm bruised when serpent tail struck.

>Chant ended thinly.

Tally continued, raising several flags for Maureen.

>High guard drops when he shouts.

"Out of my way!" Wolf shouted at her again.

Maureen crouched low before driving off her calves. She was inside of Wolf's guard before he could react. Wolf blocked a few strokes before the numbing sensation in his forearms forced him back. He gave ground while Jay watched behind his bodyguard.

Steel rang out against the rocky walls of the cavern. The smell of Wolf's sweat mixed with serpent blood filled the air. Maureen's wrists hummed from the force of her strikes.

Maureen shifted the Cross Sword to a reverse grip and smashed the hilt into Wolf's mouth. Teeth skipped across the stone floor. "Your ranks mean nothing." She kicked Wolf into the wall. His body slid down the rock face, leaving a bloody trail. His eyes closed and his head lolled forward unconscious.

>Impact with hilt removed teeth adjacent to gap caused by flicked stone on roadway.

Tally noted.

*You'd only just got used to it,* she thought to Wolf.

*Symmetry,* Tally said.

The Silver Light breath had won that bout for her.

*No. It was my own synthesis of 374 breathing techniques stored in the Skran treasury.*

Tally pointed out.

*Fine, you win that one. Once.*

* * *

Jay's lips moved again in another chant, and Tally sketched out a line for her.

>Toe of the left boot.

*No, not him.*

>That's why the toe.

The Cross Sword spun from her hand and buried itself in the stone floor next to Jay's left boot, quivering slightly. His chant broke off.

The sword hummed in the rock, the steel catching the torchlight, as the scent of Jay's rose water drifted through the blood stink in the air.

Jay backed away. "Accident, accident, I tell you it was an accident."

>Voice has no trace of spell left. Body has never been subjected to any form of breathing technique.

"Accident," Maureen Olsen repeated pleasantly, letting him unwind a little. She slapped him hard across the face with an open palm.

The flesh on his right cheek swelled red.

"You dare strike me? I am of the Olivia Family," he shouted.

She backhanded him across the face and both cheeks burned bright red. Whatever else he meant to say was trapped in his throat by her flat stare.

"Is that supposed to make a difference?" she asked, slapping him again, harder, until his cheeks burned. "A few slaps don't make up for a spell intended to kill me. You tried to kill me, Jay. That isn't very fair of you, is it? It would be much more fair of me to kill you."

She reached down and pulled the sword free from the stone.

>Blue sheen: absent.

*Tally?*

The serpent's blood, hot as it was, scoured the coating off the steel.

>Yes. Clean steel. Slight etched pattern.

*So he won't die of it.*

>Not of that.

Jay turned to run, and Maureen opened his chest with one cut. "Killing can be quick, or killing can be slow." She studied her handiwork, blood welling between the edges of torn flesh, pulsing with the beat of his heart. "We have time. We can take it slowly."

The second cut went to the bone, and Jay screamed once before the pain overwhelmed him.

"Do not speak," Maureen Olsen instructed him coldly. "We will continue."

He crawled across the floor, his hands slipping and sliding in the bone grit, leaving smears of red in their wake. Maureen followed behind at a stroll, the point of her blade trailing a thin line in the stone beneath.

>Ragged inhalation . . . two . . . three . . .

Tally was keeping count of his breaths for Maureen, now that there were no more screams. She let it.

* * *

The sudden appearance of Anthony's back, clothed in a white shirt, in front of Maureen's blade was enough to stop her. "Please. Don't do this."

"What?" she asked, glancing up at him. His chest heaved with each breath, and there were streaks of serpent ichor across his shirt.

"Don't kill him. You can't. He's direct-line blood of the Olivia wizard family. They'll never let you go."

Maureen Olsen halted. Jay caught the word "Olivia" and seized on it. "Yes. Please. I'm Olivia blood."

"Shut up!" she snapped.

Anthony rested a hand on her shoulder, peer to peer. "None of us want this. Just let it go."

Tally read regret and envy in his eyes, watched his thumb absently rubbing at the gold thread in the cuff of his white shirt, which was now slightly grey from the cavern dust. He glanced down at Jay's hand, at the ring that marked his place in the family line. It filed away the emotions in two columns—one regret, one envy—and admitted it did not have enough information to balance them.

*I know what Anthony values, Tally,* Maureen Olsen replied silently. *I know exactly.*

*Five stones' worth of you, by last count,* Tally noted.

The cavern fell silent but for the steady dripping of blood on stone. Maureen Olsen's jaw set as no one spoke.

She considered it. She still lived in the Dark Forest, where the rules were different. She didn't have the freedom to kill anyone she wanted anymore. The Olivias were a wizard family. If an Official Wizard had left some sort of safeguards on Jay, well, she couldn't take that chance.

She smiled, and Anthony's face changed at the sight.

"No, Anthony," she said, "I won't just let it go."

She brought her foot down hard on Jay's shoulder. "I know what I'm doing."

Anthony stepped back as she crouched down and patted Jay's cheek gently with her hand. "You tried to kill me." Her voice was low and even. "I've decided not to kill you, but you'll have to buy your life. Understand?"

Jay nodded jerkily, scrabbling at his belt pouches with bloody hands. He emptied them out onto the stone before her.

Tally counted quickly. [Magic stone card: 1,000 compressed.]

[Paid texts: 4 volumes.]

The rest was worthless. Tally opened up [Card: 1,000] beside her own stones and began the transfer.

*Not here.* The stone card felt cool and thin in her fingers. It slid easily inside her robe. She gathered the four leather-bound volumes and strapped them together with a leather strap. They smelled faintly of rose water, like Jay. Then she slapped him twice more for good measure, and ran the Cross Sword through his chest from front to back.

"You really did mean to kill me, didn't you, Jay?" she asked him conversationally as he writhed in pain. Her breath caught once as the blade went through his body, but her hands were steady as they drew it out.

She tossed him three green Recovery Potions. "Drink," she ordered.

The bleeding slowed, and a little colour returned to his face.

[Belt stock: Recovery 14 → 11]

"You're paying for your life in blood, Jay," Maureen said.

Tally was silent in her head, but Maureen could feel its presence. *You said that no one gets a potion from you unless they pay or you're willing to give it to them for free. Are you willing to give him Recovery Potions for free?*

*Investing in him. That's different.*

Jay began to beg to be let go. "One more step," Maureen told him. "If you tell your family about this, I will be in some trouble. Might as well kill you here." She glanced down at the texts he had given her, then gestured to a black scroll. "This should do nicely."

[Family confidentiality contract]

Tally read off the entries from the library. The signer's spirit would be bound by the terms of the contract.

*I have no data on how wizard families treat their own scrolls.*

*Use it anyway.*

Maureen held it out to Jay. It was wrapped in flame. "Sign or face the power of the contract," she said.

Jay's lips pressed together, turning white. He touched his brow, leaving a brand of light upon the scroll. Orange-red fire danced over the black parchment, consuming it until there was nothing left. The cavern now smelled of ash and Recovery Potion, with undercurrents of damp grass and metal.

*Contract burned.* Tally logged the action in its ledgers.

* * *

Anthony was at her side, sighing heavily, looking down at her with regret mixed with envy. She gave him a faint smile in return.

Jay lay on the ground, curled up like a whipped dog. Wolf's breathing was wet and rhythmic against the stone wall, the air forced through his broken mouth.

*Tally, give me the summary.*

[Card: 1,000. Texts: 4, unread. Secret: bound by contract.]

*Host heart rate: elevated since the blast. Still.*

*That last one isn't for the ledger,* she told Tally silently.

*Everything is for the ledger.*

*Fine. But don't say that again out loud.*

*I won't.*

Maureen turned and began walking towards the corpse of the giant black serpent, moving at the same even pace as when she had first stepped out from the dust.

The heat radiated off the serpent's scales as Maureen approached, the air thick with the scent of sulphur and burnt flesh.
