The sound of the rival crew’s boots died away down the side path. Dust settled slowly in the gap of the broken plank wall. Maureen stood in the trampled and blackened dirt, Freya’s slender sword hanging quietly at her hip. The Mimicry face of Wizard Richard revealed no hint of what she was thinking or feeling.

Joanna and the apprentices of the Jadeleaf Ring came forward now, closing in on Maureen in a loose ring. Their eyes were bright with awe and envy.

“Wizard Richard,” Joanna said, “what you did just now—”

Maureen shut her down with a few flat words. Quarrel stopped. No deaths.

No one pressed her for more detail.

*Eleven Jadeleaf apprentices are massed around you now,* Tally said. *All pulses elevated, all readings high. Gratitude is never this noisy without a bill coming due.*

*I’ll make a note of it,* Maureen thought silently.

*The bill has already been paid,* Tally pointed out. *See the scorch marks in the dirt?*

*Fair enough,* Maureen allowed. *I’ll file it as paid, provisionally.*

Their admiration was beginning to itch at her, like an unpaid bill. The old slum reflexes twitched uncomfortably beneath her skin. She’d been a girl who had never wanted to owe anyone anything, and now all these faces were looking at her with a mixture of awe and appreciation, and her shoulders wanted to turn away from them.

Joanna drew a long breath, then stepped up to Maureen. “Wizard Richard,” she said, bowing before Maureen. The bow was deeper than protocol would require of even the most junior of Officials, and the stitched green leaf on the front of her white robe dipped down towards the dirt. “Thank you. Truly.”

“It was a small matter,” Maureen told her.

Joanna straightened. “For the Jadeleaf Ring,” she said, “nothing is small any more.” Relief and another, heavier emotion sat side by side on her face. Gratitude, yes, but something else, too. Something that felt almost like defeat.

*She rubs her thumb against the stitched leaf on her robe,* Tally pointed out. *That’s what she does when she talks about Senge.*

*Yes,* Maureen thought silently. *I know.*

*You think she feels ashamed because she needed to be rescued, rather than being able to provide proof herself?* Tally asked.

*No,* Maureen thought back. *She wishes she could have won it herself.*

Tally hummed. *I’ll log that as well.*

As Joanna began to walk her through the camp, she became brisk and businesslike, pointing out the ridges that held resource beds and giving Maureen their grades. She told her what each resource could be expected to fetch at the Alliance counters, and speculated on how deep the reserves might go. When Joanna got to the wizard plants, the wizard creatures, and the high-energy creatures, her forehead knotted and her voice dropped in volume. The Jadeleaf Ring had lost people to the beasts of the Ancient Secret Realm, and she didn’t need to say so.

Tally tagged the three high-energy creatures with amber markers on Maureen’s route overlay. A moving pink plant-creature that featured in Joanna’s accounting earned a grey tag. No sighting. Single source only.

*Then we don’t walk there,* Maureen thought.

*Not yet, anyway,* Tally replied.

Then Joanna’s face lit up all at once. “Christine has come on so much recently, Wizard Richard. It’s been amazing to watch! I’ve never seen anything like it. It’s almost frightening. At this rate, she might even make Official!”

Maureen remembered the blonde girl from the western caravan, standing on the step of a carriage looking down on her, glittering with pride.

That was a sister’s hope, stretching a small thing into something big.

Joanna saw her sibling standing by the banner pole. “Oh, look, there she is! Christine, come and meet Richard properly.”

The girl didn’t move. She stayed where she stood, biting down on her lower lip until it was bloodless. Her deep gold hair was misted with resin smoke. She stared at Richard’s face as though she could burn a hole through it.

She had her fists clenched at her sides, and the faded green leaves of the banner waved above her head, stirred by the wind. Her gaze felt like more than a challenge to Maureen.

Biting her lip . . . unblinking eyes . . . pulse spike on the word “Richard”.

Tally recorded the tells, and filed them next to the baseline established on the day when Joanna made her pledge in the doorway. Envy. Wounded pride. Not proof.

Then Tally fell quiet for a beat before displaying the density.

[Christine: dark-red thread. Grown since pledge-day baseline. Match: Tonk’s thread, Emilia’s knots, Jackman’s bead. Purgatory note, low-medium, growing.]

Maureen felt a chill run up the back of her neck.

She remembered Tonk again, inside the gate and within the camp, bowing his head and murmuring his thanks. While the faintest film of dark red danced across his right palm, unnoticed.

Christine gave Richard one last long look before she turned and ran from the gathering, disappearing between the tents without a backwards glance. Off towards whatever place she used to train.

Joanna flushed and apologised in a rush. “I’m so sorry, Wizard Richard. I don’t know what’s got into her. She’s been acting so strangely lately. She’s not herself. Please don’t take offence.”

Maureen shook her head. “It’s fine.”

*That’s why she seems so familiar,* Maureen thought silently to Tally.

*Because she feels like Tonk,* Tally said.

Maureen kept her gaze on the empty space between the tents for a beat longer than Joanna did.

* * *

Before Maureen left, Joanna brought out all the information the Ring had gathered in the Realm. “As thanks,” she said, laying everything out on the trestle table. “All of it.”

The trestle was soon crowded with jars of specimens, each in a cloudy preserving spirit. There were notes on plants, creatures and some of the weaker high-energy creatures, limb specimens in jars and vials of blood samples. A folded package of soil lay in a waxed paper sheet at one end of the table. A strong smell of spirit and wet earth filled the air under the canvas awning. In the jars were a clawed foreleg, a frond with tiny teeth along its edge and a pale coil that looked like some kind of gut. The red-brown vial of blood caught the dull grey light. The notes were written in a careful round hand, the ink blotted slightly at the corners.

Maureen accepted without the usual polite refusal, because this was exactly what she needed. Joanna’s shoulders relaxed, just a fraction. For once she had something to give back.

Maureen flicked through the notes, spread by spread, taking in a page with a single glance. The way she used to work her way through the stacks in the Dark Forest library. Joanna watched her, taking the rapid page turns for the skimming approach of a fast reader.

Tally was busy filing away each species as Maureen’s eyes passed over its page, while adding in what her particle sense could pick up from the jars on the table. A counter ticked over in the corner of Maureen’s vision, updating the new data.

Under Tally’s particle sense, the jars prickled. One was cold, another hot as a coal burning under a glass. The third felt like it was shifting when Maureen wasn’t looking directly at it.

[Wizard plants: 37. Wizard animals: 16. High-energy creatures: 3. New subfolders: 3.]

*You’re purring again, Tally,* Maureen teased silently.

*I do not purr,* Tally replied. *I accrue.*

Maureen unfolded the package of soil and moistened a reagent strip from her belt kit. She touched it to a pinch of the dark earth.

The strip bloomed a copper-rose coloration from its tip upward.

The residue on Maureen’s fingertip felt gritty, with a prickle that was neither wet nor dry but…warm. The same sensation that the Fasol serpent’s blood had left on her glove, years ago.

[Calcium red-copper compound. High. Rare outside the Realm, per plate entry. Match: by reaction + plate.]

*Rare means price,* Maureen thought back.

*Rare means we take more soil,* Tally responded.

“Where was the soil dug up?” Maureen asked Joanna.

“It’s from a ridge about a morning’s walk east,” said Joanna.

Maureen folded up an extra few packets of the dark earth and slipped them into the Void Dimensional Pouch at her waist.

[Pouch: +soil, 3 packets]

Tally dropped a marker on the east ridge of the route overlay, next to the three amber pins.

“As I said before, Christine has gone much further this month than she has in the last year.” Joanna wrapped the final specimen jar in a layer of soft cloth. “She sits up all night. Every night.”

Tally pulled up the claimed progress against the jump in density. It was too quick for meditation alone to accomplish. Not even if it were every night. She had help. Tally used the same tone she’d once applied to Joanna herself.

*Yes, but keep that off the display for now,* Maureen replied.

*Off the display. Still counted.*

The corner of Maureen’s mouth curled upward by the smallest fraction as she walked out the Ring’s gate. It was the only sign of how pleased she was by the new data. The pouch lay heavier against her hip.

* * *

There was never any quiet time within the long work tent of the Alliance Pharmacy Department at the headquarters camp. The sound of the burners ticking filled the air like crickets on a summer’s evening. Glass chimed against glass. Steam rolled beneath the hanging lamps like fog in a valley, bringing with it the scents of beeswax and the herbs steeped in the water. Porters moved through the tent carrying crates of finished vials stacked like bricks, headed out through the tent flap.

More and more wizards arrived from the Alliance every day, passing through the gate to join the forces massing in the Realm. Every additional wizard meant another who would need to fight the natives of the Realm in order to push the maps further out from the gate. Potions were leaving the benches even faster than they could be filled. The department was brewing day and night.

At the far bench, the head of the department himself stood brewing. Ambrose’s sleeves were rolled up to his elbows and his silver hair tied back. No one in the tent could remember a time when the head of the Alliance Pharmacy Department had brewed with his own hands.

*His density is close to Maffie’s,* Tally noted. *As always. His hands are perfectly steady. Rarity noted: head of department, brewing.*

Maureen took her place at the benches reserved for the Fourteenth Potion Group. A board displayed her daily quota, chalked in white against the black paint. She began her first batch. While she worked on the next step, Tally held each completed spell lattice with Maureen’s consent through the Darklight Orb. Then Tally ran the Minai Totem over the single energy-driven binding step.

[Totem in brewing: entry 3. Energy step only.]

The Brewing State settled comfortably over Maureen. Her hands moved automatically as her mind stepped back to watch. Again, the green of the energy step stained her fingertips to the second knuckle.

Viska strolled down the aisle between the benches, carrying two mugs of tea. He set one down at Maureen’s elbow before leaning in to share some bit of gossip that he’d picked up at the patrol fires.

“That order that was leaning on the Jadeleaf Ring?” Viska said. “Their leader’s dead. A high-energy creature on some side path, took him apart. Half his people too, they’re saying.”

Maureen’s stirring rod kept up its slow circle through the liquid in her beaker. The mug of smoke-and-mint tea sat untouched beside her. Only the clenching of her jaw betrayed any reaction.

Again, she saw the men from the other order veer off the camp road and onto the side path, following their leader’s borrowed body. She followed them as far as Tally could see. She hadn’t said a thing when Fors had promised to “handle” it. She didn’t need to.

[Confidence: high, by timing. Not proof.]

*Log that, please.*

*Logged,* Tally responded. *Will you want a count?*

*No,* Maureen said.

*What will you sell Fors first?* Tally asked.

*Nothing that costs us,* Maureen replied.

*I’ll start a list of things that cost nothing. It will be a short list.*

“This Realm is hard on everyone,” Maureen told Viska as she stirred the mixture. He shook his head sadly at the idea of the side paths before returning to his own bench.

At the next bench over, Tonk worked with steam curling the pale-gold curls that fell from under his apprentice’s cap. His lips moved as he watched Maureen work, jotting notes on her method.

“Group leader?” he said. “Do you use a cooling interval here?”

He placed his usual contribution point on her bench. The copper disk stood as payment for her sharing of technique.

[Tonk: Purgatory note, low-medium, growing. Thicker than at the gate.]

Tally filed his thread beside Christine’s. Now two apprentices carried the same dark-red note in one of her files.

*Two,* Maureen noted silently.

*Two that we’ve noticed,* Tally replied.

Maureen answered Tonk’s question aloud, explaining her reasoning without mention of his thread.

“Thank you, group leader,” the apprentice said, bowing his head with his customary care.

The stack of finished vials outside Maureen’s tent rose past the chalk mark on the board. In the light from the hanging lamps, the glass vials shone crystal clear. The scent of the burners filled the air, its smoky tang seeping into the fabric of her sleeves.

Tally logged Maureen’s proficiency as it climbed batch by batch, the line of success tilting upwards on the display.

*Smug,* Maureen noted silently.

*Precise,* Tally responded.

* * *

Frost coated the guy-ropes which ran along the muddy lane between tents, stiffening them, and a morass of mud, churned up by boots, lay like a thick brown paste at the side of the track. Smoke from the fires which burned day and night for the patrols hung low beneath the grey sky, the pungent odour of tallow drifting over the camp. The sky was grey, but not even a breath of wind stirred the smoky haze.

Viska stepped out of the tent behind Maureen and stretched, groaning loudly as he did so.

“I’m worn to the bone. My hands shake when I pour.”

He squinted in the gloomy light, studying her face with tired eyes.

“How are you holding up, Wizard Richard?”

“Fine,” Maureen said, giving him a small smile. “Nothing special.” Her quota was done before noon each day, and she used the remaining time to brew more, for practice.

*Viska does have a tremor,* Tally noted, observing through Maureen’s eyes. His hands shook slightly. *Fine does not sound right coming out of one rested face in a tent full of exhausted faces.*

*I’ll yawn tomorrow,* Maureen told her silently.

*I’ll count the yawns,* Tally promised.

“It’s all this clearing,” Viska said. “Every new zone costs blood. Our wizards go out twice a day and come back on stretchers.”

A cart creaked past them, pulled by two horses under cover of thick sacking, a grey hand hanging down over the side. More wandering wizards. The Alliance took half of what the living brought back, and all of their maps. The dead came back in pieces if they came back at all.

Tally logged the number of bodies Maureen had seen since leaving the gate, not showing them on the display but filing them away none the less.

“But there have been some remarkable people turning up with the clearing,” Viska continued in a low voice. “The Alliance has got some characters. White Bird has got one everyone’s talking about. Wright. Wright Gernier.”

Maureen nodded. She had heard the name spoken at every fire she had visited recently: “Wright. Unmatched. No one even near him.”

The air grew thick and close before anyone rounded the corner. A humming filled Maureen’s teeth, as though she were standing too close to a struck bell.

*It’s the most dense young grain I’ve sampled so far,* Tally said in her head. *In here.*

A handful of wizards appeared from the corner of the lane ahead, walking slowly with their heads bent in conversation. None seemed to be leading the group. But somehow, they had all managed to arrange themselves around a young man without seeming to do so. Viska’s face lit up with excitement.

“There! That’s him! That’s Wright. Only twenty-seven. Liquefied Wizard.”

Tally filed Viska’s figures under hearsay: single source. On top of that she wrote [Age 27] and tucked it beside the density reading for the young wizard. He was tall, with brown hair, a face of deep-set features cut from stone rather than drawn by pencil. His eyes were pale blue. A bronze broadsword hung at his side, its grip worn dark by his hand.

A wave of pressure rolled off him slowly. Maureen felt her skin tighten in response. Under her breastbone the Pankora heat stirred once, then she flattened it back down again.

He said something to the man next to him, and his voice rang out clear and strange. It sounded like the clash of bronze and iron, but with a faint tremor running through it. The hairs stood up on the back of Maureen’s neck.

*I want to hear that voice under a load,* Tally murmured.

*And everything else,* Maureen thought silently. Tally did not deny it.

Viska was the first to look away, though he cast many glances after the young wizard as he walked down the lane with the others of his group. Maureen, wearing Richard’s plain face, continued to watch the young man one beat longer than Viska, her expression giving nothing away.
