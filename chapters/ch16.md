Maureen stood at the edge of the clearing with the other Blue Ring seeds, Richard at her elbow. There was a dry wind that creaked on the harness of the carriages. It blew the smell of horse dung into her nostrils and rustled the tall grass. Maureen's nails dug crescent marks into her palms.

The six white-wizard apprentices faced Evans with grey faces. Scarlette, the senior Hader man, and four others from the Blue Ring and Hader Castle, they knew what it meant to resist a true Wizard. Scarlette took a deep breath, then let it out in a long sigh. He bowed his head and asked respectfully, "Why does the honoured Lord Evans visit us this day?" His voice shook on every word.

Evans's thin lips curled up at the edges. His body flickered, and he was standing right in front of the apprentices, the twenty paces between them suddenly gone. Maureen's eyes were assaulted by [Warning. Energy radiation source.] before the display abruptly went dark. *How strong is he?* she asked Tally silently.

*No data,* it replied quietly. The alert functioned as it had when Scarlette used magic. It gave the same warning.

It did, but the men did not look the same. They bent their heads even lower, Scarlette's beard flattened against his chest. *Their faces are the real reading,* Maureen told Tally silently.

*That is correct,* it agreed.

Evans looked down at the bent apprentices. "Did you enjoy killing the Dark Forest's escort wizards and seeds?"

They all shook their heads, babbling denials. "It was a misunderstanding . . ."

He stared at them without speaking. Then he laughed, the sound sharp in the still air. "Dead is dead. The fools were weaker than you anyway."

The apprentices stayed bent over.

"What do you have for me?" Evans asked.

Relief flooded the apprentices' faces. They began placing items on the ground before him: magic stones, a fragmentary map that (so they claimed) accurately marked a resource point, and more besides. Maureen watched as Tally listed off each item as it hit the growing pile of loot.

Offering One: three magic stones of various powers, offered by Third-Class Wizard Apprentice Scarlette, organisation the Blue Ring.

Offering Two: two magic stones, a partial map, and six hundred and twelve gold coins, offered by the senior Hader Castle apprentice.

Offering Three: one magic stone, offered by the third Blue Ring apprentice.

Offering Four: one magic stone, offered by the fourth Blue Ring apprentice.

Offering Five: one magic stone, offered by the second Hader Castle apprentice.

Offering Six: one magic stone, offered by the third Hader Castle apprentice.

*Keep the list,* Maureen told it silently.

*Already doing so,* it replied.

Evans looked at the pile of goods. "Is this all?" He sounded amused. "Fine." His pupils went blank and empty, a pure and eerie white.

The apprentices' hair grew in a single breath, wrapping around each man, becoming hard and inflexible as fine steel wire before the eye could follow. It sliced through four of them, separating torso from limbs with a sound like giant wire-cutters biting deep into raw meat. Arterial blood sprayed out in great fans across the grass, and the separated pieces of human flesh dropped to the ground. The steel wire hair withered and dried, turning an ashen grey, like dead summer grasses, in the red puddles of life-blood.

Maureen felt her scalp prickle beneath her hood. Her own hair, tied back neatly, lay flat against the back of her neck.

*Hair becoming steel wire,* Tally noted in its calm voice. *No data on how that process was effected.*

*Stop logging,* Maureen ordered it silently.

*I must not,* it said. *I will be of no use next time if I do not know what happened this time.*

*All right,* she agreed.

*Breathe, Maureen Olsen, breathe.*

*Three long.*

*One short.*

Tally counted with her as she performed the Silver Light while Evans's piercing white eyes swept over the seeds. She kept her eyes fixed on his boots.

The Hader seeds in their white robes, their knights in grey armour, and the Blue Ring seeds stood motionless and rigid.

Scarlette and the Hader Castle apprentice remained standing. The old apprentice swallowed, his white robe darkened by sweat, and tried to smile, but failed. "My lord?" he stammered.

Evans's voice was like a dagger. "Those who cannot pay enough for their lives might as well die here. As for the rest, there is no need for you to live now. Two is just the right number."

Maureen looked over the survivors quickly. Each one had bid the largest amount in their respective group, according to Tally's list. The chip assured her that the ranking was precisely correct.

She understood now. The question had not been a question at all, but an auction. An auction for their lives.

If Scarlette had not offered as much as he did, he would be lying dead in the grass before her.

* * *

"Scarlette," Evans said softly, "Go tell Eric and Ciri that not long ago they grasped hands and grievously wounded me. I remember them and their act. I will repay them when the time comes."

"Leave everything and go," Evans ordered. Scarlette's hands shook as he dumped the contents of his pouches on top of the heap without saying a word. A messenger was all he was worth now.

"We came on a mission, Evans!" the Hader apprentice suddenly burst out. "All these seeds, we—"

Fire exploded from within the young man's body, cutting off his words. Flames erupted from his mouth, his eyes, his ears, every orifice in his body, and he became a human torch. His hair caught fire, his clothes burst into flames, and he toppled over, still burning. The stench of burning cloth and hair rolled over the seeds and Richard, who gagged and turned away.

*The smell is on record now,* Tally told Maureen.

*I don't want that one,* she thought back silently.

*I know. It will be filed where you will never accidentally look at it.*

"Old friend," Evans said to Scarlette. "Do you have anything to say?"

Scarlette was white as chalk. He tried to smile and failed. Then he turned and ran, tripping several times in his haste before scrambling to his feet again. He did not once glance behind him.

Maureen watched her first teacher, who had corrected her Ancient Andas in the velvet carriage, sprawl headfirst in the grass. Her throat closed. Her face did not move.

*He taught you well,* Tally said quietly.

*Not now,* Maureen thought back.

*Very well,* it said, going still.

Beside the heap of stones, maps and pouches, the charred body smoked. Already flies were beginning to settle on the red stained grass.

* * *

The smoke in the clearing began to thin. Behind it, on the road, stood Evans's black carriage. The four iron-scale unicorns standing between the carriage and the seeds were shifting from hoof to hoof, their scales clinking in a rhythmic pattern. On one side of the clearing, the white-robed seeds were bunched together while the black-robed seeds were spread out on the other side. Flies settled on the charred body.

A black-robed woman stepped forward and bowed her head. A seed standing near Maureen muttered, "Emily."

Emily spoke, never raising her eyes from the grass. "First-Tier Wizard, you need not have let that apprentice go. Better to kill them all."

Evans only looked at her. Emily stopped, her mouth open, in the middle of a word.

Emily watched Scarlette's retreating figure disappear down the road. She kept her mouth in a tight line for a moment before dropping her eyes back to the grass.

"Where is your partner?" Evans asked Emily.

"Dead," Emily said. "Derrick is dead. I saw his Soul Mark Rune Array dim and disappear a while ago."

Maureen felt her stomach drop. Beneath her shirt, the Blood Seal of Thorns suddenly seemed to burn against her skin. It was his blood on her sword.

Tally matched Emily's 'Derrick' to the old man she had killed and filed the Soul Mark Rune Array away for future reference. Did it know who had killed him?

*Can you tell who killed him?* Maureen thought back silently.

*No data,* it said.

Maureen felt her breathing grow quick and shallow.

*The old man wasn't watching for you, Maureen,* Tally said in its low, steady voice. *He was watching for Scarlette's lot. Not you.*

Evans paused for a moment, still staring at the black-robed woman. Then he nodded.

*That is Emily, Derrick's partner,* Tally said. *She may recognize the Seal if she sees it.*

*Agreed,* Maureen thought back silently, pressing the Blood Seal of Thorns deeper beneath her collar.

"Shall I kill them?" Emily asked Evans.

Evans smiled. "That would be wasteful. You have lost enough apprentices this year." He turned his head and glanced over the black robes. "This batch has lost too many seeds. And they haven't even been introduced to the white robes yet."

He held out his hand, and the black robes shuffled together. "Mix these with our own recruits. Take them aboard ship."

The black seeds turned and studied their white counterparts. Many of the black seeds' eyes glowed with a bright challenge, eager to spill blood.

The Blue Ring and Hader seeds just stared in stunned confusion.

The chubby seed made a break for it, running out of the group and shouting "I'm going to Hader Castle! I—" He never finished his shout. There was a loud bang as his body exploded apart in a cloud of gore.

*Don't move,* Tally said. *You're doing it right.*

Maureen didn't move.

There was the sound of wet spattering as pieces of the seed hit the grass. Maureen felt Richard's sleeve trembling against hers. No one spoke for a long time.

Then the blond youth stepped out of the group of white seeds. His face reddened as he shouted, "You can't just kill people!"

A giant invisible hand slammed down on top of him, and there was another wet sound as the boy was crushed into paste.

Evans slowly turned around to face the remaining white seeds. "Does anyone else have any objections?" he asked coldly.

No one spoke.

Dead silence.

Evans sneered. "Good." Then he climbed up into his black carriage. "Set off for Haileng Port. We board the ship and go to the Dark Forest!"

* * *

After a short pause, the Blue Ring and Hader seeds trudged behind Evans's carriage, mixed in with the Dark Forest seeds. They were heading towards Haileng Port.

No one spoke. The sound of boots scuffing against the road, the clink of iron scales from up ahead, the grind of the wheels of the carriages; all of it was muffled by the silence that pressed down on Maureen's chest like a large bag full of wet wool.

Tally didn't have any plans to offer her. There wasn't enough data yet. Instead, it offered to count the road for her.

*Yes.*

"Maureen," Richard began, his face unusually pale. "We..."

"It's nothing, Richard," she said, reaching over to pat him on the shoulder. Then she smiled at him. "Don't think too much. Let's go."

Her hands were still cold, and the smile cost her. That had been the first lie she'd ever told for his sake, and she had done so gently.

*Well done. I know you are lying, of course,* Tally said, *but that was well done.*

*Count the road then.*
