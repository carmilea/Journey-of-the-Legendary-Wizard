[Trap vapour: window closing.]

The shop should be full by now.

That was the point.

Fog condensed on the illusion's red hair, making the strands look almost golden. The cobblestones were slick, reflecting the light from Olit's fog lamp which hung in a yellow halo over the apothecary's sign. The fog lamp hissed above her head as Maureen paused next to the slatted shutter. She heard laughter from within, followed by the thump of a fist on a countertop.

Tally's analysis finished, sorting out the different voices by sound alone.

[Voices: 7. Est.]

Olit's smooth voice was one of them.

Count the breathing too.

[Breathing: 8.]

A thin waft of scent escaped through the wooden slats. Wet iron and cut stems, the fragrances of lamp oil and dried herbs.

She held her breath, while Tally counted off the seconds for her.

"Olit, I wanna get back to the tavern, those new fox-women are really something."

One of the hired killers, his voice coarse and unrefined.

"Quiet!" Olit slammed his fist against the counter. "This is what we'll do. You wait outside, and when the fat sheep comes out, you kill her."

"Daniel dusted her with Scentless Bug powder as she entered the shop. Each of you carry a mother bug, which will be able to sense the dust."

Tally replayed Daniel's brush against her elbow, drawing her attention to the faint dust on her left sleeve.

[Residue: unknown powder. Matches description.]

Why didn't you alert me about the dust then? she asked silently.

I did log the dust, but I did not know the dust could follow you, Tally answered in its own warm voice. There was a trace of sheepishness in its tone.

Maureen did not attempt to brush away the dust. She let it be, as she spoke silently to Tally again.

Let them come to the sheep.

The fog lamp above her head hissed softly, while the smells of lamp oil and dried herbs seeped through the wooden slats of the shutters.

"So, same old routine?" the red-haired young man drawled from inside the shop, his words muffled by the wooden shutter. "Not one new idea, eh?"

"You know the routine, so follow it!" Olit warned the hired killers. "And no skimming off the loot either!"

"I won't!" "Me neither." "Nah, not me!" All the men shouted their denial, a ragged chorus, with voices overlapping each other.

Her pulse ticked against her wrist, a regular beat, marking the seconds. Slow. Even.

"Go! To your positions!" Olit ordered, and she heard the sound of men shuffling towards the door.

"Why isn't Daniel back yet?" Olit wondered aloud.

* * *

She pushed hard against the door, swinging it open, as she stepped through.

"Daniel? He won't be coming back."

Eight heads turned to look at her, illuminated in the wavering light of the oil lamp. Chairs scraped back across the wooden floorboards as the men rose to their feet. The air in the shop was thick with the smell of burning tallow and male sweat, heavy enough to make her want to cough.

"You!" Olit shouted, his lined face twisting into a mask of anger and hatred. "It's the fat sheep! Get her!"

The men looked her up and down, grins spreading across their faces, delighted that the fat sheep had walked in by herself.

[Third-Class ×2 (lower column). Upper Knight ×5.] Tally noted, discounting Olit. [One of them hasn't spoken at all.]

The most dangerous is the red-haired young man. Loose stance, confident. He has your hair.

He has the hair of my disguise, she replied silently, her hand resting quietly on the hilt of the Cross Sword.

"Where is Daniel?" Olit demanded, spitting the words out.

"He's dead," Maureen answered calmly. "And having you all here like this saves me a great deal of trouble."

The men exchanged looks, then began reading her aura. Then they started laughing, a deep rumbling roar of amusement that bounced off the low ceiling of the room.

Her hand stayed on the hilt of the Cross Sword.

"I'm gonna make your death real ugly, lady," Olit promised. "I'm gonna make you sorry you ever lived."

He threw an arm out wide, pointing at her.

"Get her!"

> Now. They're moving. Three steps, four...

The big men crumpled as if their strings had been cut. A broadsword clanged to the floorboards. Olit slid down the counter.

They lay on the floor, gasping. Faces twisted in fear, greyed by the lack of blood flow to their heads. The air smelled of wet iron and the fresh green scent of cut plant stems.

One Third-Class by the wall still has strength in his legs, Tally said. Go there first. Wrist. Throat.

Maureen crossed the room, driving the Cross Sword into the man's wrist, then his throat, before he even realised what was happening. She turned back to Olit, who was propped up against the counter. "Remember the two vials I dropped as I left?" she asked. "I'm a potion-maker. There's always a way to take away your strength to resist."

She drove the Cross Sword into Olit's throat. The apothecary clutched at his neck, blood bubbling between his fingers, as he gasped, "Magic is forbidden here! The guard corps will—"

Maureen glanced at Tally's display. [Mana: 2.0] It hadn't changed. She drew the blade out of Olit's throat. "No magic, Olit," she said. "Just a Cross Sword." Then she stabbed him again, twisting the blade as she did so.

She worked her way down the room, one man at a time, while they cursed her and begged for mercy.

Treasure. Advanced subject texts. Spell models. Stop listing them, please, Maureen thought. Tally fell silent, but the list stayed on her display.

At the mention of spell models, Maureen's jaw tightened. She wanted those. Her hand didn't slow as it drove the Cross Sword into the last man's throat. That was the red-haired young man. A single drop of blood dripped from the point of the blade, then the oil lamp continued to hiss.

* * *

Maureen wiped the blade on the cloak of a fallen man, then slid it back into its sheath. She began working methodically, stripping the shelves into large sacks and her potion box. Formulae, materials, and finished potions were all important. Tally ran an inventory as Maureen cleared the shelves. Jars and scrolls ticked up on her display shelf by shelf.

Under the counter was an iron-bound strongbox. The lid popped open with a tap from the pommel of the Cross Sword.

The lid was cold. Inside, a collection of magic stones clicked against each other in great heaps.

[Strongbox: Superior 32 (x100). Ordinary 412. Worth 3,612.]

[Loose stones: about 4,190.]

At the bottom of the chest lay Olit's ledger, with years of fat sheep listed by date and takings.

Maureen picked up the book, and flipped it open in her hand. He kept receipts too, Tally told her.

Then we have that in common.

No, we have that in common with the dead.

She left the bodies where they lay. It wasn't worth the potions to hide them. The bare shelves would tell the story well enough. Olit's shop stood emptied. Bare shelves, split strongbox, and a green-haired old man lying in his blood. The oil lamp still hissed quietly, and the smell of blood was thick in the air.

Your sleeve.

What?

The dust is still on it, and I have no data on how many mother bugs are out there.

Maureen cut away the outer layer of her sleeve, then burned it in Olit's own lamp. The cloth curled and blackened, smelling fetid as it burned. [Watch-list: dust that follows you.]

Maureen paused at the door to straighten her collar.

Twice.

Then Maureen walked calmly out into the fog, heading towards the settlement's entrance.
