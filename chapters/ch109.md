Maureen came to herself sitting in the middle of the blackened ring. The last thin black-red flames were still flicking their tongues along the edge of the circle, licking at nothing. The smell of scorched honey and hot iron was lessening with every breath. The lamp had burnt low in its niche.

Maureen let out a long breath. “A wizard,” she said to the empty room. “A real one. At last.”

She allowed herself one sentence about what that meant. A First-Tier Wizard on the Ancient Wizard path, the old road that had conquered worlds, plundered planes. Then she let it go.

She looked at her hands in the lamplight, holding them up before her. The fingers seemed slightly longer than before, the knuckles smoother, as if an artist had returned with his chisel to clean up some lines. She took a deep breath, feeling the air fill her lungs, her bruised ribs no longer protesting.

Maureen’s start was not ordinary, and she knew it. Most who walked this path began with some lesser creature’s blood. Maureen had been ignited with the essence blood of a level-five creature—the Pankora Scarlet Serpent, the Child of Purgatory.

*Tally, what do you have for me?*

*Oh, good, I was beginning to think you’d forgotten all about me,* the impish voice said. *I’ve been waiting so politely to be asked, but your new layout deserves an audience.*

The scan appeared across the edge of Maureen’s sight, fuller and warmer than it had ever been. An amber and ember-red sat next to the pale blue that she had become accustomed to. The spinning model of Maureen’s body now had dark-red threads running through it, like veins in white marble.

[Maureen Olsen. First-Tier Wizard (Official). Path: Ancient Wizard. Strength 14.1, Agility 13.8, Constitution 13.9. Spirit 16 (+4.8). Mana 16.]

She read each number twice, holding it up against the old one. 10.4, 10.2, and 10.6 along the body lines. 11.2 on Spirit and Mana. Nearly four points on each body line in a single night. Nearly five on Spirit.

*Four point eight?*

*Yes. I think they rounded you up at the end.*

*No, you rounded me up.*

*Never! I would never!*

Tally sounded delighted to be accused of such a thing.

The slum mud, Old John’s pine coffin, the ship, the cold corridors of the Dark Forest. Each step had cost her something, and all those steps had brought her here. A small smile touched Maureen’s lips, and she let it stay for just a moment before forcing herself to consider the new numbers. To consider the limits.

A line that she had never seen before sat right at the bottom of the scan.

[Tally. Anchors: Minai Totem, Darklight Orb. Senses: particle (all-round, range est. 300 m), body. Status: settling.]

Maureen stared at the new information. Her hand drifted up of its own accord, pressing against her chest, feeling for the place just below the faint pale eye where the Orb rested. It was as if she could touch Tally through her skin. The skin beneath her fingertips was warm.

*Do you mind? Now that I’m on the page with you?*

*No, Tally. Leave it there.*

*I will. Thank you.*

Tally said nothing more for a long moment.

[Pulse: 64. Adrenaline: low. Injuries: none (ribs healed).]

*I can feel your heart now, Maureen. From the inside. You have a very calm heart for someone who’s just grown some new bones.*

Maureen sat quietly, listening to the sound of her heartbeat echoing through the still chamber. She wasn’t alone in that listening. The ward lines tick-tick-ticked away in the walls like a stove left to cool after baking.

And then she wanted to know what else the serpent’s blood had given her. What lay beyond the numbers. Closing her eyes, she turned her gaze inward.

* * *

She knew the rule from the vault, from Gregory’s books. When a wizard rose by a suitable method of sufficiently high grade, he gained an appropriate innate spell by his nature - the others spent their time hunting for templates that would allow them to fix one. The innate spell of an Ancient Wizard was created by the combination of the method and either the wizard’s bloodline or exotic energy - so Maureen’s would be the serpent’s.

Knowledge welled up out of her blood. A structured imprint. Like the crystal ball. The bronze book. Tally was there. *Would you like me to catch it? Sort it before it lands?*

*Yes.*

Heat pressed behind her eyes. Sulphur coated her tongue.

She saw a sky tear open over an alien world. A vast shape, black as the void itself and scaled to match, slid out of the breach. Black-red fire poured down in its wake and the world burned from rim to rim.

This was the Pankora, as remembered by the imprint. Favoured creature of the Purgatory World, it came through dimensional gates to consume all that lay in its path. Its fire could burn whole worlds to ash. That was why it had earned the title of World-Destroying Serpent.

Tally gave the details. [Purgatory Fire. 16.5° est. Instant. Ignores ordinary defences. Burns on: damage over time, negative effects. Cast: ~2 Mana est. Upkeep while burning: 1 Spirit, 2 Mana / 3 h.]

*The Totem has the same upkeep to the digit. I do not trust this. I will check it at first real use.*

The second spell lived in the serpent’s eyes. The power of the Purgatory World looked out to the universe through them, and no opponent could stand long against that gaze. It was the mark of a plane predator, the Child of Purgatory’s source of pride.

[Mirror Pupil. Crystallisation: 16.5° est., irreversible. Cast: ~2.5 Mana est. Spell freezing and decomposition: locked. Void dominion: locked.]

*Two of three are far beyond your abilities... for now.*

*Don’t say ‘for now’ like you’re making a promise, Tally.*

*It is a projection, Maureen. With error bars.*

Joy surged hot and hard in her throat. Two innate spells. Both of that weight. She pressed her fingers into her knees until the feeling passed.

At First-Tier, the fire was a strong spell. Nothing more. At First-Tier, the eyes could crystallise. Nothing more.

What had the Ancient Wizards dared? They had captured a Child of Purgatory and bled it dry to give to their favoured apprentices. The blood, untamed, would have burned any apprentice to ash. Gregory must have tamed it. That was her inference. Untamed, that blood might well have set loose the void itself the moment the vial shattered, consuming everything it touched with the fire of annihilation.

She remembered the stooped old man whose face slid away when she looked at it directly, calling her ‘latecomer’. She thought about what the creature had paid, and what Gregory had paid. She closed the thought like a book and set it carefully aside.

*I have filed that under Gregory, with an inference marker. Not confirmed.*

*Yes, Tally, that’s the right column.*

Maureen studied the ward lines she had laid into the walls. She looked up at the rough stone wall where she had cast them, and Tally painted over her work in fine gold threads. *Spells, you can see them now! Their bones, anyway.* Tally’s voice was bright with the new data.

The wards Maureen had cast by feel hung visible against the bare walls. There were knots where the nodes sat, and long slack lengths where her hand had not been quite so careful. This must be how wizards saw the world.

*The third node is being lazy,* Tally said.

*You fix it then,* Maureen replied.

*No, you will fix it, and I will hold the line steady for you.* Tally sounded almost eager. Maureen reached out for the slack thread and drew it taut, feeling the line steady beneath her as she worked. [Mana −0.1]

*That was you, Maureen, not me.* Tally sounded proud.

Well, that had been simple enough. Time for the thing that the Ancient Wizards had leaned upon above all else. She had a lens to see through now; let us see what it showed.

*Tally. Analysis, please. The Minai Totem.*

[Minai Totem: active, near-permanent. Level 1. Amplification +1.2° on your spells. Weakening −0.9° on spells against you. Upkeep 1 Spirit, 2 Mana / 3 h. Below your recovery.]

Maureen thought about the bronze book’s description of a ten-degree spell emerging at fourteen or fifteen degrees. *That was a mature Totem,* Tally said. *This one is only a day old, level one, but it will grow as you feed it.*

*Feed it? What does it eat?*

*The book said powerful bloodlines, but I would rather not think about the menu quite yet.*

Small numbers, but she knew what they meant. Every full wizard’s attack spells were at least ten degrees. A degree of amplification on everything she cast, almost a degree of weakening against every spell cast at her... those numbers didn’t add up to one plus one when wizards fought. Not even close. And the upkeep ran slow enough that it was below her recovery rate. The Totem would always be on.

She could feel it now, along the lines beneath her skin, a slow warmth. Like a banked fire, humming away within a closed stove.

* * *

Tally’s voice suddenly became cold, right in the middle of a thought.

[Source: HIGH. Range 260 m, closing, above ground.]

*I can feel him through the floor,* Tally said. *It’s like there’s a stove in the next room over.*

Maureen froze. There was a faint shiver through the stone beneath her, and a line of dust sifted down from one of the seams in the ceiling to land on her sleeve.

*For the first time, I have a labelled wizard to compare against,* Tally said. *You. This one is close to you. A shade thinner, not by much.*

Maureen didn’t need a name for him. Only one man had chased her this hard, across so many towns and cities. One man who had lost both his grandsons to her. Barok of the Olivia Family. Jay’s grandfather. Frey’s grandfather.

A voice rolled down through two floors of earth and stone, like a rockslide thundering down a mountainside. The actual words were blurred past recognition by all the matter they’d torn up along their way, but the shape of a word could still be heard clearly enough. Her name.

Then it came again, closer now, the sound slightly dulled by the stone.

“Maureen Olsen! Crawl out here and die! For Frey! For Jay!”

*Jay?* Tally said quietly. *He said Jay. His death was staged as an underground raid. Either he saw through it, or he no longer cares whose fault anything is.*

Overhead, Tally’s display painted a picture across the ceiling. It was everything Tally could sense and report on. There was a heat-shimmer of density sliding back and forth across the grounds above. Around it, a scattering of small mortal lights that danced like sparks thrown off from a forge, some of them drifting closer to the centre.

Then there was a man’s voice, coming faintly through the stone. A man’s voice, uncertain but polite. “Sir? Sir, can we help you—” Then the crunching of boots on gravel. The manor’s gardeners, walking towards him.

The lancing density punched out sideways without turning, and three small lights winked out simultaneously. *Three. No. Four,* said Tally quietly.

A dull crack came from overhead, filtering down through the floor. Thin screams scattered in all directions. Maureen’s body went cold and motionless from the top of her scalp down. Her jaw clenched into a tight, hard line.

*They’re running,* Tally said. *He’s pooling something in one hand. It’s big. The density is building up behind it like water behind a dam. Enough for... the whole grounds.*

Then Maureen smiled.

She waited for the fear to come. It had been her companion for days now, travelling from inn to inn as she fled across the country. It didn’t come, and its absence worried her just a little. She’d been so used to its presence that not having it was like finding a missing step when she was going downstairs. Her foot hit the empty space where the stair should have been, and she lurched.

*You’re very calm,* Tally said, her voice holding a strange note that wasn’t quite approval.

“So you’ve caught up with me, Barok,” Maureen said, speaking aloud to the empty chamber. Her voice sounded soft, almost lost in the stillness of the hidden room. “Good timing. I’ve been wanting a look at your face.”

*I could ping him,* Tally offered. *A particle would tell us where he is, map out the grounds. He’ll feel it though.*

*Let him,* said Maureen.

[Ping: −0.2 Mana. Sources: 1 HIGH, 4 mortal, fleeing]

*He felt it. He’s turning towards us. The working in his hand has stalled.*

The particle ping spread away from Maureen like the ripples thrown up by a stone dropping into still water. She felt the disturbance travel through her own bones, pulsing through her body before it faded away into nothingness.

Black-red flames surged from her skin, roaring like a rushing wind as they filled the chamber. They flared to the ceiling, consuming everything in their path, and Maureen’s feet left the ground. She hung suspended in the centre of the fire.

*Wall north. Bluestone. Two feet. Purgatory Fire will go through.*

*Yes, it will.*

*Then I’ll just have to count your mana.*

[Mana: 15.7 → est. 14.]

The fire slammed into the north wall and bluestone erupted outward in a fountain of chips and splinters. A thick column of black-red flame tore upwards out of the earth, punching its way into the grey sky like a pillar linking the ground to heaven. The smell of sulphur hung in the air, the stink of fire and brimstone, and the flames roared like a thousand voices screaming all at once. Beneath her feet, a phantom serpent coiled into existence, formed of dark particles and veined with strands of black-red fire. Only its eyes were distinct, cold and blood-red, peering out from the smoke that billowed around them.

Daylight. Trimmed hedges, gravel pathways raked clean of leaves, and a red smear across the green lawn where the gardeners had been standing. Above it floated Barok, stern and middle-aged, standing upon empty air. Dark particles boiled around him, churning like smoke around a torch.

The black-red column began to thin and draw away, and Maureen stepped out from within it, standing upon the air at the same level as Barok. As her aura washed over him, his expression changed.

Tally noted the transformation, coldly and quickly. His pulse jumped at the throat, visible even at this distance. His weight shifted in the air, drawing back half a hand’s breadth.

Barok stared at her, his face unreadable. “Who are you?”

*Tell him.*

“You don’t know?” The corner of Maureen’s mouth twisted upwards. “You’ve chased me across half the country, Barok.” A glint of crimson flickered deep within her black eyes, there and gone in an instant. “Maureen. Maureen Olsen.”
