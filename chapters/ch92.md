“Which of you killed Varo?” Jason demanded, the tip of his cane planted firmly in the ground. His voice sounded like a cup full of black powder sitting an inch from a spark. “And the other one, my girl from Hader?”

Maureen stood among the Dark Forest survivors, wearing her own face once more. The taste of the green potion was still strong in her mouth, and the tips of her hair tickled her neck. She had cut it herself with her knife, leaving it short and uneven, but not wanting to waste even a moment to do better. Her eyes fixed themselves on a single patch of flattened grass a few feet away, and she kept her hands loose at her side.

*Jason’s knuckles are whitening against the cane six times per minute,* Tally said. *At muster, it was four times per minute. He is angrier now.*

*Don’t look at him.* Maureen instructed Tally silently.

Tally replied, *I only see what you see. You are the one who looks.*

Maureen lowered her eyes another inch.

The other girl from Hader was Anna. The veiled girl whose cracked green bracelet Maureen had left lying on the body. She kept her breathing slow and even, feeling the points glyph sit cool and quiet on the back of her hand under her sleeve.

*Varo. Anna. Beck. All on the same list. Your list,* Tally whispered.

*Hush.*

Tally fell silent. After a moment, it spoke again. *Three for three.*

“And Beck,” said the White Bone Tower master. “Mine. A shoot like that comes up only once in a generation. I intended to graft him into my own family.”

His blood-red robe hung over his tall frame, patterned with so many dark lines that it seemed almost black. His bleached bone mask hid whatever expression his face held, but the force of his anger radiated through the air like heat from an open forge. The apprentices closest to him shuffled back a step, even though they didn’t seem to realize that they had moved. The hairs on Maureen’s arms rose.

Both men turned their eyes along the rows of apprentices, looking them up and down as if they were buyers at the market inspecting a pen of sheep, trying to decide which animal to break first. The air between the factions seemed to compress, growing tight and difficult to breathe.

“Jason!”

“Stark!”

“Enough!”

The other faction leaders cut across them, one after another. Tally opened a new file, [Stark. White Bone Tower master. Bone mask. HIGH.] *Two people who want Jack dead now.*

*Three, if you count Barok.* Maureen thought silently.

*Barok wants Jay’s killer,* Tally said.

*Same person.*

Augurit Jones spoke, his voice low and mild. He never raised it, or shouted or threatened. He didn’t need to.

“Want a fight, do you? Forgotten our oath already? Then remember what it takes from a man who breaks it.”

His voice remained as mild as a kindly grandfather’s, and that was the threat. The hum of the gate sounded loud in the silence after Augurit finished speaking. Even the insects in the grass seemed to fall silent.

Jason and Stark exchanged one long look. Jason’s beard worked as he swallowed whatever he had been going to say. Stark turned his bone face away.

Tally added another note to the file. *Both men backed down with a single word. Whatever this oath is, it isn’t cheap.*

Maureen nodded silently to herself, filing that information away next to Adrian’s ashes on the brass. *Old men with leashes on each other.*

Augurit let the silence linger for a few more seconds, then he continued. “Now, the Skoll Soul-Hunting Fruit within has ripened. Shall we go and look? Let’s not forget why we all came…”

Maureen thought about the hounds tearing at the screaming shades, about the two warm purple fruits she had placed against her own breastbone. These leaders had come for a harvest that she had already eaten from. She kept her face bored.

Before Augurit had even finished speaking, there was a flicker of motion at the edge of the crowd. A figure, faint and almost see-through, appeared for half a heartbeat before it plunged into the grey gate and disappeared.

*Evans,* Tally noted, matching the way the figure moved to the ship and the auction ground.

*Of course it’s him.* Maureen thought.

*Of course.* Tally agreed.

The faction leaders exploded with anger. “Picking fruit off a tree he never watered!”

“Evans! Nobody shields you this time!”

“That madman. Augurit, say what you like. Today he dies.”

One after another, the leaders flashed forward and threw themselves into the rippling grey gateway. Its surface danced like a pond after a stone had been tossed in. In seconds, only the apprentices remained on the field.

* * *

Blast after blast came through the gate, dull explosions like a hammer inside a barrel. They were fighting inside.

Maureen felt each thud in her teeth and the soles of her feet. To her left, the thin apprentice mouthed numbers, trying to keep count of the blasts. He would forget one, and his lips would mouth the same number again. None of the other apprentices said anything. The grass quivered slightly every time another explosion boomed through the gate.

Tally tried to keep count as well, but gave up at forty. [I don’t have a view in there.] [All I have is your ears, and they are full of noise right now.]

“What is this?” Augurit Jones’s voice suddenly broke through. It was sharper than Maureen had ever heard it before. “Dark elves? So that’s why none of you came out. You were after something else all along!”

[That is Augurit Jones’s voice.] [And I counted zero dark elves coming out this morning when I took inventory.]

*Zero.* It sounded offended, as if everyone should have known that already.

“Power,” one of the other leaders’ voices shouted over the sound of more explosions. “Feel that. Real power.”

“Blood,” another leader cried out. “That’s bloodline.”

The voices were tumbling out on top of each other, half disbelieving, half delighted.

It sounded like greed. They were old voices, and the note of delight sounded very similar to Julo’s laugh when he’d held up a pouch full of magic stones. Maureen felt her stomach tighten. Men that strong weren’t delighted by anything that ended well for the person who owned it.

A woman’s voice rose in fury and then in alarm, but more blasts drowned her out.

[That is Eve, Night Mother.] [By voice.]

“Silver Soul Hand!” someone roared. “Damn you, you bloodline mongrels!”

Another voice called out, almost gleefully. “The Child of the Silver Soul? So the stories were true. A blood-sorcerer who’s drunk from Purgatory itself!”

*The Child of the Silver Soul,* Tally said in its own warm voice. *Another new name. The Silver Soul Hand. I’m pulling the old file now.*

Maureen’s display flashed with one line: [Silver Soul Token: function no data. Owner’s title: now known.]

*The title belongs with the follower who called Aaron Your Eminence,* Tally noted, and moved both names out of the unknowns.

The token was in her inner pocket, pressed against the cracked Seal. It suddenly felt heavier. Colder. As if hearing its owner’s title had added weight to it. Her right hand began to drift toward her coat.

*Not here.* Tally’s voice was quick and cold.

Her hand stopped, then returned to her side as if it had only been brushing away a speck of dust.

“Run, then, Child!” laughed one of the leaders, the sound carrying eerily through the shivering air. “You’re far too young. I’ll catch you and open you up and see what’s running in those veins.”

Maureen thought of the anatomy lecturer’s pinned blue-blood frog, magnified on the wall at the front of the hall. She thought of the count’s son who’d written down at the age of six that he intended to marry the girl next door when they were older. Her jaw set before she could stop it.

“No! The Skoll fruit, the fruit that lifts an Official Wizard!” cried one voice. “Evans, get off it!”

“Evans, you, you’ve already… ah!”

*Already done that with the last fruit,* Tally said quietly. *Evans, First-Tier Wizard. Longest time in files.*

*The last one,* Maureen thought back silently.

*One of the last,* Tally corrected her gently. *There are two of them inside you, Maureen.*

*Keep your face.*

A surge of energy poured out from the gate, a flood of particles, radiation, and pressure. Along the rows of apprentices, young men and women began to turn white, swaying on their feet. The thin apprentice next to Maureen stopped mouthing his counts to himself. One boy doubled over and retched into the grass.

The flood threatened to knock Maureen over as well, pushing behind her eyes like the crystal ball had. But where the other apprentices’ spirits gave way under the onslaught, Maureen’s held firm. She just narrowed her eyes, making sure she caught every word that was shouted through the gate.

* * *

The grey surface of the gate shuddered. It began to bulge outward, like a sail filling with a sudden gust of wind. The humming noise climbed in pitch until it was a high whine.

[Surge: incoming. By sight.]

*And everyone else is going down,* Tally added quickly.

There was a flat crack like a whip and the gate burst open. A wave of energy swept across the field, like water cascading over the weir of a dam.

Maureen’s mouth flooded with the taste of struck flint and scorched honey. Her teeth began to ring with a high-pitched note that came from nowhere and threatened to shatter them. The rich, sweet air of the realm began to rush past her ears in one long exhalation, like a sigh forced from the mouth of the dead.

The torrent of energy swept through the apprentices like a storm wind and knocked them off their feet. Shouts arose as they fell in heaps, the thin boy next to Maureen thrown flat on his face.

She could have stayed on her feet through the surge. But a heartbeat after the boy fell, Maureen dropped to one knee, placing a palm flat on the grass, and bowed her head like all the rest.

*Nicely average,* Tally commented.

*I’ve been practising,* she replied silently.

Down at knee height, Maureen breathed in the scent of crushed grass and grit that blew up around her. Through her eyelashes, she watched the other apprentices moaning and covering their heads with their arms. Figures began to shoot out of the broken gate. Among them a vast black serpent phantom reared up, its head lifted high and a tremendous hiss bursting from its mouth that rolled across the field like thunder.

It was made of coils upon coils of sulphur smoke, with tiny flames running along its body like a fuse. Maureen felt the hiss in her breastbone just as she had felt the hum in the ship from Evans.

[Odour: sulphur, fire-smoke.]

Tally didn’t need to tell her whose.

Several members of the Silver Soul Hand ran beneath the serpent phantom, chanting in short, urgent bursts. As they ran, their thumbs—wet with their own blood—drew quick, jagged lines across their faces. The runes were scratched into their skin so hastily they might have been drawn by a child. With each red glistening stroke upon their pale faces, the serpent grew more solid, thickening from insubstantial smoke toward something with weight. Their silver robes streamed out behind them like tongues of flame caught in the wind.

Lying along the broad, spectral head of the serpent was Aaron, his silver robe torn and muddied from his fall. His face was as grey as old paper and his eyes squeezed shut.

*He’s hurt,* Tally said. *Pale, eyes closed, breathing . . . I can’t count it from here.*

*Count anyway.*

*Four,* Tally said after a moment. *Maybe.*

That was close enough to a guess that it was allowed one. Something twisted in Maureen’s chest, right below her ribs where the token rested. Her throat grew tight, but she kept her eyes on Aaron, refusing to name the feeling.

*Worry,* Tally said quietly. *For a friend. That’s what I’ll log it as.*

Maureen let the word stand unchallenged.

Driven by the bloody runes, the serpent surged away, its coils twisting as it flew over the field and up the slope, carrying Aaron and his order toward the horizon faster than any horse could manage.

On the other side of the wreckage, Eve, Ani and several other elves broke into a run.

Between them they carried a crystal box.

Inside the box, a purple heart expanded and contracted, expanded and contracted. It beat, wet and glossy. The light from the heart pulsed over the elves’ pale hands. Eve’s red lips were drawn in a hard, straight line.

*Twenty-two, twenty-three, twenty-four, twenty-five,* Tally said. *Watching.* That many times the heart had swelled since Maureen had first seen it. *Twenty-five swells in the same amount of time as the Seal Stone’s seventy-two.* *Slower,* it said. *Not the same rhythm. And the stone was found next to a failing sealing array. This heart came out of something that was sealed.*

*We sold them the key to whatever that came out of,* Tally said.

*We sold them a key we couldn’t use,* Maureen answered silently.

*Both columns,* Tally said.

Evans exploded out from the ruins, followed closely by the faction leaders. He dissolved into a black streak of energy that shot away from the gathering ground. His face was flushed, and he moved as though he were wounded, but he wore a grin.

It was the same smile he’d worn as he stood over the ship’s dead. Cold slid down Maureen’s spine, and she pressed her palm more firmly against the warm grass.

The leaders roared as they burst out from the wreckage, then split into three groups, all heading in different directions. One group pursued Aaron, another the elves, and the third the Shadow Hunter. They moved in their own peculiar wizardries—one became a gust of wind, one a flock of shadowy birds, one a streak of pale fire.

For long moments the sky was scored with light as the factions sped toward their prey. Then the sound began to draw away, echoing off the hills until it faded to nothing.

* * *

Quiet returned. Around Maureen, the apprentices groaned. After a while, they began to get up slowly.

Above the flattened grass, a scorched ring still hung in the air where the gate had been. Particles of grit drifted in the low sunlight.

Maureen rose with the others. Her movements were as slow as theirs, and she brushed a seed from her knee. Grit had settled in her throat, and her teeth still rang faintly.

She coughed lightly into her fist, then raised her eyes to the hills where the serpent had gone. Her expression was unreadable to those near her.

*You want me to keep the bearing?* Tally asked.

*Yes,* Maureen answered silently.

Tally obeyed. *Bearing logged. Line to horizon. Beyond that there is no data.*

There was no one powerful left on the field. The apprentices sat in the flattened grass and said nothing. The shadows of the scorched gate posts stretched across them, lengthening with the sun’s descent.

Tally measured the time of their waiting by the shadows. *One hour, fifty-seven minutes,* it told Maureen.

*Bored yet?* she asked it silently.

*No. Waiting professionally.*

At last the leaders returned. Their faces were inscrutable—neither triumphant nor defeated. No one spoke of what the chase had won.

[Tells: none. Not proof.]

*They’ve been practising those expressions for centuries,* Tally noted.

*So have we,* Maureen responded silently.

*You and I have only had a few years of practice,* Tally said. *We need more time.*

 The leaders exchanged a few words, too low for Maureen to hear, then nodded in agreement.

Each order would take its own surviving apprentices home.

Jason did not look up and down the lines anymore. His cane remained still. Stark’s bone mask was turned to face the hills. Whatever they wanted from Jack, they had put it aside for the day. She could not tell for how long.

Augurit Jones looked at the scorched ring where the gate had hung. “As of today,” he said, “this realm is a corpse.”

Then he turned to the handful of Dark Forest apprentices who remained. He smiled at them, a gentle expression on his aged, mild face.

“It’s over. Come. Let’s go home to the Dark Forest.”

Maureen fell into step with the thin line of black robes. The token burned coldly against her ribs, and she looked back once at the empty hills.

*Home,* Tally repeated.

*The Dark Forest,* Maureen corrected silently.

*Home logged under protest,* Tally said.
