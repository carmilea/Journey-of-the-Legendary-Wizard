Karl was waiting for her inside the examination hall and he walked her back outside, still talking about the exam, still glowing with pleasure. “Congratulations, Richard. Truly. Nobody passes that one.”

Now they would visit the head of the department, and once the head confirmed her, Richard would be a potion master of the Pharmacy Department.

The smell of the Wind-Soul Hunting Potion was still in her sleeves, a bitter smell like green leaves and the smoke of a lamp left burning overnight. Her fingertips remembered the half-beat she’d held against Tally’s timing.

That was the right half-beat.

*You know, Karl’s said congratulations three times since we came out that door,* Tally noted in her head.

*Let him. He means it. Every time.*

*I’m just saying. I’m not complaining. Just making note.*

Ahead of them, where the passage curved around the tower’s spiral, Maureen could see the Jadeleaf party waiting at the balustrade. Joanna stood with Christine and four other green-robed apprentices, all of their faces turned toward the hall door as though it might bite them.

*Joanna’s heart is beating fast, her pulse visible at her throat,* Tally said. *Christine’s jaw is set. The four apprentices are whispering behind their hands. They stopped when they saw Karl’s purple robe come round the curve.*

“Richard has passed,” he told them, his voice filled with a little proprietary pride.

“Then the Ring will—” Joanna managed to get out.

“The ranking tournament is no longer of consequence to us,” Karl interrupted her. “As long as Richard remains in the department, the Ring will draw a larger share of resources and be granted wider permissions. You will never have to fight for your place again.”

The faces of the apprentices changed from carefully blank to brightly smiling, all in a moment. One of the girls even laughed aloud before she clapped a hand over her mouth. The faded green leaves on their robes seemed less shabby all of a sudden.

“You don’t know what this—” Joanna began again, and then she faltered. “We were—”

 The Jadeleaf Ring had been at its weakest when Senge was coughing up blood into his silk handkerchief. This share meant that they stood a chance, finally, to grow stronger slowly instead of dying slowly. Her lips moved into the beginnings of a smile before she consciously decided to let the expression go. A small warmth sat under her breastbone, but she sternly refused to give it a name. On Richard’s plain face, it became a mild nod.

*Log that as relief, please,* Maureen thought silently to Tally.

*Done,* Tally replied back, just as quietly in Maureen’s mind. *And also . . . something with teeth in it. From Christine.*

The girl with the deep gold hair and bright blue eyes stepped forward. “Congratulations, Wizard Richard,” she said in a low voice. Every syllable was polite, every syllable had first been sharpened on an oilstone.

Christine’s hands were clenched together, her knuckles white from how tightly she gripped her own sleeve. It was envy that made her so angry, but buried beneath that was the vow she’d made at the dinner table of the Jadeleaf Ring, aimed like a spear straight at the rank that Richard wore so effortlessly.

“The bargain still stands,” Maureen told Joanna, using Richard’s flat voice. “I will continue with Karl. Do not wait for me.” Joanna bowed her head even lower than a steward would before an Official Wizard.

As they walked away, Tally spoke up in the back of Maureen’s head. *No crimson. Your eyes stayed black through the smile.*

*Good,* Maureen replied silently.

*I’ll keep watching anyway.*

* * *

“We are going to Wizard Ambrose then,” Karl explained. “He has been running the potion brewing out at the Resource Point for weeks now. He’s a busy man and can’t come to us, so we must go to him.”

“Two Night-Crown Eagles,” Karl snapped his fingers at one of the servants dressed in grey livery. “We’re going out to the Resource Point.”

“Yes sir,” the man nodded quickly before turning and dashing off towards the mews.

Minutes later, two great eagles emerged from the stone building. Their heads were hooded, and they were tethered by chains to the servant who’d fetched them. Each bird stood as high at the shoulder as a horse, but their feet were much larger in proportion to their bodies. A crown of dark feathers ran along their heads, and their flat yellow eyes stared unblinkingly ahead as their sharp talons clicked on the frost-rimed flagstones of the plaza. The stink of musk rolled off the birds, mingled with the copper reek of old meat.

Tally began building a profile as Maureen eyed the creatures. Wingspan approximated against the reach of her own arms, an analysis of the stiffness of the feathers, a faint sheath of wind particles running the length of the flight feathers. [Altered by wizardry. Sight. Method: no data.] Profiled and filed away with the horned beasts that had pulled the carriages during the muster.

Maureen allowed the servant to help her into the saddle, then she ran her hand over the stiff feathers of the eagle’s shoulders. “So this is how Emilia flies.”

Karl laughed as he climbed into his own saddle. “Once you’ve ridden an eagle, you’ll never want to ride in a carriage again.”

Maureen felt the feathers creak beneath her palm, like oiled leather. The heat from the eagle’s body radiated up through her thighs, and the great bird’s lungs pumped like a blacksmith’s bellows beneath her.

*Can you ride this for me?* Maureen asked Tally silently.

*No,* Tally answered. *But I can count your knuckles as they turn white.*

The eagles’ great wings beat down four times, lifting them from the open plaza behind the tower. The spiral stone of the tower fell away quickly, followed by the walled manors of the wizards’ tract, and finally by the pale shimmer of the city’s wards.

The cold wind slapped Maureen across the face as they climbed towards the sky. She held her breath at the top of their ascent without realizing it, her fingers locked deep in the dark crown of feathers atop the eagle’s head. Richard’s borrowed face remained exactly where Mimicry had placed it.

*Eleven seconds,* Tally noted silently, *and every one of your knuckles is white.*

*I’m breathing now,* Maureen answered silently back.

*Mimicry is working very well in this wind too. No strain, no drain,* Tally continued.

Over the brown plains and the glinting channels that snaked through them, Maureen shouted out loud to Karl, “What is a Resource Point then?”

“Wizards require materials for all experiments, all conjectures, all refinements upon a spell,” Karl shouted back over the wind. “Nature provides these things but does so very reluctantly, and hides them as well. So those with power turned their attention to the land itself. They bent it to their will, creating places where the materials they desired grew thick and strong. These places are called Resource Points.”

In the era of Ancient Wizards, every wizard tended his own Resource Point. They were capable of providing enough material for their own experiments, and enough excess to trade. That was one of the reasons they shone so brightly.

 Below her the channels shone like silver wires stretched across a vast piece of brown cloth.

“The lineages broke,” Karl called out again. His voice had dropped lower and was carried away by the wind. “The techniques were lost to us. Now we only maintain what the Ancients left for us, and harvest it from there.” He sighed. “The entire Alliance controls only two or three such places now.”

Tally ran Karl’s words against her file on Ancient Wizards. The bronze book mentioned cultivated material grounds in its catalogue, and Gregory’s lair possessed a Planting tunnel and a greenhouse. [Consistency: high.]

*So he wasn’t just boasting then,* Maureen noted silently to Tally as she regarded the horizon.

Her eyes remained fixed there, flat and far away as the wind snatched at Richard’s coat.

* * *

It was opening up ahead, the land dropping down into a wide bowl. At a line as sharp as if drawn by a ruler, the frost vanished from the plain before them, replaced by green.

The air changed as they crossed over that line, in the space of a single breath. It grew sweet and wet, like the scent of rain upon warm stone. Something deep inside Maureen’s chest eased by another notch, something that had grown tight since they left Peaceridge.

[Air ahead: energy distribution abnormal. Density high, even, rounding towards centre.]

Tally painted the flow of particles in amber across the landscape below. Laid over the ordered beds of wizard plants, the threads of amber curved gently inward from every edge, like water running to a drain. The particle density grew thicker and thicker as it approached the heart of this place, forming a smooth bell of energy over the centre. No natural ground Maureen had ever stood upon looked like that.

[Beneficial to body. By inference: pulse −5, breathing easier.]

Tally added, *I can’t name a single gas in the mix yet. Still lack nose. But your body is voting yes.*

Maureen did not need Tally’s analysis. She could feel the difference in her lungs. This was good air.

A prickling sensation crawled up the back of Maureen’s neck. *I’ve breathed this before,* she thought. *This exact weight of air.*

[Profile match: Proan Canyon valley. 71%. By particle log.]

Tally compared the old record with the new, laying one over the other. The hidden valley in Proan Canyon existed now only as dead data in Tally’s log files. Maureen saw it again, the white petals spinning in the swift stream, the yellow pollen thick in the light, the bees buzzing between blossoms. One foot had been standing in the height of summer while the other remained at the edge of winter. Then the vortex had folded closed behind her, like an eye shutting its lid.

*He grew his own?* Tally asked quietly.

*We’ll find out how,* Maureen responded silently.

After that, Maureen said nothing for a long stretch of wind. Her fingers had grown stiff once more, locked in the eagle’s feathers, and Tally did not bother counting them this time.

“Look! There, the Preserve!” Karl stood in his stirrups and pointed down. “”

*“Preserve” is the third thing in our files under that word,* Tally noted. *After Gregory’s realm and the trial realm. Do you want me to file it separately?*

*File it as the Preserve.*

Karl laid his palm flat against the top of his bird’s head. It gave a great scream that echoed back off the fields, then tipped into a slow spiral descent. Maureen repeated the gesture, and her own bird followed suit.

The ground swung up beneath them in a long curve, the ordered beds below spinning in a blur of green, silver-veined grey, and red so dark it was nearly black, each block sharply squared off against the next like columns in a ledger.

They came to rest on a stone apron at one edge of the beds. Karl dismounted from the saddle, slapping dust from the sleeves of his purple robe with an air of quiet pride, as though he had been personally responsible for the construction of the place.

Maureen could feel warmth from the ground, rising through the soles of her boots. The air buzzed with a low hum, like bees trapped behind the walls of a house. Fine silver lines edged every bed of plants, glyphs so faint they were easier to feel than see. Tally read them with the particle sense: nodes in an array, laid out according to an ancient and patient pattern. Parts of Gregory’s rune shelves were represented here, but in a different configuration. There were also breaks in the pattern at intervals, where the work looked newer and the lines more crudely drawn, like mortar slapped over cut stone.

“We keep it alive,” Karl said without shame. “But we don’t understand it.”

“Who would be able to make one now?” Maureen asked.

Karl laughed. “Nobody living, Richard.”

Richard’s face remained politely interested in the conversation, but Maureen’s eyes strayed past Karl to where the green of the fields met the frost of the plain. They lingered there too long.

*I have a profile of one,* Tally noted, *and a library of fragments, but no data on how to construct the whole.*

*We’ll get that data now,* Maureen responded silently.

Tally opened a new file, and titled it “resource point, our own”. Maureen left the name alone.

Karl led her down a gravel path between the plant beds, toward a long, low hall constructed from blocks of pale stone. A lazy stream of brewing smoke curled from vents running along its roof.

* * *

When Karl and Maureen entered the hall, all heads turned in their direction. The murmur of low conversation in the room died like water suddenly escaping a cracked jug.

Tally sorted them by density, using the Barok sample as a benchmark. She didn’t assign specific ranks, just told Maureen which ones were thicker and which were thinner than the others.

Karl raised his voice to call over the sudden silence. “Everyone! This is Richard. He passed the examination this morning. He’s joining the department.”

“Newcomer?”

“Haven’t seen one of those in years.”

The Pharmacy wizards exchanged glances, finding the same surprise reflected in all faces.

That surprise slowly shifted into welcome. “Welcome, Richard!”

“Welcome to the department!”

Several of the potion masters clapped their hands, while one woman even lifted a steaming beaker as though in toast.

In moments, the hall warmed up with mint smoke, the smell of burnt sugar, and laughter. It felt oddly difficult for Maureen to stand in such a mass of goodwill. Everyone was smiling so easily, laughing at the slightest excuse.

Maureen walked down the line, greeting each of the wizards in Richard’s flat voice. Her face was polite and interested as she bowed slightly to each in turn. Behind that face, though, she was counting.

*If this is everyone in the Pharmacy Department,* Tally noted, *then the entire Alliance has about twenty-odd potion masters. They could probably all fit in this single hall, benches included.*

*Worth knowing.* Maureen responded silently.

*I already wrote it down.*

An old man made his way through the crowd toward Maureen and Karl, walking with a slow but unhurried step. His white-gold robe stood out against the purple robes of the other potion masters. His hair, too, was silver, and combed with a meticulousness that made not a single strand lie out of place.

White and gold.

For half a breath, Maureen felt her throat close up, remembering Adrian’s robes draped over his chair at the lab bench. Adrian’s voice. No. I don’t sell my apprentices.

*Colour only,* Tally added. *He’s also the densest source in the room. Density estimates put him in Maffie’s range, medium confidence.*

“Richard.” The old man extended his hand with a courtly gesture. “Congratulations on passing our entrance examination. I am Ambrose, head of the Alliance Pharmacy Department. Welcome.”

“Wizard Ambrose,” Maureen said, bowing her head slightly.

Ambrose gestured for her to place her palm flat against a small slate of black glass. As she did so, Maureen felt her palm brand sink into the stone, then glow faintly. With that, Richard, Honorary Professor of the Jadeleaf Ring, became a potion master of the Alliance Pharmacy Department.

Ambrose regarded Maureen with a speculative expression for another moment. “The entrance examination proves you are capable of brewing,” he said. “But it does not prove how well.” He turned his head toward the long hall. “Before I put you anywhere, I want to see what you can really do. Then we will find a potion group, and then tasks.”

“Different tasks earn different contribution points,” he added, watching Maureen’s face. “You understand.”

“Yes, Wizard Ambrose.” Maureen nodded politely.

Contribution points were the Alliance’s own currency, earned by completing tasks and exchanged for resources, materials, and magic stones. In this place, they had more value than any purse of coins could hold.

Tally opened up a new [Contribution: 0] column next to [Magic stones, all columns: ~14,200].

*New column,* Maureen thought silently.

*More than a column,* Tally responded with quiet delight. *It’s a whole new currency.*

*Yes, it is,* Maureen agreed.

Maureen agreed to the assessment with a nod, then stood quietly as Ambrose made further small talk. Through the tall window that stretched along the side of the hall, she could see the ordered beds of the Preserve humming beneath their silver lines.
