Florian’s head lay in the mud at their feet. Nobody moved. From the edge of the crowd, hiding behind Richard’s weak-jawed face, Maureen went over what had changed.

An hour ago, every sense the caravan possessed had reported Alice as an ordinary girl. An hour ago, Florian had been just another Second-Class apprentice, willing to pay ten thousand gold for the privilege of being the first to turn her over in the mud.

The horses felt it before the people did. They stamped in their traces, rolling the whites of their eyes, and the crisp smell of pine resin became thin under something else, metallic and spoiled honey. The hair on the back of Richard’s neck rose.

*An hour ago there was nothing here,* Tally said quietly. *Now there is a wall.*

[Decode: 18:41:07]

*Finish it,* Maureen told her silently.

The noblemen were grinning no longer. They were backing away from the floating girl, shrieking, “What are you? Who are you? What ARE you?” The entire crowd began to back away with them, boots slipping in the churned mud. No one wanted to be in front any more.

Alice rose into the air. Her bare feet left the ground, her toes pointed down as if she had been hanged. Her eyes were gone, the blood from them running freely down her face. The grey rags she wore were soaked red from the collar to the hem.

“Maureen... my Maureen... the game... Alice came to find Sister Maureen,” Alice murmured in a sing-song voice that sounded as though it had been broken on the wheel.

She had no eyes, but her face still made expressions. She smiled at them, a smile that did not sit right on her mouth.

Then she was gone from the air. One of the young noblemen, edging toward the trees, turned around and found her face a hand’s span from his own.

Red light flared, swelling with a soft, wet sound, and he was gone without even a grunt of pain.

Pieces of his fine embroidered silks drifted upwards to settle in her open hands, blown by a breeze that wasn’t there. Alice looked down at them, pouting, suddenly looking small and lost and alone. Everyone who saw her flinched back, as they would from an adder hiding in the long grass.

“Not you,” she said sadly. “You’re not her.” She tilted her head to one side, and Maureen could hear the bones shift. “But you said. You all said you were my Maureen, and she wanted to play with Alice.” The smile reappeared. “So let’s play. Alice is coming to find you.”

She blinked out of existence, then back into being next to another nobleman. Again red particles boiled over him before his mouth had finished opening to scream, and when they settled there was nothing left of him.

Tally’s Mirror Pupil lens laid a web over the girl, a web of faintly glowing red threads, but Maureen could see no seam in it. It was recording every flicker of her, Tally said. The decode would want all the data.

Maureen kept her hands loose at her sides.

One of the mercenaries spoke out loud what everyone had already realised. “She heard them. Their game. She thinks her Maureen is one of them, and she’s checking them one by one.” Six or seven of the noblemen lay dead already.

The brown-haired man’s face reddened to brick, then deepened in colour still further. “All of us! Now! When she’s finished with the noblemen and still hasn’t found her Maureen, who do you think she’ll come for next? None of us alone can stand against her, but thirty together...!”

Spells went up raggedly, then in a concerted burst as people realised the others were attacking too. Fireballs flared in the late afternoon air, acid sprayed in sour smelling green globules, and wind blades keened like banshees. They were all swallowed by the blood-red light as stones are swallowed when dropped into a still pond.

The air stank of scorched hair and wet ash.

Maureen stood in the second row, not casting any spells. To these people, Richard was just a Third-Class apprentice wizard.

The decode finished, and Tally’s cold register switched on.

[Wraith Doll. Akd clan, Purgatory World. Str ??? Agi ??? Con ??? Spirit ??? Mana ???. Danger: strong.]

Her warm tone returned. *The question marks are honest, Maureen. I would not pad a data file.*

*What is it?*

*A curse made from a maiden and an energy signature. It will hunt that signature to the end of the world, unless the doll itself is slain. It will not recognise faces. Only the energy pattern of its target.*

*Whose energy pattern?* Maureen asked silently.

*Yours, Maureen. Whose else?*

For the first time in days, Maureen Olsen’s breath caught. It was a small thing, a hitch beneath her ribs, barely noticeable, and no one in the crowd could see it.

*The Purgatory World,* Maureen said silently.

*The Pankora. Gregory’s gate. Aaron’s blood. One thread again,* said Tally. *I do not like the number of threads that end with that word.*

Maureen’s eyes narrowed. This was more tangled than she had thought, and somewhere out there, a hand was pulling on the thread. But for now, she had to deal with the doll.

A list of countermeasures scrolled up in Tally’s pale blue text.

>Purgatory Fire (no data on ability to burn curses)

>Mirror Pupil crystallisation (untested on spirit-stuff)

>potion brewed from decoded curse entries

Maureen’s eyebrow rose at the final entry. *That one doesn’t exist yet, does it?*

*No, Maureen. We have the formula, but it would need to be brewed.*

*Then I’ll brew it.*

Under the massed volleys, Alice thinned to a mist of red and drifted away between the trunks. No one cheered, because the mist didn’t look defeated. It just looked bored.

They counted heads. “Seventeen dead,” said someone standing near the wagons, and the young noblemen were among the seventeen. The caravan rolled onwards with cracks of whips, before dusk fell and the wheels began to squeal, everyone eager to put distance between themselves and the place where Alice had killed.

Maureen climbed into her carriage and tied shut the curtains.

*I’m keeping the dead off your display, like you asked me to do long ago,* said Tally quietly. *But I’m counting them.*

* * *

The surviving young noblemen crowded around the fire, shoulder to shoulder, passing around a wineskin while keeping their backs to the flames. Sparks from the blaze flew up into the dark, but the wall of trees beyond the rough circle of wagons was black and impenetrable. A guard choked out a warning. Everyone turned.

There was Alice, standing at the treeline. She looked exactly as she had when she had first approached the caravan. Her grey dress was in rags. Her skin was pale. Her features were delicate. She was a small, fragile girl with a frightened face.

In the light of the fire, she was any little girl lost in the woods. The kind of child who would make strangers want to wrap her in a blanket for warmth. But no one in the circle felt anything but cold.

First the young noblemen jumped up, spilling the wine.

“She’s here!”

“She’s HERE!”

The brown-haired man roared at them to fall in line, and then spells began pouring from every member of the camp. The air bucked under the force of so many spells. The volley ripped through the night, scouring the treeline.

Alice was gone.

For the space of a heartbeat, someone began to laugh.

*I felt every spell that was launched,* said Tally. *But where the girl was standing . . . there wasn’t any density. There wasn’t before, and there isn’t now. She’s not there. As a source, the doll doesn’t exist for me. I only have Alice through your eyes and ears, Maureen.*

A voice came from the other side of the campfire. It was small, and teary. “Sister Maureen . . . which of you is my Maureen? Which one?”

The Wraith Doll was crouched in a high tree branch just above the light of the fire, her thin arms wrapped around her knees. She stared down at the people in the camp as if they had hurt her feelings. “Damn it,” someone whispered, and the camp’s courage died, snuffed out like a candle flame pinched between thumb and forefinger.

Maureen slept in her carriage. The curtains were drawn across the windows, fastened shut with their cords knotted tight. No one could look inside, not even by mistake. It was a habit she’d retained since the ship.

She slept lightly, barely skimming the surface of unconsciousness. She woke at every crackle of the fire.

At 03:10, a chime rang deep within her head and Maureen was instantly awake.

[Source: none. Sound: bare feet on leaves.]

She lay still in the dark, listening. The sounds of soft footsteps circled the wheel of the carriage, then stopped beside the door. They were close enough that only a thin panel of wood stood between them.

The footsteps began moving again. They walked away from Maureen’s carriage, continuing on towards the wagons where the young nobles were huddled.

*Is that her?* Maureen asked silently.

*It sounds like a girl’s weight. Barefoot,* Tally said.

*By density?*

*There is none. I told you.*

*How sure are you?*

*Seventy per cent Alice, thirty per cent not. I hate that number.*

The footsteps moved away. In the grey of early dawn, one of the noblemen’s bedrolls was found empty. His blanket had been thrown back, and lying on the damp grass beside the bedding was a long strip torn from the collar of his shirt.

* * *

The caravan drove hard for Emilia over the following days. The horses could not have stood much more and still travelled at any speed. Alice followed them, a ghostly presence. Every day another one or two of them would die, and almost always they were one of the noblemen who had joined the game.

Outside in the morning, the remaining noblemen rode in a tight knot at the centre of the column. No one would ride beside them, and no one spoke to them. The horses’ flanks were white with lather.

Inside, with the curtains knotted shut to stop them from rattling, Maureen had turned the carriage into a laboratory. Glassware was wedged into the straw on the bench seat, and the flame of the small burner jumped with every rut. The carriage smelled like cold metal and something floral.

[Pouch: −3] ticked over as Maureen drew the Olivia storehouse darkness materials out from the Pouch at her waist, along with a twist of the loot from the trial. Before she let Maureen light the burner, Tally had simulated the entire formula twice in Maureen’s head.

Maureen held the flame to the base and watched it reduce. While Tally’s silent voice talked in her head, Maureen’s mind went back over those nights in camp. The doll had walked right past Maureen’s bed twice, without stopping. It had searched every other face though. So the doll was keyed to Maureen’s apprentice signature, before the rite. *But who had held a live channel to the apprentice Maureen?* Tally asked.

*Daniel’s brand,* Maureen responded silently.

*The Olivias keep receipts,* Tally said in her head. *Confidence: medium.*

*Maker?*

*No data.*

Maureen’s thumb found the spot on her wrist, just below Adrian’s brand, where Daniel’s mark used to burn. She’d felt its touch go cold. It felt like a door someone else had kept a key to.

*You are holding the flame half a beat too long,* Tally said.

*On purpose,* Maureen answered silently.

*Pause. Correct. Again.*

The base reduction cleared to a deep blue, almost glassy colour. It settled down with a faint sound, like the humming of a choir heard from across a lake. Tally read out the name from the decoded entry. The Lament of Flowing Music.

Tally directed Maureen through the trigger, the line-tongue words Sethan ilvaré. Maureen moved her lips and tongue around the words, making no sound. Her tongue curled back against the roof of her mouth, held low for the second word. *Not aloud near the glass,* Tally said. Maureen did not need telling twice.

*You slept in four pieces last night,* Tally said. *Your right heel has been tapping on the floor for a quarter of an hour now.*

Maureen made her heel stop.

Someone knocked on the side panel. She quickly stoppered the vial, gathered up the other pieces of glassware and swept them into the Pouch. Only then did she untie the curtain.

The deep-blue vial was the only thing remaining in her hand.

“Lord Richard,” Joanna said, leaning in. She kept her voice low. Her sister didn’t know. “You’re an Official Wizard. Why haven’t you done anything about her?”

Maureen turned the vial in her fingers, letting the light run blue through the thick glass. “Not yet.”

Joanna’s eyes flickered down to the vial, then back up to Maureen’s face, before dropping again to the vial. She didn’t ask twice.

*Seventeen the first night, one or two a day since,* Tally said.

*Not yet,* Maureen answered silently.

*I know,* Tally said. *I’m just saying I’m counting.*

Maureen’s fingers did not tremble at all around the cool glass.

*Soon,* she thought. *Very soon.*

Out by the wagons, Christine was laughing. Alice had only taken those who had participated in the noblemen’s games, and Christine had never played. She was safe. The shunned noblemen were now hanging on her every word, and she was receiving far more attention than she ever had back home. Christine glanced over as her sister stepped down from Richard’s carriage. Her mouth thinned into a disapproving line.

“What does she want with a Third-Class nobody?” she said, loud enough for her words to carry back. She gave Maureen one cold look before turning away.

*She thinks you’re Third-Class,* Tally said with a hint of smugness in her voice.

*Let her,* Maureen answered silently.

Tally filed the glare anyway, under “unconfirmed”.

* * *

The quarrel came to a head at the lead wagon. A knot of mercenaries and apprentices grabbed their packs and left. “It’s their mess! It started with the nobles! Let them play with her!” They threaded their way between the trunks, packs thumping up and down against their backs as they ran. After fifty paces their voices were lost amongst the trees.

Then the screams began. Short, loud and close. People ran towards the sound, but when they arrived they found nothing. No bodies. Just bits of cloth strewn across the leaf litter.

[Remains: cloth. Bodies: 0.] *Four went out,* Tally said. *There are four missing now. Left side, against the oak.*

Alice crouched against the trunk, her arms wrapped around her shins. She blinked up at them, shrinking away slightly as if they had frightened her.

A coldness crept through everyone, at the same time. They all felt it. No one needed to say anything. She was a burr stuck to their sides, and there was no running from her.

The tension became too much for one of the burly swordsmen. “What are you? What ARE you?” He pulled his broadsword from its sheath. “Come on then!”

He charged Alice, bringing the blade down with both hands, aiming for her head.

Tally’s lens showed the blade passing through Alice without hindrance, like a knife slicing through a smoke ring.

[Physical: no effect.]

*Fire then,* Tally said. *Or your potion. Not steel.*

The broadsword bit deeply into the oak behind Alice, sticking fast in the wood. Alice blinked and reached up to stroke the man’s stubbled cheek.

“You’re not... my Maureen.”

Her eyes, so dark and clear a moment before, were once again two bloody holes. The swordsman began to soften, as if he were a candle left too long near the fire. He melted, dripping to the ground until there was nothing but a yellow puddle and two scraps of cloth lying in it. The smell of tallow and bile hung in the air.

One of the apprentices let out a high-pitched shriek. “We can’t fight that! Break up, run different ways! If we stay together we all die! If we split up some of us might live!”

The words went through the crowd like a spark thrown into a barrel full of dry powder. They ran, scattering in every direction, mercenaries, apprentices, nobles, Joanna and Christine among them. In a matter of moments, the road was deserted save for one wagon that lay half across the ruts. One of the horses, still hitched to the wagon, stomped the ground with an impatient hoof. The sound of hooves and crashing brush faded away between the trees until there was nothing but silence.

*We’re staying, aren’t we?* Tally said. It wasn’t really a question.

*Yes.*

* * *

The road was very quiet now. An abandoned pack had spilled its contents so that a blanket lay draped over one of the deep wheel ruts. A discarded water skin lay on its side, its contents leaking out slowly to form a dark puddle in the mud. The last horse snuffled in confusion then snorted once at nothing in particular.

Alice remained crouching down beside the oak tree, with the broadsword sticking up from above her head. She looked lost and alone, like a small child left behind after a fair.

[Seed: Joanna, dormant. Moving away, north.] *Joanna’s running and so is the flame seed,* Tally said.

*Good,* Maureen thought silently. *That’s one less to worry about.*

Maureen walked out into the middle of the road and stood with her hands loose at her sides. She smiled. “It’s me you’re looking for, isn’t it, Alice?”

Her voice sounded strange to her, coming out of Richard’s throat and reshaped by his vocal cords. The name Alice sounded especially odd. For half a breath she thought of the other Alice, the girl on the ship who wouldn’t eat.

“Sister... are you...” Alice tilted her head slightly and the bloodless little face stared at Maureen as if searching for something. “Are you my Maureen?”

*She’ll know you now,* Tally said.

*That’s the point.*

*Range ten metres.*

*I’m with you.*

Maureen took a deep breath and let go of what she’d been holding down since Peaceridge. The field of energy around her, the aura of an Official Wizard, broke free all at once. Alice’s voice died in mid-word. Leaves jumped off the road in a perfect ring around Maureen’s boots. The harnessed horse screamed and threw itself against its traces, trying to get away from whatever was happening. In the air around Maureen the dark particles were leaning towards her, drawn by her presence, like grass bending in the wind.

*The old note still sings beneath the new one,* Tally said. *The apprentice beneath the Official Wizard. That’s what the doll is keyed to.*

Alice froze, absolutely motionless.

Then slowly the Wraith Doll tilted her head back, looking up at Maureen, and the timid, bloodless little face broke open in a smile of such sweetness and delight that for a moment Maureen almost forgot what she was doing there. “Sister Maureen,” Alice breathed. “It really is you. It’s really you!” Alice clapped her small hands together. “Alice found you at last!”
