She sat at her desk with the lights burning low and her fingers interlaced. For once the burner was cold.

Maureen would soon be leaving the Dark Forest. In two years she had made very few sales. A trickle, not a flood. That had been deliberate. She needed to remain as unnoticed as possible.

Now she needed stones for the road ahead and a departing apothecary selling off her remaining stock was not uncommon.

[Days since landing: 720] Tally displayed.

Two years is such a nice number, isn't it Tally?

Yes, Maureen. It is a neat number.

Neat numbers are your weakness Tally.

Maureen reached a fingertip out to touch the black stone wall behind her desk, right where she knew her cache was hidden, enlarged after she achieved Second-Class Wizard Apprentice. "Manas" she said in the language of the ancient Andas.

The surface of the stone rippled like water beneath her finger, blurring for a second before sharpening again on the cavity that lay within.

Your first illusion Maureen, still holding strong. Well done.

Thank you Tally. I know the book I found in the library wasn't definitive and the simulation was only speculative but-

Yes, but it worked. Excellent.

Bottles and vials lined up in rows, all corked and sitting in cloth to stop them rattling together. Greens, violet blues, ambers and reds, all catching the light from her lamp in the darkened study. Nearly every beginner potion that she had made over the last two years was here. "What I sold was just a tiny part of what I've made. This is my real stock."

[Cache: Recovery 52, Berserk Blood 47, Stamina 44, Pest Repellent 26, Antidote 19, others 14. Total 202. Beginner potions success rate 96%.]

Maureen ran her fingertips over the necks of the vials as she counted them, feeling the ridges in the glass that told her what was inside. Tally's data confirmed the count. You're the best partner a wizard could have, Tally.

Say that aloud sometime Maureen. It will make me feel good.

Never Tally, never.

* * *

Maureen knocked lightly on the cluttered wooden counter that ran across Gandolf's shop. Above the stoppered jars of ingredients, the lamp hissed quietly. There was a smell of tallow and dried herbs in the air.

"Ah, my dear, you are here." Gandolf popped up seemingly from nowhere. "You must be more careful. The entire district knows about your troubles with the Olivia Family."

"I am being careful Gandolf," said Maureen, placing a small black wooden box onto the counter. "Here are some finished potions for you. Will you buy them?"

The wooden box held vials of potion which gave off a slight misty sheen. Gandolf's voice stopped mid sentence as he noticed the shimmering potions. His eyes remained fixed upon them.

[Pupil dilation, darting eye movement, tongue flicks to wet dry lips, beard twitching.] Tally flagged up the tells on Maureen's display. He wants those very badly. Badly enough to forget his pride in front of you, Tally commented.

More than he fears the Olivias? Maureen asked it silently.

No. His fear of the Olivias is greater.

"Just a moment ago I had some visitors from the Olivia Family," Gandolf said, "and they made it very clear that whoever bought any of your potions would have to face the consequences." He spread his hands wide in apology. "I'm sorry Maureen, but I am only an ordinary Third-Class apprentice. I can't afford to make an enemy of the Olivia Family by buying your potions."

Maureen took the box back without a word and left.

She clenched her jaw as she walked under the arcade back out into the Trading District. The Olivias got to Gandolf before I did.

Tally interrupted her thoughts, He broke his deal, not you. Recovery is yours now and you can sell it wherever you want.

Fair point. I lost a counter, not my word.

"Buy?! Of course I'll buy!" George laughed with a smile on his curly blond haired face when he found out what Maureen was selling. "I'm not afraid of the Olivias, and besides, I've got a family of wizards holding my contract. They back me all the way!"

"How does an ordinary potion apprentice like you afford to study pharmacology?" Maureen asked.

George shrugged. "It's a wizard family. They're backing me for their own reasons. Besides, I have some uses for fully developed potions myself." Tally noted that George didn't even flinch when Maureen mentioned the Olivia Family. [Patron family: Unnamed, unafraid.]

George looked through the contents of the box (24 Recovery, 18 Berserk Blood, 18 Stamina) and paid Maureen at her old rates without any haggling. [Magic stones: 51 → 255].

The potion apprentice's light freckles stood out in stark contrast against his tanned skin as he grinned at Maureen while she packed her things up to leave. His sleeves smelled faintly of pickling brine from the jars of roots displayed in his stall. The bag of magic stones bumped heavily against Maureen's hip as she walked, causing her black robe to sway with each step.

* * *

Maureen rapped on the huge, dusty glass window.

"I want to buy spell models."

"You again," the grey-haired shopkeeper grumbled as he threw the large pale green catalogue onto the counter top, not bothering this time to explain what it was. The book landed with a thump, kicking up a small cloud of dust that settled on the already scarred countertop. Maureen flipped the cover open, the pages felt cool and dry under her fingers.

Tally checked off the three models Maureen currently owned, marking each entry with a pale blue check mark.

>Spell model: Blast Fireball Owned
>
>Spell model: Hand of Negative Energy Owned
>
>Spell model: Mantik Death-Aura Field Owned

All of the remaining models were listed by school of magic.

Maureen ran her finger down the four columns of spell models in the catalogue. Darkness, Shadow, Necromancy, Fire.

>Spell model: Acid Missile Unowned
>
>Spell model: Undead Wind Arrow Unowned
>
>Spell model: Corrupting Black Cloud Unowned

Tally helpfully added up the number of unowned Rank Zero spell models for Maureen.

[Unowned: 11]

Maureen pushed the catalogue back across the counter.

"Darkness, Shadow, Necromancy, Fire. Apart from the ones I marked, I'll take all of them."

All of them? Tally asked, delighted at the prospect of new data to process.

Yes, all of them, Maureen thought back silently.

The grey-haired shopkeeper sat bolt upright and stared at Maureen. Expression: First ever, Tally noted, sounding rather pleased with itself.

Maureen paid for her purchases. Spell models were one of the most expensive goods that the Dark Forest carried, especially for apprentices. Materials could be purchased outside, after all. Maureen walked out of the shop light on her feet, a tower of thick books cradled in her arms. They felt cool and dry under her fingers, one even had a faint smell of mould.

* * *

The model books were stacked on her desk as she flicked through each book, spread by spread as the counter rolled over in her vision. [Models recorded: 11. Analysis queue: 11. First up: Acid Missile.]

Some models required prerequisites that Maureen did not have, Tally warned. Most of those prerequisites were already covered by the free stacks available in the library.

A pale blue flicker passed behind Maureen's eyes as she turned each spread. Each page felt soft beneath her fingers, almost like cloth.

The serpent's blood had scoured off your coating, Tally noted as she worked. Gloved, Maureen carefully recoated the Cross Sword's edge, before moving on to her bolts. [Edge: blue. Bolts: blue.] Tally ran the black-powder focus for Dark Wind Ring while Maureen pulled out the serpent fang she had kept from the Fasol cavern. It tagged the jar as she started grinding it with her pestle. The stone tool scraped against the mortar, reducing the fang into an inky black powder fine as soot.

Maureen set aside a roll of padded trap tubes, carefully bound pairs of black liquid and green powder. The reagents had been brewed according to a formula she had found in the library, adapted by Tally for her needs. Their vapour clung to cloth fibres and lung tissue, sapping the strength from anyone who exerted themselves after exposure. She packed her own counter-dose beside the roll.

She hesitated at her brewing bench, its racks evenly spaced like they had been at Jing Sheng's. Finally, she left them standing where they were.

Richard? Tally asked quietly.

Safer if he doesn't know where I went, Maureen thought back silently.

That is a reason, Tally noted. Is that the reason?

Maureen's throat tightened, but she gave no answer. Tally did not press the issue.

Maureen emptied her wall cache into padded crates for the carriage.

[Cache: 0. Carriage: 142 vials.]

* * *

The carriage and its black horse had been bought from a livery stable in the Trading District that afternoon. [Magic stones: 57 → 48.]

A shadow servant rose from the flagstones, a human-shaped outline drawn in smoke on glass. It bowed, its footfalls making no sound on the stone. “To leave the Dark Forest, take this passage.”

[New route. Logged. Not Hugh and Iman gate.]

Tally noted the turns down to the lower stable levels.

Maureen led the horse and carriage onto the black iron flooring at the end of the passage. She stood still.

The chains holding up the platform groaned as they took up their slack. There was a sound of gears grinding together and biting into one another, then the darkness slid down past them.

The floor pressed up through the soles of Maureen’s boots, making her rise with it. The horse shifted uneasily at the movement, and there was the sound of its harness creaking as it bore its weight.

There was a heavy thud as the platform stopped moving.

I never knew the Dark Forest had a way out like this, Maureen thought.

On the day the mission team rode out past Hugh and Iman, I had measured the statue gate too narrow for wheels, Tally said. This must be the freight route for carriages.

A guard held his lantern up and peered at her. She showed him Adrian’s token, and he checked it before waving her through.

There was a door at the top of the platform, and it opened, revealing warm yellow light. Maureen blinked as the sun’s rays fell across her face. Her shoulders dropped for the first time in two years.

Sun again. Logged, Tally noted.

Warmer than last time, she thought silently.

Data supports you, Tally said.

“Let’s go.” From the driver’s box Maureen cracked the whip over the back of the black horse, and the carriage drew her away from the Dark Forest and out onto the open road.
