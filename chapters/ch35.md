In the ten days following her promotion, Maureen did nothing but meditate. Adrian's advice to not tax a fresh Rune Array too much still applied. The second array felt like a new wall of mortar behind her brow. Every sitting in front of the mirror helped stabilize it a little more.

On the tenth evening, Tally announced that her spirit task was done, and it showed her once:

[Spirit: 5]

Is this you being smug? she asked silently.

I am merely being accurate.

A Second-Class Wizard Apprentice could cast spells. A First-Class Wizard Apprentice could not. Maureen decided that she had to buy some spell models immediately. She didn't know how soon she might need to defend herself. Maureen washed up before she headed out. Her Dark Forest robe was still a bit dirty, so she changed into the spare robe she brought with her. The silk pouch with her stones was tied against her chest.

You are taking that out? Are you going to look or spend? Tally asked as she adjusted the knot.

Look.

Oh. I have heard that before.

* * *

The Dark Forest operated this shop personally, selling materials, spell models and specialist knowledge, at prices that could be considered outrageous by most. However, apprentices who needed such things often came anyway.

Behind the huge dusty glass window was an old man with grey hair. He was sitting back lazily in his chair, looking very slack. He yawned for the third time since she entered. And again. He looked frail, almost half asleep.

Maureen lightly tapped on the counter to get his attention. "Excuse me, I would like to see your spell models, please."

The old man opened his eyes and squinted at her sideways. Without a word, he tossed a thick pale green catalogue onto the counter. "Take it. Choose yourself."

As he looked back up, Maureen felt Tally fire off its alert. [Warning: energy radiation source.] Not the high band.

Tally, can you compare it to Gandolf?

Yes. At least Gandolf, possibly more.

Maureen nodded politely to the old man, perhaps a bit too fast. His eyes had already drifted shut.

Good instinct, Maureen, Tally said.

Your instinct, she silently answered. Tally did not object to the credit.

The pages were soft and flexible from handling, the edges coated with a fine grey dust. The cover had a greasy look where thumbnails had held it.

Tally began filing away each spread as Maureen leafed through the pages. The prices and prerequisites were filed together. Most of the models were darkness and undead-shadow schools, but Tally made sure to file away Corrupting Black Cloud and the rest as well.

Acid Missile: A highly corrosive ball of liquid that splashes on impact. Requires Basic Experiments in Negative Energy and Nika's Thirty-Six Principles of Irregularity. 15 magic stones.

Blast Fireball: A powerful fireball spell that bursts on impact, dealing splash damage. Requires Compendium of Negative Energy Principles and Spellcasting and Wizardry. 15 magic stones.

Undead Wind Arrow: Gather energy into a finger and fire off as an arrow. Causes lasting corrosion and weakness effects. Requires Charles's Orbital Principle and Tactile Experiments in Neurology. 20 magic stones. Hand of Negative Energy, also 20 stones, was listed further back.

Maureen frowned. She knew that a model required complete mastery of all the prerequisites. A basic pass would not be enough. This was a lot harder than she thought it would be.

Tally immediately cross-referenced the prerequisites against its library record and her study sessions. All prerequisites are present. All prerequisites have been mastered. Maureen silently insisted it was the library's doing, but Tally pointed out that it was both her studying and its data processing that made it possible.

Tally compared the Sunset Canyon affinities, darkness and fire, against the list of available models. Fireball was better for open fights, while the Hand was better for ambushes. They cover each other's blind spots, Tally noted.

Maureen handed back the catalogue across the counter. "I'll take Blast Fireball and Hand of Negative Energy."

The grey-haired shopkeeper mumbled the names like an old man trying to remember what he ate for breakfast last week. He then flipped through the pages unconcernedly in the smell of old paper and lamp oil. The pages rustled under his fingers before he tossed a grey book and a black book onto the counter. "Fifteen and twenty. Thirty-five."

The grey book felt dry and light in Maureen's hands while the black one had a cool touch and more weight than it should have had for its size. She counted out thirty-five stones from her silk pouch onto the counter. Tally's display changed to show the results of the transaction. [Magic stones: 41 → 6.]

In the background noise of stall cries and boots on stone, she felt the silk pouch flatten out against her chest. "Out of money," she muttered to herself. "Never thought I'd see the day."

I saw it coming eleven days ago. I was just too polite to mention it.

Polite? Is that what you call it?

Tactful then.

She laughed at herself as she left the shop. The Mox Fusion Stone had bought her months of time. That was worth every stone. Paid knowledge and spell models strangled every apprentice. Apothecaries earned stones in heaps. That was why apothecary apprentices had to pass an assessment before they could become full members.

* * *

She turned the lock and checked through all the rooms before sitting down and opening the book. A perfect eye peered at her from the black cover, following her hand as she pulled the book across the desk, moving with perfect realism. The wetness on its surface caught the light, the pupil dilating ever so slightly when the book tilted.

No matches found. The eye is not like the pale eyes of the Seal, Tally said.

Unperturbed, she spoke the activation phrase, saying it softly. "Hila-Fasos."

Hila is the word Rheas's ring answered. It is on my heard-spell list. Same sound, no data on whether it means the same thing.

The book glowed a faint black and began turning its own pages. Words, pictures and diagrams flashed up like a storm.

Like a flock of birds taking off, the pages rattled. An almost imperceptible wind brushed her face as the old ink moved through the air. Maureen sat still, expression fixed.

Tally watched each page as it appeared for a moment before the book turned to the next one.

[Rank Zero: Hand of Negative Energy. Recorded. Analysis ETA 105h.]

About four days, she thought back silently. Good.

The book snapped shut by itself, dropping down onto the desk with a light thump. Maureen repeated the process with the grey Blast Fireball book, and again Tally recorded it all.

Watching the progress bar at the edge of her sight, Maureen thought about what she'd seen so far. Rank Zero spells were an apprentice's domain. Real spells, powerful spells that could do serious damage required a full Wizard to cast. That was the line between them.

The pale blue bar crept across the edge of her vision while the lamp hummed away by itself.

* * *

"Veal kidney, please," Maureen asked the serving girl.

You have 6 stones, Tally objected.

We're celebrating, Maureen replied silently. She wasn't going to let a little thing like Tally ruin the evening.

The dish cost two stones. [Magic stones: 6 → 4] Tally informed Maureen in a pointed tone.

It arrived glazed with a dark gravy, perfectly cooked so that it melted in Maureen's mouth. The rich taste of butter and black pepper exploded on her tongue, nothing like the free bread she'd been eating. She dabbed at her lips with the white handkerchief, before murmuring, "A chef whose family has catered Gasha's royal banquets for generations earns his name."

Both models fully analysed, Tally reported. Do you wish to receive them now or wait until after dessert?

Now, Maureen answered silently.

There might be some pressure behind your eyes during this process, much like when I transferred the Spirit Rune to you. Is that acceptable?

Go ahead, Maureen thought back firmly.

There was a pale blue flicker behind her eyes, and suddenly there were the models. They settled into her memory, not as a single mass of facts, but as images and text that explained every step of the process with absolute clarity. It was as if she'd known the spells forever, could never forget them, and had just remembered what she already knew.

Maureen sat very still, the white handkerchief folded in her lap, as the sounds from the tables continued all around her. She had the correct models right away, without any time wasted analysing them, and without putting her own spirit at risk by building them herself.

 When Tally was done, she looked up from her plate.

That is the greatest gift a wizard ever received, Maureen told it silently.

I will hold you to that, Tally responded. Maureen rose from her seat and left for the Spell Testing Area.

* * *

The Spell Testing Area consisted of a black stone counter with a black stone slab sitting on top of it, followed by a long corridor of black stone doors. There were faint thumps and a scorched smell emanating from behind the doors.

The Dark Forest had found this area necessary so that people would have a place to test new spells. Casting spells anywhere else without restrictions resulted in damage and a strange contamination.

Maureen tapped her fingers on the counter as the clerk, an expressionless middle-aged woman, approached. "I'd like a spellcasting room, please."

The clerk's flat eyes rolled over Maureen once before the woman pointed a grey-glowing finger across the black slab.

"Rooms five, nine, seventeen, twenty-one, and forty-three are free." The clerk paused for a moment. "One magic stone an hour."

Twenty-one, Tally suggested into Maureen's mind. It was the furthest door from the counter, and thus the least likely to be overheard.

Maureen frowned at the clerk. That's a thief's price for an hour, she thought back at Tally.

A little smugness crept into Tally's voice. Worth every stone.

Maureen sighed. "I'll take twenty-one, then." She paid the woman one stone. [Magic stones: 4 → 3.]

The black stone key felt cold and heavy against her palm. The clerk didn't even bother glancing up at her again.

Walking down the long row of doors, Maureen spoke silently. Status.

[Maureen Olsen. Upper Knight. Second-Class Wizard Apprentice. Strength 8.3, Agility 8.4, Constitution 8.3. Spirit 5. Wizard energy 5.]

Tally said, Your spirit number is a few days old. Wizard energy is based off your spirit.

Hmmm, wizard energy? Maureen said in her head. That sounds strange. Call it mana.

Tally responded, You insisted that we use the term 'wizard energy', rather than my superior suggestion.

Fine, fine. I've grown since then.

Very well. Wizard energy renamed... mana... under protest... again.

Maureen's vision blurred for a moment as Tally made the change in her head, but when she could see again, the line now read [Mana: 5].

The corner of her mouth lifted, and her steps grew light against the black stone floor. She arrived at room twenty-one, and pressed the key into the lock. The door opened with a click.
