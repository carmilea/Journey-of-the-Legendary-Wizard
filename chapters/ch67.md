Adrian’s projection hung in the air over the ring of magic stones. The projection was made from dark particles, and some of those particles drifted away from the edges. Like soot rising up from a candle’s flame.

“Do you remember the conditions I gave you for becoming an Official Wizard?”

Maureen straightened herself from her bow and sat down on the chair next to the ring of magic stones.

“You said that my body and spirit needed to be up to standard, and I had to meet all the advancement requirements. I had to take the Finlan Potion, learn a formal spell template, and learn an Official-Wizard grade meditation method.”

“Very good.” Adrian pushed his glasses back up his nose. The projection was very detailed. As he adjusted his glasses, the lenses caught the lamplight and flashed gold for a moment.

“I’m glad you remember. I’m even more glad that you’ve discovered the problem yourself and came to ask me about it. Now I have a reason to tell you.”

Maureen leaned forward. Adrian’s eyes were serious behind the lenses of his glasses.

“Everything I told you before about advancing to Official Wizard was false.”

Maureen’s hand froze as it absently played along the edge of the table. The lamp hissed. Somewhere below in the castle, one of the shutters banged open and closed as the wind blew.

His contract with the Dark Forest allowed him to teach apprentices only the false method of advancing. Unless an apprentice discovered the anomaly herself. Every Official Wizard under every other organization had the same contractual restrictions.

Tally spoke quietly into her head, *This explains why he never corrected your list.*

*I asked the wrong question until too late,* Maureen thought.

The first stone in the ring lost its magic glow and turned grey. Adrian’s projection flickered once before it became stable again.

Tally noted the loss of magic. *One.*

*Keep counting, and keep your voice down,* Maureen silently ordered.

* * *

“The key to advancement is the meditation method,” Adrian said. “If you have an advanced meditation method, you don’t need the Finlan Potion, you don’t need a template. You just need to meet the body and spirit standards.”

Tally spoke up. *This contradicts the requirement list I filed weeks ago. As stated, not verified. I will change Finlan Potion and template requirements to ‘false’. Body and spirit requirements will remain standing.*

*Enjoy being right quietly,* she silently told Tally.

*I am being very quiet,* Tally said.

The projection looked over her shoulder at nothing. The dark particles that formed his sleeves slowed, like smoke left in still air.

“Everything that wizards now possess can be traced back to the Ancient Wizards. Where wizards originally came from has been lost to time.”

Maureen nodded. From the Dark Forest library, she knew of two great breaks in wizard civilization: when the Ancient Wizards vanished, and another break later when wizardry was in its revival stage. A war had set wizard civilization back by centuries, and it was only now slowly recovering from that second break.

*Both breaks are in my Ancient Era file,* Tally noted. *Now I know one thing that was lost in those two breaks.*

Through those two disasters, many advanced meditation methods had been lost or broken. On the Passise Subcontinent, those methods were now rare. Families and organizations gathered the ones they could and seized them as their own.

“But what do the strong do when advancement requires a method, and methods are scarce?” Adrian asked. He didn’t pause for an answer. “Control.” A cold spot formed at the back of Maureen’s neck. She felt the hair on her neck rise as her shoulders grew stiff beneath her robe.

“The Finlan Potion and the template,” Maureen guessed.

Adrian nodded.

“They turned the advanced methods into junk meditation methods. If an apprentice used one of those to advance, they would be capped inside First-Tier. Without some stroke of luck, their life as a wizard is over.”

“Junk meditation methods are harsh. Very few people can use them. The Finlan Potion takes care of that. Since an Official Wizard needs an innate spell, they provide a template to supply one.” Adrian sighed. “It is the least successful and worst path to becoming an Official Wizard.”

Maureen kept her head down. Her fists clenched on her knees as she felt a tremor run up her forearms. She willed her arms to remain still.

*Maureen Olsen,* Tally said in a soft voice.

*I’m fine,* she silently replied.

*Disputed,* Tally responded.

“Is there another way?” Maureen asked in a dry voice.

“Yes, you can train directly on a true advanced method,” Adrian replied. “But the fit is important.”

“If you’re suited to a method, you naturally form your innate spell while using it, and you go far,” Adrian continued. “If you’re not suited, you use the Finlan Potion to force the fit and then choose a formal spell to match either the method or your constitution.”

“After that, all later advancements require you to find spell models that are compatible with the first innate spell you formed. Your path narrows further and further. It’s still a good route, but…”

[Type 1: Junk Method, Finlan Potion, Random Template Innate Spell]

[Type 2: Unsuited Method, Finlan Potion, Chosen Formal Innate Spell]

[Type 3: Suited Method, Innate Spell Forms Naturally]

Tally displayed the information on Maureen’s display. She was still working on the information, trying to figure out how it all related. There was a fourth line that Tally added to her display.

[Type 4: Heirs of an Ancient Wizard Lineage? (hearsay)]

Hearsay, that meant rumour, or possibly just legend. Above the other three types of Official Wizard paths, there might be another way. A way reserved only for the heirs of one of the branches descended from the Ancient Wizards. If such people ever existed.

Maureen thought of Bina’s dying sneer, the proud Blue Ring Third-Class apprentice who had been so sure of her place in the world. Bina had taken one look at her, and decided that she was looking at a future Type 1. A garbage wizard.

Garbage wizard, the word had smouldered on the pyre with Bina’s body. Now it sat in front of Maureen, displayed with cold efficiency by Tally, and it fit.

* * *

Maureen raised her head.

“How does someone get a true advanced method?”

“Very difficult,” Adrian’s projection answered. “Families hold onto them fiercely. Each one represents the potential for a strong wizard.”

“Couldn’t you buy one? With a price?”

“Trades do occasionally occur, but only between families. Almost never with an apprentice, and only for weak methods. No family wants to accidentally help raise a strong rival wizard.”

The air in the small room felt thick and hard to breathe. Maureen drew a slow breath through her nose, held it for three long counts, breathed out for one short count. “So these families are not worried about revenge? From the wizards they cheated over the centuries?”

“Is there any point?” Adrian asked. “Against a truly strong First-Tier or Second-Tier Wizard, a crowd of garbage wizards is nothing. It would be a massacre. No, the cheated submit. The families make compensation payments, and the contracts ensure their silence.”

*They cannot win... they take the payment... they find small families and pin their hopes on their children, which the great families also do.* Tally was sorting the reasons for Maureen. *And fourthly, there is spite. If I am finished, why should anyone else rise?*

*The most stable.* That was Tally.

*The ugliest,* Maureen thought silently.

“It’s very well built,” she said aloud. “Carrot and stick. They run the organizations, and don’t want any more than fodder and enforcers. Strong wizards, that’s only for those of their own blood.”

“Yes.” Another of the magic stones winked grey, the faint tick audible in the quiet room. The edges of Adrian’s dark projection were thinning, like breath on a windowpane in winter.

“The present rule is a concession,” he continued. “It represents great progress. Now, if a wizard discovers the anomaly, or an apprentice shows exceptional aptitude, the wizard may tell them.”

“Laughable progress,” Maureen said.

* * *

“That’s one way of putting it.” Adrian gave a short, dry laugh in his usual quiet and calm voice. “Long ago I fought desperately to kill an apprentice of one of the wizard families. I got the truth from him, and he told me an advanced method.”

Adrian pushed up his glasses again, smiling. “A very strong method. Suited me very well. I was lucky.”

Tally said, *One line in the Adrian: unknown file moves to known.*

*Very short line,* Maureen replied silently.

“Evans too,” Adrian said, referring to the First-Tier Wizard of the Dark Forest who had escorted her seed group by ship. “He had a suited method as well. A gifted wizard, he fit almost perfectly. He swaggered there, and others put up with him.”

Maureen thought about the ship. The brass bell that rang for rations. The deck rising slightly with each slow breath of the giant tortoise. Evans holding up the dead wizard Ciri’s head by his hair. She smiled thinly. Frost was creeping across the inside of the shutter.

“Only Black Wizards could have invented such a scheme,” she finally said.

Adrian shook his head. “No, you’re wrong. The White Wizards invented it.”

Maureen frowned. “But why? If the white robes really are gentle and good, then why did the Black Wizards, who are the better fighters, not wipe them out?”

“Every wizard that dies means more resources for those remaining.”

“Black is black,” Adrian said. “But is white necessarily white?”

She thought about it for a moment, then smiled. “You are right, Mentor. It makes sense.”

One by one, the last magic stones dulled, until only the lamp remained, a single bead of gold light on its wick. Slowly the projection began to break apart, dark particles drifting in the air above the table before evaporating away.

*What now?* Tally asked in her head, in its warm voice.

*I will not be a garbage wizard,* Maureen answered silently, *no matter what the cost.*

*Then we need data on methods,* Tally told her, and opened a file.
