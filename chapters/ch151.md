Maureen lay flat along the ledge. Water ran down the stalactite, ticking onto her neck with cold, slow precision. Her concealment had become as thin as her breath, but she didn’t dare use any more power than necessary. Below, the shadow stretched and shifted behind the heart, folding and unfolding, never taking the same shape twice.

*Void Mirage,* Tally said, taking Claude’s word from a moment ago and running it against Gregory’s library and the bronze book’s catalogue of creatures from the Ancient Era. [Match by name. Now attempting match by description: formless shadow, void-dwelling. Match.]

Tally overlaid the creature entry on top of the shifting shadow on the cavern wall, and for a heartbeat Maureen saw what it really was: an empty place, lightless and airless, at the edge of the worlds. A shapeless thing drifted through that nothingness, and the spirit projections of great wizards were torn apart as they tried to cross, ripped to shreds like paper caught in a draught.

According to Gregory’s entry, the Void Mirage wandered the void between worlds and dimensions, feeding on the energy that leaked out from them. The void was its home, and although it was mindless, it could command the void by instinct. Its name, according to the old texts, was the Child of the Void.

The entry continued. In the Ancient Era, Fourth-Tier True Intent Wizards had been able to open Dimensional Gates, using their spirit projections to explore other planes of existence. While such projections crossed from one plane to another, a passing Void Mirage had consumed them whole. Many of the wizards had been crippled by the damage done to their spirits. Some had died where they sat, their hearts giving out from the shock. Even the more powerful Fifth-Tier Spirit Ring Wizards, who had managed to heal their wounds, had been forced into sleeps that lasted years.

[Label: Gregory’s entry. No sample.] Tally appended that note next to every rank.

*Peak Fifth-Tier?* Maureen thought.

*Yes,* Tally said. *According to a dead man who’d never met one, either.*

Maureen read the ritual lines written below the creature description, and saw what the elves were really doing.

No one could summon a true Void Mirage from the void. They were instead extracting essence blood from the creature’s heart, using that to pull its projection down into this world, and steal its power.

*That fits the entry,* Tally said.

Anyone else in this cavern might have gone cold at the idea of stealing power from a creature that could kill even the most powerful of wizards. Maureen’s lips parted just a bit. Heat crawled into her hands, her fingertips throbbing against the wet stone as the blood pulsed through them. Her pulse quickened, but she didn’t try to slow it down. Why bother?

[Bloodline: active. Totem: drawing. Pulse 64 → 96. Mana: 19.3 → 16.8.]

*Should I give you a countdown?* Tally asked.

*No,* Maureen told her silently. *Not this time.*

*That was your Totem’s appetite, Maureen,* Tally said. It was also Maureen’s own appetite, and she couldn’t tell where the one ended and the other began.

Maureen walked the Ancient Wizards’ road, but she knew what that meant. The only way up that road was to devour powerful creatures and exotic energies. The wet limestone dripped under her palms. Pale vapour rose from the milky pool below, drifting over the tips of the stalactites as they glowed a faint white. For Maureen, this was not a disaster. This was the rarest chance anyone on the Passise Subcontinent would ever get in their lifetime.

It also made sense of Eve. This was why the Night Mother had started a war with half the wizard orders at once. With this power, the dark elves could rule the Underworld. They could stand level with the human orders.

She thought back to the fern clearing. The Bloodline Seal Stone had left her hand in an underhand toss, and Ani’s eyes had been drawn again and again to Freya’s sword at Maureen’s hip. Later, at the collapsing gate, Eve and Ani ran out from the cave with the purple heart cradled in a crystal box. Maureen had sold them the key.

*That key bought us seven stone cards and a war,* Tally said. Then, in her own warm voice rather than in her data voice, she added, *Both columns.*

*We’re about to close the account,* Maureen told her silently.

*This is all theirs,* Maureen thought to Tally plainly. *They built it all. The heart, the blood, the war. None of their plans accounted for me, and now I’m here.*

If she devoured the heart... she’d have what she needed to advance to Second-Tier.

Tally raised the ladder over Maureen’s sight. Phantom, where she stood. Liquefied. Solid. The door to Second-Tier hovered above it all. *You’re standing on the first of three rungs beneath that door,* Tally said.

*Then it’s fuel for later,* Maureen said.

*It’s fuel that makes the Totem eat,* Tally responded. *I have medium confidence that it will help you. Low confidence that you will still be you when it’s done digesting.* Maureen’s silent reply was simple: *Then watch me.*

*Every second. Always.*

Maureen drew her hood forward and smiled into the darkness. Behind her, in the ember-red glow of the Totem, the Pankora Scarlet Serpent’s crimson eyes opened, then slid half shut again.

* * *

Below her, Edward lay where he had fallen, among the hires. Richard’s weak chin was tilted up toward the stalactites, the right side of his face turned toward Maureen. A black scorching ran down his left flank from neck to hip. Seeing her own borrowed face lying on the wet stone still made Maureen’s skin crawl.

Jackman had both hands flattened against the inside of the dome. His old face was grey. “A Void Mirage. Eve, you— your people have had its heart all this time?”

His voice was hoarse, and it cracked at the middle.

Eve stood at the edge of the high stone platform. She wore a robe that fell around her like poured water. Her red lips were curved in an amused smile, and she stood perfectly still, like a blade laid flat upon a table. She looked at Jackman with the expression a priestess might give to a goat who has just asked a question.

“Had it?” she said. “You fool, Jackman.” Then she laughed, low and throaty. “It is ours. Our sacred beast. We were burning offerings to it before your Alliance could even spell its name. We found it again through divination, after a sacrifice.”

Tally read the woman’s body language. Heartbeat steady. Hands relaxed, fingers not clenching. Nothing to indicate that she wasn’t telling the truth. *But that isn’t the same thing, Maureen. Or, rather, her body reads like someone telling the truth as she sees it. Medium confidence.*

The divination method was filed away under no data.

“We should thank you, truly,” Eve continued. “We would have made do with two. You and Claude. I’ve lain awake more than once, fearing that two bodies would run thin on us. Then you go and hire half the West for me!”

Maureen’s eyes went over the hires’ bodies. There was the red-haired man whose ribs she’d drawn a talon from during their descent, and the woman she’d billed for a burn salve treatment. Their potions were still in their pouches. Eve raised her arms, as though she intended to embrace the entire cavern with them. “Denise’s spirit blood. Your wizards. My Freya, waking to carry the beast. It’s all ready.” Her voice sank to a murmur. “The dark elves finally have a future, and it belongs to us.”

“You’re mad,” Jackman spat through the dome. “All of you, mad!”

Eve’s eyes narrowed. She spoke a few low words of the chant. The dome blazed white, flooding the cavern with light up to the roof. Both men screamed, huddled inside the light, and Claude’s voice climbed into a howl.

“Help—”

Maureen squinted against the glare through half-closed eyelids. It picked out every drop that hung from the stalactites. Then abruptly the screams stopped, as if someone had shut a door on them.

The light faded, and Jackman and Claude lay inside the barrier. Both of them were still alive, but their bodies were broken in places. Blood pooled around them, spreading across the wet stone. They each had enough breath for one or two more heartbeats, perhaps.

Eve raised a single finger. A thick black beam jetted from the tip, punched through Jackman’s skull with a smell of struck flint and singed hair, and the old wizard folded at the knees like an empty sack. Eve turned her back on him without a second glance.

[Jackman: density faint. Lit.]

*That one isn’t finished,* Tally said.

*Eve thinks he is,* Maureen answered silently.

*Eve isn’t counting,* Tally said.

Claude coughed up blood and dragged himself a hand’s width closer to Eve. “No— wait, wait— I’m Oserroba. The family. Stores, materials, anything you name, anyt—” The last words came out as wet gurgles.

[Claude: pulse 160. Tells: terror.]

Beside it, Tally displayed Maureen’s own line, unchanging at 96.

*Like Lauren,* Tally said.

*He sold his niece for his hide,* Maureen told her silently.

*Noted,* Tally replied.

Eve never paused in her stride across the platform. She raised her finger again, and a second spell caught Claude in the middle of another word. He went still.

The dome’s humming dropped to a lower pitch. Claude’s blood crept down the inside skin of the dome like a slow sheet of bright red lamp-lit wine, and the sweet vapour from the pool curled over it.

Two lines appeared side by side on Maureen’s display. The first was Lawrence at his window, saying anything at all, with the 0.8 s pause that Maureen had logged. Beneath it ran the words Denise’s uncle had just spoken: anything you name.

*Two Oserroba brothers,* Tally said. *Only one of them meant it.*

* * *

The chanters’ faces were young. But their lips were going grey, then their chins, and the grey spread outward as Maureen watched. Tiny black motes lifted themselves off the surface of their skins, like ash rising off a piece of paper held to a flame, and they floated toward the heart. Their song was a humming that did not stop for a breath, even though they were burning their lives into the array.

[Chanters: 31. Each losing ~3% per minute, by density.]

*Their lives,* Tally said. *Lifespan, essence, everything.* At this rate, she forecast, every last one of them would burn out in under an hour.

*Did they know?*

*By their faces, yes.*

Maureen said nothing further, and Tally let the silence stand between them.

Eve stood quietly for a long moment, regarding the ring of dark elves. Then she said, so softly that Maureen could barely hear her, “No one’s gift is wasted. Every one of you will be repaid.” She turned back, facing the heart.

Maureen’s throat closed once, spasmodically. People were dying on their knees for a door, and someone else had sold them the key to it.

The heart had been fed well. The hired wizards, the Spring’s pure essence, Denise’s blood as the opening rite, the dark elves’ great array, all of it had made the heart grow. Now it was four or five metres across. Behind it, the shadow of the Mirage rose from the pool to the roof of the cavern, its power beginning to manifest itself in a visible form.

Beneath the heart Freya floated, lifted from her clear coffin. Her black hair drifted around her head and shoulders as if she were under water. The heart’s purple light fell on her in thick, slow threads. Maureen remembered the ferns, how they had been lit by the same light, and how that small body had burst there. She remembered the smell of fallen blossom on her cheek.

[Freya: no density of her own. Drawing from the heart.]

*A cup, not a person,* Tally said.

*She was a person, once,* Maureen replied silently. Tally logged it under Freya’s file, twice dead.

“Good,” Eve whispered. “Everything’s in place. Now comes the part that matters.” She lifted her head and called out across the cavern, “Alja!”

The tall warrior with the broad Cross Sword strode forward and dropped to one knee. “Night Mother!” His near-black eyes were bright with something that looked very much like joy.

Tally filed the name as heard: Alja. His grain matched the source that had paced the guest corridor for forty minutes on the first night. *Medium confidence,* she said. *And now we know which one wasn’t happy with the arrangement.*

“I’m going to pour everything I have into the rite,” Eve told him. “Freya drinks the beast’s power, and until she does, this cavern is yours to hold. Don’t make me regret you.”

Alja didn’t reply. His face settled into an unreadable mask, and that seemed to be his answer.

The scent of fallen blossom grew more pronounced as the chanters weakened, growing sweeter and heavier until it was all but obliterating the iron smell of the spilled blood. Soon Maureen could taste it at the back of her tongue.

[Denise: pulse 64. Eyes closed.]

*Unconscious.*

*She won’t see what comes next,* Tally said. *That is a gift.*

Eve opened herself and released every scrap of her wizard energy into the array. The whole pattern blazed purple, and the air in the cavern shook and blurred as though seen through heat rising from a forge.

The purple glare caught the Spring’s vapour, turning it violet. The stone beneath Maureen’s palms began to throb in time with the heart’s beat. The stalactite beside her face rattled as grit sifted from it.

In Tally’s lens, the gold knots of the barrier were coming undone one by one, their light draining down the threads towards the heart. [> Barrier: 40% and falling.] [> Eve: committed.]

Eve never even looked around. “Alja,” she called, “the Oserroba girl. Open her and let every drop run out.”

The tall warrior rose to his feet and drew his Cross Sword from its scabbard. He turned towards Denise, lying unconscious on the stone.

[> Now.]

* * *

Maureen moved before she had fully decided to move. She didn’t know what it was that made up her mind but she thought it might have been the motion of Alja’s sword rising over Denise’s body. She reached deep inside and threw open the Totem the way she would throw open a door.

Heat exploded upward from her breastbone. Crimson flooded the edges of her vision. The Orb pulsed beneath her ribs like a second heart. The ledge stone warmed to her palms in a single breath.

[Serpent phantom: −3. Mana 16.8 → 13.8.]

Tally’s voice was terse. Clipped. It sounded right there in her ear. *The lattice is holding. Go.*

A small sharp sound crossed the cavern. A crack. The sound a mirror makes when it breaks.

The elves’ defensive array shattered from end to end.

In Tally’s lens, the dome was riddled with hairline fractures. The slackened gold threads snapped one after another like the strings of a lute. Bright flakes of light spun away from the dome and tumbled down towards the pool before being extinguished upon contact with the water.

Above Maureen’s head, the serpent poured downward out of the darkness. It was a colossal creature. Black as the night sky, wreathed in so much black-red flame it seemed less a serpent than a sun coiled into the shape of a serpent.

The scent of sulphur and scorched honey filled the cavern. The serpent’s fiery breath washed over Maureen. The heat felt like an oven door had been pulled open and held before her face. All around, the water dripping from the stalactites hissed and evaporated into steam.

The serpent’s crimson eyes fixed upon the heart and it swam through the empty air as if it were water. Coils of its body rolled and twisted as it moved. Straight for the pulsing mass of purple energy hovering over the still pool.

Alja froze, his broad Cross Sword half raised over the unconscious form of Denise.

“Damn you—!”

He abandoned Denise and threw himself between the serpent and the heart. His sword rose high over his head, his body crouching in the stance of a warrior willing to give up his life to stay an enemy’s attack.

[Warrior. In the path.]

*I see him,* Maureen told Tally silently.

Black-red fire blossomed in the air.

It bloomed inside Alja’s body and outside at the same time.

Purgatory Fire burned him from both sides simultaneously. His flesh ignited at the touch of the flames.

[Purgatory Fire: −2. Mana 13.8 → 11.8, est.]

He managed one scream before there was nothing left of him to scream again. No ash. No bone fragments. Not even the ringing of his sword striking the stone.

He was gone. A hot, empty space remained in the serpent’s path.

*The sword never fell,* Tally said.

*No, Tally,* Maureen responded silently. *It didn’t.*

Denise still lay unmoving upon the platform. One curl of her golden-brown hair fluttered in the serpent’s passing, but nothing else disturbed her sleeping form. Her face was slack, peaceful in the light of the black-red flames.

The serpent’s body swept behind the platform, and then Eve caught its full force. She flew away like a sack filled with rotting meat, tumbling over and over through the air before she slammed into the wet limestone.

For the first time the chanters’ song faltered. Three voices dropped out, only to return seconds later, their song thinner than before. The hum of the array wavered like a candle flame caught in a draught.

Eve coughed, blood spitting from her red lips. She pushed herself up, shaking, onto one elbow.

[Eve: alive. Hurt. Witness: serpent.]

*Witness? Yes or no?*

*No data,* Tally said. *No data on whether she could connect the serpent to Richard or not. I don’t like no data when it has a Night Mother attached to it.*

Maureen’s pulse was slamming in her ears. The serpent’s hunger came surging back up the Totem, burning in her own chest so that her mouth watered.

Eve saw where the serpent was going and what it intended. “No—” Her voice tore at the word. “No! NO!”

The serpent did not slow as it bore down upon its target. Its jaws unhinged wide, and it gave a hiss that set grit dancing from the roof.

[> Jaws on target. Heart defence: rising.]
