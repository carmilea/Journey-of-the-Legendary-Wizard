She looked down at the body. It didn’t matter how accurate Tally was. Even with a Middle Knight’s body, there was no way she could have fought a real wizard apprentice. They were on an entirely different level from any wizard seed. As it was, she had run into one who was already dying and empty of magic. That was lucky.

She crouched down and began to search him. She found a fine silk pouch in one pocket, a thin parchment scroll in another. Around his neck, she felt a round object hanging on a cord. It was many-faceted, like a gem, and cold to the touch. Carved into its surface were coils of blood-red vines with pale white eyes set in them. Holding the seal gave her an odd feeling, but she couldn’t quite name it. The pouch held some small black stones that glowed faintly with a soft light. Tally matched them against the five stones she had collected from the wizard seed she had killed earlier. Same type of glow, same size range. Twenty-three of them.

“Magic stones,” she said, remembering his offer. Tally dutifully filed all twenty-eight stones under that name, while Maureen tied the pouch back up and tucked it away in a pocket. The branch was burning low now, the flames licking at the ground, throwing orange light in all directions. The smoke from the smouldering leaves mingled with the coppery scent of blood. The night birds were silent again. The parchment between her fingers felt dry and brittle, like onion skin. Carefully she unrolled the scroll.

The letters swam before her eyes, as if she was reading them under water. She looked around and saw the trees bending in the same way, as if the world was made of glass and was melting. The cold voice cut into her thoughts.

[Warning: host affected by unknown force field.]

The world snapped into focus, the trees standing straight once more, the writing on the parchment sharp and clear. She was herself again, but her breath came in ragged gasps, her palms damp with sweat. If she hadn’t received that warning, combined with her few Spirit Runes and the mild radiation remodelling, she would have lost control again.

Why did it work this time? she asked Tally silently.

You gave me two bad nights to learn from, Maureen Olsen, it replied. The crystal and the contamination.

Thank you, Tally, she said silently, something she didn’t do often.

Tally was silent for a moment, then spoke.

I will log that too.

The scroll now appeared innocuous, just another magic text. Its title was unexciting - Basic Principles of Pharmacology. Maureen unrolled it completely, then ran her eyes over each line of writing.

New category, I think. I'll call it Pharmacology.

Scarlette told you about apothecaries. I wanted data on that subject, but had none. Now I have some, Tally said, sounding almost hungry.

For a penny, for a pound, Maureen thought and carefully poured the expensive viscous liquid from the clay flask over the body and the scroll. The corrosive’s acrid fumes made her nose twitch in protest as flesh and parchment sagged into a black slurry, which hissed as it ate away at the ground.

Maureen hung the seal on a thin cord around her neck, under her clothes, giving it one last touch before slipping into the darkness.

“Maureen?” Richard's voice was the first one Tally filtered out of the cacophony of noise in the caravan camp. He was talking too fast.

Richard was alive. He had a shallow cut on his forearm, which was badly bandaged. His eyes were wide as he talked about the attack, not stopping to take a breath.
