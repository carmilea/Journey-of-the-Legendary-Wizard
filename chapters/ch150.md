Maureen lay along the narrow limestone ledge, the wet stone cold beneath her coat. The concealment of the “Manas” sat over her skin like a second coat, thinner and smoother than the first. She kept her breathing shallow so the veil wouldn’t ripple. Far below, among the bodies of dead hires, was Richard’s face. The weak chin, the mousy eyes set close together, the grey travel coat. Edward.

Maureen had made him fall when the hires dropped, using just two fingers to tell him. He’d landed right where she’d told him to fall. Seeing her borrowed face lying slack and lifeless amongst those corpses made her skin crawl. It was the second time this morning it had happened. The first time had been when she’d first seen the Richard face on him. She pressed her tongue hard against the back of her teeth and forced her eyes away.

[Edward: core lit. Left flank scorched. Illusion: holding.]

*Of course it’s holding, it’s my face,* Maureen replied silently.

 *Not your face. Your face with a scorch mark on it. You’ll be wanting a mirror later, I think.*

Last night’s wine had brought them here. Tally matched the sealing agent in the wine to the Bina file, then Maureen drew out the agent with her palm and burned it away. At dawn she walked Edward out of the void pocket and sent him in her place.

She followed the column down into the Spring of Life, hidden behind her “Manas”, her aura pressed flat against her body, its heat damped through the Orb. With the Orb’s help she’d managed to keep the damping field tight enough to avoid detection, and had made it all the way up onto this ledge before the hires even reached the pool. One by one, as they approached the water, each hire who’d drunk the spiked wine walked three or four steps towards the water before collapsing.

[Mana: 19.7. Damping: −0.2/hour.]

 *Claude and Jackman killed with spells, not hands. That was lucky, because a hand at Edward’s throat would have found veilsilver instead of a pulse.*

*Someone could have knelt by him, and drawn a knife across his neck.*

Maureen’s fingers clenched slightly where they rested on the limestone. Luck. She filed that away with the repairs that Edward’s flank was going to need when she got him back.

[Pulse: 64.]

*Even for you, that’s flat,* Tally said.

*Flat is the point,* Maureen replied silently.

Below her, in the cavern, all was quiet. The Spring of Life lived and breathed around her, pale vapour curling up past her ledge to waft around her face. The air was sweet, like the vapour that rose from sugar as it boiled. The tips of the stalactites glowed with a pale light that slid wetly over the limestone walls. Somewhere behind her, out of sight, water dripped into a pool. Claude and Jackman stood among the corpses they had created, unhurried. Eve stood waiting on the high stone platform beside Denise, who was bound to its surface. The dark elf woman wore no armour, just simple clothes, but the red of her lips was as bright as any warrior’s blade, and like a blade she stood motionless, the small smile of someone who hadn’t even begun yet playing about her mouth.

*Something’s wrong with that smile,* Tally noted. Since they got here, her laugh always started 0.4 s before the smile. Now she wasn’t laughing at all.

*Yeah,* Maureen agreed silently, settling her chin down onto her folded hands. This was unexpected.

The corner of her mouth twitched upwards, just a fraction. Whatever happened next down there, it was going to be worth watching.

* * *

Jackman and Claude felt it at the same moment. They exchanged one quick look, and then Claude was chanting.

Lightning cracked from Jackman’s fingertips as a lance of blue light leapt from Claude’s palm. Both spells were aimed directly at Eve.

There were two flashes of brilliant white light, followed half a second later by the sharp crack of the spell’s discharge bouncing off every wall in the cavern. Drips shook loose from the stalactites above and pattered past Maureen’s ear as the lightning and the blue lance burst into life a stride before they reached their target, held back by a wall that none of them had seen rise.

A huge translucent dome had closed over the two men, and all the lightning and the blue lance did was illuminate its curve. Through Maureen’s Mirror Pupil lens Tally highlighted the dome in golden filaments that wound about each other in complex patterns. There were knots in the strands wherever a dark elf sat, chanting.

[Barrier: closed. Fed from array. Nodes: 31, by lens.]

*Medium confidence,* Tally noted. *That’s going to hold against anything Jackman’s got. It’s not a wall, Maureen. It’s a mouth.*

Jackman’s green eyes were flat and hard as agates. “Eve.”

Beside him, Claude’s face had taken on the grey tinge of long burned ash. Eve looked down at the two men with the same mild expression she’d given the hires at her gate.

“Your fate was settled the moment you walked in.” Her voice was mild, like a mother speaking to a disobedient child. “You’re offerings. For the Holy Beast. That’s all.” The word “offerings” echoed around the cavern twice before dying away. The sound ran off Maureen like water off oilcloth.

*Why didn’t the river take her?* Maureen asked silently. *She swore with them just two nights ago.*

*No data on terms,* Tally replied. *My guess is they swore over the Spring, and she’s just giving them back to it.*

*Lawyers,* Maureen commented.

*Rivers.*

Maureen followed Jackman’s gaze as the old wizard’s head turned. The young dark elves had taken their places in an array. They hadn’t just seated themselves anywhere - only from Maureen’s height could she see how the odd, uneven places they had chosen formed a pattern that linked up across the stone floor. The blood from the murdered hires was still running out of their bodies faster than it should. It wasn’t pooling where they lay, but instead was spreading out across the stone in fine threads that made their way towards the milky pool in the centre of the cavern. Some of the dead already looked shrivelled, as though life was being sucked out of them along with their blood. Tally drew the red threads across the stone floor for Maureen, fine lines overlaid on top of each other in the darkness, and began counting the bodies that were shrinking into mummification.

*Stop counting,* Maureen told her.

*Display off,* Tally responded. *Record remains.*

*I know.*

The bodies were drying out into a plaster white, like the dry dead of the trial realm. Their lips were splitting along the length of their mouths, the skin around their cheeks was collapsing in on itself. The coppery smell of fresh blood was beginning to creep up through the scent of the fallen blossom, now lying thick at the back of Maureen’s throat.

Jackman drew a spear of lightning from his own hand. The electrical energy flowed smoothly along its length, sparking slightly as it crawled over itself. The old black wizard threw it with all his strength, the lightning going white as it flew through the air.

There was a dull thump as it hit the huge translucent dome, a sound that Maureen felt as much as heard, transmitted through the stalactite beneath her palm. The dome flexed inward with the force of the blow, then shivered before smoothing itself out again like disturbed water.

*0.6 s flex and recovery time logged. Prediction correct,* Tally noted. *I wish I hadn’t been right.*

Jackman stood there panting, his shoulders up around his ears.

“Useless,” Eve said, almost gently. “All of it. We’ve paid too much for this day, my people have. For what comes after it.”

She turned to look back at the stone platform where Denise still lay bound and helpless, her wrist opened and the blood running down a single groove that had been carved in the rock. Something moved behind Eve’s eyes as she gazed upon the Oserroba heir.

“Yes. Spirit Blood. The blood that wakes the Holy Beast.”

Denise’s golden-brown hair was stuck to her cheek, her lips were grey. Her eyes were open but unseeing, fixed on the roof of the cavern above. Her bound hand twitched involuntarily against the cord that secured her, as though counting off the knuckles of some unseen person’s hand.

[Denise: pulse 118. Falling.]

Her pulse dropped another beat as Maureen watched the blood run down the groove in the rock.

Eve turned from Denise and looked up at the tall dark elf with the broad Cross Sword standing silently at her shoulder. “Everything is ready. Alja. Begin.”

Without a word, the dark elf sank to one knee before Eve, the point of his sword resting on the stone floor.

*Alja, spoken by the Night Mother,* Tally noted, filing the name away. *Match to the tall warrior with the Cross Sword. Also match, confidence low, with dense source that paced corridor for forty minutes on first night.*

* * *

A rumbling ran through the cavern floor beneath Maureen’s feet.

The stone platform on which Denise was bound began to rise from its base, hovering above the surface of the Spring. It remained perfectly still, the rock humming quietly.

Then the water in the pool began to boil. There were no clouds of steam from the heat, only great gulps and heaving as the milky white liquid rose and fell, slopping over the edge of the rim. The sweet vapour grew thicker until the glow from the stalactites had the softness of candlelight shining through a layer of gauze.

Something was rising through the water. A transparent coffin streamed with water as it rose slowly towards the surface. It rose slowly from the depths until it floated just beneath the platform that held Denise.

A girl lay within the coffin. Small and delicate, she had fair skin and long black hair that spread out around her head like an ink spill, water streaming off the ends. Her ears were slightly pointed at the tips. Her eyes were closed as though she were sleeping the deepest sleep there could be.

“Freya.” Eve’s voice was soft, almost a caress, and Maureen saw her face do something that she hadn’t seen it do when the dark elves had gathered for the muster, or stood waiting at the gate, or sat drinking and eating at the feast.

[Visual: Freya. Conflict: fern-clearing record, body dispersed.]

Tally’s cold, alert tones came up. *I saw what you saw that day, Maureen. This one shouldn’t be whole. Mechanism: no data.*

Maureen remembered the ferns bursting apart, sprays of dark purple blood drying on her cheek, smelling of fallen blossom. Her hand went to her inside pocket where the Seal had rested so securely, her fingers seeking its smooth surface. They found nothing but empty cloth and lay still.

*Someone gathered her up afterwards,* she told Tally silently.

*Someone who wanted her very much. Very much indeed.*

Eve’s eyes went to the girl’s chest. There, resting on her breastbone, was a small box made of crystal, finely crafted. Within the box, something moved. A heart, purple and pulsing, swelled and shrank, swelled and shrank. It made no sound. But the cavern did.

A beat ran through the rock, slow and deliberate. The stalactite beneath Maureen’s palm began to hum with the vibration. The hum crawled up Maureen’s wrists, settling in her teeth, making them ache.

Tally matched the beats of the heart to the tiny crystal box Eve carried through the collapsing gate: 22-25 swells to 72 beats of the Bloodline Seal Stone. *Same heart. That’s our key’s door, Maureen.*

*We sold the elves that key,* Maureen answered silently, *at a ruinous price.*

*Ruinous to them, not to us. Not yet.*

“Soon,” Eve murmured. “So soon now.”

She tipped her face toward the floating stone platform. Her voice took on a warmth that seemed reserved for Freya alone. “Once the Holy Beast’s power is ours, my people will have a future again.”

Only then did Eve lean down over the transparent coffin. “My child. You died in that realm. But it gave us this.” The Night Mother gestured toward Freya’s sleeping form. “Asleep, you can carry the Beast’s power, but feel none of it. When the Spring and the Spirit Blood call it down, I’ll take what’s left and bring you all the way back.”

Tally moved a line out of the unknowns:

[Freya: revivable.

Night Mother + Spring + leftover summoning power

Heard.]

*That’s what the dark elves had staked on her at the muster ground,* Maureen answered silently.

Eve cast one last glance at the heart within its crystal box. “My daughter’s people ripped this from that realm with their own hands. We went to war with the wizard orders to get it. I’d do it again.”

Maureen thought of the gate again—Eve and Ani running, the crystal box pressed hard against Eve’s chest, the wet purple gloss of the heart. And the stone she had sold them in the fern clearing, for seven cards.

“Eve!” Jackman shouted through the dome. “What is this? Who’s the girl? What is that heart?”

Tally caught the man’s voice rising a half tone, the pulse jumping at his throat. *The old black wizard who’d cut down the hires without a pause is afraid,* Tally said. *That’s the first sample of his fear on file.*

*I saw it,* Maureen answered silently.

The old wizard held one palm flat against the inside of the dome. Sparks danced from his fingertips, dying as they touched the dome’s skin like moths hitting a glass window.

But Eve did not look at him. Instead, she turned to face Claude, who stood as if he’d been struck by lightning. “Master Claude,” she said. “Thank you for bringing Denise to us. Truly, thank you.”

Claude swallowed. Maureen watched him work it out. The dark elves had wanted Denise from the start—her soul intact, her body unharmed. Without Claude, they still would have gotten her. They’d just find another way. He’d been nothing but the door. “Damn it,” he breathed.

Maureen looked down at Denise on the floating platform. Tally fed her the reading:

[Denise: pulse 96]

*At medium confidence,* Tally forecast, *Denise will faint within minutes. Anything she sees past that point will be meaningless.*

Maureen filed that away. It would make a better story later.

* * *

A surge of power rose up behind Eve. Black and surging like a waterfall running backwards, it roared straight up towards the roof of the cavern.

It was the weight of it that reached the ledge first. Maureen felt it hit, heard the sound of it like a great engine. Drips of water stopped falling and hung suspended in the air. She felt the fine hair at the back of her neck rise up. The air smelled of hot flint and cold blossom.

Eve began to chant.

“Tur-kes-mas-ti-kan-er-fa...”

The seated dark elves took up the chant. It was a deep chant, a wide chant, rolling around the cavern like floodwater around a pillar.

*This is not Ancient Andas,* Tally said in Maureen’s head. *Nothing in my files matches it. I’m logging the sounds, but there are no references for translation.*

*Keep them,* Maureen thought silently. *Keep every one of them.*

The heart floated free from its crystal box.

An array of purple light flared into existence beneath the floating heart. A line of black motes left each of the seated dark elves, drifting down into the beating heart.

Tally displayed the lines as they converged on the heart. [Array: 31 nodes → feeding heart. Elf density: dropping.]

*At medium confidence,* Tally predicted, *the dark elves will emerge weaker than they entered.*

As each mote left an elf, the skin it had come from grew a shade greyer. At the edge of the ring a young elf swayed, his face already grey, and kept chanting, his lips moving faster to try and keep up with his peers.

Eve threw back her head, and cried out in a language that Maureen understood. Ancient Andas.

“Honoured one! Show us your power!”

A column of water shot up from the pool. It was Spring water, milky white, and it poured upwards from the pool, filling the hollow heart. The heart blazed purple, flaring bright enough to glow through Maureen’s eyelids. It beat harder, hard enough to rock Freya’s crystal coffin on the platform.

Tally’s display showed what was going on below. [Denise: pulse 80. Unconscious.]

Denise’s head had rolled back to rest against the stone. Her jaw hung open and slack.

*Denise is out,* Tally told Maureen. *The poor girl isn’t going to see any more of this.*

The beat had become a pressure in Maureen’s ears. She could feel her own pulse trying to fall into step with it, and she didn’t like that at all.

From nowhere, from everywhere, from the air itself came a hoarse, slurred cry. Like someone speaking through a throat they’d never spoken through before.

And with the cry came a shape. It was black like shadow, it had no fixed edge, it folded and shifted and changed and never for a moment stopped moving, never for a moment settled into anything that Maureen could put a name to.

The cavern grew heavy. The air heaved and churned, violent as the hour before a storm breaks. The sweet vapour was torn apart, ripped into rags by the fury of the air.

[Source: off scale. No labelled sample at this density.] came up on Tally’s display, bright cold blue. Then Tally’s voice was there again, warm inside her head. *It’s not here Maureen, it’s a shadow of being here, but the shadow is bigger than Barok.*

Inside the dome Claude and Jackman were gasping for breath. Their spells were dropping into the gold without a ripple. Their energy was bleeding away from them, sucked out through the floor and towards the pool.

Claude looked up at the shifting shape, and every bit of colour left his face. “The Void Mirage!” His voice shook on the words.

Jackman looked up as well. The old wizard’s green eyes stared at the creature forming above the pool, and in them Maureen saw a man who’d read of something once and never expected to stand beneath it.

The elves didn’t pause in their chanting.

The sound of their voices rose like they had been waiting all their lives to reach the final word.

Then, inside Maureen, unasked, the Minai Totem woke. Her eyes became cold and emotionless. The pupils were vertical, threaded with dark red patterns.

[Bloodline: active]

[Totem: drawing]

[Pulse 64 → 112]

*Not yet Maureen,* Tally said.

*Ten.*

*Nine.*

*I’m not moving,* she said silently.

*Yes you are. Your eyes are moving. If anyone looks up now they’ll see two red coals burning in the ceiling.*

Maureen narrowed her eyes to slits, feeling the heat in her bloodline dampened even further by Tally through the Orb.

[Mana: 19.5 → 19.3]

The glow sank back under her concealment, until it was just stone again.

Inside the Totem the phantom serpent rose, the Pankora Scarlet Serpent. Its eyes settled into position exactly over Maureen’s. Then the snake raised its head, staring at the shadow, and hissed without a sound. Maureen felt the hiss inside her, vibrating her breastbone.

Her mouth watered, wanting. The pressure holding the drips suspended in the air didn’t touch her. There was nothing but the shape below, and the heat behind her eyes.

*Void Mirage.* She shaped the name in her thoughts, tasting it on her tongue.

*Logging that as hungry,* Tally said.

*No. Log that as “interested”. Interested.*

*Both columns,* Tally said. And she logged the smile as well, the one Maureen hadn’t even known she was making.
