The back room was a box made of stone and shadow. The single lamp cast a small pool of light. The table was marble, cold beneath Maureen’s palms. The air was thick with stale beer and the smell of old tallow candles. Five masks ringed the table, each one representing some animal. The black mask rested coolly against Maureen’s cheekbones.

Night Owl was an odd name, but they asked no questions. The man in the black bear mask broke the silence first. “No one else will be coming this evening,” he said in a rasping voice. He chuckled, a deep laugh that sounded like a rough cloth being pulled over a cobblestone road. “I’ll start. Call me Black Bear.”

One by one, the rest of the group introduced themselves: Bat, Crow, Nightingale, Fox. Each voice was flat, as though it had been forced up through a tube. They spoke quickly, wanting to get the introductions over with so they could begin. Maureen matched each voice to its corresponding mask. She said nothing of her own.

Tally tagged each mask with a small bit of text as it was named. She stored density and gait readings beside them.

*You’re making name cards for them,* Maureen told her silently.

*Seating plan for a funeral. Probably someone else’s.*

Maureen removed the blood-red token from the marble tabletop and slipped it into one of her robe’s pockets. “What is this job, then?” she asked in Night Owl’s low, rough voice.

Black Bear nodded. He muttered a quiet incantation and tapped the empty air before him. Particles of fire bled from nothingness, forming a screen that hung in the air. The screen was a dark red colour, and lines of script scurried across it: the High Ring White Tower, and a single Starfish Gem.

The red light from the screen washed up beneath the masks, giving the animal faces an eerie half-life. The room smelled faintly of burnt sugar, the scent of particles spending their energy.

Tally took in every frame of the screen. *That’s a better map of the Tower than any we have. Someone inside must have drawn it, or paid someone who could.*

The Gem, Maureen knew only by hearsay. It was rare, with few uses, but was irreplaceable for certain experiments. The Tower she knew by its weight, one of the few truly strong houses in the entire Alliance, an institution whose place was unshakable.

The name caught in her throat. Tally placed the Gem next to the Skoll valley’s Starfish Tear Stone, the two names sounding similar enough for her to notice. The Starfish Gem might be a different stone altogether. Tally had no data on familial relationships between the two. For the space of a breath, Maureen was back there: the purple grit beneath her nails, the array humming away, the pale wisp drifting northeast.

*Eleven apprentices for one stone, last time,* Tally said.

*I remember the count,* Maureen told her silently.

*But you asked me not to display it. I didn’t want to distract you.*

Her jaw clenched beneath the mask. She let go of the valley and forced her eyes back to the red screen. None at the table saw her eyes move.

“I think,” Nightingale said, her voice flattened by magical means, “that going into the Tower is suicide. There are too many arrays, and too many wizards in residence.”

Crow and Fox nodded agreement. Bat said nothing at all.

Tally could make nothing of the scrubbed pitch of the voice, but the inhale before the words and the set of the shoulders marked this wizard as female. By inference, not proof, Tally filed Nightingale under she.

“Our client has given us the layout of the Tower,” Black Bear said. “He’s given us the shift-change times, and he’s given us the gaps between the arrays.” He studied the data, then swept his hand through the air, rearranging the display into a defence map. Nightingale and Fox leaned forward, studying the map. The tension faded from their shoulders and they both nodded.

Bat’s fists closed on the table top. His mask covered most of his face, but the name of the High Ring White Tower seemed to weigh on everyone in the room. It weighed most heavily on him.

Tally highlighted the fists and the breath he held for 4.2 seconds before his chest expanded with another inhale. Distress. Not proof, but definitely a tell.

*He knows the place,* Maureen thought.

*He does. The way you’d know the Skran kitchens.*

*He’ll come.*

Maureen looked up, meeting Bat’s eyes.

*He’ll hesitate once, though, when he reaches the wall. But he won’t sabotage it. The contract will kill him first if he does that. Medium confidence level on that one.*

“So,” Black Bear said, “we can do this.”

No one argued. No one needed to.

“Then let us make this binding.” Black Bear looked around the table, letting his gaze rest on Bat for just a beat longer than the others, warning in his eyes.

The contract was simple. Each member of the team would expend their utmost effort on the job, and no member would strike another within a set time after the job’s completion. A thread of light rose from each wizard’s forehead as they agreed, and the contract scrolled itself in the air above the table. When every condition was laid out, the scroll flared and burned to nothingness.

Tally’s lens traced the shape of the contract. The utmost clause, the non-aggression clock. Bindings: 4. One expiring. *This one has a clock,* she said. *I’ll keep it on the display.*

*Count it down,* Maureen told her.

*Already counting.*

Night Owl exhaled slowly behind her lacquer mask. Another contract. Another cold thread twisting itself into her soul, taking its place among the others. She let it sit there for a moment, then rose to leave.

* * *

The six slipped out from Emilia’s dark. Black Bear had some wizardry mounts waiting for them, and these carried the team quickly through the dark farmland to the outskirts. Tally tagged the beasts as they ran.

>Altered by wizardry.

>By sight.

>Method no data.

The frost lay white over the stubble fields. It was quiet in the plain, blue-black beneath the dark sky. There was no moon this night and Night Owl drew in the cold air, and the stillness, with something that felt like contentment.

The base rose from the fields before them. Pale stone observation towers spread across the land, and guard posts were scattered between them. A low stone wall surrounded them, marking the boundary of the long tracts of Tower land beyond.

Tally counted them by sight, then reached past the walls, using her particle sense to count what lay inside.

>Towers: 4

>Posts: 7

>Sources: Official 2, both thin; apprentices 12; mortals 40

Black Bear waved them down into cover. The shift change, the gap in the arrays, was close now. Bat stood beside him, staring at the Tower with a grim set to his entire body.

* * *

A countdown appeared on Tally’s display, overlaid on the array gap on the south face, pulled directly from Black Bear’s timing. A gold weave marked where the ward was active.

> Gold slack, south face.

> 3.

> 2.

> Now.

“Go,” said Black Bear in a flat, emotionless tone, and the team moved in.

Bat hesitated for a long breath, his eyes never leaving the Tower, but he followed as they went over the wall.

The south wall of the base exploded in fire and noise. Flames caught in the thatch above the stable roofs, and the sound of horses screaming in their stalls filled the air. The smell of burning lamp oil hung thick and the iron tang of blood was under it all. Somewhere, a bell began to ring, but it was cut off in mid-stroke. Shouts sounded from within.

“Who are you?”

“Where are the resident wizards?”

Four armoured knights spurred their warhorses through the smoke that was beginning to fill the lanes between buildings. They came straight at her.

>Riders. Four. Left.

Night Owl’s lips barely moved as she spoke the cutting words of the chant. Her pulse was almost steady as the spell left her hands in a volley of several Blast Fireballs. They streaked through the air and found their mark. The knights went over backwards in a rolling ball of fire, the horses beneath them screaming as they fell.

Mana ticked down on Tally’s display after each cast, and Night Owl’s left hand drifted towards a fourth casting.

*Your old tell,* Tally said in Maureen’s head. *You don’t need it here.*

A plump wizard froze in the lane, staring at the owl mask. His hand hadn’t even begun to rise yet.

> Density thin. Below Soros. Not elementalised. Halo not raised.

*He thought the shift change was a nap,* said Tally.

*It was,* Maureen answered her silently.

An Acid Missile ate a hole through his chest before the hand ever came up. Green smoke poured out of his body, and he folded up onto the ground like a puppet with its strings cut.

Behind an overturned cart one of the apprentices half rose to cast.

*Apprentice, behind the cart, low,* Tally called out.

The Hand of Negative Energy rose up out of the darkness, and closed over him. He went still.

*Mana: about fifteen down to about eleven. And your pulse didn’t even skip a beat. It’s becoming a habit now, not a superhuman feat.*

Fire ran the length of the roofs, and the buildings began to sag and drop. The others cut a swath through the yard, and as she watched the battle Night Owl noted with detached calm that the wizards she travelled with were powerful indeed. Her eyes swept past the fighting, to the storehouses at the heart of the base.

*That’s the treasury face,* Tally said.

*Slum girl, Skran, remember the day you riffled the Duke’s shelves?*

*The mission is one thing,* Maureen told her.

*And side business is another,* Tally responded. *Fine, I’ll start a column.*

A smile that she couldn’t stop tugged at the corners of her mouth behind the mask. Storerooms, hidden higher knowledge, far more valuable than the job itself and never within her grasp otherwise.

Night Owl turned away from the chaos in the yard, and headed for the storehouses. There would be time to take what she could while the base burned around them. Greed, Tally called it. Fondly.
