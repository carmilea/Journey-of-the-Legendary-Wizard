Barok Olivia hung in the air at Maureen’s height, mouth working soundlessly, as if the syllables of her name had just now penetrated his brain. “Maureen Olsen? No. That cannot be. Maureen Olsen is a Third-Class brat. You’re...”

[Pulse: jumping.] Tally’s amber text floated in Maureen’s vision, pinpointing an artery at Barok’s throat. *And he drew back half a hand when you rose from the ground,* her warm voice added inside Maureen’s head. *He hasn’t come forward since.*

“Am I?” Maureen asked lightly, her smile promising and denying everything at once. She let him look at her as long as he wanted.

Far below her, on the ground, the concentrated heat of her breakout had softened the leading of the manor’s tall library window. The glass pane had warped and sagged, leaving her a mirror. The last rays of the setting sun hit the pane at an angle, so that the image reflected was edged with gold. Her image was bent and distorted by the mis-shapen pane, but she could still make it out. It was her, and it wasn’t.

The ragged crop of black hair that fell across her forehead was the same. So were the dark eyes and the matching black slashes of her eyebrows. But the skin wasn’t right. It was too pale. It didn’t just look white, but almost translucent. Beneath the skin, her bones and muscle were more defined. There was a flickering crimson colour deep in her eyes, behind the black of her pupils and irises.

Tally overlaid the day-one scan on top of her reflection. A ghostly, pale blue outline of her starved, fourteen year old body appeared over the image of her face in the window. *We do not have data on whether this change will stabilise or continue,* Tally said quietly inside Maureen’s head.

*Will a stranger recognise me?* Maureen asked silently.

*No. Anyone who makes a career out of measuring people? Probably.*

Barok Olivia’s eyes went past the new lines of Maureen’s bones, seeking something deeper. His jaw tightened. “Maureen Olsen,” he said, this time as a statement of fact, rather than question. “Official Wizard,” he added, as if the words tasted like ash in his mouth.

“The family has been watching you.” He didn’t smile, but there was something faintly pleased in his eyes. “You were reported Third-Class, only barely. How long ago was that? A season?”

Barok’s tone changed, going from disbelief to arithmetic.

Below them, the broken bluestone still radiated heat from Maureen’s passage. Great waves of warmth drifted up from where she’d forced her way through the ground in a column of force. Smoke from the singed hedge danced between them. The scent of crushed green wood mingled with the smell of beeswax and dust sheets that drifted out of the shuttered library.

Barok’s mouth twisted into a smile that didn’t reach his eyes. “Genius, child. You are a genius. But you are a fool as well. Any other sane child would have run off at that point, but no, you had to come up out of the ground to confront me.”

“Why would I run?” Maureen asked.

Barok snorted. “You don’t even know what you are yet, do you? Engraved a single Official model? Fixed your field? You’re a strong apprentice in a wizard’s coat, girl! You’re asking to die!”

As far as ordinary wizards went, he was right. According to the promotion notes, a new Official Wizard generally retired to a quiet location for a month while they engraved their models and practiced their gestures. Like a crab that has just moulted, they were soft and helpless until their new powers settled. Ordinary rules had stopped applying to Maureen somewhere around the rite.

*Ordinary wizards need time to acclimatise themselves to their new capabilities,* Tally said. *They need a quiet month to adjust. You’ve spent your quiet month in a manor fire.*

*That’s an overrated month,* Maureen told her silently.

*I shall log that as an opinion.*

Tally’s voice faded into the background as she projected a forecast for Maureen. Amber text, medium confidence. *He will attack in order to cover the half-hand. He will do so loudly.*

*Loudly?*

*One flinch. A voice that keeps rising in volume.*

Maureen said nothing. She let the silence stretch out between them until Barok’s nostrils flared, and tiny beads of orange light began to form along his sleeves like sweat.

* * *

And then it happened all at once. The power burst from him, great rings of orange-red flame billowing up and over his body. They washed over the robe and the hair, until he was hanging in the air before the backdrop of the sunset, looking like some demonic fire god from a shrine painting. The air between them shimmered with heat and dryness, and the first wave of heat hit Maureen in the face like an opened oven door. Somewhere below, one of the trimmed hedges crackled and burst into a column of flame. She could smell hot slate, scorched sap and the dust of a century of housekeeping.

Barok Olivia lunged forward, clawing at the air. A giant hand of flame, bigger than an ox cart, swept through the place where Maureen had been standing only a second before. It roared with the force of a forge bellows, but closed on nothing.

Maureen was already twenty paces to one side, standing on the air, and she hadn’t even made the conscious decision to move. Some primal instinct buried in her new blood supply had felt the grab coming, and shifted her out of reach before she even knew what was happening.

*You’re getting faster,* Tally said. *That landed in your body before I could even call it. Congratulations, you were faster than me that time. I shall log that as a grudge.*

Maureen’s heart didn’t even begin to beat faster. The muscle was thudding in her chest with slow, heavy beats. The black-red flames danced across her skin, giving off a low hum that might have been mistaken for contentment.

*Tally?* Maureen asked silently.

*Yes, Maureen?*

*Particle density readings please.*

An amber density bar appeared next to Barok Olivia, hovering in midair. A bar of black-red, Maureen’s scanned Spirit 16, was next to it for comparison. It was the first time Tally had ever been able to directly compare Spirit values against an Official Wizard. [Barok: Spirit est. 15 ± 1. By comparison to host.]

*Tally, can you give me his full profile?*

*Of course. Keep him alive and moving for me though. I need samples, not a corpse.*

*Fair enough,* Maureen thought. The calculations were simple. She was stronger and faster. She could probably finish him now, but she’d never faced an Official Wizard in combat before. Specimens this rare did not come twice in a lifetime. It was better to study him first.

With Maureen’s consent, Tally activated the Mirror Pupil lens and condensed the instructions into a single line. [Task: Barok Olivia. Full profile. Recording.] Thin amber range rings blossomed around Barok while a timer fixed itself over his chest, ready to count his casts.

The shock faded from his face, replaced by something far hungrier. His eyes roved over her in the same way Julo’s had roved over the slaves in their cage, like a merchant calculating prices.

“You shouldn’t have been able to dodge that.” His voice was firm and unrelenting. “Not a wizard only a few days old.”

Then he laughed. “Grenade shards all around Frey’s body. Every brat who crawled out of that realm babbling about Jack the Ripper and his iron eggs. They all said ‘he’. It was you.”

Hearing Jack’s name pass the lips of an Olivia felt as strange as listening to someone read aloud her own handwriting. She kept her face as still and impassive as ever. Somewhere in the back of her throat, she could feel the rasp of Jack’s voice, disused since the trial.

A mottled flush rose in Barok’s face. “Some ruin… some dead master’s leftovers…” His voice grew louder with each word, more confident, more filled with anger. “This is how a slum apprentice kills my grandson and rises so quickly? Your windfall, your grenades, everything you have, they’re mine now!”

[Grenades: 5]

*He’ll be disappointed this time,* Tally said smugly.

Maureen replied silently to Tally, *Don’t tell him.*

Barok continued his boasts as he gathered power, the heat radiating from his body becoming visible. He’d torn her hireling in half on the west road and run down the scarred woman on the south. He’d burned the inn at Maplefall with its keeper still inside, closed every mouth in Gladton that had seen her, cast for her direction when the trails went thin.

Tally’s voice was quiet in Maureen’s head. *I have both their faces on file. The mercenary had debts, the woman had a scar through her left eyebrow.*

*Keep them,* Maureen instructed silently.

*I always do.*

Maureen’s jaw set, her free hand clenching into a fist by her side. She could almost feel the weight of their coins in her palm again, the gold paid to buy distance, the gold that had bought them graves.

[Pulse: spike, one beat.]

*Do you want me to log that?* Tally asked, using her colder register.

*Yes,* Maureen told Tally silently. *Log it.*

Tally added a footnote to the old rule: Careful men were slow men, but angry men killed the help.

Then Barok roared, and the rings of flame tore loose from his body in waves, one after another rolling across the sky towards Maureen. From horizon to roofline the sky was orange. The setting sun was swallowed in a tidal wave of orange-red flames. The trimmed hedges became torches, the bluestone walls split apart, lead ran like tears from the slate eaves, and a river of smoke poured from the shattered library windows, the air filled with the smell of melted beeswax and dustsheets. The gravel on the drive glowed orange as Barok’s laughter crowed down at Maureen, telling her that she had nowhere left to run.

The air dried until Maureen’s lips cracked. The edges of the display bled ember-red, a colour previously unknown in Tally’s palette. Maureen moved through the waves almost lazily, always half a beat ahead of time. Where the orange fire reached her, the black-red flame on her skin ate it, leaving no trace, like grains of sand swallowing droplets of water, without a ripple or a sound.

Tally monitored the cost carefully, then displayed it. [Purgatory Fire: burning. Upkeep observed: 2 Mana / 3 h. Cast cost confirmed ~2.] *The imprint gave us that upkeep figure twice,* Tally noted. *Now I believe it once.*

Barok’s arms were now a running stream of half liquid orange fire, and Tally had helpfully labelled him. [Elemental Phantom State, by sight + Dark Forest promotion notes] Next to him she had stacked three rungs on an invisible ladder. Phantom, Liquid, Solid. *So he’s on the bottom rung,* Maureen noted silently. *A ladder you are not climbing,* Tally answered. *Yours has extra rungs.*

On the drive below them, the four house servants had stood frozen, faces turned up towards the sky. Then they broke. Two ran for the gate, empty handed, while the other two ducked back into the burning building, in search of silver and gold. Beneath their feet, the lawn baked and split into pale plates of turf. An entire robin’s nest, hidden in the guttering, flared once in the sun and was gone.

At the edge of Maureen’s senses, Tally noted four small sources of warm light beneath the two great sources in the sky, then they began to dim. [Mortal sources: 4 → 2 → 0. Cause: radiation, by inference.] *Not now, Tally,* Maureen told her. *Not shown,* Tally answered. *Still counted.*

Maureen saw one woman fall to the gravel, coins spilling from her apron pockets, glistening orange in Barok’s light. The woman’s breath came in short, shallow gasps, then stopped completely.

Tally refined the profile live, while displaying the source for each figure. Strength came from the craters that his blows punched into the bluestone, agility from his speed relative to hers, spirit and mana came from density, and from how quickly he managed to refill between waves. [Host Mana: est. 12.]

* * *

A lance of orange fire came at her quickly. She flicked a hand, diverting it away, and asked silently, *How we doing?*

[Task: complete. Barok Olivia. First-Tier. Elemental Phantom State. Str 10.8 / Agi 11.1 / Con 10.9 / Spirit 14.8 / Mana 14.9. Recorded.] Tally ran through the sources again. Impacts, speed, density, recovery.

*That was the first complete Official Wizard profile I have ever held,* Tally said. *Thank you for not killing him until afterwards.*

*You’re welcome,* Maureen said silently. *Now watch this.*

*Every second,* Tally said.

Maureen allowed the black-red flames around her skin to grow quiet. For the first time since she’d risen out of the floor, she stopped moving. The air immediately around her took on a cool feel. She called his name once, and the syllable carried cleanly through the roar. Barok Olivia stood with a ball of fire spinning lazily in his palm. He looked up at the sound of his name, ready to throw the spell. Then he paused, frowning slightly. Something was different about the sky. His orange flames were still everywhere, but now they had no answer. There was nothing to balance them against, no black-red flame to match his own. It was as if she’d given up. In the sudden lull, Maureen could hear the sounds coming from the house below. Timber groaned in agony, glass popped and snapped under the building heat, but not a single human voice remained inside.

A smile of delight spread across Barok’s face. “There it is! The trump card! Come on, girl, show me what you got! Your iron eggs? Let’s see these famous grenades of—”

[Serpent phantom: est. −3. First use. Error band: wide.]

The ember-red threads within Maureen’s body model flared like veins within marble, lit from inside.

Cold flowed out of her, not from her skin but from her bones.

Black mist poured from her robe in great, rolling sheets. It had a smell, that cold mist, hot iron and sulphur. Tally had first recorded the scent back in the Fasol serpent’s cave.

The orange fire around Maureen and Barok suddenly blew outward in one vast gust. A serpent made of black mist uncoiled itself across the sky, tens of metres long, metallic scales shimmering through the shifting particles of darkness. Its head was broad and flat, its eyes cold scarlet.

Those eyes fixed themselves on Barok Olivia.

The Olivia Family wizard choked on his words, the fireball in his hand sputtering. All he could manage to say was her name, twice, brokenly.

The serpent hissed.

Maureen felt the sound echo through her breastbone, just as she’d felt Evans’s humming on the ship. She felt the vibration more than heard it, and below her the burning hedges seemed to lean away from the cold sound.

Maureen stood atop the phantom serpent’s head, light-footed on the cold scales. She looked down at the stern middle-aged man who had chased her through three cities.

Her expression was hard and quiet. She was done with this experiment.

*Pulse 64. Mana est. 9.*

*Enough,* Maureen told her silently.

“It’s over,” she said softly.

The phantom flashed forward faster than Barok’s eyes could follow. Its jaws opened wide around him, a well of infinite darkness between its rows of razor-sharp teeth, and began to close. As if to swallow him whole.

The last sound to echo above the burning manor was a long, cold hiss.
