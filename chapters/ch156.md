Sweetwater Town was mud and hope frozen together. A single low wall ran around the outskirts of town, with a dozen roofs rising crookedly above it, like broken teeth in a drunken mouth. The wagon ruts in the roads had hardened to the strength of iron, and every rider who came into town looked as though he had the secret to making his fortune by spring.

Maureen had not taken the direct route home from Conta Manor. She had let Tally calculate an alternate path, a new route that wended its way through as many wizard gathering points as she could reach on the way west. It would take longer, but it would give her a chance to see what lay beyond the Alliance’s borders.

Tally’s ribbon still hung in Maureen’s sight, each gathering point marked by a tiny amber pin. Each pin bore the legend archive, may be stale. Tally had pulled all the information from the family letters stored in the Oserroba archives.

*I was going to go shopping,* Maureen told her companion silently.

*Efficient survey, with shopping,* Tally corrected. *It is nice to see you taking an interest in the outside world, Maureen.*

What Maureen had seen so far had settled one matter in her mind: none of the orders she’d visited were even remotely comparable to the Wizard Academy Alliance. The gap between the Alliance and the rest of the West was so wide, in fact, that the Alliance could have justifiably claimed itself to be the only true order of White Wizards in the West. Everyone else lived under their shadow.

The door creaked on frost-rimed hinges, and the sounds of the room washed over Maureen like heat. Men bellowed in drunken arguments, rolling dice on tabletops and slamming cups down. A barmaid shrieked in mock anger when a hand pinched too hard; the laughter that followed was more wet ale than humor. The floorboards had soaked up the sour scent of spilled beer, and the air was thick with the smell of it.

Maureen pushed back her hood. She stood in the doorway for another heartbeat—longer than any cautious traveller would dare—to examine the room. She knew this place from the archive’s list of gathering points, and wanted to see it properly.

*Just a bit of data collection?* Tally asked. *It is interesting. The name “Grinlando” is only one letter removed from “Grimlando’s Conjecture,” which is one of the three books behind the Dark Erosion Fireball. Of course, I have no data on any connection.*

*It’s a tavern,* Maureen replied silently. *Not a footnote.*

*Regardless,* Tally said smugly, *I will file it away just in case.*

Behind the counter, an old man in a stained apron wiped the same cup round and round, the motion almost hypnotic. He had a pair of grey whiskers hanging down from his lip, and he watched the room with eyes that had seen it fill and empty so many times that nothing inside could possibly surprise him.

*Forty-one strokes,* Tally noted. *On the cup.*

*There is also something odd about this room. Every breath here except his is fogged by the door’s draft. I have no data on why.*

*Later,* Maureen ordered silently. *Put it in a drawer for now.*

Maureen took a chair and sat at the table, letting her fingers tap against the scarred wood for a minute as she read the room. Then she rose and walked up to the counter.

“Take me to where the magic tide surges,” she said in Richard’s flat voice.

The tavern keeper’s eyebrows rose slightly at the tone. He was a man who expected a password to be breathed with some deference, at the very least. He frowned, and his head had already begun to turn in refusal.

Maureen met his gaze. Her eyes were black. There was no light in them. The eyes of a cold night sky without moon or stars. And right behind those eyes sat the thing that she held under her pressed-down aura.

The tavern keeper’s hand stopped. It was halfway to setting down the cup he’d been polishing. Without another word, he put the cup down and turned towards the back of the building.

*Wanted some reverence there, eh?* Tally noted.

*He’ll get punctuality instead,* Maureen answered silently. Tally logged the phrase in the archive password file as working on the first try, which was more than she could say for most archive passwords.

Maureen followed the tavern keeper through the kitchen, past the smells of grease and spices, and past two sharp turns. Finally they entered a small storeroom. It was cold enough inside to bite. Sacks of barley filled the room, along with a stack of empty casks, and a broom that probably hadn’t been used in the last month.

The tavern keeper walked straight ahead, towards the far wall. The plaster swallowed him without a ripple.

Tally’s lens began to analyze the wall, spreading out a network of thin gold lines that covered the surface. She displayed an analysis at the bottom of the wall:

[Illusion: wall, by lens.]

[Threshold ward: passive.]

Maureen didn’t bother waiting for the analysis to finish. She stepped through the illusion.

*I see you did not wait for me to finish reading the wall,* Tally said, *just for the record.*

*It was a wall that a man had just walked through,* Maureen replied silently.

* * *

Behind the wall was another world. There were lamps, lit with pine, set to burn low and golden. Their sweet resin hung on the air, mixing with the scents of the huge oil paintings that stood on either side of the passage. The paintings were at least as tall as a man, and they depicted ancient hunts and even older battles. Varnished layers of brown and grey obscured what was once bright colour. Maureen’s boots made no sound as she followed the tavern keeper down the runner laid between the paintings.

The tavern keeper stopped at a tall door carved with scenes of dancing, drinking and music making. He stood still, silent, waiting. A moment later a short man appeared out of the door itself, his shape appearing gradually through the wood grain as though he was rising out of water. In another moment, he stood next to Jerry on the runner, dressed in a coat of brilliant saffron yellow crossed with bands of deep green. A crimson sash was wrapped around his waist.

[Liquefied Wizard, by comparison to Claude and Jackman. Labels: hearsay. Danger: high. Not hostile, by tells.]

*A Liquefied Wizard who dressed like a festival puppet probably enjoyed doing so,* Tally added.

The man’s face was round and babyish, matching his figure. The bright colours of his clothes should have been comical. However, through the half open door, Maureen could see the wizards inside the hall. They glanced at the short wizard before returning their attention to their business, but their faces were careful, watchful.

“Welcome, young wizard,” the short man said, his voice soft and pleased. He bowed his head, bending a little lower than even a Liquefied Wizard needed to bow to anyone else.

They exchanged a few polite words about the road and the frost. “That will be all for now, Jerry,” the short man said, flicking two fingers at the tavern keeper. “Off you go.”

Jerry turned without a pause and walked back up the passage towards the tavern. Tally placed her lens over Jerry’s back as he moved. [Construct. Lattice: fine. No pulse.]

*A puppet,* Tally said. *A much better puppet than Edward ever was.*

*Edward is improving,* Maureen replied silently.

*Edward is currently in a bag,* Tally pointed out.

So that was what didn’t breathe by the door. Maureen kept her face as still and expressionless as Jerry’s had been.

The baby-faced wizard ducked his head, as though caught in an indelicate act. “Ah, well, I must admit, a mere puppet. But you’ll have to forgive me the little trick.”

Maureen smiled. “You’re too modest. Decades behind a bar, and the entire town knows him by name. Your method of making puppets is unique, and very powerful indeed.”

Tally made a forecast, medium confidence: the short man would name a steep price for his method, but would come down once, and only once, if Maureen proved that she actually understood real lattice craft.

*Log it and keep an eye on it,* Maureen told her silently.

The air of the long passage beyond Grinlando’s storeroom smelled of dried roots, sealing wax and something else, too. Someone had scorched their powder somewhere, and the scent was still hanging in the air. Tables lined both sides of the passage, running the length of the hidden hall, each table laden with wares. Glass jars, animal bones and folded hides were common, as were books laid flat and closed. The murmur of voices rose no higher than a low hum.

For two full days, Maureen worked the trading hall in Richard’s face. She sold part of her stock of Conta potions, bought a few raw materials and listened more than she talked. She soon learned that there was really only one thing worth buying here.

On the second day, she approached the baby-faced man directly. “I’ll buy your method,” she said.

He tilted his head slightly and named a price. “Twelve thousand stones.” He did not blink.

Maureen did not argue about the number. “You know how to score along a veilsilver lattice,” she said. “If you wake the core before the lattice is set, then Fallen Star Sand sets permanently.”

“And?” He raised an eyebrow, his face round and smooth.

“And I have a core that has already died once.”

His eyes sharpened from soft and dreamy to shrewd and hard. He looked at Richard properly for the first time. Then he laughed. “Eight thousand stones, and four Official-grade recovery draughts. A wizard who knows what he’s talking about should get a craftsman’s price.”

Tally noted that the baby-faced man had come down once on his price, exactly as forecast. She was being very quiet about it. Then she put up the ledger.

[Magic stones, all columns: ~33,800 → ~25,800.]

[Official recovery draughts: −4.]

The baby-faced man laid his method out on one of the tables, a thick volume bound in plum-coloured leather. He let Maureen study it right there. She turned the pages spread by spread, taking her time. Tally recorded each page as it passed beneath Maureen’s eyes, storing the information away.

As the pages flipped past, Tally compared them against the unsealed texts on necromancy and enchantment from Nightingale’s contract.

[Lattice craft: practical. Complements theory.]

Then Tally flagged a line, indicating that one step directly contradicted the spreads from the White Tower construct texts.

*Ask him,* Maureen said silently.

*I’ve already written the question,* Tally replied.

Maureen spoke it aloud. The baby-faced wizard beamed at her, as if she’d just paid him a second time. “Ah! Yes, that’s right. The White Tower binds the core before the lattice cools—but they lose a tenth of the strength doing so. I always wait until the metal stops singing.”

Maureen nodded, committing the advice to memory.

Her fingertips moved slightly against the edge of the table as she read. She wasn’t consciously aware of tracing the lines of the lattice at first; the old itch to work with a bench came back to her, a throwback to her time in Jing Sheng’s lab during her first life. No amount of killing had ever been able to cure her of that.

On the third morning, Maureen thanked the man for his time, pulled her hood back up over her face and left the hall. Grinlando and Sweetwater Town were behind her now.

* * *

Maureen Olsen was alone again, riding west along the frozen road toward Emilia. The reins lay slack across her gloved hands as the wheels crunched over the frost. She found herself humming Ivan’s old marching song under her breath.

*Humming?*

Tally noted the phenomenon, along with the steady warmth beneath Maureen’s chest that had accompanied it. *Counts as happy?* she asked.

*Counts as quiet road,* Maureen answered.

Happy, provisionally logged.

The girl’s thoughts kept returning to the plum-coloured book. With this method, she could repair Edward. More than repair him—she could make him stronger. She could reinforce his lattice, give him more options. She absently rubbed at her chin as she smiled to herself.

Edward’s last reading flashed beside the method’s diagrams. [Edward: in pouch. Left flank scorched. Lattice scoring: ~12%.]

*A bench and a week,* Tally said. *At least now I have instructions.*

Five men stepped out from between two hedgerows where the road bent ahead of Maureen. Rusted blades gleamed in their hands, bowstrings creaked taut as they held their arrows aimed at her chest. “You can either hand over all your gold coins,” the leader of the bandits called out, “or you can let us take your life instead.”

*Five. Two bows, left,* Tally announced, her voice flat and cold. There was a red mark beside each bowstring.

Maureen didn’t even pull on her reins.

She raised a single hand in a languid wave. Pale white arrows streaked away from her fingertips, a Rank Zero spell known as Undead Wind Arrows, and bored clean through the five men before the first bow could release its shaft.

[Mana: 23.0 → 20.0.]

*Your heart rate did not change at all during that event,* Tally noted. *I am logging this in the same way that I logged Lauren.*

*They asked for my life,* Maureen replied.

*They asked for your gold. You gave them the other thing.*

The bodies of the bandits lay twisted on the frosted ground, grey corrosion spreading out from the holes in their chests. The horse snorted at the foul smell, and Maureen drove past without even turning her head.

Tally ran through the tallies of the trip while the road unrolled beneath the carriage wheels.

[Gatherings visited: 9.]

[Potions sold: 31.]

[High-energy creatures: 4.]

[Wizard attacks: 6.]

[Survivors of those: 0.]

[Road sales: +~400.]

[Magic stones, all columns: ~26,200.]

*Ordinary bandits are not worth a column of their own,* Tally added.

After the thick knot of gathering points, the amber pins on Tally’s ribbon began to thin out. The road stretched straight ahead, and three days later the irrigated plain spread out before Maureen. The walls of Emilia shimmered with ward-light in the distance.

Before they reached the city gates, Tally double-checked the Richard face, which had held perfectly since Conta. She also examined Maureen’s eyes, ensuring that they were still black. Once they reached the wizards’ tract, she swept the city with her particle sense, checking for the dark-red knots she had found during her last visit.

[Emilia: density knots, 2 → 5, by particle sense. No data.]

*There are more of them,* Maureen noted.

*Either there are more or you are listening harder,* Tally responded. *I’m putting this in both columns.*

* * *

Hooves rang off the frozen cobbles of Connpa Street. Maureen jumped off at her own gate. Two servants with brooms froze in the middle of sweeping the yard. One of the two looked up, stammering, “M-master?”

Within minutes, the entire household was out. Eight members of the bound staff ran in eight different directions, doors banged open and closed, the cook yelled for more hot water, and the cat strolled through the middle of the chaos as if the house belonged to it.

Maureen let them fuss over her. She sat down to breakfast and ate. First, she ate bread and butter, then eggs, ham, and finally porridge. One dish after another disappeared down her throat like some slum girl who didn’t know when her next meal would be coming.

*Wizard Richard is eating like Maureen Olsen of the Skran slums,* Tally noted. *The cook is watching you eat.*

*Let her watch,* Maureen responded silently.

*Excellent data. Porridge logged.*

After breakfast, Maureen bathed, then walked up the stairs behind her own bedroom ward. Finally, she let the Richard face peel away. Free of its spellcraft, she fell into bed and slept until the next morning.

When she woke, Tally gave her the night.

[Night: 1 pass, cat, 02:40. Ward drew 0.1. Mana: 23.0, full.]

Maureen recast Richard’s face in front of the mirror. It was a cool crawl across her skin. Tally double-checked the left cheek and the eyes before Maureen unbolted the door.

Ike stood outside, waiting with an open ledger in his thin hands. His eyes, as always, never rose above her collar. “Master, this is what the villa has spent during your absence.” His voice dropped to a whisper. “We have spent the entire time, and there is very little gold remaining. Without new income, Master, you will go bankrupt.”

[Gold: low. Magic stones, all columns: ~26,200.]

*Gold is stones by the roadside to a wizard,* Maureen answered silently.

*True, but Ike doesn’t know that. He is very upset about it.*

Maureen smiled behind Richard’s plain features. She nodded once. Ike’s shoulders relaxed, dropping half an inch. He seemed to think that a nod was all the reassurance he could expect from her.

Before he left, Ike put a small stack of sealed letters on the table. “All the messages that arrived during your absence, Wizard. I kept them as you ordered.”

Most were written in Joanna’s neat handwriting. They were addressed to Wizard Richard. Maureen set them aside to look at later.

That evening, she returned to the coal bay. The wall opened at her command of “Manas” and the hidden chamber lay before her. The energy pool glowed green within. Tally checked the Rental Centre’s ward-knot, as she did every time, and confirmed that it still ended against Gregory’s second ring. Holding.

Maureen reached into the void pouch and drew Edward out. The puppet lay in her hands. [Pouch transit: tolerated, 5 tests. Core: lit.]

She laid the puppet down in the green glow of the energy pool. The scorch ran down his left flank like a black line of ash. She opened up the plum-coloured method beside him. Tally noted the time in her record. Over the next few nights, Maureen began her work, scoring the lattice clean, step by step, waiting for each bit of metal to stop singing before she bound anything else.

[Edward: lattice scoring 12% → 7%. Upgrade: blade-arm frame, design stage.]

*Best progress report I’ve put on your display in a month,* Tally said.

*The only one I care about,* Maureen answered silently.

Veilsilver was cold beneath her fingers. The glow from the energy pool washed over her hands, turning the backs of her knuckles green. For those hours, she forgot about the war in the East. She forgot that anyone was looking for her at all.

* * *

In the Pharmacy corridors of the hollow spiral tower, lit by hanging beeswax lamps, with wizards gliding between balconies overhead, the greetings came quickly. One after another, each person Maureen passed stood aside and smiled. “Wizard Richard.” “Wizard Richard.” All were friendly smiles, no matter what they might be thinking behind their faces.

Tally kept count of the smiles as Maureen walked. Nine smiles that reached the eyes, four that stopped at the mouth, and one man who did not smile at all. He looked down at the floor until Maureen had passed him. *Keep that one,* Maureen said silently to Tally.

Maureen knocked once on Ambrose’s door, then walked in without waiting for an answer. Camphor and the scent of old paper filled the office. Rows of stoppered glass jars sat on the shelves beneath a narrow, frosted window.

[Ambrose: pulse up on sight. Tube unstable, by smell.]

Tally’s alert flashed half a heartbeat before Ambrose moved.

The head of the Alliance Pharmacy Department had been gently swirling a glass tube filled with a green potion. When he saw Maureen, his hand jumped. The liquid in the tube exploded with a soft whump, and a cloud of green smoke rolled upward toward the ceiling. Cut grass and burnt sugar filled the air. With a sigh, Ambrose set the empty tube carefully on his desk and turned toward Maureen, looking at her with an expression of long-suffering resignation. “Richard. You’ve been gone a long time.”

*I was going to warn him,* Tally said.

*You mean you were going to warn me,* Maureen responded silently.

*Warn you. Yes. That is what I meant.*

Maureen’s shoulders squared anyway. In the East, she was a deserter, with a hunt waiting for her. Here, she was a group leader, and she kept disappearing. Ambrose was the only man in the building who could take that title away from her.
