“Everyone knows the story!” he laughed.

“It all started with—”

He let out a scream that would have put a banshee to shame as Tally alerted her that the bricks under Burton’s chair were heaving, mortar between the bricks splitting apart. Maureen was already moving, bringing her Cross Sword down hard through the red bricks, lifting up a serpent with scales the colour of blood. Thick as a man’s arm, it had been run through the head by Maureen’s sword. It let out an angry hiss and twisted around the blade. The serpent’s scales were coated in gritty dust from the red bricks that it had been hiding under. The tip of its tail was slamming into one of the legs of the table, making the table jump with each strike.

Tally tagged the serpent as sample two, following the dead sparrow, and asked for a closer look at it. *Later,* she told it silently.

“Burton, continue with your st—” Maureen began to say but stopped when she saw the young man’s face. His skin was white as a sheet while his hands shook. “Let’s go out together, kill these monsters first, and then we’ll have a proper chat.” She swung her Cross Sword down, cleaving the serpent into several pieces. Shouts came from outside, followed by a brief scream abruptly cut off. The slapping sound of bowstrings filled the air, along with the scent of burning pitch from torches.

“Burton, stay by my side. Understand?” She strode towards the door; the young man stood there for a moment, stunned, before he seemed to come to his senses and hurried after her.

* * *

Guards in uniform were fighting the beasts with spears and bows. Many of the creatures fell, pierced with arrows or impaled on spears, but many of the guards were being pulled under a mass of writhing creatures to be torn apart.

Torchlight flickered on the red cobbles, the light dancing off the red tiled roofs in the street. The light shone on red stone walls, jumped off them and danced again. There was a sound of boots scraping backwards across the wet cobbles, leaving long dark tracks. One of the guards was being dragged away from Maureen, his spear gone.

Up close now, she saw that the monsters were just ordinary dogs, swine and goats. They had grown to an oversized scale but were still recognisable as the animals they were. Except for their eyes. Their eyes were filled with burst blood vessels, making them red rimmed and blood shot.

“Only a few days ago these things were half this size,” Burton shouted.

Tally displayed [Hostiles: 19, by sight] and highlighted the nearest creature. *Keep the count running,* Maureen told it silently. She frowned and turned to the young man.

“Burton, I said keep close.” And with that she charged into them.

*Left. Swine, breaking for your flank.* Maureen spun and opened its throat. Blood steamed in the chill air, running black between the red cobbles. Burton was still at her shoulder.

These beasts were nowhere near her level. As she carved through them, they fell like brush being cleared away. Torchlight gleamed and slid along her blade. With Maureen in the fight, the beasts began to pull back towards the walls. The number of hostiles on her display dropped rapidly, as did the number of bodies at her feet. The guards’ spears prodded at her rear, giving her room to move and fight.

Tally boxed off a knot of six beasts against a red stone wall on her display. *Wait two breaths, let them bunch up.*

Maureen began the incantation. She swallowed the first syllable. Am… the vowel was bitten off. Tally flagged it. *Low, back, like Scarlette taught you.* She took a breath.

Maureen opened her hand, and spoke the syllable cleanly. “Amra-Keis!” A ball of smoking acid, the colour of rotting vegetation, flew from her palm and detonated in the middle of the massed beasts.

There was a tearing hiss, accompanied by shrieks of pain and terror, before all was still. The beasts lay pitted and smoking, the stink of torn hide filling the air. The street was almost clear of hostiles now, Tally’s count dropping with satisfying speed on her display.

* * *

The guards and townsfolk were looking at her. “Mys,” one said. “Mystic.”

They left a wide circle around her. A mother pulled her child back behind a doorway by his collar. Tally docked the missile, [Mana: 3.2 → 2.8], filing away the word ‘Mystic’ along with the fear she heard in the man’s voice.

Maureen saw Albert, Aubrey and Daphne a short distance away. They had been in the fight as well. She tilted her head in a small smile. “Maureen,” Albert said, nodding to her. “Well done.”

“Maureen,” Aubrey echoed, bowing stiffly.

All three gave her slight bows, Albert’s smile sour, Aubrey’s bow stiff, Daphne muttering under her breath. Their wizard’s robes were relatively unmarked. Albert was again mopping his brow with a lilac handkerchief, wheezing slightly.

Maureen nodded, then turned to look at the pile of corpses. Even from this distance she could smell the sulphur, the fire, the smoke. Her throat tightened reflexively and she breathed in shallowly.

*The odour is identical to the serpent on the Fasol.*

*Yes, Tally. I know.*

As she walked closer something appeared in the palm of her hand. A faint circular silver sigil appeared on her skin and countless silver threads began to move in a pattern that made one's eyes swim if they stared too long. The silver patterns moved in a rhythm that was hard to follow. As soon as it appeared Maureen closed her fingers over it, tugged her robe straight and held one breath.

*Did you do that?*

*No, Maureen. That is my decode of the line patterns within Patton’s ancient book, connected with the silver line patterns I worked on before. I only make them visible when you focus on them.*

*But I wasn’t focusing.*

*Then neither you nor I are driving. It manifested as we approached the bodies. I have no data on why.*

She crouched down and inspected the bodies, using a small silver knife to turn some of them. Like the black serpent, these had been ordinary animals.

*Comparing all bodies. Comparing the sparrow and the serpent.*

[Marker match 78%. Particle residue 77%. Odour 85%. Est.: one species.]

*Sulphur line is most dominant.*

The knife came away warm, even a little greasy. She lowered her eyes and her face stayed impassive.

* * *

“These are their animals,” Burton said in a low voice. “In the last while some of them go mad, get bigger and attack anyone on sight.”

“You told the city guards?”

Burton nodded. “They weren’t interested after they heard ‘wild beasts’. Laughed at us too, said we couldn’t handle our own animals. Bastards.”

His fists clenched, his voice cracking slightly with anger.

“These are monsters, not beasts. You’ve fought them, you know what they’re like.”

[City guard contempt is also data. No help will be forthcoming from that quarter.]

*I know.*

“Which direction do these monsters come from?” Maureen asked. “Or, which part of town has been attacked the most?”

Burton scratched his head. “I don’t know. I never thought about it.” He looked around then pointed. “That way. Southeast. Where that bastard Ange has his lair.”

[Southeast bearing marked. File on Ange available. Shall I open?]

*He’s mortal. Just a mortal.*

[One line maintained.]

Maureen looked southeast, over the red roofs of the buildings, into the dark sky.

Cheers went up as the jug made its circuitous way around the crowd. The cheap ale smell wafted across on the breeze, cutting through the pervasive sulphur smell.

The captain of the guard was doing his best to calm down the now delighted townsfolk.

Maureen didn’t join in the celebrations. “We’re going back,” she said and Albert’s party nodded quickly.

* * *

Soon the celebrations were winding down, and the town fell quiet again. Maureen opened her window and looked around carefully, before deciding that the hour was right for her to move. She pushed the shutters wide open and the cold night air rushed into the room bringing with it a thread of sulphur and the wet smell of tallow from the torches that had burnt themselves out.

Maureen slipped on a black robe. The crowds in the town had prevented her from scouting out the territory, but the deep night was perfect for it. She swung her legs out the window and rested her boots on the sill. With a push of one hand against the stone she dropped down, Tally counting her descent for her, before landing silently on the cobbled street. [Landing: silent] it told her. Maureen took a couple of steps, her boots making no sound on the red cobbles and glanced up at the inn behind her. No lights shone from any windows. Tally helpfully displayed her bearing to the southeast and informed her that it would maintain it for her if she so wished. Maureen nodded silently. Southeast. She walked off into the dark.
