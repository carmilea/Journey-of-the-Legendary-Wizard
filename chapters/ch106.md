Maureen woke flat on her back in the heavy bed. Her boots were still on her feet. All of the muscles from her hips to her shoulders felt like rope that had been left out overnight to dry and then wound into a ball. Her short hair was still damp with horse sweat, and smelled of that and of the smoke from the garrison. She had not been asleep for very long—even Tally thought so.

[Sleep: 6h 12m.] the first line hung in front of her eyes, though she hadn’t quite opened them yet.

*That’s rather short,* Tally informed her. *Especially when sentinel one is still live.*

*Short is what I have,* Maureen replied silently.

After setting fire to the Olivia garrison, she had ridden through the night on one of the horn-scaled blacks with the second beast following on a lead rein. She had reached the nearest town during the small hours, paid for this room with gold taken from the garrison chest, and fallen asleep where she dropped. She lay there for a moment, listening. There was a cart wheel squealing somewhere in the street below, and a woman’s voice cried out, “Onions!” There was a clang and a curse as someone dropped a pan downstairs.

The town had a name painted on its gate, but Maureen had not bothered to read it as she rode in. Now, she did not want to remember it.

Tally showed her the night’s recovery.

[Mana: 5.0 / 11.2.]

*Riding gave you back more than I expected,* it told her. *Enough for a mirror, but not an argument.*

*I’m not planning an argument,* she replied silently.

She sat up on the edge of the bed and picked up the cup of tea that had been sitting on the windowsill. The liquid was cold and furred with tannin, leaving a bitter taste at the back of her tongue. She wiped her hands on the sheet. There was still black soot from the Olivia garrison in all of the creases of her knuckles.

Maureen poured water from the jug on the washstand into her cupped palm and spoke the words of the mirror spell under her breath. A disc of water rose from her hand and hovered at eye level over the wine-coloured velvet carpet. Its rim trembled like a soap bubble. [Mana: 5.0 → 4.9.]

Tally framed the disc with a thin border of pale blue and pinned a label in the upper left-hand corner. [Sentinel 1: the ruins. By relayed sight. Sound: thin.] It was a reminder to Maureen that everything inside that border was secondhand. Whatever she saw came to her through the eyes of a stone.

The image within was grainy, and its colours were washed thin, like a reflection seen in the bottom of a bucket someone had just set down. Blackened bluestone walls still stood, but their stones were cracked. Here and there, a few flames still licked at fallen beams, but they were low and yellow. Above them, smoke was rising toward a flat grey sky. The wall-walk where she had laid her ring of rune nodes was now a broken circle of black stone.

Above the ruins hung a dark figure, wrapped in what looked like a cloak of dark particles that glowed faintly. He was suspended in air, unmoving, unhurried, as though he were a statue carved into the side of a temple. Below him, several men dressed in Olivia Family robes stood in the ash, their eyes fixed on their boots. Each man wore the silver family pin on his shoulder.

Tally matched the stern face of the hovering man against its records. [Barok. Olivia Family. Match: projection, Adrian’s lab.] Unfortunately, it could not band him for Maureen, since there was no particle sense to be had through a glass of water. *By sight alone,* Tally said, *he flies, he isn’t in any hurry, and the others won’t look up at him. That is a rank.*

Maureen put an elbow on her knee and rested her chin in her fist. It was the position Jing Sheng had adopted so often, sitting on the white bench beside a reaction she wasn’t allowed to touch, watching it with complete attention.

Barok was muttering something, but the sound was thin. At first, it came through as an incomprehensible crackle from the mirror. As Barok flew upward, the words became clearer.

“... How? How does this — how?”

The last “how” was a roar, and the Olivia Family members below flinched as one.

“It was last night, Honoured sir,” said one of them, a young man with the silver Olivia pin on his collar. His voice was small, almost lost in the stone eye’s crackling. “We saw the glow and came straight here.”

Barok did not glance down at the speaker. He didn’t need to. The silence spoke more than the roar had.

Then Barok went still in the air. “Where’s Frey?” He looked down at them. “He came to me for my seal. Sixteen apprentices out of the family, for one girl. Maureen Olsen, he said. So where is he?”

*There’s your leak,* Tally told Maureen. *He told his grandfather who you were.*

*He had to tell him something about sixteen apprentices,* Maureen replied silently.

*Families keep receipts too,* Tally noted.

“No word, sir.”

“Not a thing, sir.”

Barok’s mouth thinned to a line. “Then find him. He can’t have gone far.” The men scrambled away, disappearing into the ash and beyond the edges of the frame. The stone eye could not follow them.

*Sentinel two?* Tally suggested. *Where Frey Olivia lies?*

*Yes,* Maureen thought.

The image tore apart like wet paper, then knitted itself back together. [Sentinel 1: fading.] appeared on Maureen’s display beside the first label.

The new image showed the trampled clearing where she had ended Frey. A fan of scorching from the grenade spread across the earth. Ferns had burned down to wire. At the centre of the devastation lay Frey, on his back, his golden hair dark and stiff with blood. Already, flies had found him. They crawled over his skin and settled in his staring eyes.

A distant shout came through the mirror. “Here! Over here!”

Barok dropped out of the flat grey sky like a stone. His descent was a rush of black particles that made an almost solid column before he hit the ground next to Frey’s body. The entire image shuddered as he arrived.

His face twisted. He knelt, and one hand extended toward the boy’s face but stopped a finger’s distance away. Then a roar burst from him, though it broke midway into something lower and rawer.

“Frey. Frey—”

Maureen’s jaw tightened. She’d expected rage. She’d prepared for it. But she hadn’t prepared for the hands of a man who didn’t know where to go.

*He knelt like a man who had loved the boy,* Tally said quietly.

*So?*

*So he won’t stop,* said Tally. *That’s the data.*

For half a breath, Maureen thought about Adrian two years ago, burning the projection of this same man in mid-word, without her asking, just for her. I’m not in the habit of selling my own apprentices. She thought about what it meant, to be someone’s heir.

Then she closed the thought, like a book.

Barok looked up from the body. He stared at the smoke rising on the horizon, from the garrison she’d been posted to. The sum made itself.

“Maureen Olsen.”

He stood up.

“Tell the others I’m going after her. I’ll kill her myself. With these hands.”

[Threat: Barok. Timeline: none.]

Tally filed the information away in an efficient line of text on Maureen’s display.

*He’ll find me,* Maureen said silently.

Tally opened its file on Official Wizards. *You have seen Evans step through space. You have seen Adrian burn a projection through his glasses. We have no data on Barok’s tracking abilities. Assume all of it,* Tally said.

Black particles closed over Barok, and he blurred out of the frame as a dark streak. The men below nodded to the empty air. Quickly. Not one of them objected.

At the edge of the frame, from the stone where she had pressed the sentinel’s silver lines, came a faint flicker that rose and fell again. None of the men turned.

[Sentinel 2: fading.]

* * *

The mirror of water lost its shape, then fell with a slap. A puddle of black spread across the velvet. Maureen’s eyes narrowed as she looked down at it. “Barok.”

[Sentinel 2: faded.] Tally said.

It had lasted long enough. Barok hadn’t seen it.

Her hands went cold. She watched it start in her fingertips. The same chill that had seeped into her bones after the crystal at Sunset Canyon, a cold that had lingered for hours. She looked at them. They were cold. It was a fact, like a number on a dial or a line of text. Her eyes turned back to the spreading stain of water.

*Your breathing has become short,* Tally said. *From what your ears are detecting, I infer this.*

*I know,* Maureen said silently.

*Regardless, I will log that information, just as I logged Lauren and Blake when you did not modify your breathing pattern.*

She worked through the facts. Frey needed his grandfather’s seal to borrow apprentices from his court. He had given Barok her name, and she had been one of the apprentices who had been posted to the garrison that burned. No genius required to put those together.

Two silver eye-patterns pressed into stone. One in the rubble, one beside a dead boy. Without them, she’d have slept another night in this soft bed, smelling lavender again, and woken to see a black streak in the sky.

*You placed them there upon my suggestion,* Tally said.

*Yes.*

*Logging. You agree.*

*Log it smaller.*

But the relief didn’t last. Barok wasn’t some petty court wizard. He was an Official Wizard, and they had ways of hunting their quarry that even Maureen couldn’t name. Against an Official Wizard, an apprentice was nothing more than a lamb at the butcher’s block.

*Frey sat at the top of the column,* Tally said. *I have no column for Barok.*

*What do you mean?*

*I mean every number I hold on him is a guess.*

And then it came. It was so obvious that it was almost funny. If she had been recording the numbers in a ledger, the total would be right there on the bottom line, staring her in the face.

She needed to stop being an apprentice.

*I need to become an Official Wizard myself. Face him as an equal.*

The checklist filled Maureen’s vision. Tally had been keeping track since the time in Gregory’s realm. Line by line, the checklist scrolled past.

[Body: 10.4 / 10.2 / 10.6, met. Spirit 11.2. Mana max 11.2. Minai Totem: 85%. Materials: 4 of 4 in hand. Pankora blood: sealed vial.]

Maureen’s hand dropped to the pouch at her belt. Somewhere in its dark sat the Demon-Face Flower. A round, white blossom with black veins tracing out a face across its center. Veins that formed a smile, or maybe just half a smile. The laughter of the flower was faint, and the rest of its petals hid it from sight. Her eyes couldn’t follow it, nor Tally’s vision.

*Conditions are met,* Tally said. *You have everything you need now.*

*All I need is a room no one enters and a few hours no one interrupts.*

*Distance,* Maureen thought. *We need distance.*

*Distance first. Agreed.*

This town was too close. Barok would reach it within a day if he was willing to search stone by stone, and anyone in this town who had seen her face would be worth his while.

Maureen stood.

Her boots laced up tight, she felt over her body with flat palms. The Seal, cracked and rough against her fingers. The Silver Soul Token, cold against her skin. The stone cards. The bronze book, hard against her ribs. The long box of dark gold. She was down the stairs before she’d even finished being afraid.

*Pouch is present.* Tally noted as her hand found it. *Token is present. Cards are present. Book is present. Box is present.*

*All items accounted for. The puddle will probably dry by evening, and the innkeep will likely blame the washstand.*

* * *

At the counter, Maureen paid the innkeeper with gold from the garrison chest. Paid too much, more than the room was worth, so that the memory he kept of her would be a good one and a short one. He bit the coin, bowed her out, and that was all there was of that. The stable boy stood well back from the two stalls. The black horses tossed their ridged forehead plates, the horns gleaming dully with each toss. They were black, their shining scales ran down their flanks like rivulets of obsidian, and steam poured from their mouths. The stalls smelled like hot iron. The pail of raw beef that Maureen had paid for at dawn had been licked clean.

She saddled one of the beasts herself and tied the second beast behind on a lead rein. The carriage was ash now in the garrison yard, along with everything that she hadn’t fit into the pouch.

*Two sets of tracks.* *That’s suspicious,* Tally noted.

*Two sets of legs,* she answered silently.

*Fair point.*

*Fair enough. Legs are the more useful column.*

Maureen rode through the town’s north gate at a walk, not wanting anyone to remember her leaving in any sort of hurry. Once the buildings began to thin out, though, she opened up the pace to a hard canter.

The road stretched away north between two hard ridges of frost. The flat lid of grey sky was still overhead. The wind, coming off the hills to the east, cut through her robe. The horse’s breath steamed like a kettle and the hoofbeats rang out loud on the frozen earth.

Before sleeping, Maureen had pulled out the inn’s regional map, spreading it across the bed. She’d studied it by lamplight. The route was already in her head, but it was good to look over it again.

It was in Tally’s head too.

 The second horse trotted along on the lead rein, behind and to her right.

A pale blue ribbon unrolled itself in Maureen’s vision, stretching ahead along the road. Tally highlighted three cities along its length. Frostleaf, Maplefall, Gladton, Peaceridge beyond them. *Three cities between you and the ruins,* Tally said. *Three cities to vanish into.*

With every stride of the horse under her the bandolier bounced against her ribs. Five shells, plain black iron, all that was left of the trump card she had played so spectacularly to earn a name in the realm.

*Five shells. That’s all we have. Five shells, and against him... polite.*

*We don’t argue with wizards.*

*Not yet,* agreed Tally.

Maureen’s plan was simple. Put as much distance between them as she could, by putting some cities between them. Get to Peaceridge, or at least somewhere past it, then find four walls nobody would be knocking on for a while. Come out the other side as something he wasn’t expecting. Something he hadn’t planned for.

*How far is far enough?* asked Tally. *I have no data on how fast he flies.*

*As far as the horses will take us,* Maureen replied silently. Tally began counting down hours for her, the numbers appearing in the corner of her sight.

Behind her the town shrank, becoming a smudge on the horizon, a blur of chimney smoke. Her hands were still cold inside the gloves but she kept them steady on the reins anyway.

*I’ll worry about the hours, you just drive,* said Tally.

*This is the oldest arrangement we’ve had.*

*True,* agreed Tally, *but it’s also the best.*
