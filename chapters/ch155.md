The ash lay across her palm, black and unbroken and faintly warm still. Letters appeared through the ash, pale grey, one line after another like frost creeping over a windowpane.

Aaron has woken. He is asking for you. Come to the estate outside Hastan at sundown tomorrow.

There was a long tail to this message, running down through the ash like the streamers of a comet, expressing Clair’s thanks for the potion, but her eyes didn’t stop on any of it. Her pulse jumped from sixty-four to eighty-eight over two lines of reading, Tally noted. *Also, your right hand has automatically gone to your inner pocket where the Seal used to ride,* she added mildly.

*Yes, I know,* Maureen thought silently. *Log it as habit.*

*It has been weeks, not three to six days,* Tally pointed out. *May I log my forecast as wrong?*

*No, log it as late.*

*Late and wrong. I think I’ll take both.*

The last word faded from view and the black ash dissolved in an instant, crumbling between Maureen’s fingers to leave nothing but soot. She brushed the soot into the frozen basin of the fountain, and it lay there like a thumbprint against the grey ice.

The next afternoon she found the steward in the front hall.

“I’m going out,” she said without expression.

The steward started, then bobbed his head. “Of course, Wizard Richard. At once. Entirely at once.” He was already waving for a carriage to be brought around.

*Four bows again,* counted Tally. *Just like when you got back from the Underworld. If you stand still long enough I think he’ll manage a fifth.*

*Don’t encourage him.*

Maureen walked out through the front gate without trying to hide. With Claude dead and Denise holding Conta Manor, there was no one left inside that she needed to slip past. The guards at the gate simply straightened up as “Wizard Richard” passed.

The horned beasts blew steam into the cold air as they trotted down the road, their massive flanks covered in scales that quivered under the harness. The smell of coal smoke hung over Hastan ahead of them, smudging the pale road. The low sun turned the frost on the stubble fields copper for an instant before it faded to grey as the light went.

Tally checked the borrowed face against her muscle map as the carriage rattled along. *So far, Richard’s left cheek appears to be holding,* she said. [Tail: none, 300 m.]

*Fine,* Maureen thought. *Keep watching the road behind us anyway.*

The estate rose in view as they approached the outskirts of Hastan. Grey stone had deepened to blue in the dying light, and the fountain in the forecourt was still a dull plate of ice. A breeze with the night already in it billowed the carriage curtain as they stopped, and Maureen narrowed her eyes against it.

* * *

They met this time in the main hall, not down in the cellar. The heat that had rolled up through the flagstones from the cellar was there again, but now it seemed to radiate from Aaron himself, like standing too close to a banked forge. Clair and his three Phantoms stood by the wall, smelling of camphor and road dust.

Aaron was on his feet now, a little too pale, his breath catching and starting again, but alive. He smiled when he saw Maureen, and that was all it took for her jaw to unclench at last. “Maureen.”

The Totem stirred in response to Aaron’s presence, and Tally immediately obliged with her own report. [Bloodline: active. Totem: drawing. Weaker than last time.] The borrowed face hid Maureen’s frown as she reached through the Orb to damp the Totem’s power. [Mana: 22.6 → 22.4] *Still wants him,* she thought silently.

*Less now,* Tally said. *His line has stabilized, so there is less reason to draw against him.*

Before Tally could expand further on the details, Maureen silently interrupted, *We don’t feed it our friends.*

 *That one was mine, and I am glad you have learned it.*

“Thank you,” Aaron said. “Clair told me what you did down there. Seven days at a bench . . .”

Maureen shook her head. “I had a formula and the materials. You had the bad luck. Call it even.”

Aaron’s body language was still a little unfamiliar, but Tally got a good reading of him with just a glance. [Aaron. Liquefied, by comparison to Claude and Jackman, labels hearsay. Line: Black Mando, stable. Breath: 11/min.]

*Will he hold?* Maureen asked silently.

*Medium confidence,* Tally replied. *That is better than any numbers I gave you in that cellar, though.*

“You’ve gone Liquefied,” Maureen said.

Aaron didn’t bother trying to hide it from her. “A bit of luck, that’s all.” His mouth twisted in a lopsided smile. “And you’re Phantom now. Don’t pretend you weren’t going to mention it.”

He stood for a moment, studying Maureen’s borrowed face, as if looking for something beneath the illusion. “You’ve had some luck as well, I think. Bigger than mine, if I had to guess. Your potions alone would indicate that much. But I won’t ask.” He shrugged. “This blood has taught me what asking can cost.”

*His blood answered yours the moment you walked in the door,* Tally said. *The kin sense that Clair warned us about. Maybe he feels something odd about the heart beating under your skin.*

*But on your fortune, he’s just guessing,* Maureen thought back. *He doesn’t read density like we do.*

*True,* Tally admitted. *No proof either way.*

They walked out into the gravel paths that ran through the grounds, crunching loudly in the night’s chill. They passed the frozen fountain and strolled away from the house. Aaron’s breath formed clouds in the air—first a short puff, then another, before a long exhale, then starting over. Behind them, Clair and the three Phantoms followed at a respectful forty paces.

Aaron talked of those days as if they had happened to someone else. Someone he’d known in a former life. Someone who’d gone through things he would never know. Maureen listened, nodding occasionally, as Aaron described the Dark Forest with calm detachment. He spoke in that flat tone she remembered from their days on the Fasol road, like dried leaves scraping against one another. Beneath that calm surface, she could hear something hollow. Something very cold.

Her throat tightened at what he didn’t tell her. The heel of hard bread on the ship. The trap wires she’d strung across her cabin door. The fresh purple bruises she’d seen at his wrist, when she’d met him in the stone alcove. The way he’d always left before the food came out.

Tally flagged a change in his voice. Whenever he mentioned his “mentor,” it dropped half a tone. *Three times now,* Tally said, *but I guess you heard it too, Maureen.*

*I did,* Maureen thought back.

*I know. Just logging it for both of us.*

After his bloodline took, he explained, he waited quietly, remaining near the Dark Forest. Then, when the opportunity presented itself, he killed his mentor and Rheas.

“Rheas had the same mentor,” Aaron said dispassionately. “He laughed at me every day. He was very good at finding new ways to do it.”

*Rheas’s mentor is listed as Parn,* Tally said. *So Aaron’s mentor was Parn too. One of the Dark Forest’s most powerful wizards. Not quite as strong as Evans, but not by much, according to Alice’s hearsay.*

Maureen stood very still.

*He did it anyway,* Tally continued. *Closed the bruising file under “mentor” and marked Rheas “deceased.”*

Maureen remembered the man on the deck of the ship. The tall, golden-haired Rheas, flushed with pride, wearing the ring that cast a sea of fire upon Hila. Twelve crossbows beneath those cloaks. Now, either she or Aaron could have crushed him like an ant beneath their cuff.

Aaron kept talking, and he talked for a long time. But in all that time, he never once mentioned Ellena—the girl whom, at the age of six, he’d decided he would marry.

Maureen rubbed her nose.

*Nose rub noted. Ninth on file. Interesting that this is the first one that didn’t come after a win,* Tally pointed out.

*Log it as “the cold,”* Maureen thought back.

*Logged as “the cold.” Disputed.*

Aaron’s gaze dropped to the left side of her coat, where the Silver Soul Token lay in its special inner pocket. She began to reach for it. “Keep it,” he said. “Where I’m going, it’ll open doors. And close a few mouths.”

He stopped walking and turned to face her on the gravel path. “I’m leaving, Maureen. Leaving the Passise. I’m going to the Wizard Continent.”

Maureen frowned. “The Wizard Continent?”

“Yes. There’s something . . . I have to do there.” His eyes darted past her, and she could see something pass over them like a shadow. His expression hardened, became grey, inscrutable, in the last light of day.

“Lady Maureen,” Clair said, coming up the path behind them, “His Eminence must stand for assessment as Child of the Silver Soul, and the End-of-Years Selection for the Lord of the Silver Soul is only a few decades off, so—”

He stopped. Maureen glanced at him. He flushed, under his borrowed face, and lapsed into silence.

[Child of the Silver Soul: assessment pending. Lord of the Silver Soul: End-of-Years Selection, decades away. Source: Clair, one voice, regretted.]

*He regretted saying that?* Maureen thought back to Tally.

*That’s exactly why I believed it.*

Darkness had fallen while they were talking, and the wind was becoming sharp. The smell of coal drifted up from Hastan City, and the first pinpricks of light appeared in the town below. The heat radiating off Aaron was the only warmth on the grounds now.

Aaron stared at her for a long moment. “I think I’ll find you on the Wizard Continent, Maureen,” he said finally. “See you there.”

Maureen nodded once. “See you on the Wizard Continent.”

She let herself breathe during the exchange, and as he turned she saw his right hand move, his thumb worrying at the hem of his left sleeve. An old tell from their days together on the convoy road.

She said nothing.

They left immediately, packs already strapped on their backs, horses already saddled. They had stayed only long enough for Aaron to see her once more before he left. Clair pressed a small redwood chest into Maureen’s arms.

“From His Eminence,” the wizard said. “Spell books from the Silver Soul Hand’s collections. Experiment notes. Data . . .”

Maureen nodded her thanks. It was heavier than it looked. She stood alone on the gravel path in the darkness, breathing deeply one last time, her breath hanging before her in a white cloud. She had few friends, and now there was one fewer.

*It’s always been you and me, Maureen,* Tally said quietly after a moment.

*Yes. But now it’s quieter.*

* * *

The carriage rattled back through the dark to Conta Manor, the chest sitting on the bench beside her. The scent of cedar filled the cramped space and the brass corners of the chest knocked against the panelling every time they hit a rut.

Maureen carried the chest into the east court herself, placing it on the grey flagstones within the circle of silver runes that defined the ward. It wasn’t until she was standing within Gregory’s protections that she let Richard’s face go.

Before Maureen turned the first cover, Tally ran her check. [Active book: none detected.]

And then, for each cover after that, Tally insisted on doing it again.

*It’s from a friend,* she told Tally silently.

*Gregory’s was from a dead man,* Tally responded.

Maureen turned the pages spread by spread through the remainder of the night and into the next day, barely a glance needed for each spread, while Tally kept a running tally in one corner of Maureen’s vision.

The hands were not ones Maureen had seen before: brown ink made with iron gall, blue ink flecked with silver that caught the lamplight as she turned the pages. The margins were packed with notes, scribbled in three different directions. Grey dawn crept over the box hedges and the small oil lamp sputtered out, unnoticed.

*The Silver Soul Hand’s handwriting is even worse than Gregory’s,* Tally complained. *I didn’t think that was possible.*

*Magpie,* Maureen told her silently.

*Library goat. You’ve been chewing on those pages since before dawn.*

The information was laid out along lines that no Passise tome had ever followed. Spell theory and experiments involving bloodlines were organized in a style unique to the Wizard Continent, one that Maureen had never encountered. More than once, she stopped with her finger resting on a margin, an idea half formed.

[New category: Silver Soul Hand: Wizard Continent style.]

By late afternoon, Tally reported, [Silver Soul Hand corpus: ~4,300 spreads. Recorded: 100%. Unopened: 0.]

*Once more?* Maureen asked silently.

Tally ran the check again.

*Zero unopened,* she confirmed.

Maureen reached up, then.

Black-red Purgatory Fire exploded from empty air over the chest.

Chest and books went together in a single breath, without a wisp of smoke.

No ash remained. No charred wooden boards. Not even a trace of brass. The frost melted in a neat circle where the chest had been, leaving the grey flagstones exposed. For a moment, the air smelled of hot cedarwood, then it smelled of nothing at all.

*There’s nothing left to find there,* Tally noted. *I can’t get you anything more. Purgatory leaves none behind.*

*Yes,* Maureen thought back. *Aaron would have wanted it gone too.*

*I don’t have any data regarding Aaron’s desires,* Tally said. *However, I will log that as “likely”.*

Over the next few days, Maureen Olsen did not pay any attention to Denise’s family affairs. With Richard’s face, she walked the large trading houses of the nearby cities, purchasing whatever caught her eye as valuable or unusual. She also sat through one local wizard gathering, coming away relatively unimpressed. After the Dark Forest and the Alliance, she knew far more than any wandering wizard or small-family hopeful sitting on the benches.

*Everything they offered is already on file,* Tally said. *Mostly from the Dark Forest library.*

*That’s the most useful thing to come out of this entire afternoon,* Maureen thought back.

The gathering hall reeked of burning tallow and damp wool. Men haggled over cracked vials and debated the value of a single spell model for an hour. Throughout it all, Maureen sat with her back to the wall, idly counting exits until the “gathering” was done.

* * *

Denise sat at the desk in what had once been her father’s study. A stack of ledgers lay by her elbow, and a beeswax candle burned bright and clean before her. The cuff of her sleeve lay quietly on the desktop, not a flicker to be seen. The tell was gone. It had disappeared some time during the past weeks.

“I have to go, Denise,” Maureen said without sitting down.

Denise blinked, then comprehension dawned. “You’re leaving,” she said. “Back to Emilia, Wizard Richard? Back to the Alliance?”

Maureen nodded.

Tally read Denise’s mouth opening twice, closing twice, her hand rising toward the ledger and falling back down. Tally didn’t have enough data to match the motion with any words, but she told Maureen they would probably translate into a request to stay.

*There is no reason to grant such a request any longer,* Maureen thought silently.

The arithmetic was simple. Maureen had promised one season of protection to Denise, as long as she remained within the walls of Conta Manor. She had gone into the Underworld, and brought Denise back out again. Claude was dead, his family almost gathered into Denise’s hands.

Denise let out a breath. “Then thank you, Wizard Richard,” she said. “For coming to this house. For all of it.”

One corner of Maureen’s mouth pulled up in a tight smile. “It was a deal, Denise. We both kept our ends.”

“Yes,” Denise said, standing. “This is the way the contract was sworn, Wizard Richard. You have done your job here, and I release you from my guard.”

The thin, cold collar around Maureen’s throat loosened. It grew slack, then faded away like breath on glass. [Bindings: 4 → 3.]

*One less collar to wear,* Tally added.

At the same time, three sealed spreads unsealed themselves in Tally’s record of the necromancy and enchantment books. [Puppet gaps covered: 4 of 7 → 7 of 7. Animation core: full, by text.] *Unless the seals are lying,* Tally noted.

Denise reached into her drawer, pulling out a bound volume and sliding it across the table to Maureen. “The advanced alchemy you requested for your services,” she said. “Owed.”

“Paid.”

* * *

That night, the great hall blazed with beeswax candles and silver plate. There were roast fowls and spiced wines, and all the Oserroba family was there, dressed in their best. The steward bowed four times before the meal began. Denise took the seat at the head of the table. The chair beside her was left empty.

*Wizard Richard was toasted seven times during the course of the meal,* Tally noted with satisfaction. *More interestingly, the sum total of mentions of Claude throughout the entire dinner was zero.* [Claude: 0 mentions.]

And not once did Denise’s hands even move toward her sleeve.

*Houses heal in their own way,* Maureen thought.

*Or hide,* Tally replied quietly.

The Oserroba family rose as one to toast Wizard Richard, the guest who had returned their heir alive and well. Every face at the table was turned toward the plain young man seated at Denise’s right hand.

Back in her room, Maureen turned the pages of the new alchemy book spread by spread, letting the light from her lamp play across the parchment. It took her a while, but eventually she had gone through it all.

The next morning, Maureen left Conta Manor in an Oserroba carriage, provided at Denise’s insistence. As the vehicle rumbled away, Denise and the rest of her household stood watching solemnly from the gate. The black crepe that hung from the gateposts had frozen into stiffness during the night, and it rattled slightly in the wind.

The horned beasts at the front of the carriage snorted twin jets of steam from their nostrils, and the thin layer of ice that had formed in the ruts cracked under the wheels. Maureen looked back once before the road bent, catching a final glimpse of Denise standing small at the gate, one hand raised in farewell. The pale stone of the manor rose behind her. Then the road turned again, and the tall hedges swallowed them both.

Tally laid out a blue ribbon over the road, pointing west toward Emilia. A line of text appeared beneath it. [Edward, stowed: lattice scored ~12%. Wants a bench and a week.]

*Emilia has benches,* Maureen thought.

*Then I suppose we should hurry.*
