Maureen walked away from the cave and from the dark elves. The grey cloak brushed against the tops of her boots. With each step she took, the lump beneath the robe knocked against her breastbone. The Seal Stone was hard, as solid as the rounded pebble of a river stone, and it was warm. Warmer than any rock should be.

She pressed her palm flat over it, feeling the heat through the cloth. She had no idea what the Bloodline Seal Stone actually did. All she knew was how the elves had looked at the mouth of that cave, an entire party going white at the sight of a fist-sized lump of veined rock. That told her all she needed to know about its cost.

Underneath her hand, she felt the stone beat, its rhythm slow and even.

[Pulse: 72. Steady.]

Tally noted dutifully. It also pointed out that the stone had been rubbing against the inner pocket where the Seal lived. Maureen shifted the stone to the other side of her robe without breaking stride.

*Two things with opinions sharing one pocket? Not a good idea.*

*Since when does a rock have an opinion?* Maureen asked silently.

*Since this one started beating,* Tally replied. Its voice sounded pleased to have the last word.

Maureen thought about the faces on those dark elves when they found the cave empty, and laughed. The sound was dry, a hoarse rasp, not her voice at all.

"How interesting."

The thought of giving the stone back to them never crossed her mind. Dark elves had no column on any ledger she kept.

The forest began to thin out, and grew quiet. There were no voices, no blasts in the distance, no figures running through the trees ahead. There was only the crunch of her boots against the dead leaf litter and the faintly sweet air sitting at the back of her throat like syrup.

No wonder it was so empty. Aaron and his black-robed people must have swept clean this stretch before continuing onward. Or perhaps it was the pack of apprentices who had passed through here first. Either way, it was quiet.

Tally reported that it didn't see anything moving ahead by sight. It also had no data regarding where Aaron had gone.

*That's kind of the point of Aaron,* Maureen told it silently.

Ahead of her, lying in the shade of a great tree, was a body dressed in a black robe. The robes were of the Dark Forest, its order's mark embroidered at the collar. Blood still ran from a wound in its side, pooling between the gnarled roots that gripped the ground like knuckles. Maureen glanced down at the body and turned away. Another black-robe dead in the trial realm wasn't news.

Then something caught at her.

Maureen stopped, halfway through the turn. There was too much blood. It was too bright against a face that was too pale. The robe hung loose on its shoulders, stiff as a board left forgotten in a cellar for years.

Maureen went back and turned the corpse over with the tip of her Cross Sword. The face was white as plaster, the lips split and cracked like clay at the end of a dry summer. The limbs were locked in place, rigid.

The sword's steel barely touched the forearm and it crumbled away, like a doll left out baking in the sun. There was a dry hiss as a cloud of dust escaped the robe's sleeve. Inside there was no wetness, no stink of rot, as if a thousand years had passed under this tree since morning.

Tally put up:

[Odour: none.]

[Moisture: none (by sight).]

[Blood: wet at wound.]

*The most interesting thing I've seen all day is the contradiction here,* Tally told her. *Moisture present, moisture absent.* In a smaller voice it added, *I do not like this.*

Tally opened a new file, labelled 'dry dead', but there was no data to enter. It hesitated, then added one line:

[Pale wisps of refused points observed drifting away northeast. Possible relation.]

*Possible,* Maureen repeated silently.

*Yes, that is exactly why I said 'possible'.*

A coldness crept up the back of her neck beneath the short, dyed hair. She stood up, keeping the sword held low. The amused smile she'd carried from the cave was gone.

"What on earth happened to you?" she muttered to the pile of dust at her feet. Then her eyes moved slowly across the trees, just like a woman would check a dark room before stepping inside.

* * *

The wind blew steadily against her back, all the tops of the leaves tilted in the same direction. On one side of the wood, the leaves rustled more quietly than on the other. Not the silence of a still forest, but the specific quiet of someone holding their breath.

At the very edge of her sight, a single leaf flipped over against the wind.

> Left, 40°. By sight.

Maureen kept her head aimed straight forward, her eyes darting left and right as they did. Her palm snapped up sideways. "Amra-Keis!" she barked in Jack's low, hoarse rasp.

As the spell began to form, Tally corrected the first syllable, telling her to say it lower and further back in her mouth. A ball of sour green acid spat out from Maureen's hand and hissed as it flew through the air, disappearing into the brush.

Something black intercepted the missile. The acid burst harmlessly apart in a fine spray that steamed as it hit the tree bark, and a small figure tumbled out from the undergrowth. In one fluid motion she rolled to her feet. Freya brushed a fallen leaf off her shoulder.

Maureen recognised her immediately - the girl from the gathering ground. Petite with long black hair, pointed ears and dark leather armour. There was a slender sword at her hip. It was Freya. The girl whose hand Eve had held.

> Alone. By sight. No others detected in trees behind.

Maureen nodded imperceptibly, keeping her eyes locked on the dark elf. Up close Freya's skin was flawless and fair, her features finely drawn. There was no fear in her expression, just cold anger at being discovered. Her black eyebrows drew together into a frown, the only imperfection that Maureen could see on the girl's proud face. She'd been caught by the animal she was hunting and it offended her.

"Human wizard," Freya said in Ancient Andas. Maureen understood every word, without Tally's assistance. "So, you saw me. I did not think your kind had the eyes for it."

"You took the Stone and slunk off like a thief," Freya continued, her voice flat as a death sentence. "For that, you die." There was no preamble this time, no offer, no talk of payment. "Die."

Maureen didn't move her hand towards the pocket. Instead Jack's thin mouth curled up into a cold smile. Beneath the borrowed features her weight shifted back onto her rear foot.

> Blur. Closing. 0.8 s.

Freya turned into a faint smear of shadow just above the leaf litter. Ahead of her, a torrent of dark particles spewed from her hands, streaking forward as luminous black darts. All of them were aimed at Maureen's chest.

"Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa."

Red thorned armour closed over her. The pale eyes on the chest opened, [Seal: 0.0 s]. The vine arms hauled her backwards across the ground in a smooth, unnatural slide. Fine cords of blood-red fibre whipped out from her sleeves and lashed at the elf.

The black streaks raked across the armour's surface, scraping against the thorns with the sound of nails on slate. The taste of sap and iron flooded her mouth, and as she slid backward she noticed how much brighter the Seal's glow was than it had been at the gate. Whatever this realm was doing to her, it was feeding the Seal.

Surprise flickered across Freya's face. "A strong enchanted item!" One breath later and she was composed once more. She reached to her hip and drew a slender cross sword from its sheath. Black light flowed over the metal like oil, the cold cutting deep into the air.

She swung the sword once, a hard slash that sliced the air. The binding vines fell away in neat segments, dropping limply to the leaves.

Tally identified the sword as an attack-type enchanted item, powered by black light. Rare, according to the library catalogue. *It cut her vines.* *I noticed,* Maureen thought back at it.

*Yes, but you should know,* Tally continued, *just in case you were planning on relying on those vines against this one.*

[Seal: 3.8 s.]

> Release.

[Seal: 3.8 s.] > Release.

The red armour unravelled into nothingness, and the dull ache behind her ribs returned, familiar and unwelcome.

The corner of Freya's mouth had only just begun to lift in smug satisfaction when two black eggs arced out from Maureen's bandolier. "Kerrash," she muttered under her breath as the eggs left her hand.

Fire and Hillan shredded through the brush. Heat slapped her cheeks, and a shower of dry leaves fluttered down like dirty snow. A high whine echoed in her ears, and would not go away.

Freya staggered out from the smoke. Her fitted leather armour hung in long strips from her body, her long black hair was wild around her face, and small cuts dotted her pale skin. She wiped her cheek with the back of her hand, then glared at Maureen. "Strong. I'll give you that. But you can't be carrying many of those. Listen, human, I—"

Freya stopped in mid-sentence. Across from her, Maureen Olsen was tossing a third black egg in her own palm, catching it, and tossing it again. The egg arced up and down in her hand with a lazy motion, like a boy playing with a pebble.

"Eh?" Jack's low, hoarse voice asked. "What were you saying?"

Maureen flicked her wrist in a sharp, backhand motion, sending the egg spinning across the space between them. "Kerrash," she repeated, and Freya's expression changed.

"No! Don't—" she began to cry out, but Maureen's aim was true.

The roar came. When the dust settled, Freya was still standing, though there was a smear of blood at the corner of her mouth.

[Grenades: hand 8 / pack 59.]

> Again, before she stands.

Tally set the rhythm for the attacks. Freya opened her mouth, and Maureen gave her a cheerful beckoning wave with two fingers. "Come on. Let's keep going." The fourth egg sailed through the air, and the blast caught Freya full in the chest, throwing her backward into a tree trunk with enough force to shake its crown.

> Now.

While the elf was still coughing, Maureen threw the fifth egg. The force of the explosion slammed Freya off the tree and into the brush.

[Grenades: hand 6 / pack 59.]

*Five on one girl,* Tally noted.

*One elf,* Maureen corrected it silently.

*One elf,* Tally agreed. *One elf with a sword that saved her four times.*

The last explosion rocked every tree around the clearing, sending a deluge of dry leaves down from their branches. The leaves rustled like paper in a high wind, and smoke rose grey into the grey cloud until Maureen could no longer tell where the smoke ended and the clouds began.

Maureen lowered her hand. "That should do," she said quietly. Her voice kept its light tone, but she felt a tightness in her chest. Five shells gone. Thirty-five magic stones of Hillan. Weeks of burned fingertips, all gone in the space of a few breaths, all used up on one person.

She had known who the elf was from the first glimpse she got of her, back when they were so fresh out of the cave. But Freya had come in for the kill without so much as a word about bargaining, and that decided it for Maureen. She didn't have time to waste on people who came to kill her.

* * *

The smoke continued to drift away in bits and pieces, tugged by the wind. It revealed the bare, scorched trunk of the tree, the crater of blackened earth around its base, the shards of Hillan that twinkled among the curled leaves like flakes of mica.

There was no one leaning against the tree.

There was no one in the clearing at all.

Maureen looked down at the back of her hand. The Ancient Andas numeral that represented her points glyph was still unchanged, and no ghostly sensation had tugged at her mind through the connection it represented.

[Points: 9. Unchanged.]

The elf hadn't died here, then. Not by Maureen's hand.

She walked toward the treeline, spotting a spatter of dark purple blood on the carpet of leaves. There was a broken line of droplets leading off into the wood.

Maureen frowned. That many shells should have taken out any apprentice she'd ever met. Her thoughts wandered back to the slender sword and the cold, black light running down it.

Tally replayed what her eyes had recorded. With each explosion there had been a flare of black light around the sword, slicing away the worst of the fire from its owner.

*Those grenades should have been enough,* Tally said. *The sum simply didn't include the sword.*

Maureen's eyes narrowed. "How interesting," she murmured. It was softer than when she'd said it the first time that day.

She crouched down, touching a single fingertip to one of the beads of purple blood. She raised it to her nose.

[Odour: fallen blossoms. New. Saved.]

The smell was sweet and faintly rotten at the same time, like a bouquet of flowers that had been left in a gutter after a rainstorm to be washed around by the run-off. It had no business in this clearing, redolent as it was of scorched resin and hot metal.

[Particles. New. Dense. Dark.]

The residue against her particle sense prickled, and Freya's blood carried the same school as hers, Tally said. *Darkness.*

She unrolled her belt kit and slid one of the clean test tubes out. Cautiously, she scraped the wettest drops off the leaves into the tube, then shoved the stopper home with her thumb.

*You are planning something,* Tally said.

*I am collecting a sample,* she replied silently.

*You are planning something,* Tally said.

Maureen held the tube up to the grey light. The blood inside was a deep, bruised purple, its dark sediment slowly settling to the bottom of the glass. Jack's mouth curved in a smile - the satisfied smile of someone who had just been handed a key.

[Gathering ground. Eve's hand wrapped around the girl's.]

Quietly, Tally reminded her of what had happened at the gathering ground.

*She came to kill me,* Maureen responded silently.

*Yes,* Tally said. It only kept the record.

She tucked the tube back into her kit. The stone continued its steady beating against her ribs, and she turned her back on the trail of blood and walked away into the trees.
