The room smelled of plaster dust and the sweat of the crew leader. Gregory’s runes ran along the doorframe, and they hummed with a faint silver sound. Through them, Joanna and her people laughing in the front court were muted, distant. It sounded like there was a party happening on the other side of a thick sheet of glass.

The burly man whom Maureen had kicked through the wall pushed himself up to sit on the cot. When he spoke, his voice was old and shrill. The words came out of his middle-aged mouth like air forced through a reed whistle, with a long crack running down it. His eyes had become a sickly, glowing green. His joints cracked one after another as he settled into his new body, like a man shrugging into an overcoat that had been cut for someone with narrower shoulders.

“I picked this body at random,” the voice said with annoyance, “just to take a look at this Alliance camp. You’ve beaten it so badly that I can barely keep my own aura from leaking out. Useless. So, I suppose I’ll have to drive it myself.”

*The masked grain you flagged inside him isn’t hiding any more, Maureen,* Tally said. *Now that it’s taken control, it’s spreading out, filling the host like ink dropped in water. But, I don’t recognize it. It doesn’t match any of my samples.*

*All right,* Maureen said silently. *Watch his hands.*

“Isn’t that right, little one?” asked whoever now sat across from her.

“Who are you?” Maureen asked.

The man snorted. It was the snort of an older man, but it rattled in a younger throat. He seemed to enjoy the question. “Fors. Fors Charman.”

The name stuck somewhere in the back of her memory. Like a burr caught in wool, she couldn’t quite pull it free.

Tally said, *I have that name on file, somewhere. Just give me a breath and I can find it for you.*

*Take the breath,* Maureen said silently. *Keep the other half of your mind on those green eyes.*

* * *

Before any light showed, Tally’s cold register interrupted her search. > HIGH charge. Southeast, 35°. One second.

The particle density within the rival leader’s body was folding upon itself, moving toward the man’s hands.

> Strength: above every sample except Wenger—

Maureen was already in the air before she thought anything else. Somewhere deep inside herself, her legs had made their decision all on their own.

A lance of greasy black-green light tore out from the man’s palm, streaking through the space where she had been standing.

The floor caved where Maureen had been standing. Flagstones split beneath her feet and fell into a crater as wide as a cartwheel. The air shook with particles of energy churned by the blast, prickling up Maureen’s arms like static electricity before a lightning storm.

She hit the ground in a crouch, her knees burning with pain, grit clattering between her teeth. The stone dust hung in slow, shifting sheets in the beams of light that shot down from above. The scent of struck flint and singed hair filled the room. Maureen could hear her own pulse thudding in her ears.

Outside the sealed chamber, Joanna’s people continued to laugh. Gregory’s runes swallowed every last bit of the explosive force, not even leaving a ripple. No one in the front court had heard so much as a stone fall.

Tally said, *You jumped before I even finished speaking, Maureen.*

*I didn’t need the rest,* Maureen thought back silently. *Your warning was fast enough. How would you have finished your sentence?*

Tally replied, *I had no second Second-Tier sample to finish it with.*

Then Tally had it: Fors Charman. He appeared on Maffie’s notebook list of the Black Wizard Alliance’s most powerful wizards. Second Tier, notebook hearsay, one source only. She hung the information beside Jorran’s broadcast and the severed head held up by its hair.

Maureen let out a long breath. “Fors Charman,” she said slowly. “One of the Black Wizard Alliance’s few Second-Tier Wizards.”

The man’s green eyes flickered with surprise before they settled into a look of pleased amusement.

*That’s not the end of it,* Maureen thought silently. *The Bloodcoat Guild’s absorption has a Second-Tier of the Black Wizard Alliance behind it. And he’s scouting our own camp!*

*Heads rarely are the end,* Tally agreed.

“Only now?” Fors laughed. “Brewing must be very hard work if that is all it takes to slow you down. Isn’t that so, genius apothecary?” He paused for effect, savouring his words, then let them loose. “Mister Richard.”

Maureen’s stomach dropped, and for a heartbeat her fingers turned cold on the grip of Freya’s sword. The stone dust in her mouth tasted of chalk.

“The disguise is very good, Mister Richard,” Fors said. “It would fool even the white robes, and most black wizards. Not me, though. I can smell the blood on you. It’s too thick, Richard. It’s soaked into your bones. You’ll never wash it out.”

Tally separated what Fors had seen from what he had guessed. He was reading her grain and the blood-family resonance, but not her face. He had not used any other name. *Does he know who I was?* Maureen asked silently.

*No sign,* Tally answered. *Men like this use what they have.*

The out-breath came back—three long and one short—and with it came the ledger. “Say what you want, Fors.” Her voice was as flat as Richard’s always was.

“Very sensible, Mister Richard. Very sensible. What I want is irrelevant. What you should choose is more important. Leave the Alliance. Come home, back to the Black Wizard Alliance. You find the white robes sickening, don’t you? Deep down, that is. Yes . . . I thought so.” Then he added almost as an afterthought, “If you were not a black wizard, I would already be through with you.”

The room became very quiet. Even the stone dust seemed to stop falling. Tally laid out what she read:

> Projection density drop: 31%. Particle sense. Second-Tier by notebook hearsay; Official-grade host. Host body under significant strain.

> Blasts left at that strength: 1-2

> Confidence: medium

Maureen smiled. “You’re not sparing me because I’m a black wizard, Fors. You can’t afford another one. You’re just a projection in a borrowed Official’s body. How many of those can it carry? Two?”

*You’re welcome.*

Tally said it quietly in her head. The number Maureen had just thrown at Fors had come from Tally.

*I said it better.*

Maureen shot back silently, and Tally conceded the delivery, but kept the number as her own.

One of Fors Charman’s eyebrows lifted. “Sharp eyes, for an ordinary Phantom. Interesting.”

Then the air cracked.

Dark particles gathered around Fors, thickening into tentacles that gleamed like cold steel chains. There was no metal in them, but they still clinked as they swayed. The sound was like ice settling onto a pond, and the cold from the chains pressed against Maureen’s cheeks.

*Route it through the Orb.*

Maureen ordered Tally silently.

Tally obliged. The Pankora heat was dampened so much by the time it reached Maureen that only raw pressure emerged. There were no serpents in this conflagration. *Good thing you kept it so far back, Maureen.*

[Orb routing: −0.3.]

Maureen didn’t move a muscle. But her aura did. A faint whirlwind of black spun around her, lifting the hem of her cloak and sucking the dust out of the crater in a slow spiral. The two pressures met in the middle of the room, grinding against each other like millstones turning dry. Maureen could feel the force of it in her back teeth.

*Don’t tell me I’m winning. Show me.*

She told Tally silently, and two bars appeared over the room, Fors Charman’s chains against her whirlwind. Maureen’s bar crept up until it was a finger’s width past his, and held there.

That was more satisfying than it should have been. She could feel the corner of Richard’s mouth trying to curl upward, and she let it do so. Just a bit.

* * *

*It’s time we spoke,* she said silently.

*You’re going to try and sell the people who pay your stipend?* Tally asked.

*No. I’m going to try and sell what they’d sell me for if they knew,* Maureen replied.

*Same thing, really.*

*Fine. Logged. I’ll keep the ledger, not the conscience.*

*Keep them both.*

*Fine. Both columns it is.*

“You’re right about one thing,” Maureen said aloud. “I’m a black wizard. Some things don’t change.”

Fors Charman’s chains loosened slightly as the spirit studied her. He looked like a man waiting to see if a door would swing open in his direction.

“Join you?” Maureen asked. “I’m sorry, Fors. I have no such plan. But we can work together.”

Maureen raised a single finger. She would provide him with whatever the Alliance knew: orders, movements, strategies. The Black Wizard Alliance would provide resources of equal value in return.

“An equal exchange,” she said. “That seems fair, doesn’t it?”

Fors Charman did not answer immediately. The green light in the eyes of his host body flickered like a lamp in a draft, while somewhere underneath the borrowed face a jaw worked as though tasting the offer.

Then he laughed. The high-pitched cackle bounced off the stone walls.

“Fair? Yes, very fair. Done.”

*We should draw up a standard contract, not a Styx Pact,* Tally said. *Nightingale claimed that the river is lined with those who broke their word. You yourself refused to form one back at that stone table.*

“Let’s draw up a contract, then, Fors,” Maureen said. “A standard contract. Not a Styx Pact.”

Again, Fors did not hesitate. Tally flagged the exchange:

> Accepted instantly.
>
> Reason: no data.

*Perhaps he desires something breakable as well,* Tally guessed with low confidence.

Maureen nodded silently. *Two of us, then.*

The scroll burned in empty air, red flame without heat, and on either side of its flame, two thin brands of light lifted from the brows of wizard and spirit. The paper fell to nothing, leaving no ashes upon the flagstones.

Another thread of cold settled at Maureen’s collarbone, joining the old ones, and Tally logged the event.

> Bindings: 3 → 4

“And now I’ll give you my own brand, Richard,” Fors said, “so that I can call drops or come to you directly.”

He held up the hand of his host and the faint lines of a dark mark spun within the palm.

*Daniel’s brand was like a leash,* Tally objected. *It led us into the pines.*

Maureen nodded her agreement to Tally without speaking. *Let me read it before anything touches my skin.*

Under the lens of the Mirror Pupil the lines became text.

> Structure: projection anchor.
>
> Directionality: one way, him to you.
>
> Locating knot: none visible.
>
> Read limit: what’s in view.

*That’s as far as I can read a Second-Tier’s handwriting,* Tally added.

Maureen nodded and extended her forearm. The brand sank in with a chill like burnt wine. Tally added it to the brands ledger.

The spirit looked her over with an open air of approval. “Black to the bone, Richard. And really, not bad at all. Not bad in the least.”

Maureen let the compliments pass her by, as though they were weather.

She nodded toward the body Fors inhabited. *He still has command of his own mind. Will he remember any of this?*

Fors only smiled. “Leave that to me. I’ll handle it.”

*You didn’t ask him how, Maureen.*

*No, I didn’t.*

Tally logged the silence. She said nothing else about the matter.

* * *

Maureen Olsen came out first. The leader followed a step behind. By the time the two reached the front court, the green was gone from the man’s eyes. They were a muddy brown again.

To Joanna, Maureen gave the short version. “He tried casting on me in there. I handled it. The floor might need relaying when you’re done with the room, but...” She shrugged. “Nothing that concerns the Ring now.”

Tally double-checked Maureen’s story against what Joanna had seen: the kick, the wall, Richard hauling the man inside. It checked out. Joanna’s pulse rate fell from 104 to 88 as she listened to Maureen. That was relief, not proof.

The rival crew woke up where they’d fallen. They rubbed their sleeves where they’d been scorched, and their cracked lips. One kept spitting out a mouthful of grit onto the frost. None of them would look at Richard for more than a breath, and when they did, it was with bloodshot, sore eyes.

“Gather your things,” the leader told them. His voice sounded like his own again—gruff and deep. It was a fair imitation. “We’re going.” The crew stared at him. An hour ago he had been ready to tear the place down.

“Going?” the apprentice demanded, rounding on his leader. “We’re just leaving? Over the Jadeleaf Ring? They don’t even count in the Alliance! Nobody would blink if—”

He stopped in mid-word. The leader hadn’t moved a muscle, except to turn his head. But the young wizard’s face lost all color, and he clapped his mouth shut. Then he took a step back into line.

*I felt that too,* Tally said silently. *A thread of that same chain-cold. Gone in a heartbeat.*

*He won’t be asking again,* Maureen replied silently.

The green leaf banners stirred gently on their poles. Somewhere behind the kitchens, a pot boiled over with a sudden hiss of steam. Joanna let out a long breath, seeming to realize only then that she’d been holding it.

*Party: 7,* Tally said. Counting them through the side gate. Maureen stood quietly, hands loose at her sides, while Tally followed the densities of the rival crew along the camp road.

At three hundred metres—the very edge of her range— Tally reported, *Seven turn off camp road. Enter side path. Trees.*

And then, nothing. No data.

Maureen didn’t ask Tally to guess.

She remained standing in the court until the cookfire smoke had changed direction twice, feeling the sweet air of the realm heavy in her lungs, and feeling the new brand on her forearm, still cold beneath her sleeve.
