The giant lizard head was only an arm's length away, close enough to smell the wet stone. Hugh had stepped down off the crag in one leap, a heavy landing that had shaken loose a sprinkling of grit. Now it crouched over the girl, its huge steel trident resting against its shoulder. Maureen tipped her head back, craning her neck to look up at those crimson eyes, and said quietly, "Lord Hugh. It's been a long time."

In her sleeve, her fingers still gripped the stoppered test tube, feeling the warmth of the glass from her hand. One more step, and she would have tried the brew she made to slip past the patrols. Her face felt stiff, as though the cold had set it.

*You are in the presence of the great Lord Hugh,* Tally said in its stuffy formal register.

*Of all the welcomes I have on file, this is the largest,* it added, switching to its own voice.

Up close, Hugh was an imposing wall of grey stone, darkened by the damp so it looked almost black. Its eyes were the size of shield bosses, and the huge steel trident rested over its shoulder like a soldier's spear. A scarlet tongue snaked out, the end of it flipping over to leave a string of spit that fell to the ground with a hiss, steaming as it burned away the dew.

"Ho-ho! H-ho-ho!" The statue bent lower, its stony nostrils working, drawing in the scent of the air. "An apprentice, all by herself. Lucky m-me." Its eyes narrowed, and it smiled, showing rows of sharp teeth. "Adrian's l-little one, yes? I remember. Interesting little one."

Maureen gave it a small smile, and nothing else. Nothing else was safe. The statue's tongue snaked out, flicking the air next to her ear. "You d-dared come back, eh? Brave. Or s-stupid. I like both."

The statue fell into step beside her. Its legs were long enough that it could match her pace with only half steps of its own. It didn't stop talking, not even for a moment, and the stammering words rolled out like the peals of a bell.

"There is war, little one. With the other orders. The woods are watched. F-full wizards walk the patrols, not one or two, but m-many. A net, little one. Over everything."

Maureen's shoulders drew up under the straps. The padded packs full of grenades seemed to pull at her back even more, and the cloth-wrapped trunk of Sodara thumped painfully against her spine with every step. Suddenly, she was sure that a patrol would want to open those packs.

*Lord Hugh said full wizards,* Tally noted. *Plural. My alert bands only go to high. I do not like a net I cannot count.*

She felt at her sleeve. How much did she have left?

*It has never been tested on full wizards,* Tally told her.

*I know,* Maureen thought silently. *It hasn't been tested on anything else either. I'm not going to test it on the people who will hang me for trying.*

*Good. Keep it.*

"N-nobody wants trouble at home," Hugh said. Then the statue's voice dropped in pitch and slowed its stammering rhythm. "But how this w-war ends... how many wizards d-die in it... nobody knows. Nobody."

The sigh seemed to come from deep within the stone, as if some great millstone were settling into place. For just a moment, the great stone thing didn't seem to be something made by wizardry. It sounded like an old soldier, one who had buried too many friends already, and knew he'd bury more before the end.

*Hugh is sad about the war,* Tally said quietly. *I did not know statues could be sad.*

*Probably didn't know it themselves,* Maureen answered silently.

Now she saw things clearly. Slipping back into the Dark Forest without being noticed had never really been a plan. In times of war, there would be no gaps for a little powder to fill. *Is it really this far gone already?*

The air pressed down upon her, the great lid of black cloud overhead seeming to weigh against her chest. Her breath settled into Silver Light without her even asking, three long, one short, and her fingertips found her sternum through her coat.

As the path branched toward the castle, Hugh swung off to continue its patrol. The huge steel trident flashed with dull light as it moved. "Don't g-get eaten, interesting little one. Ho-ho!"

The ground shook with every step the statue took until the dense black trees swallowed the sound.

*I will log the bearing it takes,* Tally said. *I will keep it, in case you ever need to know which way the great Lord Hugh walked.*

* * *

Maureen stopped in front of the gate she knew. One long breath escaped her lips, condensing into a puff of white smoke in the cold air. She began to walk towards the gate at an ordinary pace, as if she were just another apprentice returning home from a task.

Hugh's and Iman's places were empty. In their stead, two new guards stood vigilant watch over the Dark Forest's gate. They towered over everything else within sight, taller even than Hugh and Iman had been. The new constructs were made of steel plate bolted over grey stone, the rivets and seams beaded with moisture. The entire mass gave off a smell of cold iron and wet mortar.

Two points of red light flared to life in the eyes of each construct as Maureen approached. A voice rang out from deep inside the constructs' chests, flat and emotionless as the sound of a struck anvil. "Who."

*No match to Hugh or Iman,* Tally said. *New faces. Nothing on file. No established habits to read. Please stand still. Allow me to watch their eyes.*

"An apprentice of the Dark Forest," Maureen said, unhurried. "Back from a mission."

No one asked for the old password. During times of war, the brand upon an apprentice's wrist had taken its place.

*First syllable, keep the tongue low and back,* Tally reminded her. *Elevate.*

"Elevate," Maureen said softly.

Adrian's brand stirred beneath her skin.

The sigil rose from her wrist, as black as the castle walls and the cloud overhead. It was not the crisp lines of an ink drawing or the clean edges of a lead pencil. This sigil was too alive. Its lines curled like ink not yet dry, still deciding what shape they would take. The sigil gave off a chill that spoke of cellars and root vegetables, buried deep in the ground. Each time she saw it, the sigil looked just a little bit more real.

Red light poured forth from the constructs' eyes, washing over Maureen's wrist. The light crawled over the sigil, tracing each line individually. She held her arm out, keeping the rest of her body perfectly still.

Tally spoke in her head, counting silently for two point one seconds. *Red light. Two point one seconds. You have passed.*

*You may breathe now.*

*I have been breathing, thank you very much.*

*Barely.*

"Enter."

The red light blinked out of the constructs' eyes. They stood completely still, as still as any carving in stone. They might never have spoken at all. The sigil retreated back into Maureen's wrist.

Inside the gate, little had changed since her last visit. The same black flagstones lay beneath her feet, beaded with moisture from the air. The same incense hung heavy on the air, laying a sweet cover over the scent of rot and damp. The same cold radiated from the black stone walls and floors, chilling the soles of her boots.

But the courtyard was crowded. Full wizards and apprentices crossed from one end of the courtyard to the other with purposeful strides, faces drawn and grim. No one lingered in the open air, no one stopped to speak with another. Tally counted forty-odd robes that she saw in the span of a single minute, and it noted the alerts that had gone off. Five high-band sources had crossed the courtyard in that minute. Normally there was only one. Everyone was home.

Robe hems slapped against the wet stones. Somewhere in the distance, a door boomed open and shut again, muffling a conversation of low voices. The war was coming, and it hung over every wizard like the eternal cloud over the castle. The courtyard was not just cold from the stone. There was a gloom here, and none could escape it by stepping out from under it.

Maureen did not turn towards her dormitory. She turned instead, heading in the direction of Adrian's laboratory, and Tally counted the turns of the corridor as it always did.

* * *

She knocked on his door, and it opened to reveal Adrian alone. A pair of scissors was in his hand, and he was clipping away at a potted plant that stood upon the bench, a curling mass of black mist around it. He looked up to her, and she bowed where she was, standing in the doorway.

“Mentor. I'm back.”

There was a snip from the scissors, loud in the quiet room. Black mist curled around the blades as they cut, sliding away from the leaves that fell from the plant. Otherwise, if one ignored the potted plant and its curling cloak, Adrian was simply an old man fussing over a plant upon his windowsill.

A burner sat upon the bench ticking away like a clock in the kitchen. The scent of glassware and reagent filled the room, the scent of Jing Sheng's white bench. Something tight in Maureen's chest let go by another notch before she even realised it.

Adrian put the scissors down and smiled. “Olsen. You're back. Back, then.” He glanced over her for a moment. “Now, of all times. I can't tell whether that's your luck or your bad luck.”

His eyes moved over her, like they were reading her aura. They stayed there, as if he had seen something interesting. “You've come a long way, Olsen. How long has it been? And you're already Third-Class.”

*Noted,* Tally said. *Directly from aura. No more 'Not yet, Mentor.' Three cover stories ready.*

*Which is best?*

*Story two.*

*But he's not asking...*

The stories were ready on Maureen's tongue, constructed from the bits she had pulled out of Gregory's realm. Each one seamless, ready to be told. Her mouth felt a little dry around them.

Then she met his eyes, peering at her over the top of his glasses. They were mild, unhurried and calm. All three stories dissipated, leaving her tongue dry and useless.

He'd always let his apprentices keep their secrets. He was letting her keep these.

*Rank withdrawn from withheld list,* Tally noted. *Patton, tree, the Seal, and stones remain.*

*He let you keep those too.*

*This merits a column of its own. Again.*

She bowed her head just a fraction. “The Dark Forest called, Mentor. Of course I came back.”

Adrian pushed his glasses up and huffed. “You. I tell you the truth about wizards, and now you come home. It’s for an advanced meditation method, isn’t it?”

She didn’t say anything, letting that stand. It was a convenient lie, meant with kindness, the same kind she had let him believe about the Olivias. Besides, it was half true.

His grey eyebrows drew together as if he was already working on the problem for her benefit. “A good idea. Just not realistic. They’re very hard to come by. You...” He trailed off, kicking a stool towards her.

Maureen sat on it. Adrian picked up his scissors again, removing a single dead leaf from the plant. The curling black mist around it sighed as he cut. The silence between them was almost like the old days.

Then he stopped, shaking his head. “Old. I’m really getting old, to forget a thing like this.”

Maureen froze. “Mentor? Forget what?”

Tally flagged the pause, the moment before he answered. His hand had stopped with the scissors and hadn’t moved since.

“The Dark Forest, the White Bone Tower, the Mist Labyrinth, the Blue Ring, Hader Castle, the underground races, dark elves among them. They’ve all agreed. Every apprentice goes into a secret realm, for a trial.”

>Enemies and allies are listed together. Opening files on the Dark Forest, the White Bone Tower, the Mist Labyrinth, the Blue Ring, Hader Castle, the underground races, dark elves...

Adrian set the scissors down. “The point is simple. One word. Kill. Kill the apprentices of the other side.”

Maureen’s stomach fell. Her hands fell flat on her knees without being told to do so. The tick of the burner became the loudest thing in the room.

She looked up at him. “Mentor, why?”

Something flickered across Adrian’s face before it settled down into an expressionless mask. “Some reasons are not for you to know. Not yet.” He let out a long slow breath, like a bellows deflating. “Many powers sat down and argued. This is what came out. Don’t ask any more questions, Olsen.”

>Mentor’s expression noted. Jaw set, eyes down 0.6 seconds. He knows reason, but will not give it.

*I can ask,* Maureen thought back at it.

*You would receive either a lie or silence.*

“I don’t want to go,” Maureen said without thinking about it.

White robes, underground races, they were enemies, all of them. The black orders listed in the file might be allies by agreement, but that didn’t mean much. She was a black-robe herself, she knew how it worked. You could work with a black wizard. You could never trust one. Their dictionary didn’t even have a word for ally.

The grey light slanted down from the high window, illuminating the racks of glassware on the long stone bench. It did nothing to warm the polished brass or the cold stone. The chemical smell seemed sharper than before. The curling black mist surrounding the potted plant continued its slow dance, as patient as ever.

Adrian looked down at her and Maureen couldn’t read his expression. “I do not desire you to go either, Olsen.” His calm voice was so flat it sounded almost emotionless. “But you cannot refuse. Strong apprentice or weak, it makes no difference. They all go.”

*Damn.*

Her heart sank like a stone tossed into a well. Her face took on a hard set before she had time to soften her expression. Her hands stayed where they were, lying flat on her knees.

For a long moment Tally didn’t say anything.

Then, in a voice that was softer than usual, it spoke. *I am here. We will make a plan. We always make a plan.*
