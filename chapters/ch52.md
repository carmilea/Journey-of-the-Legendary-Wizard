"I will make the arrangements immediately," the nobleman said. He bowed himself out of the room, eager to please her. It was still morning, so it was possible he could arrange the banquet for that evening.

He provided her with his best guest room and every comfort she could desire. He even offered her the use of several of his attendants for her rooms. She refused, though, and that was final.

One door, two windows, one chimney too narrow to climb, Tally noted for her.

That will do, Maureen replied silently.

Old Hank, Jess, Bram, and Marla were provided with comfortable quarters off the servants' wing as well, though that was a courtesy extended only for Maureen's benefit. The slave warriors were more than used to roughing it in far less comfortable surroundings.

Maureen sat by the fire, pondering. The split pine snapped occasionally, sending up a shower of sparks, while outside the frost ferns crept up the window panes. The smell of the beeswax candles reminded her of the Skran duke's house where she had once been a serving girl.

Hank and the three slaves will eat better tonight than you did your first few months in the Dark Forest, Tally observed.

They'll need it, Maureen responded silently.

Dusk came early in Wintercold City, the sun setting in a blue blaze across the snow-covered rooftops. The first carriage wheels creaked into the courtyard below.

From the tall window overlooking the courtyard, Maureen watched as lords and ladies dressed in fur trimmed robes and gilt edged cloaks stepped carefully down into the lighted snowfall. The music drifted up from the banquet hall, rising through the floorboards, warming them beneath her feet. Her host served her a warm cup of milk and honey tea. A gift from the royal family of the principality, it was only now and then that the Marquis was able to acquire a small portion of the tribute.

Taste is data, Tally said, making its case for the honey.

For once, Maureen agreed with Tally silently.

I know. I'm trying some right now, she thought back, before taking a sip. The honey sweetness exploded across her tongue, the thick horse milk coating the roof of her mouth. She swallowed with a smile. It was good.

"Yes, you're right, this is very good," she told him, setting down the cup.

Maureen's jaw tightened though her expression remained pleasant. Anyone still serious about the wizard's path wouldn't be found in Wintercold City.

"You could suffer permanent damage from a spell with just one slip," the fat apprentice warned. "Even die."

"I'm aware of the danger," Maureen assured him. "But if you will give me a full account of the fighting ability of this apprentice I shall consider it worth my while." The four Mystics exchanged looks before nodding their agreement. Maureen would submit the bill to Umas.

As Maureen questioned them Tally noted the gaps in each Mystic's account, checking and cross-checking the four different versions against each other. When Maureen came to choose which gaps to explore further, Tally had the information at hand.

The candles had burned down the width of a finger by the time she was finished. The fat apprentice was wheezing with every breath and the tea in her cup had gone cold. Maureen paid them for their time and let them leave. As they reached the door, the fat apprentice turned back to her.

"My friend," he said earnestly, "I urge you not to be too rash. That wizard apprentice is called Kane."
