The dormitory door shut with a quiet click behind Maureen as she exhaled and placed George's combined spells tome on her desk. The hard cover was cool beneath the flat of her fingers, the strange patterns and designs seeming to glow slightly in the yellow light of the oil lamp. Tally read out the first page's incantation, recorded when she was in George's stall back in the old skim. She began to speak the first word when Tally corrected her tongue down, like Scarlette instructed. "Iwen-Monas," she said and the cover seemed brighter. The book lifted from the desktop and hovered in the air. It began turning its own pages slowly.

Maureen leaned forwards to study the pages, the text danced before her eyes. As she studied the words, her eyelids grew heavy, threatening to close. The page blurred, swaying slightly as though she was seeing the reflection of the words through some semi-transparent gauze.

The room changed, breaking up into blocks of faded grey-white. It looked like an old photograph, as though centuries had passed since she started reading. She heard the laughter of children, somewhere out of sight. She was alone.

She could not move. Strength was draining out of her limbs. Her arms dropped limply to the desk, the hands that rested there were thin, wrinkled and spotted. Old woman's hands.

There was no cold alert. Tally's page counter kept ticking over in the edge of her vision, recording the pages that the book turned. The room was still a dull grey, but Tally was registering it as the room.

A book on small human arms and legs hopped across the flagstones, its middle split by a fanged mouth, a red tongue dripping drool from between sharp teeth. The book lunged and bit her head.

Darkness.

Maureen sat bolt upright, cursing furiously. Nothing had changed. The desk was where it always was, the brewing bench ran along the wall as ever, the bed was in place and the oil lamp still cast its yellow light down on the black stone desktop. The book was still open at the same page. But her head throbbed, her stomach churned and her hands were young again.

[Unknown force field. Triggered at 19:04 of reading. Mild concussion. Spirit disturbed.]

And then Tally's voice came again, quieter this time.

[I didn't see it. I only saw what you saw.]

I know, Maureen thought. No one would have.

[I'm not no one. This changes how I watch books.]

It made sense, Maureen realised. At the nineteen minute mark the explanation had become very detailed, delving into areas that even most apprentices wouldn't be able to follow. After that it had switched to cipher-script. A small trigger array had been sitting at the join. That was why George could never sell it.

[That book with the fangs looked like the mouth in Adrian's study, the one that swallowed the burning book. No data on any connection. Filed under books that eat.]

The fanged book wasn't there any more. Maureen carried on reading and nothing triggered the array. Tally made her turn every page, including the cipher pages, until [Full copy: 100%] appeared, then Tally loaded the sorted basics into her memory. Everything was clear and ordered now, so easy to understand.

[Decryption task: ETA 98h.]

[Totems and line patterns: unanalysable. Filed: Unknown.]

[P(combined spell) now: 12%. After decryption: 87%.]

Maureen remembered that 87% before she'd made her first potion.

Lucky, huh? Tally said.

I don't believe in luck, Maureen thought back.

Well I believe in 87, Tally said.

I'll wait, then, Maureen decided.

* * *

Maureen spent the next few days going from Adrian's lab to her dorm, to the library, and meditating in any spare hour she had. Third-Class was going to need twelve more Spirit Runes and a third Rune Array before she would be ready for it. Tally's [Third array: 2/12] remained stubbornly at two day after day. It didn't say anything else about the arrays, but she noticed its silence.

"Maureen." Across the long table Lilith blinked her blue crystal eyes at her through the smoke of tallow candles that hung from the ceiling. "Why have you been so busy lately?"

The girl reached over with a plate. "This is honey cake, made by a royal master chef of the Principality of Chez. Try some."

"Yes, thank you." Tally noted that Lilith had sought Maureen out four times already this fortnight. The apprentice's eyes were focused on Maureen's face, rather than the plate of cake.

Log that as unconfirmed. Tally. Maureen thought silently.

Maureen took a bite of the cake. It was dense and crumbly, but the honey was soft and warm, melting on her tongue. The smell of the sweet treat pierced the smoke of the dining hall. She opened her mouth to tell Lilith how good the cake was.

[Decryption complete.]

Tally interrupted. Maureen's eyes widened as she told Tally to store the information in her memory. She rose from the table, the wooden chair legs scraping against the stone floor.

"I'm sorry, Lilith, I have something I need to do." Maureen hurried away without waiting for an answer, leaving half a bite of the cake on her plate.

You didn't tell Lilith the cake was good, Tally said when they were back in the corridor.

Later.

You always say that.

Later!

Fine. Tally kept the image of Lilith's face as Maureen had left it on file, but did not display it to Maureen. After a second, Maureen silently ordered Tally to begin gathering all relevant combined spell data, then deduce new models for combined spells. Tally began its task.

* * *

Maureen sat at her desk for several days, reading the notes Tally had sorted out, until finally her expression cleared. So that was how it worked. Maureen immediately headed for the Trading District.

The shop smelt of old paper and lamp oil, and behind the dusty glass window the grey-haired old man looked like he was half asleep, idly yawning as he sat on a stool.

"I need some spell models," Maureen said, drawing the man's attention. He glanced at her, then tossed a thick green catalogue across the counter with a grunt. "Choose yourself."

Tally began ranking the models against the combinations it had deduced, and pushed Mantik Death-Aura Field to the top of Maureen's thoughts, to pair with Hand of Negative Energy. She bought that one, and two cheaper backups, using Jay's card. [Card: 1,000 → 950]

It took another few days for Tally to analyse the models, before they were ready to be used. During the nights, Maureen had Tally transfer the new models into her mind. Then she tapped on the black slab of the Testing Area. The middle-aged woman sitting behind it already had an impatient expression on her face.

"I have some models I want to try," Maureen said.

"You have to pay-"

Maureen interrupted her, tossing a bulging black bag of stones in her hand. "I have stones. Plenty of them. I just want a room." The clerk hesitated, but eventually passed over a key. "Room twenty-one."

[Magic stones: −24]

A full day paid in advance.

That was a bit of showing off, Tally told her.

That was kind of the point.

Maureen walked into the large Room 21, and spoke the word, "Famir", activating the construct. The construct knight in black plate armour formed in front of the wall of flowing black light.

Dark particles gathered above Maureen's hand, forming a glove that covered her fingers and wrist. It felt cold, and there was the barest hint of weight to it. The air smelled of old scorching.

Tally began running the combined spell model on Maureen's display, alongside what she was doing. > Hand first. Field wraps it. Half a beat later.

Maureen ignored the timing recommended by Tally, and instead released the Field before the Hand spell finished forming. The Field tore itself apart as soon as it was unleashed, while her Hand collapsed back into a stream of grey smoke. The construct remained untouched by the failed attack.

Tally laid its own timing line over Maureen's display, but said nothing.

Say it.

I won't.

A breath later Tally's voice spoke up again, using the same warm tone. Half a beat.

Hours passed. Maureen sat cross-legged on the floor of black stone between each attempt, waiting for the Mana counter on her display to replenish. The construct always reformed after being hit with a spell.

Late in the day, the Hand closed around the construct while the Field wrapped around it on the beat. A small tornado of howling darkness appeared in the room, black light tearing at the plate armour of the construct.

"Combined energy attack detected," the cold female voice of the room spoke aloud. "5.9 degrees."

You have competition again. How about Coronal Negative-Energy Vortex Synthesis? Tally suggested, using its warm voice again.

Dark Wind Ring.

Basic all over again. You need a powdered focus for the full form. You can brew it on your bench before you leave.

Maureen's hands were hot from the repeated use of spells, and they tingled from the energy running through her palms as she shut Room 21's door almost a full day after she had opened it. "It worked." She whispered to herself as she walked out. It was another trump card to pull out, a second one now beside the Blood Seal of Thorns. This one she could use in the open, and it would only grow stronger once she reached Third-Class.

* * *

Maureen sat at her black-stone desk. The lamp was lit and the three model books were stacked by her elbow. How long until Third-Class at this rate?

[Rune Array (third): est. 5 years, 8 months, 9 days.]

Maureen went still. Her fingers curled around the edge of the desk and her knuckles turned white.

Is there a faster way?

No data, Tally admitted. Its database was not comprehensive enough to make any useful guesses past this point. The library said that Mox Fusion Stone was useless for the final twelve runes.

"Shit."

The last twelve runes were riding on aptitude alone. That was why wizard orders valued high aptitude so much. If Maureen stayed here, it would take her almost six years to reach Third-Class.

But I was wrong by three months last time. Tally pointed out gently. Isn't it nice to be wrong sometimes?

That's not funny.

I wasn't joking.

Maureen stared at the ceiling as she made up her mind.

Maureen stood up from her desk. She had made her decision. It was time to travel again. Two years here had given her everything that she could possibly gain as a Second-Class, and she wasn't about to sit around in this black stone room for another day. The Blood Seal of Thorns and the Dark Wind Ring gave her enough nerve to leave. She left her study to go ask Adrian for permission to travel again.

* * *

The same evening, the Central District Hall.

Her footsteps echoed in the high black-stone vault above her. The air was chilled and tasted of lamp smoke.

Eleven black robes moved to block her path.

"Who are you?" Maureen asked, her voice echoing.

"Don't you know who we are, Maureen?" snarled a voice from within the robes. There was fear and hatred in the man's tone.

[11, by sight] Tally counted. [Wolf, Jay] Her chip identified Jay and his follower. Wolf, the burly man with the yellow beard, was crouched slightly back on his heels like a man ready to step away from her. Jay, with his flaxen hair, looked healed now. His face twisted into a mask of venom.

"You are the Olivia Family," Maureen said evenly.

The tall young man at the centre of the group smiled mildly. He was a head taller than the rest of his people and had bright golden hair. "Did you think that putting a secrecy contract on Jay would stop us from finding out what happened? We are not so easily fooled, Maureen. Some things are not yours to do."

He spoke with the same pleasant tone throughout. His smile never changed, even when Jay's rose water floated into the cold air beside him.

"Ah, forgive my manners," the golden-haired young man said as if suddenly remembering his social duty. "I am Frey, Jay's elder brother. Frey Olivia."

Maureen nodded.

Maureen stood still as her fingers moved towards the hilt of her sword. Tally noted that every black robe in the hall would be able to see this fight. [I have no data on the rules of combat in this castle.]

I know, Maureen thought back silently.

Then... smile, Tally suggested.

Maureen didn't say anything, but the corner of her mouth turned up slightly in a cold, calm smile. Her hand settled down onto the hilt of the Cross Sword and curled firmly around it.
