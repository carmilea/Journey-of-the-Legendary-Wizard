Jing Sheng's head seemed to be splitting open.

Her last memory had been the explosion of her plane, a fireball that no one could possibly have survived.

There was no fire here, no screams of terror or agony.

The light was grey and watery, the air filled with the reek of coal smoke and boiled cabbage. Beneath her thin shoes she could feel the thick, wet mud. She stood in front of a low, crumbling hut made from wooden planks, surrounded by men and women, all blond and blue eyed, dressed in patched clothes of grey wool. A body lay on the hard packed ground in front of her, an old man wearing tattered clothing and his tangled hair a matted grey. Flies were beginning to land on the corpse.

"Such a shame," murmured one of the neighbours, a middle aged woman with strands of blond hair escaping from beneath her patched shawl. "Poor Maureen Olsen."

"What do you think happened to Old John?"

"I don't know."

"What's poor Maureen going to do now?"

No one answered.

Maureen Olsen heard the language being spoken around her and knew it was not one she'd ever heard before. But for some reason she was able to understand it perfectly, every word crystal clear. She absorbed the fact, filing it away like an anomalous bit of data in an otherwise normal experiment.

Then came another wave of pain, accompanied by broken images flashing through her mind. She staggered back, grabbing at the doorframe for support, feeling splinters digging into her palm.

"Child!" The woman caught her arm, steadying her. "Are you all right?"

Jing Sheng looked up into the woman's face and saw that it was both familiar and strange. "I'm... better now, Aunt Mary," she said with effort, forcing a smile.

She could hear Old John calling her name, his voice cracking with age. She could see a maid's apron in what appeared to be a manor house cellar.

It was coming together now. She had somehow wound up on another world, one that seemed similar to Earth during medieval times, but where people possessed mysterious powers. She had crossed over, to this place and to this body.

The body of a girl named Maureen, age 14, who had been orphaned and taken in by Old John. She had taken his surname, becoming Maureen Olsen.

Maureen Olsen. The name echoed through Jing Sheng's mind.

She had died and Old John had died as well. But the girl Maureen had only just died herself, overcome with grief. And in her place, Jing Sheng had come awake.

* * *

The girl looked at the ragged old corpse, and felt waves of grief wash over her. It wasn't her own grief, but the body's natural reaction to losing its adoptive grandfather. The sorrow welled up inside, causing Maureen's throat to close, her eyes to sting with tears. She could feel it, and she knew what it meant, just as if it were a symptom of some illness. That was far more disturbing than seeing the corpse on the dirt. "There now," Aunt Mary cooed soothingly. "It's all right now, Maureen. It's over."

The blacksmith came over as well, and gently patted the blond haired girl's head, ruffling her hair. "Don't cry like that, little Maureen," he rumbled. "Be brave."

Maureen looked up into the blacksmith's face, his big hands smelling of soot from the forge and iron. She gave him a forced smile, trying to show that everything was fine. Then memories began surfacing. Memories of days spent with this old man. The girl's face became a complex set of expressions for a moment. Then she said, "Uncle Andrew?"

"Yes?"

"Would you buy me a coffin so I can bury Grandpa properly?" asked Maureen.

"Eh? Ah... of course, Maureen," he answered, pausing only briefly in surprise. Then he clapped the girl on the shoulder, shaking her slightly with the force. "It's no problem. A small thing."

A small thing. Commoner's coffins were not elaborate things. Just a box constructed of thin planks, but still, cheap enough to be within a blacksmith's purse. The crowd began to break up and drift away, back to their daily lives. Tragedy was an everyday event in the slums. This was just one more event that would provide fodder for gossip over the coming days, nothing more. Life went on. Soon the sounds of the lane returned, the clanking of hammer on metal from the forge further down the lane, the wailing cries of a baby, the squealing of a wheel as a cart rolled past.

* * *

She headed inside to sort through the old man's belongings.

Daylight penetrated the hut through the gaps between the wooden planks, casting a series of bright lines across the floor. A flattened straw pallet lay in the corner, and there were occasional scratching noises from within the walls as rats ran along their hidden paths.

Old John had left little behind him, a few silver coins, some broken odds and ends that he had probably never thrown out, and a small piece of white bread.

The bread was his gift from the day before. White bread was food of the nobility, it was hardly a treat to them, but to a slum child it was something that they wouldn't dare dream about. The small piece of bread sat there like a relic. Maureen reached out a hand to grab something with which to write down her inventory, then paused as she realised there was nothing. She noted the lack of writing equipment on her list.

Then the door kicked open, the wood frame shuddering beneath the force. "Maureen Olsen." A rough voice called out and a man barged through the doorway, a stocky build with a full beard. Angrey, a thug from the slums who made a living as a swindler and thief, and who preyed on the weak.

Maureen looked him over. He must have weighed twice as much as her thin 14 year old body, he was stronger and had a longer reach, and he had placed himself between her and the door, his feet caked in mud from walking through the slums.

"Angrey? What do you..." she began.

"I see the old ghost did leave something behind," he cut her off, staring at the coins and bread. "A bit of bread and a few coins. Surprising."

With no hesitation, Angrey grabbed both items.

"Those are mine," Maureen said.

"Yours?" Angrey asked, not even bothering to look at her. "Who says? I've seen them so now they're mine."

He crammed the entire piece of bread into his mouth, chewing it in three bites while looking at Maureen with a self-satisfied smirk. White bread, the food of noblemen. Then he stuffed the coins into his shirt pocket.

Maureen's face went white and her hands remained flat on the table where she had been sorting. Angrey dusted his hands off before giving her the reason for his visit. "The Duke's banquet is about to start. You need to get going, now."

Angrey spoke as though Maureen hadn't just lost a valuable meal, but the girl sat there, coldly patient beneath his bullying. "The Duke has decided to take this seriously," Angrey continued. "The guests are special, some kind of Mystics or other. If you're late, you'll regret it."

With that, Angrey kicked the door open again and strode out, patting his chest so the coins rattled in their pocket, laughing to himself.

* * *

Maureen Olsen, Duke Skran's maid. Friend to Richard, Duke Skran's second son, who was a year or two older than her. She could go to the manor first. Cause no trouble while everything was still so strange. The rest could wait.

She looked up the lane Angrey had taken, thinking about him. Angrey. Bully, afraid of the strong, knows nothing about the Duke's house. Otherwise he would never have dared lay a finger on her, nor stayed in the slums. She had plenty of ways to deal with him, when the time came.

Maureen took the dress down from its peg on the wall and pulled it over her head. It smelled strongly of lye soap and felt stiff as she walked. Then she headed off through the lanes towards the manor.

The lanes rose gradually, climbing out of the mud to a street paved with flagstones worn into hollows by years of traffic. Maureen counted her steps as she walked, keeping track of the distance.

The servants' entrance was cold, with draughts whistling down from somewhere above. The walls were decorated with chipped gilt work, and maids scurried silently along them. "Maureen! Over here!" Richard called out, his voice loud in the quiet hall. "There you are, what kept you?"

"Young Master Richard," Maureen nodded her head.

"Come on, hurry up," he said, grabbing her wrist and pulling her inside. "We've got Mystics visiting, legendary wizards, Father's bowing and scraping to them. It's going to be exciting!"

Richard's small fist closed around her wrist, tugging her along before she was ready. Maureen felt herself being pulled towards another hallway, decorated with more chipped gilt.
