"Evans has taken every last wizard seed of the Blue Ring," the man yelled, his face red with anger.

Particles of dark surged again as Evans formed in mid-air, laughing softly. "Ah, it's Ciri."

"Ciri, do you wish to fight?"

The two men shot towards the horizon.

[2 sources: out of sight.]

I can't keep track of them, Tally admitted in its own voice.

Keep track of Emily then, Maureen ordered silently.

Evans was too far away for anyone to see now, but his voice rolled back over the waters. "Emily, the defensive array. Don't let this trash frighten my little darlings that pull the ship. You know the consequences."

Emily lifted her skirt and scurried belowdecks.

She is afraid of disappointing him more than she is of those monsters, Tally said.

A faint grey light spread over the entire ship. The monsters rose twice as high as a man and hurled themselves again and again against it.

[Impact 13.] [Impact 21.] [Impact 47.] The deck hasn't rolled once, Tally noted.

My boots agree, Maureen answered silently. The wet planks beneath her feet had remained still since their arrival.

Each impact of the monsters against the grey shield sounded like a fist hitting a padded door—dull and flat. The spray from their blows ran down the barrier like rain on glass.

Maureen glanced around. None of the other wizard seeds moved. All eyes were tilted up to watch the monsters attack, and there was a new hunger on the faces of those around her. Her own hands had curled into fists, the fingernails digging into her palms.

Again the dark particles surged, and Evans reappeared. He was smiling, and in his hand he held a middle-aged man's head by the hair. Blood dripped from the neck and splattered on the deck's wooden planks.

> Head

Tally noted before she looked at it. [Ciri: named by Evans at the auction. Blue Ring.]

She thought of Scarlette stumbling away down the road with a message for this man from Evans, and Maureen felt her throat tighten.

"My lord," Emily stammered.

"Finally," Evans said. "You've actually done something right." She bowed her head, but Maureen saw her glance once at the Shadow Hunter's back.

The monsters were still attacking the ship. "What of those?" Emily asked.

Evans opened his mouth, and there was a hum that went out from the ship. The heads of the monsters exploded in a spray of red and white.

[Audio: below range. No data.]

Tally noted in its display. However, it also logged the vibration in Maureen's chest and the sensation of her ears being pushed into her head. This is how a weapon that you can't hear sounds, Tally explained.

The surviving monsters turned and fled, a tide of finned blue bodies moving through the green water. They shouted guttural words as they left, Ma'er, Kaka, Jumili, and Evans murmured softly, "Infrasound modified from a sea-hawk harpy. Quite deadly against sea creatures."

Sea-hawk harpy? Tally asked, adding the name to its list of spells it had heard. Yes, Maureen told it silently. Keep the question mark. I don't know what it is either.

Evans drifted back over the ship, and the wizard seeds scattered in chaos. In the confusion, Maureen had managed to grab two or three portions from the black-armoured knights. She gestured for Richard and Aaron to leave as well, but neither boy seemed able to do more than nod.

The coppery smell of blood remained by the railing, and the feeling of cold on the back of her neck remained all the way down below.

* * *

In her cabin, she divided up the food into five servings, and placed the girls' shares in front of Lucy and Alice first. Then Richard and Aaron and the girls went off to their own cabins, and she was alone.

The tallow stub smoked at the end of the wick. The blankets were damp with salt. The deck rose and fell gently with the slow breathing of the beast beneath.

Maureen Olsen stood in the doorway, looking within. Her eyes went to each piece of furniture in turn while Tally ran off its inventory in her head. The false floor set over the bilge gap, the chains that held the bunk in place, the storm chain, the bent iron hooks, the grappling claw.

She strung the bell cord across the door, armed the sleeve crossbow at her cuff, and laid the Cross Sword by her hand.

And then Tally spoke to her in its warm voice, telling her what it always told her before she slept. It could not watch over her when she slept, but if there were danger it would hear what she heard.

That is enough, Maureen told it silently.
She lay down on top of her blankets, fully dressed, the Seal snug in her inner pocket, the collar pulled up over the eye-mark. She slept lightly.

* * *

Despairing screams roused her from sleep. Her heart slammed as her hands automatically found the sleeve crossbow. A guttural tongue roared, and Tally matched it against the recording it had made that afternoon. The weight of the footfalls was heavy, too heavy, and Tally's alert lit her display.

[Warning: sea monsters, by sound. Est. range under 30 m, closing.]

The door burst open with a crash as the three to five monsters barreled through. They bellowed their triumph, blue-skinned faces leering.

> Floor. Now.

Maureen kept herself back against the bunk, out of range of their claws.

 She felt the floor give beneath the monsters' feet, their legs dropping halfway through the gap into the bilge. Her chains snapped taut, the bent iron hooks at their ends ripping deep furrows across the monsters' skulls. The creatures roared like a beast in pain. The stink of the bilge mingled with the smell of fish-oiled hide and the monsters' bellowing roars. Only their thick hides kept them alive. More creatures ran in at the noise, two or three more, and Maureen pulled out the Seal and spoke the words quietly.

Vonmenjaye Soss Orenmosa.

I didn't have to correct your tongue this time, Tally said.

You can brag about that later, she told it silently as red, thorny vines with pale eyes closed around her body, leaving only her eyes visible.

One, two, three... Tally counted off the seconds in her head as the vines lashed outward, binding the monsters in place and crushing them to pulp. Four, five, six... seven, eight...

Nine seconds. A fraction of a second less than her limit. She cut off the flow of power and the armour fell away from her, leaving her standing on the cabin floor. There was a wet crunch, then nothing but dripping in the pitch darkness.

[Drain on host: moderate. Seal glow: dimmed.]

Tally said the Seal had paid for the spell from its own store but did not have any data on how much remained. She slipped it back into her pocket without answering. The ache behind her ribs seemed lighter than she would have expected and that scared her even more. She pulled her collar up over the eye-mark.

From a fist-sized dent in the bulkhead Tally provided the information: [Est. ≥ Middle Knight]. It reminded her that she was one Middle Knight in a corridor full of them.

Evans's voice filled the ship. "I hope you're having a pleasant evening. I've taken the liberty of placing some sea monsters aboard your vessel to liven things up. They're quite fond of humans. In fact, they find them delicious. I believe that they're quite hungry." Maureen's teeth set on edge. "Slaughter," she whispered.

Tally did not argue the point. Richard's door is three steps away.

Maureen ran for it.

A monster's fist smashed into Richard's wall where his head had been a moment before, as he jumped away from the creature. The beast drew back one of its massive legs to kick him.

> Left leg. Wide.

Maureen went wide.

The Cross Sword flashed silver, taking off half the creature's head. "M-Maureen!" Richard forced a smile.

"Richard, come on. We're joining Aaron and the girls." The two of them hacked their way through the monsters blocking the corridor.

* * *

Aaron was holding his own, but one monster had cornered Lucy and Alice in their cabin. Maureen, Richard and Aaron were just in time, the Cross Sword cleaving the blue-skinned beast's hide like paper.

The five of them moved from hiding place to hiding place in silence. Blood-soaked wooden planks squelched beneath their feet. Lamps swung from the ceiling, casting eerie shadows across the ship's timbers. Every time a monster found them, they killed it.

> Group, six plus. Starboard. Wait.

Maureen held her friends back with one raised hand. Through the wood she heard heavy footfalls. Tally kept count on her display.

Six monsters. By the light of swinging lamps, Maureen saw one of the beasts grab a seed by both legs and rip him in half.

Another monster punched its fist into a girl's chest, pulled out her still-beating heart, and stuffed it into its mouth. Blood ran down its blue chin.

There were bursts of wizard light. Tally highlighted a line that appeared to hang in front of Maureen's eyes.

> Anna and Rheas. Guess. They've been draining their items nonstop for some time. It will damage them.

Maureen knew what that meant. Whoever was using the item was draining it continuously without pause. That would damage the item permanently, rendering it useless. It meant that whoever was using the magic didn't care anymore.

For an hour, the five of them crouched behind stacks of crates in one of the ship's holds. The air smelled of wet timber and brine, while the sound of heavy feet stomping past was deafening. Lucy's cold fingers remained locked on Maureen's sleeve.

Inside Maureen's head, Tally spoke in its warm voice, meant for her ears alone, keeping her company as the minutes ticked past in the dark. Tally counted the four other breaths with soft precision until the footsteps faded away.

* * *

At dawn, the ship fell quiet. All the monsters were gone. The holds were littered with blood, organs, and broken bodies.

No one vomited, or even sat down. The survivors walked out onto the deck in silence, their arms covered in blood up to their elbows, their hair matted and damp with gore.

"Maureen," Aaron said softly.

She shook her head, and didn't say anything.

Tally spoke up unprompted, counting the deck sector by sector, before displaying a single number for Maureen's eyes only. [129]. Then, gently, [547].

Maureen did not respond.

"Well," Evans said, smiling and standing out in his black robe, "I must admit I am pleasantly surprised at how many of you survived. It makes playing with those monsters worthwhile."

A deep red flame issued from his finger, incinerating the monster corpses into ash. Tally dutifully logged the colour, noting that it matched the fire around Ciri.

Emily frowned. "Why did you cull them? We wanted seeds..."

Evans turned his warm smile on her. "Questioning me is such a bad habit, Emily. We want wizard seeds, not trash." He looked over the remaining boys and girls, all dressed in torn black robes. "This batch is actually quite good. Congratulations on living. And here is your reward. Welcome to the Dark Forest!"

A thin black coastline stretched ahead. The ship docked, and the survivors made their way onto land without a word. Evans followed, drifting down like some huge black bird.

Once they were on the sand, Richard turned back, looking at the ship. His expression was troubled.

Maureen patted him on the shoulder. "Come on, Richard."

He nodded and turned away from the ship.

The black robes moved further inland until they disappeared. Behind them, the ship remained anchored, gulls circled overhead. The sun rose, the day promising to be a fine one. The wind blew in off the ocean, carrying the scent of ash.
