*Held breath 2.1 seconds,* Tally counted for Maureen, *then exhaled through nose. Pulse jump at throat, slowing already. Composure reasserting itself. And you just rubbed your nose,* Tally said.

Maureen’s hand dropped back to her side. Richard’s face had none of its own mannerisms or habits. That tiny motion had been all hers. An unconscious gesture from her days in Jing Sheng’s lab, when an experiment had come out the way she’d bet on.

*You do that when you win,* Tally said.

*I do not.*

*Yes, you do. The seventh time on file.*

Denise recovered herself. She tilted her head, letting a touch of amused speculation creep into her eyes. “The Pharmacy Department’s rising star. And under the robe, Night Owl. Half the cellars in Emilia would pay for that.”

“And the heir of the Oserroba house keeps a seat at a guild table under a tavern,” Maureen replied in Richard’s flat voice. She smiled. “The families would pay more.”

They stood there for a moment, smiling at each other like two fencers, blades touching before the bout began. Two agents, laying their cards down one at a time. Each checking how many the other already held.

Denise laughed again, this time a real laugh, and stepped back, waving Maureen into the room. There was the scrape of metal as she shot the bolt behind her, and her shoulders finally relaxed. For the first time since they’d met in the corridor.

*You didn’t need to tense those,* Tally reminded her. *You matched her breathing and the set of her shoulders to Nightingale’s the instant she walked into the corridor. Denise Oserroba, confirmed. Codename: Nightingale.*

Denise poured two cups of something dark from an earthenware jug. It smelled of cloves and old money. Maureen looked around the room, noticing for the first time the trunks stacked in one corner. Good leather, brass corners, polished bright by the hands of generations of servants.

“So the delegation from your family came regarding the realm.”

“Such a thing that size cannot be hidden,” Denise said, not even bothering to hedge. “Everyone knows about the feast. Now the fighting begins over who gets a chair at the table.” She sat back with a cup of the dark liquid, waving for Maureen to do the same. “The ancient realm is thick with resources, and its wizard energy runs unusually active. This is good for cultivation. So it will be carved up between East and West, between orders, and within every order. Our elders are negotiating with the Alliance even now, and I hear your leadership will begin their own division soon.”

*Got it,* Tally said, filing it away under Denise’s account. *One source only. Checking against Ambrose’s eastern hearsay and Gregory’s imprint. He didn’t say much about this, just that only those wizards powerful enough left a realm behind. Yes, consistent on every line.*

Maureen let the talk of quotas and shares wash over her like weather. In this tower, Richard’s name opened doors, and she didn’t expect this one to remain closed.

She set her cup down. “And our deal? You wanted me at your family’s house for a while.”

“Once our elders and your leadership have shaken hands,” Denise answered. “Then we make for my family. Before the realm opens, if we’re lucky.”

Denise’s eyes slid to the fire as she said it. She exhaled, a long, careful breath, and her hand rose to her sleeve, two fingers worrying at the hem.

*Exhale, hand to sleeve,* Tally said. *Whatever’s waiting for us at her family’s house, she’s afraid of it.*

*Yes, I know,* Maureen told her silently. *Don’t ask her about it yet.*

*I wasn’t going to,* Tally said. *Just making a note of it so the two of us can ask about it together later.*

Maureen nodded, turning back to Denise and chatting about inconsequential matters as if she’d noticed nothing amiss. When her cup was empty, she rose and bid the woman goodbye. The corridor outside bit cold after the warmth of the fire as she made her way back to her own dormitory.

* * *

News of the delegation from Denise’s family rippled through the Alliance’s middle and upper ranks over the next few days. By the morning when the news was to be announced, nearly every Official in the great hollow tower had heard the story. They knew of the ancient realm that Denise’s family had come to bargain over. Sentences ended abruptly as someone approached down the corridors, only to start again once they passed. Pharmacists who’d never cared much about politics were standing in their doorways, counting on their fingers how many apprentices their orders might be able to send.

Maureen sat on one of the dark wood benches as Wizard Richard, staring out at the packed assembly hall. The benches creaked whenever someone shifted their weight. Beeswax candles fought bravely against the tallow smoke pouring up from the lower rows, and a thin light like frosted white fell from the high windows. An Alliance speaker stood at the front of the chamber, his dry voice reading aloud the decision. Each member order would be given places within the realm. The weaker orders received about ten places, while the stronger orders got at least a hundred.

Then the speaker gave the High Ring White Tower’s number. Three hundred and thirty-four. A ripple went through the assembly, running along the benches like wind through a field of wheat.

In Maureen’s head, Tally was busy creating her table as the numbers fell. [Weak orders: ~10. Strong: 100+. High Ring White Tower: 334.] *The White Tower’s three-hundred and thirty-four places will likely be the largest single block in the realm. That’s Gus’s order,* Tally noted. *With his master hunting down whoever killed him.*

The quotas only applied to apprentices, the speaker continued. Official Wizards were allowed to enter the realm without restriction, this being their privilege as Officials. The speaker said it as if the assembled wizards needed reminding.

*You don’t have a quota either,* Tally told Maureen. *And no ceiling.*

*What about the Jadeleaf apprentices?* Maureen asked silently.

*Ten,* Tally said. *If they’re lucky.*

Along the lower rows, the heads of the small orders were slumping forward, their shoulders falling. Someone laughed too loudly from one of the back rows. Their places would carry the Alliance’s name, and that name would act as a wall protecting them from the wandering wizards who would otherwise pick them off individually.

Someone stood up in the topmost rows. He was a strong-order wizard, one of the big ones. His face reddened as he shouted, “We bleed for this Alliance, and now we hand our seats to orders that couldn’t hold a corridor? It isn’t worth it.”

A bench creaked as another man stood up, then another. The great assembly hall began to feel warm and close. Tallow smoke began to thicken beneath the high windows as voices piled upon voices.

At the head of the hall, Irina stood up. As the head of the Wizard Academy Alliance, she represented every wizard in attendance, but she also represented her own order, White Bird. She was a beautiful woman, middle-aged and full-bodied with a mature grace. She stood up unhurriedly and let her gaze sweep over the audience. Before she said a word, the air around her thickened and grew heavy with her power.

> Irina.
>
> White Bird.
>
> Above Barok.
>
> Above Maffie.
>
> No labelled sample at this height.

The display hovered in front of Maureen’s eyes, Tally’s voice sounding delighted and slightly shaken. *She’s the most powerful Official I’ve ever seen,* she told Maureen. *I’ve never held a density like this before, not even close. I’m going to have to build a whole new column.*

Irina’s gaze moved along the rows of the strong orders. “We decided this together,” she said calmly. “The argument is finished.”

Tally placed small pulse counters above each of the wizards who’d complained, and as Irina’s eyes moved across each of them, the numbers dropped, one by one.

*Not a single one of them argued back while their own eyes were open,* Tally noted dryly to Maureen.

Maureen felt something akin to a scientist’s respect. Irina hadn’t needed to cast a spell or raise her voice. Her power had simply been too great to ignore. Nothing wasted. Her own hands rested flat and still on her knees.

Irina was holding the weak orders close, making sure that they didn’t drift away. The alliance couldn’t be allowed to split down the middle beneath her.

* * *

The room that Maureen sat in was small. An ash table was scrubbed so often that it was almost pale. A beeswax lamp burned at a low level, and the scents of camphor and dried herbs drifted in from the workrooms. She sat at the table as Wizard Richard, but the door was left half open onto the cold stone corridor.

“Wizard Richard?” Joanna appeared in the doorway. Christine came with her, but stopped at the same doorway, half in and half out. She leaned against the frame, one shoulder resting against it.

Joanna wore the Jadeleaf colours. A faded green leaf was stitched upon a white robe, and one cuff showed signs of having been mended recently, but with careful stitching. Her back seemed straighter than Maureen remembered, and she did not fidget.

“Jadeleaf Ring goes into the realm,” Joanna said as she bowed. It was a pledge. “You’ll go in as you please. If there’s anything in there you need done, tell me, and our apprentices will see it done.”

Tally compared Joanna to her old file. The hands were steady, the voice lower in pitch, and the pulse was 82. When Maureen had seen Joanna in the tree outside Peaceridge, her pulse was 150. *Joanna had stopped asking,* Tally noted. *She’s offering now.*

*People change.* Maureen thought silently to Tally.

*Not usually this fast,* Tally responded. *She had help.*

*Was any of that help mine?* Maureen asked.

*Some.*

The girl that Maureen had met in the tree outside Peaceridge had been brave, but lost. Desperate, she’d been ready to bargain with everything she had. That girl was in this room somewhere, buried beneath the new woman. Someone who had learned to count.

“I put it in a letter,” Joanna continued. Her voice was her own, unadorned. “I wanted you to hear it from me. In the realm every apprentice of ours will obey you as they would obey me.”

Obey. At the word, Tally noticed Christine’s jaw set hard, her knuckles white where they gripped the doorframe, and a spike at her throat. Envy, Tally filed it, not proof. Maureen kept her eyes on Joanna and let it pass.

Before Joanna finished speaking, Christine pushed herself away from the doorway and disappeared. Her quick footsteps faded down the cold corridor. Joanna did not turn to look.

“The Ring?” Maureen asked.

Joanna’s answer did not come quickly. “The Steward hasn’t long. Your potions are all that’s keeping him alive now.”

Maureen’s eyes narrowed. That made Joanna acting head of the Jadeleaf Ring. As Senge lay dying, Joanna ran the affairs of the order, great and small.

While she spoke of Senge, Joanna’s hand rose to her chest, her thumb finding the embroidered leaf that marked her robe. She rested it there, as if the emblem needed holding up.

 *Log a miss. Mine. At the Ring’s dinner I gave him weeks, not months. I was wrong by months. Your potions did that.*

*You don’t mind?* Maureen asked silently.

*I like being wrong when it’s this sort of wrong,* Tally said.

There was a thread of cold running beneath Tally’s words. The potions were holding Senge up by his collar, keeping him alive past his time, and every vial only moved his date of death.

“I should be going,” Joanna said with another small bow, and turned to walk out of the Alliance.

*Soon,* Maureen thought, *the “acting” will disappear from Joanna’s title.*

Maureen sat alone in the little receiving room and considered the offer. Richard was the Pharmacy Department’s genius, and could ask even the Alliance itself for assistance, so a small order’s help was not worth much. The offer was more than practical—it was a gesture that tied the Jadeleaf Ring to her. She was already its honorary professor.

Maureen discovered that she did not resent being leaned on. How far the girl had come was warm in her chest.

*You’re proud of her,* Tally told her.

*No,* Maureen said silently. *I’m not.*

Tally logged it as not, but her tone made it sound like a yes.

* * *

Denise’s party was going to be leaving soon. Maureen intended to ride out with them. She went to Ambrose’s office to request leave from the Fourteenth Potion Group.

Ambrose sat behind his desk, writing under the frosted window. His silver hair was combed neatly into place, and the room smelled like camphor and old paper. Several rows of glass jars stood on the shelves against the walls, each filled with some root or other pickled specimen, and each catching and reflecting the grey light that came through the window.

“Sir, might I have permission to leave the Fourteenth Potion Group for a short time?” Maureen asked.

Ambrose’s pen stopped moving. He looked up at her over the tip of it, his face showing puzzlement. “Again, Richard?”

[Leave requests: 3.]

Ambrose paused for a full 0.9 seconds before he responded. *Ambrose is taking count as well,* Tally told Maureen. *Should I stop him?*

*Let him,* Maureen replied silently. *He’s earned it.*

*Ambrose is the only man in this building who can take your title away,* Tally responded.

“Very well,” Ambrose said, sighing. He set his pen down and stretched. “Viska can keep things running in the Fourteenth. Just come back soon— the leadership will find the realm’s entrance any day now, and then we get to go in. The Eastern envoys have arrived as well. Oran Olivia himself is leading them. Have you heard of him? Young genius, everyone’s talking about him. I haven’t met a wizard yet who’s sat across the table from him and come away unimpressed.”

Oran Olivia, Tally flagged. There were three Olivias in her files, Jay Olivia, Frey Olivia and Barok Olivia, all deceased. Philip Olivia was said to be looking for Maureen. As far as Tally knew, none of the Olivias had ever seen the Richard face.

A small cold touched the back of Maureen’s neck.

Her hands stayed loose at her side. Richard’s plain face showed polite interest.

Maureen smiled politely and let him talk. She’d run into geniuses before. Most of them had died in the Dark Forest’s trial realm with their points still on their hands.

Ambrose rose for the leadership meeting and Maureen walked out with him, falling into place half a step behind the sweep of his white-gold robe sleeve. The corridor curved inwards as it followed the tower’s spiral, and the light of the beeswax candles swam in a golden river along the highly polished stone floor. Their footsteps made only the barest whisper, the muted conversations from the balconies floating down from above.

As they reached the corner, Maureen could see a knot of people coming towards them along the curving corridor. Ambrose raised his chin. “There. That’s Oran Olivia. The Eastern wizard organizations sent him to speak for them.”

The man was young and handsome. His smile tilted oddly at one corner of his mouth, giving him a rakish look. He wore a long black robe, shot through with glittering golden threads, and it pooled around his feet, then trailed out behind him as he walked. The robe was designed to draw the eye, like a peacock’s tail, and he moved with the careless grace of a man walking through his own house.

*Tally, what do you know about him?*

*No file. Density reads Official-band, but I need some time with that. Very dense attendant at his shoulder, unnamed, worth watching.*

Maureen’s eyes locked onto Oran Olivia and would not move away. Something beneath her skin stirred and sat up.

Heat began to rise from deep inside her, slowly, almost lazily, like magma making its way up a volcanic vent. Her throat went as dry as dust, and something opened up behind her ribs, an emptiness that had nothing to do with hunger for food.

The floor seemed to tilt towards him, and a crimson tide pressed against the backs of her eyes. Every cell in her body felt like it was cheering and boiling all at once.

Voices rose from the blood, not her own, and not Tally’s. They were hoarse, and there were so many of them. She didn’t know how she knew, but somehow she knew what they were saying.

“Purgatory... Purgatory...”

Without a sound, Maureen reached inside to her friend.

*Tally?*

*I’m here, Maureen,* Tally replied. *That wasn’t me, that is not my voice. I can feel the surge through every line of your body, but I can’t translate it into data. There are no words in there. Just heat. But if you want me to count, I will count with you.*

*One, two,*

the voices began to crest, rising to a crescendo over Tally’s measured tones, her voice as slow and even as Silver Light breathing.

*Three, four,*

the voices rising in a crest over the top of Tally’s voice,

*five, six,*

louder now, shaking Maureen’s teeth,

*seven, eight,*

and now she could make out the words,

*nine, ten,*

and the words were loud enough to shake the bones in her head,

PURGATORY WORLD!

Maureen stood rooted at the corner of the corridor, with her blood roaring in her ears and her hands burning from within, and Tally’s calm counting continued beneath it all, a thread of sound in the chaos of her mind.
